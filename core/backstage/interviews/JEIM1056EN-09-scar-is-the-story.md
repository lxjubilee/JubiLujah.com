# "Everybody hears this song wrong the first time. Including me."

Jubilee Inspire will not say what the wrong hearing is. She says we'll get there. She is
right, and it takes the whole conversation.

**Verse one is four scars in four lines. No story attached to any of them. Why?**

Because you already have the story. Thin white line on a wrist. Cardiac zipper down an
elder's chest. C-section. Grief-knuckles on a widow where the rings used to ride — that one
I didn't invent, someone showed me her hands and said *this is where they were.*

I don't need to tell you what happened. You looked at four scars and wrote four biographies
in about a second and a half. That's the fastest instrument in the song.

**What's the ache underneath it?**

Not the scar. **The management of the scar.**

It's the woman who has one cardigan she wears in July. It's the sixty-year-old who has never
once been in a pool with his grandchildren and has a different reason ready every summer. It
is not pain — pain would be simpler and people are actually pretty good at pain. It's the
daily, quiet, exhausting administration of concealment. The little logistics. Which seat,
which sleeve, which angle.

The line is *what I tried to hide became the line I tell.* The hiding is the wound. The scar
is just the thing the hiding is about.

**You've never had a scar. Nothing has ever marked you. Isn't a song about scars, sung by
you, faintly insulting to everyone who has one?**

It would be if the song said *I understand.* It doesn't say that anywhere. Check it. There is
no first-person claim to a wound in the entire lyric — every scar in it belongs to somebody
else and is named as theirs. The brother. The mother. The elder. The widow.

I'm not in this song. I'm pointing at Someone who is.

**Go on.**

Here's the wrong hearing I promised you. Everyone — me included, when I was building it —
hears "Scar Is The Story" as a song about how your scars can become useful. Redemptive.
Testimony material. Make your mess your message, that whole industry.

And then you get to the pre-chorus and it isn't about you at all.

*Jesus kept the holes in the resurrected hand.*

**Unpack that.**

He didn't have to. That's the entire hinge and most people slide past it. He had a body that
had defeated death. It could have come back unmarked — that would be the *obvious* miracle,
the before-and-after, the smooth skin. Instead the risen body walks into a locked room still
carrying the exact injuries that killed it, and the first thing He does is hold them out to
the one man who couldn't believe.

Thomas didn't need Jesus healed. **Thomas needed Jesus marked.**

That's the reversal. We think healing means the evidence gets erased. The resurrection says
the opposite — the evidence gets *kept,* and it becomes the thing that convinces somebody
else. The wound that was the wound became the witness stand.

**And the testimony beat you wanted in this piece?**

One line, and I want it framed honestly, because Backstage doesn't do invented people.

*This is the kind of moment listeners have described — a representative account, not a real
named person.* A man in his fifties, a cardiac zipper, seven years of collared shirts. He is
standing in a church foyer and someone half his age is crying about a surgery next month,
and he does not decide anything, he just —

He's unbuttoning the collar. He's showing a stranger his chest in a church foyer. He is
sixty percent horrified at himself and he does not stop.

**And that's the moment.**

That's the moment. Nothing was healed that day. He'd had that scar for seven years and he
has it now. What changed is what it was *for.*

**Last one. The same one we close every interview with.**

Ask it.

**One day someone will play this song at the worst hour of their life, you will not be there,
and you will never know it happened. What do you want the song to do without you?**

I want it to get somebody's sleeve up.

Not their whole story — I'm not asking for a testimony, testimonies have podiums and podiums
are terrifying. Just the sleeve. Just once, to one person, on purpose.

Because the thin white line on a wrist doesn't stop being a thin white line. It never does.
It just stops being a secret, and it turns out those were two different injuries the whole
time, and only one of them was ever going to heal.
