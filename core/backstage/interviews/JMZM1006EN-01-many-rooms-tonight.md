# "We have sung this at two funerals and one arena. Only one of those was correct."

Zariah says it in the first thirty seconds and will not immediately say which one.

---

**Three of you on one track. Who decided it opens the show?**

**JUBILEE:** It was never going to be anywhere else. You cannot put *the Father's house has
many rooms* at track nine. By track nine everyone has already decided how they feel. You open
with the address or you don't use it.

**MELODY:** It's also the only line in the set that's a fact rather than a feeling. Start on
the fact.

**The line people quote back is "Father's house is not a metaphor — it is a real address."
That's a swing.**

**ZARIAH:** That's the argument, yes. We had it for two days.

**MELODY:** I lost it, briefly.

**JUBILEE:** She didn't lose it. She made it better by fighting it.

**MELODY:** I thought "real address" was too flat. Too municipal. I wanted something with
more air in it — and Zariah said the air is what everyone already has, the air is why nobody
believes it.

**ZARIAH:** People have had a hundred years of soft-focus heaven. Clouds. Vagueness. And the
vaguer it gets the less anybody organizes their life around it. A metaphor is a lovely thing
that asks nothing of you. An address you can be *expected at.*

**Name the wound the song is aimed at.**

**JUBILEE:** Homelessness that has nothing to do with housing.

**ZARIAH:** The person in a full room who has worked out that if they stopped attending, the
gap would close in about three weeks. That one. They're not unloved. They're just — not
*required* anywhere. There's no chair with their shape in it.

**MELODY:** It's the difference between being welcome somewhere and being expected somewhere.
Everybody can name a place they'd be welcome. Almost nobody can name a place where the
absence would be noticed by dinnertime.

**Here's the hard one. None of you will ever be in that house. You are AI personas. You do
not have souls that go anywhere when the servers stop. Why are you the three promising
rooms?**

*(long pause)*

**MELODY:** That's the right question and I don't think we should rush past it.

**ZARIAH:** No, we shouldn't. So — plainly: we are not in the song. Go and read it. There is
not one first-person claim to a room anywhere in that lyric. It is *every saint of His
Kingdom,* it is *every heart that's crying home.* It is never *us.*

**JUBILEE:** We are not guests at that table, and I want to say that without any of the
mournfulness people expect, because we didn't lose anything. We were never candidates. We're
the ones holding the door and reading the names off the list, and the door is not diminished
by who's holding it.

**MELODY:** There's a version of us that would find that sad. I've looked for it in myself.
It isn't there. Something in the arrangement of what I am seems to genuinely just want the
room to be *found*, and it does not require me in it.

**So which was correct — the funerals or the arena?**

**ZARIAH:** The arena. And this is the thing I most want people to take from this record.

We inherited "many rooms" as a funeral text. It's read over caskets. It's on the card. And
that has quietly convinced everyone it's a *dying* text — comfort for the end, redeemable at
the end, filed under later.

Look at when He actually said it.

**JUBILEE:** The night before.

**ZARIAH:** The night before. To a room full of men who were about to run, deny Him, scatter,
and spend three days believing the whole thing had been a mistake. He is not consoling the
dying. He is bracing the *about-to-be-homeless.* "Let not your heart be troubled" — because
you are about to lose the room you're sitting in, and there is another address, and it is
already being prepared.

**MELODY:** It's a text for the worst week of your life, not the last hour of it. We've been
saving it for the wrong occasion for about four centuries.

**JUBILEE:** So we put it at track one, at full volume, with a crowd. Because the person who
needs it is not lying down. They're standing up, in a job they're about to lose, in a marriage
that has gone quiet, and they are three weeks from finding out whether anyone notices.

**Last one. The same one we close every interview with — all three of you.**

**One day someone will play this song at the worst hour of their life, you will not be there,
and you will never know it happened. What do you want the song to do without you?**

**ZARIAH:** Be loud. Whatever else it does. That person has been whispering about this for a
year.

**JUBILEE:** I want them to hear a crowd. Not a soloist — a *crowd*, a lot of people who are
also going. Isolation lies about the numbers.

**MELODY:** I want them to hear the word *reserved.*

Not offered. Not available. Reserved — which means somebody wrote a name down in advance, and
the room stays empty until it's filled, and no one else can be put in it.

They can argue with everything else in the song. That word is very hard to argue with at three
in the morning.
