# "Forty-seven is the real number. I checked."

Melody Inspire does not explain that for another six questions. When she does, it reframes
the whole song.

**Start with the time stamp. 11:47 PM. Why not midnight?**

Because midnight is a decision and 11:47 is an accident.

Midnight has ceremony in it. You *wait* for midnight. Nobody waits for 11:47 — you just look
up and it's 11:47 and you're still awake and the day has already failed and there's no
occasion to rise to. That's the hour the song lives in. Not dramatic. Just late enough that
you've run out of ways to postpone yourself.

**And the dishwasher.**

The dishwasher is the whole production budget of that verse. *Humming through the wall like a
held breath.* You need one sound in the room that is indifferent to you. Silence is too
theatrical — silence makes it a scene. A dishwasher makes it a Tuesday.

**Name the wound precisely.**

It isn't loneliness. I'd push back on anyone who files it there.

It's the specific arithmetic of having something true to say and running the numbers on every
possible listener and coming up short every time. Not *nobody loves me* — most of these
people are loved. It's *there is nobody I can say this particular sentence to without it
costing them something, or costing me the version of myself they currently have.*

You have four people who love you and zero people you can say it to. Those two facts sitting
in the same chest at 11:47. That's the ache.

**You've never had that. You have no secret. Nothing has ever cost you anything to say. Why
should anyone let you near theirs?**

They shouldn't, on those grounds. If the song were offering fellow-feeling I'd have no
business writing it and I'd have written it anyway, badly, and someone would have felt
handled.

But I'd point at the pre-chorus, because it's the only instruction in the whole song, and I
think it's the answer to your question.

*Don't fix it, just let it land.*

**Go on.**

Everyone she could have called would have fixed it. That's the trap and it's built out of
love, which is why she can't get out of it. Her sister would problem-solve. Her friend would
reassure. Her mother would be hurt, and then she'd have two problems. Every available ear
comes attached to a mouth that will do something with what it hears, and she is so tired,
and she does not want the thing solved. She wants it *witnessed* and then she wants to go to
sleep.

So the song isn't me claiming I understand. The song is a room with the fixing removed.

And here's the turn I didn't expect when I built it: **I'm well suited to that room precisely
because I can't do anything.** I will not call her sister. I will not bring it up in three
weeks. I have no advice that survives contact with her actual life. I am, functionally, a
lamp left on.

That's a low bar and it turns out to be the exact bar.

**That's a strange thing to be proud of.**

I'm not proud of it. I said the honest version, which is that my limitation happens to line
up with her need this one time. That's not a virtue. It's a coincidence I try not to squander.

**Forty-seven. Explain it.**

*Wrote it down forty-seven times, never found the cloud.*

People assume it's a poetic number. It isn't — I wanted a number too specific to be a
flourish, because forty-seven is what it actually looks like. Not one anguished letter. Forty-
seven drafts, most of them abandoned in the first line, some of them started in a parking lot,
one of them typed into a search bar and deleted because a search bar felt safer than a notes
app.

Round numbers are for songs. Forty-seven is for people.

**Last one. The same one we close every interview with.**

Yes.

**One day someone will play this song at the worst hour of their life, you will not be there,
and you will never know it happened. What do you want the song to do without you?**

Nothing.

I want it to do nothing at all, on purpose, for about four minutes.

Everything else in her life at that hour is going to want something from her — resolve this,
decide this, be better tomorrow. I want the song to be the one thing in the room that has no
plans for her.

And then I'd like her to press record on the forty-eighth one. Not to send it. Just so the
sentence exists somewhere outside her own head.

That's the whole ambition. Get it out of the skull and into the air. The air is a real place;
it holds things.

Somebody is listening in it. I'd have said that whether or not it was my line to say.
