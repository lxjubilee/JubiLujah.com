# She underlined it at nineteen and it meant nothing.

That's the part she wants said first. Not that it comforted her and then stopped. That it
never worked at all, for twenty-two years, and she has the pen mark to prove it.

---

She was a good student of the faith and that turned out to be the problem.

Nineteen, a proper study Bible with the ribbon, four different coloured pens, the whole
apparatus. She underlined *I will give thee the treasures of darkness* in a careful straight
line with a ruler, because that is who she was, and she wrote a small neat note in the margin
that she is now embarrassed by and will not quote.

She had, at nineteen, no darkness whatsoever. She'd had a hard week once. The verse went into
the collection the way you press a flower — a pretty thing, filed, admired, structurally
irrelevant.

**And here is the ache, which took her two decades to be able to name: she had built an entire
faith out of verses that had never been tested, and she did not find that out until she needed
one of them to hold weight.**

Because when the dark came — and she is deliberately not itemising it here, and asks that
nobody assume which kind — she went to the shelf, and she opened to the page, and she found
her own neat nineteen-year-old underlining waiting for her like a note from a stranger who had
never met her.

And it did nothing. Not one thing. She has described sitting on a bathroom floor with a book
open on her knees reading a sentence she had personally marked as important and feeling
absolutely nothing move.

She said afterward: *I thought I'd lost my faith. What I'd actually found out was that I'd
never spent it.*

---

The song did not console her. She is very clear that it did the opposite for about four
minutes.

*There is a verse I underlined years before I understood.*

She has said she stopped it and played it again, because she had assumed — for twenty-two
years — that she was the only person on earth who had done that.

Then the pre-chorus.

*The dark is not the absence of You, the dark is where You store the things You have been
saving.*

**That was the moment everything changed**, and what it changed was one preposition.

---

The turn, in her words.

Every single thing I'd been taught put God at the *end* of the dark. Hold on, endure, keep
walking, and He'll be there when it lifts. Which means that while you're in it you are, by the
arrangement's own logic, alone — and the whole enterprise depends on the lifting.

Mine wasn't lifting.

That line doesn't say He's waiting at the end. It says the dark is *where He keeps things.*
It's a storeroom. It's not the corridor between you and Him, it's a room He is already in and
has been stocking.

I'm sitting in a car outside my own house at eleven at night and I'm working out that if that
is true, then the last three years were not an interruption of my life with Yahuah. They were
a location in it.

I have never in my life been so angry and so relieved in the same ninety seconds.

---

She went home and got the Bible and found the page and underlined it a second time.

Same verse. Different pen — a cheap biro, nothing ceremonial — and she pressed so hard that it
scored the paper and you can feel the groove from the other side. Two lines under one sentence
now, twenty-two years apart, in inks that don't match.

She has been asked whether the second underlining was the real one and she says no, and gets
slightly sharp about it.

The first one had to be there. That's the whole point. If she hadn't marked it at nineteen,
as a pretty thing, filed and untested, there'd have been nothing waiting on the page when she
finally arrived — and Yeshua does not appear to be in a hurry about the gap between the two.

She thinks now that the nineteen-year-old was doing something more useful than she knew.

She was leaving supplies in a room she had not yet been sent to.

---

The dark has not fully lifted. She'd want that in this account, because she has stopped
reading testimonies that end with the lifting, and she has said, more than once, that they are
written by people standing in the daylight describing a country they have left.

But she reads by a low lamp now, most nights, and she has stopped waiting to be let out before
she is allowed to be given anything.

*Riches that the daylight could not.* Not riches *despite* the dark.

Could not. Two different things, and she says the whole song lives in the difference.
