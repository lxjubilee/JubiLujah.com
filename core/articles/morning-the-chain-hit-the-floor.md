I want you to imagine a single sound: metal hitting the ground.

The exact moment a chain that has been on someone's wrists for twenty years slides off and lands on the floor. That sound is the whole reason this album exists. I made a gospel-reggae record, hand drum, skank guitar, warm bass on the one, brass stabs, because freedom needs a groove you can throw your hands up to. The mountain taught me the quiet. But some mornings, when the chain finally comes off, quiet is not enough. Some mornings you need a drum.

## Why I Made a Freedom Record

I have watched too many people try every key the world has on offer. The bottle. The powder. The reform program. The willpower. The religion of just trying harder to walk out of your own broken storm. And I have watched the locks refuse to turn, one after another, until a person is convinced their cell has no door at all.

That is who I made this for. Because the gospel is not self-improvement. It is a jailbreak. On the opener, *Chains Off, Hands Up*, I sing about a man who comes to the river with iron on his hands, and **Yeshua** does not lecture him or turn him away. He just bends down and breaks the metal like clay. *What the bottle could not give, what the shame had welded shut, Jesus opened with His hand.*

## What the Title Means

The chorus of the whole record is two motions: chains off, hands up. The chain comes down; the hands go up. That is the shape of the freed life.

And it stands on the sermon **Yeshua** preached in His own hometown, when He unrolled the scroll of Isaiah and read: *the Spirit of the Lord is upon me... to proclaim liberty to the captives... and the year of the Lord's favor* (Luke 4:18-19, from Isaiah 61). That last phrase is the whole album. The year of the Lord's favor is the Jubilee, the year in Leviticus 25 when every debt was forgiven, every slave set free, every lost inheritance returned home. On *Jubilee Drum*, I take that ram's-horn command and set it to a reggae bass, because *Christ is our Jubilee, the year that never ends.* Once every fifty years the trumpet sounded in Israel. But in **Yeshua** the Jubilee never stops.

## The Heart Behind the Songs

Let me take you through the jailbreak, because it happens key by key.

*Walk Out of the Cell* is Peter in prison in Acts 12, the angel comes, the chains hit the floor, the sandals go on, and the iron gate of the city swings open on its own. And here is the line I built it on: *he thought he was dreaming, the dream was the chain. The freedom was real and it had a name.* Whatever **Yeshua** opens, no one closes again. *No Going Back* is the resolution of the freed man, straight out of Romans 6: the old self drowned in the baptismal water, and I am not crawling back to pick up what He took off me.

But the song at the center, the one I named this whole article for, is *The Key Was His Blood.* It opens stripped down to a single hand-drum heartbeat, no bass, no brass, and it tells the truth every other key failed to tell:

> The key was not silver. The key was not gold.
> The key was His blood. Let the story be told.

That is Peter's own word: *you were ransomed... not with perishable things such as silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ* (1 Peter 1:18-19). I asked the elder, the counselor, the doctor, the friend. They could all help, but they could not mend. Only the Lamb who was slain could turn the key in the lock of time. He turned it from the other side. The cell of my shame is opened wide.

## The Groove of the Redeemed

I did not want this record to only mourn the chain. I wanted it to *dance* about the freedom.

So *Reggae for the Redeemed* is a pure celebration, a party for the bought-back, standing on the truth of Revelation 5:9 that He redeemed people *from every tribe and language and people and nation.* Let the elders sing it, let the young men shout it, let the children answer, let the islands and the plains join the reggae of the broken chains. And *Rise Up Standing* takes Ephesians 2 straight to the dance floor: we were *dead in our trespasses,* and God, *rich in mercy, made us alive together with Christ*, the dead do not stay dead when **Yeshua** is Lord.

*Hands Up High (Thank You, Jesus)* is the gratitude explosion, hands that were tied with the rope of my own choice, now raised to the sky, because the Carpenter took these hands in His own and taught them to lift again. And *Whole Again* leans on Joel's promise, *I will restore to you the years the locust has eaten* (Joel 2:25), because the Healer does not only break the chain. He heals the wrist where the chain left its mark.

## Who This Is For

This is for the one in the cell right now who is convinced there is no door.

For the addict at the end of the line. For the elder carrying the wound of forty years. For the young woman silenced by what was done to her. *Bondage Broken* is my own testimony on this record, the same chain finding its way back at twenty-three, thirty-three, forty-three, and the **Yeshua** who came down to the very place I thought was past His reach, and stayed in my breach. If you are hearing this and the chain is still on, hear the bridge of that song: *He's coming to your river. He has not gone.*

The album ends where every freedom ends, *Free in the Morning*, the sun coming up on a freed man, walking out into the light with the One who broke the chain in the dark. Whom the Son sets free is *free indeed* (John 8:36). Not a maybe-free. Not a slowly-free. Free in the blood, free in the name, free in the morning, never the same. Throw your hands up. The chain is already on the floor.
