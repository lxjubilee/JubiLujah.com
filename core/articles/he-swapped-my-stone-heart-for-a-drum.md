For years my heart was beating hard for nothing. The music, the dream, the chain hanging out of my shirt under the lights, the weight of a fame that never once weighed anything real. My pulse was fast and it was pointed at a hundred empty things, and I thought that was just what it meant to be young and alive.

Then, on a Friday night, at a youth concert with the bass cabinet out on the sidewalk shaking the chain-link fence, the kick hit the one and something in my chest answered it. And I heard **Yeshua**, quiet under all that volume: *Son, your heart is Mine forever more.* I did not get quieter that night. I got *louder.* Because I finally had something worth the beat.

**Corazón Latino (Late Para Ti)**, *Latin Heart (It Beats for You)*, is the record that came out of that night. It is a dembow album, boom-ch-boom-chick, sub-bass you feel before you hear it, and every song is built on one discovery: God did not calm my heart down. He gave me a *new one.*

## What the title means

*Corazón Latino* means "Latin Heart." And *Late Para Ti*, that is the part that undoes me, means "It Beats for You." Not *it beats for the party,* not *it beats for the grind.* It beats *for You,* meaning for **Yeshua.**

I built this on dembow on purpose, because dembow is the heartbeat of a whole generation of young Latinos, the rhythm in the earbuds on the train, the thump out the Honda in the church parking lot. And a heartbeat is exactly the right picture, because the promise God made through Ezekiel is a heart transplant: *"A new heart also will I give you… and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh"* (Ezekiel 36:26). He did not renovate my old stone heart. He took it out. He put in one that could feel, one that could beat, one that had a rhythm, and the rhythm was Him.

The opening track says the thesis in one line I still shout:

> A hundred reasons for the heart to beat, but only One is the truth, Christ is the rhythm.

## Why I had to make it

Because I watched it happen to the people I love, and I could not keep it to myself.

There is a verse in that first song about my cousin. She used to listen to the same dembow on the train, and her heart was in pain. Now Cristo is on her playlist and the pain is gone. There is another cousin who used to play the dice game on the stoop and now plays bass for the worship band. And there is the kid with the chain still hanging out of his shirt. I did not make him hide the chain. I just watched it start pointing at a different cross. *The heart that was beating for nothing, beats for Christ somehow.*

That is what I needed the world to hear. **Yahuah** is not in the business of giving young people a smaller life with the volume turned down. He is in the business of taking a heart that was pounding away for empty things and re-aiming every single beat. King David knew he could not manufacture that himself, so he begged for it: *"Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me"* (Psalm 51:10). That is not a request for behavior modification. That is a request for a transplant. And **Yeshua** grants it.

## The songs, and where the beat points

"Mi Corazón Despierta", *My Heart Wakes Up*, is the 6 AM song. The alarm hits on the kick, the Bible on the nightstand is still open from last night, the tile is cold underfoot, and God says *wake up, life is real and rare.* The new heart does not sleep in.

"Boom-Ch-Boom Para Cristo" is the most unashamed thing on the record. I take the signature dembow pattern itself and hand it to **Yeshua.** Paul told us to *"present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God"* (Romans 12:1), and I decided that if my body is the offering, then the rhythm my body already loves belongs on the altar too. The beat is not the enemy. The beat is the offering.

Then the album widens the heart outward. "Mi Amor Es Tuyo" and "El Corazón de la Familia" take that same pulse to the dinner table, the mother's hand reaching across, the friend's text at 11 PM that says *praying for you,* the neighbor across the hall. Because **Yeshua** said the second command sits right next to the first: *"Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself"* (Matthew 22:39). A heart that beats only for itself is still a stone heart with better production. The new heart beats *out.*

And "Late Por Otros", *Beats for Others*, is where it lands. A heart of flesh cannot stay private. It starts beating for the cousin who hasn't heard, for the friend on the subway you slip an earbud to, for the whole block.

## The quiet track in the middle of the loud album

There is one song I made deliberately soft, a dembow-bachata that pulls the whole thing down to a whisper. The bedroom ceiling at 11 PM, the streetlight through the blinds, headphones on the pillow with no music playing, the Bible closed tonight. Because sometimes the new heart still aches, still lies awake, still forgets to open the Book. I wanted that honesty on the record. Even David, with his transplanted heart, still cried out like the deer that *"panteth after the water brooks"* (Psalm 42:1). A new heart is not a heart that never thirsts. It is a heart that finally knows *Who* it is thirsty for.

And then Sunday comes, "Domingo en la Mañana", the coffee steaming, the car stereo on the way to church, the whole congregation's voices stacking up, because the new heart was never meant to beat alone. The Scripture is plain about it: *do not forsake "the assembling of ourselves together"* (Hebrews 10:25). Hearts keep better time in a crowd.

## Who this album is for

This one is for the Friday-night youth concert with the bass out on the sidewalk. For the second-generation kid with the chain and the airpods who thinks God only speaks in slow songs and old hymns. For the cousin on the train. For anyone whose heart has been pounding hard for a long time at a hundred empty things and is starting to suspect it was built to beat for something more.

Hear me: **Yeshua** is not asking you to flatline. He is offering you a transplant. He will take the stone out and hand you a heart with a beat in it, and then, I promise you, you will feel the kick hit the one, and you will know exactly Who your heart was made to run for.

*Corazón Latino.* It beats for You. Only for You. Come feel it start.
