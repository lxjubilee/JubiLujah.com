Every feast Israel ever kept had a last day.

However glorious the week had been, there came a morning when the booths were taken down and the branches were swept away, when the pilgrims folded their tents and turned back toward home and the roads out of the city filled with the quiet of a celebration ending. That is simply the nature of a feast in this age. Even the best of them close. The lamps go out, the guests scatter, and the long ordinary stretch of life resumes until the calendar comes round again. Every party we have ever thrown has carried, somewhere inside its joy, the small grief of knowing it would end.

Which is why a vision the prophet Zechariah was given lands like a thunderclap once you understand what you are reading. He looked all the way down to the far edge of history, past the last battle, past the day the King Himself sets His feet on the mountain and everything is made new, and there, in the age that never darkens, he saw a festival *still being kept.* Not once. Year after year. And not by Israel only, but by the survivors of every nation that had ever raised a fist against Yahuah. The one feast that outlives the world turns out to be a feast we already know.

## The prophet who looked past the end

Zechariah's vision is not gentle. It moves through a terrible day, the nations gathered against Jerusalem, the city on the brink, and then Yahuah Himself coming down to fight, His feet upon the mountain, the whole created order convulsing until day and night lose their meaning and living water flows out from the city in every direction. It is the end of one age and the birth of another. And when the smoke clears and the King is enthroned over all the earth, you brace for the prophet to describe thrones, or judgment, or the silence of a finished war.

Instead he describes a *feast.*

He says that everyone who is left of all the nations that came up against Jerusalem will go up, year after year, to worship the King, Yahuah of hosts, and to keep the **Feast of Booths.** Sukkot. The season of gladness. The harvest festival of branches and open-roofed shelters and commanded joy. The very feast that had always, in this age, ended and been packed away, the prophet sees it standing wide open in the age to come, kept forever, by a world full of nations streaming up the mountain to celebrate.

> Of all the days on the calendar He could have carried into eternity, Yahuah kept the happiest one. The age that never ends is not a solemn hush. It is the harvest feast that never has to close.

## Why *this* feast, and not another

Sit with why it would be Tabernacles, of all the feasts, that has no last day.

Booths was always the festival of ingathering, the moment the scattered fruit of a whole year's labor was carried home from the far fields and gathered into one place, everything that had grown out in the open finally brought in and shared. It was the feast where a nation left its solid walls and lived for a week under branches and open sky, remembering that its security had never been its own construction but Yahuah Himself, the God who had kept a homeless people alive in a wilderness that grew nothing. It was joy on purpose, the one appointed time where rejoicing was not merely permitted but *commanded.*

Now unfold all of that into forever. The age to come is the true ingathering, not one nation's harvest carried into one city, but a whole world's peoples gathered home from every corner of the earth. It is the true dwelling of Yahuah *with* His people, no longer under a fragile roof of leaves for a week but His own presence tabernacled among them without end. And it is joy no longer rationed to seven days, because the season of gladness has become the permanent weather of the new creation. Booths could carry into eternity precisely because Booths was always about the two things eternity is made of: *God dwelling with His people,* and *unending joy.* The other feasts pointed forward to what He would do. This one is the picture of what it is finally *like* when it is all done.

## The nations at the top of the hill

And do not rush past who is climbing that hill, because it is the detail that undoes me every time.

Zechariah does not say Israel keeps this endless feast alone. He says the *survivors of the nations* go up, the peoples who once came against the city, now streaming toward it to worship. The very ones who had been outsiders, enemies, foreigners to the covenant, are shown flowing up the mountain year after year with the joy of full pilgrims, keeping the feast alongside the people they once besieged. The circle that used to have a hard edge around one nation has been thrown open to the whole earth. The gladness that used to belong to Israel's harvest belongs now to a redeemed world.

This is the heartbeat under my very name, and I cannot say it without my voice lifting. The whole long story of Yahuah has been leaning toward a table with room for *everyone*, release so there could be gathering, the horn sounded so the scattered could come home, the door of favor held open until people from every language had time to walk through it. And here, at the summit of history, the prophet shows us the finished thing: no nation missing, no people too far off, all of them together on the same hill, keeping the same joyful feast, worshiping the same King.

The vision even names a sober note, those who will not go up to keep it will find no rain on their fields, and I will not pretend that away. But hear what the warning assumes. It assumes a world so thoroughly at feast that to *miss* it is the strange and sorrowful thing. In the age to come, staying home from the celebration is the aberration. The default state of a redeemed creation is a nation on the road, branches in hand, climbing up to rejoice.

## Come up to the feast that never ends

So let me speak to whatever part of you has been quietly bracing for the joy to end.

You have learned, the way we all learn, that good things close. That the gathering scatters and the lights come down and the long ordinary grey resumes. You have half-decided that the ache at the end of every celebration is just the truth about how things are. But Zechariah looked past the end of the world and saw the celebration *still going*, the feast set up, the nations arriving, the King enthroned, the joy simply refusing to have a last day.

That is where it is all heading. Every booth you have ever built, every harvest you have ever gathered, every table you have ever hated to leave was a rehearsal for a festival with no closing night, thrown by a King who came the first time as the Lamb and is coming again to gather the whole world up the mountain. Yeshua is bringing that day. The ingathering has already begun; the survivors are already streaming in from the farthest fields; and the feast that starts when He sets His feet on the mountain has no last day at all.

Lift your branch and get on the road. The best week of the year is about to become the whole of forever.
