We hand each other warnings about love like it's a medication that might hurt us.

Guard your heart. Don't get too invested. Read the fine print before you fall. And I understand where the fear comes from, people have been burned. But one ordinary week I decided to actually read the label on the love I had been *given*, the way you'd read a prescription bottle, and I started laughing, because every warning on it was good news. *May cause smiling for a day. May cause peace. May cause sleep. May cause shine.* Side effect one: I called my mama last night. Side effect two: I forgave someone I'd planned to fight. Side effect three: I'm sleeping like a kid. That was the whole album, right there in a joke that turned out to be true. Love does come with a warning sticker. It's just that all the warnings are things you'd want.

## What "Side Effects of Love" Actually Means

The title is a reframe, and the reframe is the whole point.

A side effect is something you can measure from the outside, evidence that the medicine is actually in your system. And that turned out to be the truest thing I could say about love: you cannot really see love itself, but you can always see its side effects. You can tell who has been loved by the way they move through a grocery store. Love is not a feeling you announce; it's a change other people notice before you do. So I built the record as a pharmacy label with the danger stripped out. The world sees a warning on the bottle and runs. *Smart folks read it and they take what's done.* Because the wise one knows that when it comes to real love, the warning label is not a threat. It's a promise of exactly the symptoms you've been aching for.

> You can't see love. But you can always see its side effects, and that's how you know it was ever really there.

## The Album Is a List of Symptoms, and Every One Is a Gift

The middle of the record is just me cataloging the evidence, one joyful symptom at a time.

"Sleep Better" is about three years of not falling asleep before two in the morning, holding a grudge like a clenched fist, and the Tuesday I finally wrote the letter and let it go, and slept eight hours straight the very next night. *The grudge was the alarm, the love is the snooze.* Forgiveness turned out to be a health intervention no doctor could prescribe. "Called My Mom" is a ten-minute phone call about a tomato plant and a bad joke, and the way it left me standing tall the rest of the day. "Helped a Stranger" is an old man's dropped groceries at a corner store, fifteen minutes on a bench, and a friendship I still have, *walked away with a friend I didn't have before.* "Smiling All Day" is the mirror catching me grinning at the sink until a cashier asks if I'm getting married. These are not big miracles. They're symptoms. They're what love looks like once it's actually in your bloodstream.

The heart of the album is the quietest song, "My Grandmother's Hands." She stood at her kitchen window every afternoon, waving at the mailman, keeping a pot of soup on the stove and a brown-paper jar at the door for whichever neighbor needed it. When Mr. Smith got sick, she brought him soup until his eyes cleared. When Mrs. Garcia's husband died, she just showed up and didn't say a thing. *She never once said "love your neighbor" out loud. She just did it every day.* And that is where the record turns from cataloging symptoms to naming the source. Because I finally understood: I was not born generous. I caught it. Her hands taught me the math of a love that expands, and she taught it entirely without words.

## The Verse Behind Every Symptom

Here is the Scripture the whole album is quietly built on, and it turns the joke into a theology.

**Yeshua** said you will know people by their fruit, that a good tree cannot help but produce good fruit, and you recognize the tree by what grows on it, not by what it claims. That is "side effects" said two thousand years earlier. You don't detect a loving heart by its announcements; you detect it by the harvest hanging off its branches. And Scripture gives the actual list of fruit that grows when the Spirit is at work in someone, and the very first item is *love*, and right behind it, *joy, peace, patience, kindness.* Read that list next to my album and it's almost embarrassing: sleep better (peace), smiling all day (joy), helped a stranger (kindness). I thought I was writing a fun garage-pop record about good vibes. I was accidentally writing about the fruit of the Spirit.

And there's the deeper question the album keeps circling: where do the symptoms *start*? Because I did not manufacture my own kindness any more than a body manufactures its own medicine. Scripture answers it in one of the plainest sentences in the whole Bible: *we love because He first loved us.* Every ripple has an origin, and the origin is not me. My grandmother's hands were passing along a love she had received, and so am I, and so are you when it's working. That's why the record ends in multiplication, not addition, *one small flame and the whole block is bright.* **Yeshua** said to let your light shine in front of people so they'd see the good and give the glory to your Father. That is the entire strategy of this album. Don't preach the love. Show the symptoms, and let people trace them back to the Source. And the Bible is blunt that this is the real thing: love not with words and speech, but *with actions and in truth.* My grandmother never gave the speech. She gave the soup. **Yahuah** is the love we first received; the soup is just the side effect.

> We love because He first loved us. Every ripple has a source, and the soup on the neighbor's doorstep is quietly pointing back to Him.

## Who I Made This For

I made *Side Effects of Love* for the person who has been so warned about love that they've forgotten it's good medicine.

For the one still carrying a three-year grudge that's stealing their sleep. For anyone who thinks they're just not a warm person, when the truth is they've never been shown where warmth comes from. For the grandchild who inherited a kindness they've never traced back to its source. For everyone who has been guarding their heart so carefully they've quarantined themselves from the one thing that heals.

Read the label. Don't peel it off, don't run from the warnings. They're all favorable. Forgive the person and get your sleep back. Call your mom on a Tuesday for no reason. Help the stranger and watch a friend appear. And when people ask why you're glowing, you don't have to preach, just let the symptoms show and let them wonder about the Source. You can always tell who has been loved. Go be someone they can tell about.

Side effects of love. Every one of them is a gift.
