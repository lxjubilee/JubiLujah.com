## A Word That Wasn't in the Dictionary Yet

I did not sit down to invent a word. I sat down to praise, and the word arrived before the morning did.

I was somewhere between two of the oldest ideas the faith owns. On one side was **Jubilee**, the fiftieth year of Leviticus 25, when the trumpet sounds, the debts are cancelled, the slaves walk off the auction block, and the land comes home to its family. On the other side was **Hallelujah**, the Hebrew shout that simply means *praise Yah*, the word heaven itself keeps repeating over the marriage of the Lamb in Revelation 19. I had a hand on each of them, and my mouth would not keep them apart. They collided. And out came a word that has never been in any dictionary: *JubiLujah*.

Jubilee plus Hallelujah. Release plus praise. The year of freedom married to the shout that freedom makes. I said it once and I could not un-say it. So I built twelve songs around it, and the first sung word of the whole record is the made-up word itself, landing at the count of zero.

## Why a Coined Word and Not a Careful One

People ask why I would risk a nonsense word on the most joyful album I have ever released. The honest answer is that the old words had gotten too comfortable. We say *hallelujah* the way we say *bless you* after a sneeze, reflex, not release. I wanted a word that had no worn groove in anybody's mouth. A word you have to lean into. A word that makes a stranger turn and ask, *what did you just say?*

> Some folks wait for a Sunday to start. JubiLujah is a Tuesday in the heart.

That is the thesis of the whole thing. I am tired of joy that keeps office hours. The freedom Yeshua bought was not filed under Sunday morning; it followed the released captive into the carpool line and the diner and the ordinary Wednesday. So I wrote a wake-up call, a song that says *sing it now*, don't wait for the perfect building or the robed choir or a reason that will pass an inspection. The waiting itself had become a thief. It kept telling me *tomorrow* when today was the day the King had already set.

### Kairos, Not Chronos

There is a song on this record that unpacks the one idea that convinced me the celebration could not wait. The Greeks had two words for time. *Chronos* is the clock, the watch on the kitchen wall, tick by tick, the years that quietly make you small. And *kairos* is the other kind: the appointed moment, the ripe hour, the instant heaven breaks into the ordinary and everything changes.

> Chronos was the line, and kairos was the rhyme. The line keeps ticking, but the rhyme breaks the floor.

I had been living by chronos, waiting for a calendar to grant me permission to rejoice. But the gospel does not run on chronos. It runs on kairos. The Scripture keeps calling this *the acceptable year*, the fullness of time, the day of salvation that is always *today*. When I understood that the right time was not something I was waiting for but something that had already arrived in **Yeshua**, the whole reason to postpone joy collapsed. The watchers get paid. The waiters get to speak. The kairos hour is not coming. It is here.

## The Reason Underneath the Party

Here is what I need you to understand, because a celebration this loud can look naive from a distance: I did not make a shallow record. I made an *earned* one.

Right in the middle of all the noise, everything strips back to a single breath and a testimony. I remember the floor. Three in the morning, the tile cold against the cheek, a photograph on the dresser of who I used to be. And then the morning the laughter came up out of my chest without my trying to push it. That song is quiet on purpose. The joy on the rest of the album is only believable because that one song admits where the joy was born.

> And the joy that I sing did not start in the sun. JubiLujah was earned where the night was begun.

That is why I can dance. Not because I have never been on the floor, but because He brought me *off* of it. Celebration is not a reaction to a victory I am hoping for. It is the strategy of someone who already knows how the story ends. **Yeshua** said *it is finished* from the cross, and He sat down. The war was already won before I started singing about it. So the dancing is not denial. It is memory.

## The Moment the King Is at the Door

Every album has one song that is the reason the album exists. On this one it is the eighth track, and I will not pretend I wrote it calmly.

The whole band drops to silence. A choir whispers three words, *listen, footsteps, the door*, and everything the record has been building toward lands on a single revelation: the King is at the door *right now*. Not someday, not at a safe theological distance. Now. Revelation 22 ends with Yeshua saying *surely I am coming soon*, and the Bride answering *come*. That answer is the whole album compressed into one breath. When the band comes crashing back in after the silence, that is what heaven's arrival sounds like to me, the held breath and then the flood.

I felt the floor tremble where the tile had been. The years He brought me out of folded thin. And the joy I earned in the dark finally put on a crown. That is the arc: the pit, the deliverance, and then the King at the threshold, and every one of them is a reason to make the noise louder, not softer.

## Who I Made It For

I made this for the person stuck in a waiting room who has been told to be patient one more time. For the believer in traffic who forgot that the kingdom is already here and not-yet at the same time. For every tribe and tongue that will one day stand before the throne in Revelation 7. I even wrote a song where the word gets sung in Swahili in the morning, Mandarin at noon, Spanish in the evening, because a coined word belongs to everybody or it belongs to no one.

And I built the record to loop. The last song's final chord is engineered to drop a half-step and land exactly on the first song's opening note, so that on repeat the album never actually stops. That is not a production trick for its own sake. That is the point. The party of the Lamb does not have a closing time. It just keeps going.

So here is my invitation, and it is the only one I know how to give. Say the word out loud once, even if you feel foolish. *JubiLujah.* Feel how it makes you lift your head. That lift is the whole gospel in your neck muscles, freedom looking up because the One who bought it is almost at the door. The waiting is nearly over. You are allowed to start the celebration early. In fact, I think that is exactly what we were made to do.
