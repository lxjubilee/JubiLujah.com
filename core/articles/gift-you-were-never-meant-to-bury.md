Most of the people I most admire will never be famous.

They are the ones who show up. Who sweep the hall in the quiet of the night before anyone comes. Who carry a calling nobody claps for. I made a whole record for them, and I built it out of island hip-hop and reggae-pop over a ʻukulele instead of my usual mountain hush, because a charge this direct needed a beat you could stand up to. This one is not a whisper on the ridge. This one is a shout on the block.

The word at the center of it is *kuleana*, a Hawaiian word for your sacred responsibility, the calling that is yours to carry and no one else's. And the whole album is one message turned twelve ways: do not bury it.

## A Word Before I Begin

The same honesty I owe on every island record I owe here. I am a mixed-heritage believer who loves this music and this heart-language; I do not speak for the Hawaiian people and I claim no authenticity I have not earned. The island words are used with care and glossed in plain English, and every line points to **Yeshua** alone. *Kuleana* is too weighty a word to borrow carelessly. So I carry it the way it deserves, as a trust.

## What the Title Means

*My Kuleana*, my calling, my sacred charge, mine to own.

It stands on two Scriptures that have shaped my whole life of work. The first is Paul to the Colossians: *whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men* (Colossians 3:23). The second is his word to Corinth: *it is required of stewards that they be found faithful* (1 Corinthians 4:2). Put those together and you get the spine of the title track, *not for the clout, not the likes, not the view*, but *whatever my hand finds, I do it for the King.* The Father handed me a role. Not too big, not too small, just right for my soul. My kuleana is my honor, never my escape.

That is the thing I most want to hand you: your calling is not a burden you got stuck with. It is a dignity you were entrusted with. Nobody else can carry what was set for you, and you cannot carry theirs. That is not a limitation. That is a design.

## The Heart Behind the Songs

I wanted this record to preach faithfulness the way real life delivers it, in small, unglamorous, daily pieces.

*Faithful in the Small* is the cornerstone. **Yeshua** said, *one who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much* (Luke 16:10). So I sing about sweeping the floor like it is the throne room, mopping the deck of the canoe with nothing to prove, because the Boss is always watching and He sees the heart. The mountain is made of pebbles, one by one. The harvest is made of faithful mornings in the sun. And the only review I want when I am done is the one from Matthew 25, *well done, good and faithful servant* (Matthew 25:21).

*Hands to the Plow* takes its title straight from the Lord's own words: *no one who puts his hand to the plow and looks back is fit for the kingdom* (Luke 9:62). The row does not dig itself. And I lean on Philippians for the strength to finish, *he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion* (Philippians 1:6). He never gave a calling He will not help you bear.

*Use What He Gave* is the parable of the talents (Matthew 25) turned into a warning I needed myself: the servant who buried his gift out of fear lost the very thing he thought he was protecting. None of us got nothing, that is a lie from below. Find the gift He gave you and let it go. *Carry My Brother* widens the calling outward, because kuleana is not only what I do alone, Paul says *bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ* (Galatians 6:2). And the turn in that song is one I have lived: the day I carried him, he carried me. The strong and the weak keep trading places, and the Christ who carried the heaviest tree carries me carrying my brother.

*Steward of the Land* grounds it in the very first job description humanity ever received, *the LORD God took the man and put him in the garden to work it and keep it* (Genesis 2:15), and Psalm 24, *the earth is the LORD's* (Psalm 24:1). I care for the ground not because it is a god, but because it is a gift, and I want to hand it to the next generation better than I found it. And *Light to My Block* is the reminder that the mission field is where your two feet stand today, *let your light shine before others, that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father* (Matthew 5:16). You do not have to cross an ocean to obey.

## The Song That Ties It Together

Every album I make has a testimony at its heart, and on this one it is *Keoni Came Home.*

Keoni is a true-to-life picture of the young man everyone wrote off, dropped out, a record and a count against him, slinging on the corner where the streetlight died, wasting every gift the Maker put inside him. His grandfather prayed for him on his knees every night: *Lord, bring my moʻopuna back into the light.* And in a county cell one day, Keoni could not shake a verse he had heard once, *a steward, prove faithful*, pounding in his head next to a sentence he could not silence: *you got a kuleana that you left for dead.*

> He picked up a broom before he ever picked up a mic.

That is the whole gospel of this record in one line. He came home and swept out the hall in the quiet of the night, faithful in the small thing, the corner boy becoming a kuleana man. Now he runs the youth night, telling the lost ones, *I was you, but the Maker called me.* And his grandfather wept in the front row watching the grandson he prayed for preach. The Father ran to meet him on the road, took the weight of every wasted year, and the gift the streets had stolen, Christ gave back whole. I honor the grandfather in that song as a faithful witness who prayed, never as a savior. **Yahuah** alone restores. But He loves to restore through a grandmother's kitchen and a grandfather's knees.

## Who This Is For

This is for the faithful nobody claps for. The one showing up when the fire is low and nothing feels inspired, because *the calling ain't a feeling, it's a faithful thing*, Paul's word to *be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord* (1 Corinthians 15:58). *Show Up Anyway* is your anthem. The faithful are not the ones who never doubted. They are the ones who kept showing up.

And it is for the worn-down soul in a dry season who has started to believe their gift is too small or too buried to matter. Hear me: the small thing done in love is never small. Do not bury the talent in the backyard sand. *Pass the Mic* is the reminder that a calling which ends with you is a calling that dies, the kūpuna passed it down so you could stand, and now you hand it to the keiki, hand to hand, with the cloud of witnesses cheering from the shore.

The record ends on *Kuleana for Life*, because this is not a season or a phase. It is a lifelong carry, straight through to the day you hope to hear *well done* from the risen Son. You will answer to the Maker for what you did with the gift in your hand (Romans 14:12), not the gossip, not the applause of men, but Him. And that is not a weight. That is the honor of your life.

Pick it up. It has your name on it, and it always did.
