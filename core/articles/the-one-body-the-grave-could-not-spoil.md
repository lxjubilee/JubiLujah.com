There is a week on Yahuah's calendar when an entire nation goes hunting through its own cupboards with a candle, sweeping out crumbs. It looks, from the outside, like the most extreme spring cleaning in the world. It is actually a doctrine about corruption, acted out in flour, and it was pointing, the whole time, at a body in a tomb.

The feast is *Chag HaMatzot*, the **Feast of Unleavened Bread**, seven days, immediately following Passover, in which no leaven may be found in the house, and only *matzah*, flat unleavened bread, is eaten. The command is not soft. Not merely *avoid* leaven, *remove* it. Search it out. Let none be *found* with you. And to understand why Yahuah would build a week-long feast around the absence of a baking ingredient, you have to understand what leaven had come to mean.

## Leaven was never really about bread

In Hebrew, the leaven is *chametz*, the fermented, souring, puffing agent that spreads invisibly through dough and makes the whole lump rise. And the Scriptures use it, again and again, as a picture of something spiritual: the way a small hidden corruption works its way silently through a whole life, a whole community, until everything is affected and no part is clean.

Yeshua Himself used leaven this way, warning about the leaven of the hypocrites and the leaven of teaching that quietly poisons. And Paul, writing to a proud and compromised congregation, reached straight for this feast to make his point. *Purge out the old leaven,* he said, so that you may be a new lump, *unleavened.* A little leaven, he warned, leavens the whole lump. Sin is not static. It ferments. It spreads. It does to a soul exactly what leaven does to dough: enters small, hides well, and slowly corrupts the whole.

> Leaven is the perfect picture of sin because sin is never content to stay small. It puffs, it spreads, it sours, and left alone, it turns the whole lump.

So the week of no-leaven is a week of *no-corruption*. Seven days of a nation declaring, with its bread, *there is a life that has nothing rotten working through it.* And for centuries they kept that feast without knowing that one week, one Man would lie down inside it whose body was the thing the picture had always meant.

## The days He rested in the tomb were the feast of no-corruption

Line up the calendar and hold your breath.

Yeshua was crucified as Passover came in. He was laid in a sealed tomb. And the days His body rested in the ground were *the Feast of Unleavened Bread*, the very days on which, by ancient command, no corruption could be found in the house. While Israel swept the last crumb of *chametz* from every home in the land, the sinless body of the Messiah lay still in the earth, and the grave leaned in to do to Him what the grave does to every body it receives, and found nothing to work with.

David had sung of this centuries before, in a psalm the apostles could not stop quoting after the resurrection. He sang that Yahuah would not abandon his soul to the grave, nor let his *Holy One see corruption*, see *decay*, the rotting that is the grave's whole business. Peter stood up on the day the Spirit fell and said plainly: David died and was buried and his tomb is with us still; his body *did* see corruption. So David was not singing about himself. He was singing, by the Spirit, about One who would go down into death and come back up with *nothing spoiled*, the one body the grave could not touch.

Do you see what the feast was rehearsing? *No leaven found in the house.* No corruption found in the body. The unleavened bread and the incorruptible offering are the same doctrine, kept for a thousand years in flour before it was fulfilled in flesh.

## Why the grave found nothing in Him

Here is the doctrine underneath the drama, and I want to say it precisely, because it is the hinge of everything.

The grave corrupts because sin corrupts. Decay in the body is the visible end of a disease that began the day the human race let leaven into the lump. Every other body that has ever been laid in the earth carried the corruption within it before the grave ever received it. The grave does not create the rot. It only finishes what sin started.

But Yeshua carried no leaven. Not hidden, not fermenting quietly in some private cupboard of the heart. He was, in the fullest sense the feast could picture, *unleavened*, a life with nothing sour spreading through it, nothing corrupt working its slow way toward decay. And so when they laid Him in the ground, the grave met, for the first and only time, a body with no corruption in it to develop. It reached for the rot and there was none. It searched the house and found no leaven. He *saw no corruption*, not because the grave was gentle, but because there was nothing in Him for the grave to spoil.

That is why He is called an *unblemished* offering, a lamb *without spot*. Those are not compliments. They are the requirements of the altar carried all the way to their only true fulfillment. Every offering had to be without defect, without corruption, and every offering fell short of what the picture demanded, until the one Offering came who was actually, entirely, unleavened.

## The bread you were made to become

And now the feast turns and looks straight at you, because Unleavened Bread was never only about Him. Paul finishes his thought: *let us keep the feast*, not with old leaven, not with the leaven of malice and evil, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth. Because Yeshua is our Passover, and because His body saw no corruption, we are now told what to *become*: a new lump. Clean bread. A life with the old leaven purged out.

This is not a demand to manufacture your own sinlessness. You cannot un-leaven yourself any more than dough can un-sour itself. It is an invitation to be *made* new bread by the One who was the unleavened Bread first. He went into the grave incorruptible so that the corruption in you could be swept out like crumbs by candlelight, searched for, named, carried out of the house. The week of no-leaven is not a burden. It is a picture of the clean life He is baking you into.

## So sweep with joy

Here is what I want you to carry out of that candlelit cupboard.

The next time you go looking for the leaven in your own house, the small hidden thing that has been fermenting quietly, spreading where you hoped no one would notice, do not sweep it out in shame. Sweep it out in *the confidence of an empty tomb*. There was a week when the sinless body of your Messiah lay in the ground during the feast of no-corruption, and the grave that spoils every other body could find nothing in Him to spoil. He is the unleavened One. And He rose incorruptible so that you, crumb by swept-out crumb, could be made into the clean bread you were never able to bake yourself.

The house can be clean. The lump can be new. The One who saw no corruption is making sure of it.
