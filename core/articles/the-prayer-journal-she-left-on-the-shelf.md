There is a leather book on my shelf that is worth more to me than anything I own, and it is not for sale at any price. It is my grandmother's prayer journal. Forty years of intercession in her own careful hand, names, dates, the tired scrawl of nights I never saw. My name is in there. It was in there before I could read. And when I take it down and turn the pages, I am not reading a book. I am hearing a watch that was kept over my whole life while I slept.

That book is why I made **Keeper of Stories**. Because somewhere along the way I understood a thing that reorganized my faith entirely: I did not arrive at belief on my own. I was prayed here. And the moment you understand that, you stop being only a *receiver* of faith and you become what my grandmother was, a keeper of it.

## Faith did not start with you, and it will not end with you

We talk about faith as though it were weather, something that happens to a person, privately, on the inside. But Scripture almost never talks about it that way. It talks about faith as something *handed*, carried, kept, and passed. When Paul writes to Timothy, he doesn't say, "I admire the faith you generated." He says, *"I am reminded of your sincere faith, which first lived in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice, and now, I am persuaded, dwells in you as well"* (2 Timothy 1:5). Three generations, named, in one sentence. A grandmother is the headwater. That is the whole architecture of the album, the opening song is "Lineage of Light," and it says the plainest true thing I know: *we are not alone; the cloud of witnesses is gathered round.* That line is Hebrews 12:1 set to a porch rhythm. You are surrounded. You always were.

The second song is the one I chose to carry this whole story, "The Faithful Kept Watch." It came straight off my shelf. *My grandmother's prayer journal still sits on my shelf, forty years of intercession.* They kept the watch when the night was long. They knelt by bedsides praying Scripture over people not yet born. And here is the turn the song makes, the turn every believer eventually has to make: *the faithful kept watch, and now it's me.* The watch does not end when the watchman dies. It gets handed to whoever is still awake.

## What a keeper actually keeps

I want to be precise, because "legacy" is a word people use to mean nothing. A keeper of stories is not a nostalgic person. A keeper is a *teacher under orders.*

Psalm 78 gives the job description, and it is not gentle. *"We will not hide them from their children, but tell to the coming generation the glorious deeds of the LORD"* (Psalm 78:4). Not hide them. The default, if no one keeps watch, is that the stories go quiet in one generation. Psalm 145:4 says *"one generation shall commend your works to another, and shall declare your mighty acts."* Commend, hand across, on purpose, out loud. This is why I wrote songs like "Witness of Generations" and "Blessing Forward." The blessing was never meant to pool in you. It was meant to move *through* you, forward, to a face you may never see.

> Blessed by the prayers they whispered in the dark, blessed by the courage that refused to break, and now it is my turn to keep the watch and pass the flame to those who come behind.

And Joshua tells us *how* to keep it: with stones. After Israel crossed the Jordan, **Yahuah** told them to pile up twelve stones so that *"when your children ask in time to come, 'What do these stones mean to you?' then you shall tell them"* (Joshua 4:6-7). A keeper builds things that provoke the question. A journal on a shelf is a stone. A song your granddaughter can't get out of her head is a stone. You are stacking memory where the next generation will trip over it and have to ask.

## Glory hides in the ordinary

The song on this record I most want you to hear is "Glory in the Ordinary." Because we imagine legacy is built by the dramatic saints, the martyrs, the missionaries, the platform names. But my grandmother's monument was flour on her hands and a whispered blessing over a pot on the stove. The most faithful watch I have ever witnessed was kept in a kitchen, at four in the morning, by a woman nobody outside our county ever heard of.

This is doctrine, not sentiment. Lamentations 3:22-23 says His mercies *"are new every morning; great is your faithfulness."* Every *morning*. The faithfulness of God is delivered in daily, ordinary portions, and the keepers who catch it are almost always doing something ordinary when they do, turning a page, setting a table, sitting at a sickbed. "Memory and Grace" on this album is a whole song about a photograph on the wall, about my mother young, before the weight of years, carrying something precious she knew I'd inherit. What I remember of her isn't always clear. But what she deposited is always near.

I put a song near the end called "Rise and Celebrate," and I want you to know it is there on purpose. Because keeping stories is not a mournful business, and legacy is not a museum you tend in the dark. Every name in that journal is a name **Yahuah** answered, is answering, or has promised to answer. When I read the pages, I am not grieving a woman who is gone. I am reading a ledger of a faithfulness that never left. The proper response to a well-kept watch is not a sigh. It is a shout. The keepers who came before us are not asking us to be sad that they're gone; they are handing us a reason to celebrate that He is not.

## Who this is for, and what I'm asking of you

This album is for the woman who feels like the last believer in her family and wonders if the line stops with her. It does not have to. And it is for the woman on the *other* end, the grandmother who thinks her quiet prayers accomplished nothing because she never saw the fruit. Sit down with me. You have no idea what your journal is doing on a shelf you cannot see yet.

I called the record **Keeper of Stories** because that is the office every one of us is quietly ordained into whether we accept it or not. Someone kept watch over you. Someone's forty years of prayers are the reason you can read this at all. The only question the album asks is the one the second song asks: the faithful kept watch, *and now it's me.*

Take the book down off the shelf. Read a name. Then go add your own.
