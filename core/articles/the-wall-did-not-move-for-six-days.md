There is a detail in the old story of Jericho that no children's book ever lingers on, and it is the detail I cannot stop thinking about.

They marched around the city once a day. Priests with rams' horns, the ark carried in the middle, the whole company of them circling that impossible wall, and then Yahuah told them something strange. Do not shout. Do not let a word come out of your mouth. March, and be silent, until I tell you.

So they marched. And the wall did not move.

Day one, silence, and the wall stood. Day two, the same circle, the same dust, the same held breath, the same stone rising over them exactly as high as the day before. Day three. Day four. Six days of walking a ring around a thing that would not give them one inch of encouragement, no crack, no tremor, no sign at all that any of this was working. Just their own footsteps and the horns and a wall that looked, every single evening, precisely as unbeaten as it had that morning.

I want to talk about those six days, because the shout gets all the attention, and the shout is not where most of you live.

## The obedience nobody claps for

We love the seventh day. We love the moment the walls come down flat and the impossible thing collapses and the testimony writes itself. We put that on the posters.

But Yahuah did not hand them the seventh day. He handed them a discipline for the six that came first, and the discipline was almost unbearable in its plainness: keep circling, and keep quiet.

Think about what that asked of them. Every lap was an act of faith with zero feedback. There was no scoreboard ticking upward, no crack widening a little each day so they could say, *look, it's working.* Faith with a progress bar is easy. Anyone can persevere when they can see the needle move. What Jericho required was obedience in the total absence of evidence, walk again, say nothing, and trust that something is happening in the unseen even though everything visible is telling you it is not.

That is the assignment most of you have been given right now, and you have been reading your silence as failure.

You have been circling something. A marriage that has not warmed. A body that has not healed. A child who has not come home. A calling that has not opened. And you have made lap after faithful lap around it, and the wall has not moved, and somewhere in there a voice started whispering that the silence is the verdict. That nothing is happening. That you should probably stop walking.

Hear me as a prophet: the silence was in the instructions.

## Why He told them not to shout yet

I have wondered for years why Yahuah forbade the shout until the seventh day. They had voices. Why not let them praise from day one?

I think it is because there is a kind of noise we make to manage our own anxiety, and He was after something deeper than that. He was teaching them that the wall would not fall because they got loud enough on their own timeline. It would fall when He said, and not one lap sooner. So He sealed their mouths, not to silence their faith, but to protect it from becoming a technique.

Because that is the lie waiting for every persevering heart: that if I just worship harder, march angrier, believe with more volume, I can force the seventh day to arrive on the sixth. And Yahuah, in His mercy, would not let them turn a miracle into a machine. He made them carry the tension. He made them hold a shout in their chests for six days and trust that He knew the hour.

There is worship in a held breath. There is faith in a closed mouth that is dying to shout and chooses, instead, to wait on the word of the One who set the day.

## The song you march to before you're allowed to sing it

Now here is where my heart comes alive, because I make songs for a living, and I have learned that the deepest anthems are not written on the seventh day. They are written on the fourth.

The song that carries you around an unmoved wall is a different creature than the song you sing when it falls. The victory song is easy, the whole world will sing with you once the stone is down. But the marching song, the one you hum on day four with your feet aching and the wall as tall as ever, that song is faith in its purest form. It is a melody offered to a God who has not yet done the thing, on nothing but His character. It agrees with Heaven before Heaven has produced a shred of evidence.

That is the anthem of the not-yet, and it is the most honest music a human being ever makes. Anybody can rejoice at a collapsed wall. It takes a prophet's heart to sing steadily at a standing one.

And I will tell you what that song does. It does not make you loud. It makes you *durable.* It gets you to day six without quitting. Because the real danger was never the wall, the wall was always coming down; Yahuah had already decided that before the first lap. The real danger was that you would walk away on the evening of day five, one sunrise short of the shout, convinced that the silence meant no.

## Now the honest word, and please don't skip it

I owe you the other half, because I refuse to preach you a formula.

Not every wall in your life is a Jericho with a scheduled seventh day. Some of you have circled a thing for years, and I will not stand here and promise that this particular Tuesday is the day it falls, because I do not know the mind of Yahuah and neither does any honest servant of His. The story of Jericho is not a vending machine where six laps buys a collapse. If I told you that, I would be handing you the cruelest lie there is, that the sufferer whose wall still stands simply did not march correctly.

So let me say it plainly. Your unmoved wall is not the measure of your obedience. The faithful have died mid-lap, walls still standing, and Heaven did not read that as their failure. It read it as some of the most courageous worship a person can offer, a heart that kept circling in the dark on the strength of a promise it had not yet seen kept.

The victory of the six days was never that the wall fell on your schedule. The victory was that you were still walking when the seventh morning came. That you did not let the silence write the ending. That you carried the shout in your chest, unspent, faithful, ready.

So march again today. Say what you have to say to Yahuah in the quiet, and keep your feet moving around the thing that has not budged. Do not read the silence as no. Read it as *not yet.*

The seventh day has a sound, and it belongs to the ones who were still marching when it broke.

Keep circling. The shout is coming, and it is yours.
