Every other record I have made shouts. This one whispers, and it cost me more than all the loud ones put together.

*Prophetic Wilderness* is sparse on purpose, one weathered baritone, an open-tuned guitar, a harmonica drifting in from two canyons over, and a lot of wind in the gaps between the lines. I stripped the band out because the wilderness strips you out. That is the whole point of the place. And I could not make an honest record about it with a full arrangement covering for me.

## Why I had to make it

I had gotten good at sounding sure. That is the danger of doing this long enough. You learn to perform certainty, to preach with confidence you have not actually paid for. And **Yahuah** loves you too much to let you keep doing that forever.

So He does what He did to His own Son. Luke chapter four says the Spirit led **Yeshua** into the wilderness, Matthew says He was driven up into it, and He was there forty days. Notice who did the driving. Not the devil. Not a bad season. The Spirit. Sometimes the emptiness you are standing in is not punishment and it is not failure. It is direction. The opener of the record starts right there: "Jesus, You led me out past the last fence line, the Spirit drove You before He drove mine." I followed His footprints out past the last thing I could control.

I had to make this record because I went out there myself, and I came back different, and I did not want to lie about the trip.

## What the title means

A wilderness in Scripture is not just scenery. It is the place where **Yahuah** does the deep, quiet, private work that a crowd would only get in the way of.

Hosea chapter two has Him say something almost tender about it, "I will allure her, and bring her into the wilderness, and speak comfortably unto her." He does not drag you to the desert to break you for sport. He takes you somewhere quiet enough that you can finally hear Him say something kind. But you cannot hear it over the noise you carry. So the desert takes the noise first.

That is track two, *Silence Speaks*, the song people keep coming back to. I went out hunting a word, some ringing declaration my pride could have used, and He kept the quiet until the third day. And then His silence turned out to be louder than the noise of men. It comes straight from First Kings nineteen, where Elijah looks for God in the wind and the earthquake and the fire, and **Yahuah** is in none of them. He is in the still, small voice, the one you cannot hear until everything louder has burned off.

> The wilderness is not where God goes quiet. It is where He gets you quiet enough to notice He never stopped speaking.

## The heart under the songs

The song I would hand a stranger first is *Wilderness Prayer*. It is just a man on his knees where nobody can see, "no one to perform for, nobody but Thee." It leans on the hardest picture in the Gospels, **Yeshua** in Gethsemane praying "not my will, but thine," and it lands on the one word I most wanted this whole record to reach: *Abba.* Father. The intimate name. Out in the waste, with the audience gone and the performance impossible, His "Abba" finally became my own. That is what solitude is for. Not to make you lonely, to make you His.

And I will not pretend the desert is gentle. The center of the album is *Prophet Breaking*, and it is the altar of the whole record. It is seven minutes of a man coming apart in the best possible way, "every idol I'd carried I laid at Your feet, every sermon I'd preached lost its old counterfeit." I sing there that the breaking was kind, though not mild. That is the truest line I know how to write about God. He does not break you because He is cruel. He breaks the thing in you that was never load-bearing, the reputation, the act, the self you built to be admired, and He does it so gently and so completely that you come up off the ground with your hands empty and your heart free, saying the only thing left that is actually yours: His name.

I anchored it all on the Baptist. Isaiah chapter forty and Matthew chapter three, the voice crying in the wilderness, prepare ye the way of the Lord. Every prophet God ever used, He seasoned in a desert first. Even **Yeshua**, who needed no correcting, went to the solitary place a great while before day to pray, as Mark chapter one tells it. If the sinless Son sought the wilderness on purpose, then the wilderness is not a detour off the road of faith. It is on the road. It may be the most important stretch of it.

## Who I made this for

I made *Prophetic Wilderness* for the ones who are in the dry place right now and cannot figure out what they did to deserve it.

Maybe you did nothing to deserve it. Maybe the Spirit drove you here the way He drove **Yeshua**, not to punish you, but to prepare you. For the man who used to feel God easily and now feels nothing, and has quietly concluded that something is wrong with him. Nothing is wrong with you. You are in the school every prophet before you attended. For the woman worn hoarse by everyone else's voices, every opinion, every expectation, who needs the desert to make those voices go quiet so she can hear the One at the source.

And for the one who is breaking. I know how that feels from the inside, and I will tell you what I learned face down in the sand: the breaking is a kiss from His hand. Everything you thought you were, you are not, and that is the beginning of freedom, not the end of you. Hebrews chapter eleven honors the ones who wandered in deserts, of whom the world was not worthy. Heaven has never once looked down on a wilderness saint.

So if He has driven you out past the last fence line, do not run back too fast. Let the silence do its work. Let Him take the noise, the act, the borrowed certainty.

Because on the far side of the quiet, "Abba" is waiting to become your own word, and a man who has learned to say that in the dark can say it anywhere for the rest of his life.
