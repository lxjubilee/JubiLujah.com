For most of the Bible, a whole people is reaching for a piece of ground.

They're promised it under a night sky full of stars. They carry the promise out of slavery, across a wilderness, up to the edge of a river. They cross the river to get into it. They sing over its hills and set their tents down on it and bury their dead in it and weep for it when they're carried away from it. The Land is the ache that runs through the whole story.

I made *The Promise Walks In* about the moment that ache comes into focus, and the inheritance everyone was reaching for turns out to be a Person. The Land was real. It was also, the entire time, a picture of a face.

## A promise with feet

The opening track sets the whole tone. **Yahuah** spoke to Abraham at the oaks of Mamre, showed him a sky and said "your descendants will be like the stars" (Genesis 15:5), and the album's claim is that the promise He made then never stopped keeping pace. It's still walking. It walks in to where you're sitting still.

That's the picture I wanted: a promise that isn't a document in a drawer but a living thing with feet, closing the distance between an ancient oath and your ordinary Tuesday. Every promise Abraham held, every promise Jacob knew, still on the move, still arriving.

## The river in front of every promise

The second track, *Cross The Jordan*, tells the crossing, and it holds a detail I never want a believer to miss.

The Jordan was at flood stage, "the Jordan overflows all its banks throughout the time of harvest" (Joshua 3:15). The water was high and fast. And the river did not part *before* the priests moved. It parted when the first foot stepped in. "As soon as those bearing the ark... dipped in the brink of the water... the waters coming down from above stood and rose up in a heap" (Joshua 3:15-16). The song draws the line straight to your life:

> Yahuah does not wait for the river to recede. He parts the river when the first foot steps in.

There is a Jordan in front of every promise. A wilderness ending where a Land begins. And the God who owns the water waits for a foot in the current. That is not recklessness. It's covenant. He had already sworn the Land was theirs; the crossing was simply the moment they let their weight prove they believed Him. Faith almost always looks like a foot going into water that hasn't moved yet.

## The name at the front of the crossing

Track three is where the whole album quietly turns, and it turns on a name.

Moses brought the people to the edge of the inheritance, but Moses did not take them in (Deuteronomy 34:4). The one who led them across was **Joshua**, and here is the thing every teacher should say slowly. *Joshua*, Yehoshua, means "Yahuah is salvation." It is the very same Hebrew name as **Yeshua**. The man whose sandals stepped first into the Jordan carried the name of the One whose nail-pierced feet would one day lead a far larger people into a far greater inheritance. When Joshua met the Captain of the host of **Yahuah** at Jericho and was told to take off his sandals because the ground was holy (Joshua 5:14-15), the pattern was already being drawn. The leader who goes first, into the water, into the Land, bearing the name that means salvation.

## The inheritance was Yeshua all along

The revelation the whole record is built toward lands in track eight, *The Inheritance Was Yeshua All Along*, and it stands on one of the most honest paragraphs in Scripture.

Hebrews says of the patriarchs: "These all died in faith, not having received the things promised, but having seen them and greeted them from afar" (Hebrews 11:13). They were looking, it says, for "a better country, that is, a heavenly one" (Hebrews 11:16), a city "whose designer and builder is God" (Hebrews 11:10). Even after Joshua led them into the actual Land, the writer of Hebrews insists there remained a rest still ahead of them (Hebrews 4:8-9). The ground was real. But it was never the whole promise. It was pointing.

And **Yeshua** said it Himself: "Abraham rejoiced that he would see my day. He saw it and was glad" (John 8:56). Abraham, under that sky of stars, was ultimately seeing a face. The song puts it the way I most want it remembered:

> The Land was a picture, Yeshua was the promise.

## Every step you take is that ground now

If the inheritance is a Person and not only a place, then something changes about where you're standing right now, and two tracks carry it home.

*Every Step We Take Is Holy Ground* remembers Moses at the burning bush, told to take off his sandals because the ground was holy (Exodus 3:5). But the ground was holy because **Yahuah** was there. And now, the song reasons, the **Ruach HaKodesh** lives inside His people, so the holiness travels. Every place you set your foot is sanctified, because the Presence that made that bush-lit dirt holy now walks every road you walk.

And *The Inheritance Is Sealed* rests on Paul's promise that we were "sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, who is the guarantee of our inheritance" (Ephesians 1:13-14). The deposit is already down. No power can break the seal. The finale, *We Are Home*, gathers it all: we are home in the Land the patriarchs reached for and home in the Messiah the Land was prophesying, one inheritance, held in one Person, at last.

## Who I made this for

I made *The Promise Walks In* for the believer standing at the edge of their own Jordan, a promise on the far bank, a flooded river in the way, waiting for the water to drop before they'll move. It won't drop first. The God who owns the current is waiting for your foot in it. Step in.

And I made it for the one who has spent their faith reaching for a *place*, a circumstance, an address, an arrival they were sure would finally feel like home, and kept coming up short. Hear the album's whole discovery: the inheritance was never mainly a place. It was a face. Every promise **Yahuah** ever made was pointing to **Yeshua**, and in Him the whole thing comes home together.

The Land was always a picture of a face. And the moment you see the face, you realize the promise has been walking toward you the whole time, and it just walked in.
