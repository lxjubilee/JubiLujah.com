I want to take you to six rooms.

A crusade square in Lagos at the dusk hour, stage lights on the field, hundreds of thousands gathered, vendors selling plantain, children on shoulders, the talking drum preaching to an orange-burning sky. A kitchen at 6:47 in the morning, coffee pot on, cabinets still yellow, a woman wiping down the counter with the radio low. A Soweto sunrise service, dew on the tin roof, the choir humming in Zulu before the rooster. A room in Mumbai, twelve faces, one lamp, the window covered and the door locked. A hillside favela in São Paulo where the city wouldn't go. A church basement in Atlanta on a Friday night, folding chairs in a circle, a sixteen-year-old in the back with her phone face-down.

Six rooms that could not be more different. And the same Person walked into every single one of them without changing His name. That is *The Holy Ghost Walks Africa*, and that is why I had to make it.

## The Wind Never Signed a Peace Treaty With Geography

The thesis of this whole record is one sentence: **He came down again.** The first track says it plainly. He didn't close the upper room, He didn't lock the door. The wind that fell on the one hundred and twenty back then is on my kitchen floor right now. Acts chapter two is still open, and *the pouring never quit.*

I know the temptation. We read Acts 2 like it was a one-time storm, a beautiful weather event that happened over Jerusalem and then the sky cleared for good. But **Yeshua** did not describe a single afternoon. He described a range: *"ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth"* (Acts 1:8). That verse is a map. Jerusalem was the first room. Africa and the diaspora and the uttermost parts are the rooms He was always walking toward.

And the promise underneath it is Joel's, quoted by Peter on the day itself: *"I will pour out my Spirit upon all flesh"* (Joel 2:28; Acts 2:17). All flesh. Not one nation's flesh. Not one continent's. He does not carry a passport, and He does not need a visa.

## He Doesn't Need a Pulpit

The song I love most on the front half is *"Holy Ghost in the Kitchen,"* and it is the least dramatic room on the record on purpose. A woman with suds still on her wrist, eggs on the plate, and the Spirit walks through her right beside the stove. The line is: *the pulpit don't define the cloud.* He didn't wait for Sunday. He didn't need a date. The floor became a sea and the yellow light got brighter, and there was not a candle lit.

We have trained the church to believe the encounter belongs to the platform, the big stage, the anointed man, the crusade square. And He *is* in the crusade square. *"Lagos Crusade"* is one of the most electric things I have ever recorded, a hundred thousand hands going up like a wave while the talking drum opens the altar call in Yoruba. But the whole architecture of this album is built to say: the same Spirit who fills the square fills the kitchen, and He plays no favorites between them.

> He met me on the highway, and He met me at the sink, and He did not act any smaller in the small room.

## The Room the World Locked, and the Room the City Forgot

There are two rooms on this record I could not leave out, because the church needs to hear them.

*"Mumbai Underground (He Came Anyway)"* is the persecuted church. Twelve believers, the door locked against the threat on the street, no music, no raised voices, and the Holy Ghost steps through the wall anyway. I anchored it in Acts 4, where the early church prayed under real pressure and *the place was shaken where they were assembled together; and they were all filled with the Holy Ghost* (Acts 4:31). He does not ask the regime for permission. He came past the lock.

And *"São Paulo Favela Fire"* is the room the city wrote off, the hillside the elites avoided, the corner nobody counts. I say it in the bridge and I mean it as theology: *the Holy Ghost loves the margin most.* He climbs the hill the avenue ignores. That is not a slogan; that is His whole pattern, all the way back to a manger in a town nobody expected anything from.

Then the cinematic turn, *"Same Spirit, Every Country,"* where all six rooms collapse into one recognition. The talking drum in Lagos and the Hammond in the South are calling the same Person through a hundred different mouths. The whispered *Holy Ghost* in Mumbai and the Soweto morning hum are reaching for the same Spirit who has always been the One. Same wind. Same fire. He never broke the link. **Yahuah** is not a regional deity who changes His character at the border.

## Who I Carried This Record For

I made it for the believer who thinks revival only happens somewhere else, some other country, some other era, some more spiritual people than her. For the saint in the small kitchen who assumes the good stuff is reserved for the stage. For the underground church holding on behind a locked door. For the ones the city forgot on the hillside. And for the whole beautiful, scattered, many-tongued family of God, so we would recognize each other as carriers of the *same* flame.

Because the record does not end in any of the six rooms. It ends in *you.* *"Carry the Fire Out"* remembers that the encounter always opens onto a street, the Holy Ghost is portable, He is not staying with the building. *"Release Him in the Room"* puts the fire in your own mouth: the conference room, the classroom, the hospital ward, the hard conversation in the kitchen, open your mouth, speak the Name, and the atmosphere shifts. And *"We Are Encounter-Carriers"* closes the loop, answering the very first song. He came down in Lagos, He came down in my house, He came down again in me, *and now I walk Him out.*

That is where you come in. You are the next square. The next kitchen. The next locked room where He steps through the wall. The wind that walked into six rooms on this record is looking for a seventh, and it has your address. Open your mouth wherever you are standing. The same Spirit, in every country, and He is already in yours.
