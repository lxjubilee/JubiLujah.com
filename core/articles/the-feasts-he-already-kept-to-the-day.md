Here is a fact that should stop you where you stand.

When the Messiah fulfilled the spring feasts, He did not fulfill them *roughly*. He did not accomplish the general idea of Passover somewhere in the vicinity of Passover. He kept the appointment on its own date, to the day, and then He did it again, and again, until every spring appointment on Yahuah's calendar had a matching event stamped on the exact square where it fell.

Once you see that pattern, you cannot look at the *rest* of the calendar the same way. Because half the appointments have been kept with astonishing precision, and half of them have not been kept yet.

## Four in the spring, three in the fall

Yahuah's appointed times fall in two clusters, and the gap between them is not an accident.

The spring feasts come fast and close: **Passover** (*Pesach*), and hard on its heels the week of **Unleavened Bread** (*Matzot*); then **Firstfruits** (*Bikkurim*), the waving of the first sheaf of the barley harvest; and fifty days later, **Shavuot**, the Feast of Weeks that the Greek-speaking world came to call Pentecost.

Then the calendar goes quiet. A long summer of harvest passes with no appointed time at all, months of ordinary work under the sun.

And then, in the fall, they come again in a rush: the **Day of Blasting**, *Yom Teruah*, the feast of the trumpet-shout; ten days later the **Day of Atonement**, *Yom Kippur*, from *kaphar*, to cover, to make atonement; and five days after that, **Tabernacles**, *Sukkot*, the week of booths when Israel lived in fragile shelters and rejoiced.

Two clusters. A gap between. Hold that shape in your mind, because it turns out to be a map.

## Kept to the day

Now watch the spring.

**Passover.** On the very day the lambs were being chosen and slain for the feast, the Lamb of Elohim was led to the cross. Yeshua did not die near Passover. He died *as* the Passover, the spotless lamb, the blood that turns death aside, on the day the whole nation was handling lambs.

**Firstfruits.** On the appointed day when the first sheaf of the harvest was lifted before Yahuah as the pledge that the whole field was coming, Yeshua rose from the dead. The New Testament reaches for that exact word: He is risen, *the firstfruits* of those who have fallen asleep. The feast that waved the first of the harvest and promised the rest found its meaning in an empty tomb, the first of a resurrection harvest, lifted up as the guarantee of everyone who belongs to Him.

**Shavuot.** Fifty days after the sheaf, Israel had always gathered again for the Feast of Weeks. And it was on that very day, Pentecost, the crowds in Jerusalem for the appointed time, that the Ruach HaKodesh fell in wind and fire and the tongues of the nations were suddenly heard. The feast landed on its fulfillment to the day.

> Three appointments. Three events. Not one of them approximate. He kept the calendar of heaven with the precision of a man keeping His word.

Let that precision do its work on you. A God who fulfills His appointments to the very day is not a God who is vague about the ones still coming.

## The half that is still ahead

Because the fall feasts have not been kept. The long summer gap on the calendar is the age we are living in, the harvest months between the appointments already fulfilled and the appointments still to come. And the shape of those fall feasts tells us the character of what we are waiting for, even though it does not, and here I must be a careful teacher, tell us the date.

A trumpet-shout. A day of atonement and reckoning. A feast of dwelling, when Elohim tabernacles with His people and the fragile booths give way to a permanent joy. You do not have to strain to hear the outline of the Messiah's return in that sequence. Generations of teachers have looked at the fall feasts and seen the second coming written into their very order, the blast that gathers, the great reckoning, the everlasting dwelling of Yahuah with His people.

But hear me clearly, because this is exactly where sincere people have shipwrecked. The pattern of *to the day* fulfillment in the spring is a reason for confidence, not a decoder ring for setting dates in the fall. Yeshua Himself said the day and the hour are not given to us, and no clever alignment of feast to calendar overturns His plain word. Men have named years for the trumpet and shamed the gospel when the years passed. Do not join them. The precision of the spring teaches us that Yahuah keeps His appointments perfectly. It does not license us to schedule Him.

## Living in the gap

So where does that leave us? Standing in the summer between the clusters, in the harvest season of the world, with three appointments kept in flawless detail behind us and three still circled on the calendar ahead.

And that is a thrilling place to stand. The believer is not waiting on a *maybe*. We are waiting on a God whose track record of keeping appointed times is perfect, every spring feast honored to the day. The trumpet has not sounded yet. Atonement's full and final reckoning has not come. The great feast of Tabernacles, when He dwells with us and wipes away every tear, is still ahead. But they are on His calendar, and His calendar has never yet missed a day.

So let the fall feasts do to you what they were made to do. Not fear. He kept the Lamb's appointment for you, and He will not fumble the trumpet's. Not date-setting, that is for people who trust their arithmetic more than His word. But *readiness*. The joyful, wide-awake readiness of a people who have read the calendar, seen how faithfully the first half was kept, and lifted their eyes to the horizon for the rest.

And notice the mercy folded into the very gap we live in. That long stretch of summer between the spring cluster and the fall, the harvest months with no appointed time on them, is not empty time. It is the season of ingathering, the age in which the good news runs out across the whole field of the nations and the harvest is brought in before the trumpet ever sounds. If you are reading this, you are living inside that harvest. The delay you sometimes feel as silence is really patience: the Lord of the appointed times holding the fall feasts open a little longer so that more of the field can be gathered in before the last cluster begins.

That is the tenderness under the whole two-part calendar. He was not slow in the spring, and He is not careless about the fall. He kept the Lamb to the day, He raised the Firstfruits to the day, He sent the fire to the day, and He is keeping the gap open on purpose, for the sake of everyone still out in the field.

Three feasts kept to the day. Three still to come. And the same faithful hand holding the whole calendar.

Watch the sky.
