If you could see it, you would never sing a lazy note again.

Because there is a moment in every gathering of praising people when something is being *built*. Not felt, built. A structure rises in the room, invisible to the eye but real as timber, and Yahuah walks in and takes His seat on it. He does not bring the throne with Him. He builds it out of what comes out of your mouth.

I am not being poetic for effect. This is one of the most staggering sentences in the whole songbook, and most people read right over it because the English goes soft exactly where the Hebrew goes electric.

## The line hiding in the darkest psalm

The sentence lives in Psalm 22, and you should feel the weight of *where* it sits.

Psalm 22 is the psalm of abandonment. It opens with the cry that Yeshua Himself would later scream from the cross: *My God, my God, why have You forsaken me?* It is the lowest note in the entire book, a song sung from the very bottom of feeling forgotten. And then, one breath after that cry, before any rescue has arrived, the singer says something that turns the whole psalm on its hinge. He says: *but You are holy, enthroned on the praises of Israel.*

In the pit, with no answer yet in sight, he reaches past his own feelings and grabs onto a fact about God, and the fact is this: *You sit down on our praise.*

The Hebrew is *yoshev tehillot Yisrael*. Let me open it up, because both words are doing something English flattens.

*Yoshev* comes from *yashab*, to sit, to dwell, to remain, to be *enthroned*. It is the same posture word used elsewhere of the One who *sits enthroned* above the cherubim, the King taking His royal seat. It is not a casual sitting. It is a monarch lowering Himself onto his throne to reign. And what is the throne, in this line? *Tehillot*, praises. The plural of *tehillah*, a song of praise. That word is so central to Israel's worship that the entire book of Psalms is called *Tehillim* in Hebrew, the Praises. It is cut from the same ancient root as *hallelu*: to shine, to boast, to make a radiant, unashamed noise over God.

Put it together and the sentence says: *the King takes His royal seat on the shining, boasting praises of His people.* Where a normal king's throne is built of cedar and gold, this King's throne is assembled out of worship. He sits down on the sound.

## He does not haul in the throne. We raise it.

Stop and let that reorder your whole idea of a worship service.

We tend to imagine that Yahuah arrives already enthroned, and our praise is just the polite noise we make in the presence of a King who was seated whether we showed up or not. And it is true that His majesty needs nothing from us. He was King before the first star and will be King after the last one burns out.

But this line tells you something He has *chosen*. When it comes to His dwelling *among a people*, His manifest, felt, come-close reign in an actual room full of actual human beings. He has decided to make Himself at home on their praise. He inhabits it. He settles onto it. The tehillot are the seat.

Which means a gathering of worshippers is not merely admiring a King who is already comfortable. It is *raising the platform of His reign in that place*. Every honest line of praise is another beam laid, another plank set, until there is a throne standing in the middle of the room and the King steps forward and sits.

You have felt this. You have been in the ordinary room, folding chairs, tired people, a leader a little nervous, and somewhere in the third or fourth song the whole atmosphere changed. It got *heavier* and *lighter* at the same time. People stopped watching the stage and started meaning the words. And what you were feeling was not the sound system. It was the King taking His seat on a throne the room had, without knowing it, been building the whole time.

> A crowd that praises is not decorating His presence. It is constructing His throne, one line at a time, and summoning His reign into the room.

## Why this makes your worst days matter most

Here is the part I cannot get over, and it comes straight back to *where* the line sits.

The throne-building sentence is not spoken from a mountaintop of feeling. It is spoken from the pit, *why have You forsaken me?*, one breath earlier. The singer builds the throne with a voice that is still shaking. He does not wait until the rescue comes to declare that God is enthroned on praise. He declares it *in the dark*, and the declaring is itself the first plank.

So do not believe the lie that your praise only counts when your heart is full. The most load-bearing worship in any room is almost always coming from the person who has the least reason to give it, the one singing through the divorce, through the diagnosis, through the silence where an answer was supposed to be. When *that* voice says *You are holy, You are worthy, You reign*, that is not a weak note. That is the strongest beam in the whole structure. Heaven knows exactly what it cost, and the King is drawn to sit precisely there.

This is why the enemy of your soul works so hard to keep you quiet on your hardest days. He is not afraid of your feelings. He is afraid of your *building*. He knows that if a hurting person opens their mouth and boasts on Yahuah anyway, a throne goes up in a place the darkness was counting on to stay empty, and the King moves in.

## So sing like you are building something

I want you to walk into your next gathering and see it for what it is.

You are not an audience. You are a construction crew. Every person who came in carrying a hard week, every voice that decides to lift the shout whether it feels it or not, is laying material for a throne that the King of the universe has *promised* to come and sit on. That is not a metaphor to make you feel better about singing. It is the mechanics of the room. He said He would inhabit the praises of His people, and He does not go back on where He said He would sit down.

And notice, the line says *the praises of Israel*, not *the praise of one Israelite*. It is a people's word. One plank does not make a throne. But a *gathering*, a whole company lifting the shout at once, the strong covering the weak, the full-hearted carrying the empty-handed, that raises a seat wide enough for the King to reign over the entire room, and everyone in it goes home having been ruled by the very thing they built.

So do not mumble. Do not save it for when the mood is right. Somewhere in your city tonight a handful of ordinary people are about to open their mouths and raise a throne out of nothing but worship, and the King of Heaven is already on His way to take His seat.

Build it well. He is coming to sit down.
