I want to tell you about the loudest sound in the whole Bible, and what it turned out to actually be.

There is a man named Job who loses everything, his children, his health, his whole world, for reasons he is never told. And for chapter after chapter he does the thing we are all quietly warned not to do: he demands an explanation. He wants God in the witness box. He builds his case. He will not be comforted with slogans. And then, at last, God answers him. But not the way anyone expects. *The LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind* (Job 38:1), out of a storm, in a voice, and what He says is not a single explanation. It is a torrent of questions. *Where were you when I laid the earth's foundations? Have you commanded the morning? Can you bind the chains of the Pleiades, or loose the cords of Orion?*

I have spent my life on the wonder of sound, and I have never gotten over the fact that when God finally speaks the answer everyone is waiting for, *the answer is a voice out of a storm that does not answer anything.* It solves nothing. It explains nothing. And Job, who had cross-examined heaven, covers his mouth with dust and says the strangest thing: *My ears had heard of You, but now my eyes have seen You* (Job 42:5). He got no explanation. He got a Presence. And it was enough.

That is the seed of the whole album. The whirlwind was not an answer. The whirlwind was a face.

## The title is an invitation, not a threat

I called it **The Interrogation** because I wanted to name the thing most of us are too ashamed to admit we are doing.

We come to God with a list. In the opening song I sing it exactly as it happened to me, *I came in with a legal pad, every line a thing You had not said.* The doubt I did not throw out. The fear I never told. The questions the chapel could not hold. And I braced, the way we all brace, for God to flinch, to be offended, to withhold the room, to punish the honesty. And what I found instead is the reversal the whole record turns on: *You did not laugh, You did not leave. You pulled the second chair up close. You said, beloved, ask Me anything. I have been waiting for this most.*

That is not sentimentality. It is the pattern of Scripture. Moses argued with God at the burning bush. Abraham argued with Him over a city. Habakkuk stood on his watchtower and refused to come down until he got an answer. Jeremiah wept and accused Him to His face. And **Yahuah** did not strike a single one of them down. He *leaned in.* There is a song on the record called "The God Who Argues Back," and its whole confession is this: *a God who can be argued with is a God who can be truly known.* The interrogation is not the opposite of faith. Sometimes it is the front door of it.

## Where were You

The hardest song I have ever written sits early on the album, and it is just one question repeated. "Where Were You."

Where were You the night the call came. Where were You when the doctor's mouth turned the room into disaster. Where were You when the prayer was good and the answer was a stone. I did not want to write a resolution for that song too quickly, because grief does not resolve on a schedule and I refuse to lie to the grieving. *I am not asking for a verse,* the song says. *I am asking for a face. I am not asking for a fix. I am asking for the place.* That is the truest thing about lament. It is not, at bottom, hunting for information. It is hunting for *company.* The Psalms taught me this. *My God, my God, why have You forsaken me* (Psalm 22:1). *How long, O LORD? Will You forget me forever?* (Psalm 13:1). Those are in the songbook of the faithful. God kept the accusations *in the worship book.*

And what I found, what Job found, is that the answer to *where were You* was never a diagram. It was a companionship in the dark. *You did not hand me a why. You sat down in the silence with me. And somehow that was more like love than any rescue, any power.*

## The wonder that made me small enough

Here is what the physics of the whirlwind taught me, and why the sound of it matters so much.

A storm is the loudest thing most of us will ever stand near. And when God chose to answer the deepest human demand for meaning, He did not send a page of reasons. He sent a *sound so large it reorganized the man standing in it.* He walked into Job's deposition and turned the question into flame. Every argument I had rehearsed in the car, every point I had practiced at night, when the door clicked shut and the wind came through, they simply *took flight.* Not because they were shamed. Because they were suddenly the wrong size.

In "The Whirlwind Answer" I sing the thing I most needed to learn: *the whirlwind did not break me down, the whirlwind made me small enough to fit inside the love that holds the galaxies, the dust, the rough.* That is the gift hidden in the storm. Awe does not humiliate you. It *right-sizes* you. It takes a soul that had swelled up with its own need to understand everything and gently shrinks it back down to the scale where it can finally be held.

> The whirlwind answer was not an answer, the whirlwind answer was a face. You did not solve, You did not soothe. You filled the courtroom with Your grace.

## The questions that get to stay

I need you to hear how the album ends, because it is the whole pastoral point.

The wrestling does not tie every question off. There is a song called "The Questions That Stayed," and it is about waking up after the long night to find that some questions are still sitting at the table, *but they had lost their teeth somehow. They sat like friends, not enemies.* Why the child. Why the cancer. Why the prayer that fell so flat. *I do not know,* the song admits. *I have stopped pretending that I do. But I know the One who knows, and that is enough to walk me through.* Faith, it turns out, is not the absence of the question. Faith is the carrying of the load, loving still the Author of the chapter that came late.

The whole record is framed by another man who wrestled through a night: Jacob, alone at the ford of the Jabbok, gripping a Stranger until daybreak and refusing to let go without a blessing (Genesis 32:24-30). He got his blessing. He also got a new name, Israel, *one who strives with God*, and a limp he carried the rest of his life. There is a song called "The Limp I Left With," and it blesses the wound: *the slow, uneven walking that became my praying task.* You can leave an honest wrestling with God marked. That mark is not your defeat. It is the proof He was really there.

## For the one holding a legal pad

I made this for you, the doubter, the deconstructor, the griever who is furious and faithful in the same breath.

If you have a list of accusations you have been too afraid to say out loud in church, bring it. Bring the page, bring the pen, bring the part you cannot mend. **Yeshua** is not the God of the polished and the smiling. He is the God who argues back and loves you all the while, who has been pulling a second chair up close since who knows when. He will not flinch. He will not flee. He may not hand you the explanation you came for. But He will do the thing that turns out to matter more. He will fill the room with His face.

You do not have to solve Him to be held by Him. You only have to stop running long enough for the wind to come through. And when it does, you will find what Job found and what I found: the whirlwind was never an answer. It was Someone, at last, in the room. And He had been waiting for this most.
