For years I looked for refuge the way you look for a building, some fixed place I could get to, lock behind me, and finally rest inside. *Refuge* is the album I wrote when I finally understood that I had been looking for the wrong kind of thing.

The shelter is not a place. The shelter is a Person. And once that lands in you, it changes everything about how you survive.

## Stepping through the door

The record opens exposed, a man who has been running in the storm too long, the rain soaking the worn Bible in his hand, seeing at last a lamp in a window and a door. "My Refuge and Fortress" leans on the verse my grandmother taped to her kitchen window: *God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble* (Psalm 46:1). But here is the turn the whole album is built on. I step through the door, and I discover the walls of the fortress are not stone. *You are the shelter and the One sheltered with me.* **Yahuah** is the door and the room. That is not a location you flee to. That is a Presence you dwell inside.

From there the album goes deeper into the house, room by room. "Safe in Your Shadow" sits under Psalm 91, *he who dwells in the secret place of the Most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty*, and the relief of it is that inside the shadow you do not have to perform. You do not have to explain why you are tired. The shelter never asked for your credentials.

## The tenderest shelter in Scripture

Then comes the song I can barely sing without my voice going soft. "Under Your Wings" takes the mother-hen image, *he will cover you with his feathers, and under his wings you will find refuge* (Psalm 91:4), and holds it next to the moment **Yeshua** wept over Jerusalem and said He had *longed to gather your children together as a hen gathers her brood under her wings* (Matthew 23:37). Do you feel the weight of that? He did not say "find shelter elsewhere." He said come under My wings, come under My body, the body that opened wide on a cross. The shelter is His own wounds welcoming you in.

That is the theology the album keeps pressing toward, and it peaks on "The Cleft of the Rock." When Moses asked to see the glory of God, **Yahuah** set him in a cleft of the rock and covered him with His hand while the glory passed by (Exodus 33:22). I sang that image straight into the gospel: the cleft of the Rock is the wounded side of the Son. The Rock the prophets struck for water in the wilderness is the Rock struck for me at Calvary, and the bleeding is the shelter.

> Jesus, I am hidden in the Rock that bled for me, and the bleeding is the shelter, Lord.

I anchored that song to a hymn the saints have sung for two hundred years, *Rock of Ages, cleft for me, let me hide myself in Thee*, and I want to be careful and honest here: that line is Augustus Toplady's hymn, not chapter and verse. But the hymn is standing on solid Scripture, and the saints were right to sing it. The Rock of Ages is real, and He is the hiding place.

## Refuge is not a bunker. It is a river

Here is where I most wanted the album to surprise people. It would have been easy to make "refuge" mean a locked bunker, a hiding-away from a raging world. But "When the Nations Rage" takes the panic of the headlines and sets it under Psalm 2, *why do the nations rage? He who sits in the heavens laughs*, and refuses to let the news dethrone the King. And "River of Refuge" flat-out rejects the bunker: *there is a river whose streams make glad the city of God* (Psalm 46:4). The shelter is not walls. It is a current. It runs through the food pantry on Tuesday, the recovery meeting on Thursday, the church kitchen on Sunday, the prison Bible study on Monday. Every congregation is a tributary of that river. Refuge that only hides you is not the refuge of **Yahuah**, His shelter carries you forward, and then it flows through you to someone else.

The album also had to make room for stillness, because so many of us are holding the world together with our anxiety as if our worry were a load-bearing wall in the universe. "Be Still and Know" (Psalm 46:10) and "Selah Refuge" (Psalm 62:8, *pour out your heart before him; God is a refuge for us*) are the quiet center of the record. The Selah, the pause between the verses, is not the interruption of the work. The pause *is* the work. It is the breath that lets Him become the refuge in your body.

## Who I made this for

I made *Refuge* for the one who is soaked and out of breath and cannot find a door. For the person doom-scrolling at 2 a.m. convinced the raging of the nations is stronger than the throne. For the striver who cannot stop holding the universe together long enough to be held. For the griever who needs a place that will not ask them to explain.

The finale, "Forever My Refuge," rests on David's old prayer: *be to me a rock of refuge, to which I may continually come* (Psalm 71:3). The point of the whole album is right there in one word, *continually*. The refuge is not a season of your life. It is His own Self, and His Self does not expire. The fortress is forever. The shadow is forever. The wings, the tower, the cleft of the Rock, forever.

So come inside. You do not have to be clean first, and you do not have to be strong first. Step through the door. The lamp has been on the whole time, and the One who left it burning is the shelter and the Sheltered, the cover and the covered, and when you finally walk back out into your week, the refuge walks out with you.
