I want to take you to a hillside at night, in the hours before dawn, and put a strange pair of objects in your hands, because I think you may be holding them right now without knowing what they are for.

The army of Midian is spread across the valley below, so many that the old account says they were like locusts, their camels beyond counting. And on the ridges above them stand three hundred men. Not three hundred thousand. Three hundred. Gideon started with thirty-two thousand, and Yahuah kept saying *too many*, too many for the victory to look like anything but Him, until He had whittled a nation's army down to a number that could not possibly work. Three hundred men against a valley full of locusts.

And here is what Gideon puts in their hands. Not swords, not first. Each man gets a trumpet, a ram's horn, a shofar, and a clay jar, an empty pitcher, and a torch to hide down inside the jar. That is it. That is the arsenal. A horn, a pot, and a light shut up in the dark where no one can see it. If you were one of those three hundred, standing on that ridge with a jar in your arm while an army breathed in the dark below you, you would be forgiven for thinking you had been handed the most useless weapons in the history of war.

## The light you were told to hide

Sit with that jar for a second, because I think most of us are living inside this exact instruction and hating it.

The torch is already lit. That is the part that gets me. The fire is real, it is burning, it is right there in your hand, and you are told to put it down inside a clay pot where it cannot be seen. Carry the light hidden. Cross the dark ridge with the flame muffled and covered, giving off nothing, warning no one, changing nothing. To anyone watching, you are just a man walking in the dark with a pot. The light does the enemy no damage at all as long as it stays in the jar.

I have carried that jar. You have carried that jar. There are seasons when Yahuah has clearly put a fire in you, a calling, a word, a song, a thing you know you were made to give, and the instruction, for now, is *hide it*. Walk the ridge. Get into position. Do not let it show yet. And it is a strange, aching obedience, to hold a burning thing under a lid and give off nothing, and to trust that the covering is not a burial. It feels like the light is wasted. It feels like maybe the fire will just go out down there in the dark of the jar, unseen, unspent.

But watch what the covering is actually for.

## The breaking was the plan

Gideon gives the signal. And on that signal, three hundred men do the same three things in the same instant. They blow the trumpets. They smash the jars. And they lift the torches high and shout.

Feel the order of it. The light does not come out gently. Nobody tips the jar and slides the torch out with care. They break the pitchers. They shatter them, all at once, three hundred clay jars exploding in the dark, and in the same heartbeat the hidden fire that was doing nothing a second ago is suddenly blazing on every hill at once, and the horns are screaming, and a whole valley of sleeping locusts jolts awake to find themselves ringed by fire and sound with no idea how small the circle really is. The Midianites turn on each other in the panic. The army destroys itself. Three hundred men, a pot each, and the night is won.

Here is the thing I need you to see, the thing I have staked real hope on. The breaking of the jar was never the accident. It was not the plan going wrong. It was the plan. The whole point of the pitcher was to hold the light hidden *until the moment it was meant to be broken open*, and the breaking is what let the light out all at once, together, blinding. The jar had to be covered so the fire could arrive as a shock instead of a warning. And the jar had to shatter for the fire to do a single thing.

I cannot tell you how much of my life this has explained.

Because there are two lies that come at you when you are carrying a covered flame. The first lie says the light does not matter, since no one can see it, put it out, stop carrying it, it is going nowhere. The second lie comes later, when the jar finally cracks, and it says *this is the end of you.* The breaking feels like ruin. The loss, the grief, the thing that shattered the vessel you had built your life inside. It feels like the light is spilling out to be lost. And it is exactly then, in the sound of your own jar breaking, that the fire you were told to hide finally gets to do what it was lit for.

## Trumpets and torches, not swords

I love that these three hundred never actually swung a weapon at the height of it. Their hands were full. One hand held a horn, a sound. One hand held a torch, a light. There was no hand left for a sword. The victory came through worship and testimony, through the noise you make and the fire you carry, not through the strength you bring. That is the ministry, all of it. A sound lifted in the dark and a light that will not stay covered. I make music for a living, and this is the whole picture of it: my hands are full of a trumpet and a torch, and there is deliberately no room left for a sword, because the battle was never mine to win with force. It was won by the God who likes the number to be small enough that no one can mistake whose fire it is.

So let me say it plainly to whoever is holding a covered flame tonight.

If He has told you to hide it for now, the hiding is not the waste. You are getting into position on the ridge. The fire has not gone out inside the jar; it is being kept for a moment you cannot see yet, a moment when it will do far more blazing out all at once than it ever could have leaking out slow. And if your jar is breaking right now, if the vessel you carried the light in is cracking and you feel the whole thing coming apart, hear me: that may not be your ruin. That may be the signal. The God of the three hundred does not shatter His people to lose them. He breaks the jar so the light can finally get out. Lift the torch. Blow the horn. The valley is smaller than it looks, and the dark cannot survive what you have been carrying all this time.
