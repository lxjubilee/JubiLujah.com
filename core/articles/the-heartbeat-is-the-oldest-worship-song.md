Before a child is born, before its eyes have ever opened, it is already listening to a drum.

It does not yet know the word for mother, or for music, or for God. It knows almost nothing. But it knows the beat, steady, close, unhurried, the muffled pulse of the one who carries it. That sound is there in the dark long before there is light to see by. And so the first thing a human being ever hears is not a word, and it is not a melody.

It is a rhythm.

We have this backwards, most of us. We think worship begins when the singers stand and the good voices lift and the room finally sounds like something. We think the trained and the tuneful got there first, and the rest of us joined in later. But that is not the order things actually came in. Long before anyone could carry a tune, before there were words to set to it, there was a beat keeping time inside a body. And every single person reading this line has one going right now.

You have never been without it. You have simply stopped listening to it.

## The beat that no one had to be taught

Think about what a rhythm actually is. It is the one part of music that needs no instrument, no lesson, no gift. A person who has never sung a true note in their life can still clap. A child who cannot yet speak will still bounce when the drum starts. Somewhere in a village with no piano and no page, hands are striking a hollow log, and feet are answering, and a whole people are moving as one, and not one of them was handed sheet music to do it.

Melody is a wonderful and a learned thing. Rhythm is older than learning. It is the praise the body already knew how to make.

And here is the quiet, thrilling part, the part worth sitting still for. That pulse in your chest is not something you started and it is not something you sustain. You did not decide this morning to keep your heart going, the way you decide to hum. It was set beating before you were aware of anything, and it has kept its time through every hour you forgot it was there, through your sleeping, through your worst days, through the years you wanted nothing to do with the One who set the tempo. It never stopped keeping time for you. Even now it is going, faithful, under everything, whether or not you approve of it.

Which raises a question most of us have never once asked. If a beat was placed in you before you could consent to it, and it has been sounding your whole life without your help, what, exactly, is it keeping time for?

## Praise was in the flesh before it was in the song

Look at how the Scriptures actually sing, and you notice something. The praise there is not polite. It is not seated. When the people came up out of the sea and out from under the slavers, the first thing they did was not compose a careful anthem, the women took up hand-drums and went out dancing, and the singing rode on the beat. When the ark of the Covenant came home, the king of Israel did not conduct from a throne; he leapt, he whirled, he danced with everything in him until those watching thought he had lost his dignity, and he answered that he would be more undignified than that.

The old songs command it outright: praise Him with the drum and the dance, praise Him with clapping hands, let everything that has breath make the sound. Notice what all of that has in common. It is the body. It is the pulse made visible. Long before a lyric is chosen, the praise is already moving in the flesh, keeping time, because that is where it began.

And the very first choir the Scriptures name did not have human voices at all. When the earth's foundations were laid, we are told, the morning stars sang together and the sons of God shouted for joy. Creation opened with a sound. The whole thing was set in motion on a note. So when we are told that the very stones could cry out, and that a Maker can raise up praise from rock if the mouths go silent, that is not a threat. It is a reminder of how deep this goes. The impulse to make a joyful noise is not decoration laid on top of the world. It was woven into the world at the start, and it was woven into you before your first breath.

The drum, then, is not the primitive cousin of "real" music. It is the eldest. It is the first language the Maker gave a body, and He gave it to every body, in every nation, with no exceptions and no auditions. The heartbeat is the oldest worship song, and you have never once stopped singing it.

You have only, sometimes, forgotten Whose name it was keeping.

So here is the invitation, and it costs you nothing and no one has to be watching. Tonight, before you sleep, lay your hand flat over your own chest and simply feel it, the beat that was going before you knew your own name, the one drum you did not build and cannot silence at will. It is keeping perfect time under all of it. Let it lead you, just for a moment, back to thanks. You do not need the right words. You do not need a voice that carries. You already have the rhythm; you have had it all along.

The only question left is the one it has been asking your whole life, patiently, in the dark, for whom? Answer that one, and the oldest song you know finally has its Name.
