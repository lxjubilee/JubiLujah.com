I want to teach you a small rule of Hebrew grammar, and then I want to watch it detonate.

Here is the rule. Ancient Hebrew has almost no machinery for the words *more* and *most*. It cannot easily say *holier* or *holiest* the way we do, by bolting a tail onto the end of a word. So when it needs to make something bigger, it does the most childlike, most physical thing imaginable: it says the word again. To make a thing strong, you double it. The innermost room of the temple, the most set-apart place in the building, is not called the *holiest place*. In the Hebrew it is the *qodesh qodashim*, the *holy of holies*, holy said twice, because saying it twice is how that language reaches for the top of the scale.

Hold that rule in your hand. Now walk into the throne room with the prophet, and count.

## The word walked off the edge of the language

Isaiah is carried into the throne room of Yahuah, and above the throne stand the seraphim, the burning ones. And what he hears them saying, the one line of their speech Scripture lets us overhear, is not *holy*. It is not *holy, holy*, which would already have been the superlative, already the very top of what the grammar can carry, already *holiest, most set-apart, beyond all others*.

They say it a third time.

*Holy, holy, holy is Yahuah of hosts; the whole earth is full of His glory.*

Do you feel what that third *holy* is doing? The language had already given them its highest gear at the second word. Two was *most*. Two was the ceiling. And they went past the ceiling. The third *holy* is the burning ones running out of grammar and refusing to stop, reaching for a degree of the word that Hebrew has no way to build, and getting there by sheer, holy insistence. It is a superlative on a superlative. It is *most* raised to a power the language cannot otherwise reach. When the seraphim say *holy* three times, they are not being poetic. They are telling you, in the only way the grammar allows, that the holiness of Yahuah runs off the top of every scale you own.

> Two says *the most holy there is*. Three says *there is no scale for this*.

And here is the detail that has kept me up at night, in the best way. You can search the whole of Scripture for another attribute of God lifted to that third degree, and you will not find one. Nowhere does the text cry *eternal, eternal, eternal*, or *mighty, mighty, mighty*, or even, and sit with this, *loving, loving, loving*. His love is the theme of the whole book. His power split a sea. And still the only attribute the Scriptures ever carry all the way to that impossible third rung is *holy*. Of everything that is true about Him, this is the one the burning ones cannot say enough times.

## Holiness is not one attribute among many

We tend to file *holy* alongside the others, kind, just, faithful, holy, as if it were one item on the list of God's qualities. The triple word is correcting us.

Holiness is not one of His attributes. It is the *degree* of all of them. To say Yahuah is holy is to say that His love is not like our love turned up a little. It is love of a wholly other kind, set apart, in a category with no rivals. His justice is holy justice, His mercy holy mercy, His patience holy patience. *Holy* is the word that takes every other true thing about Him and lifts it clean out of the range of comparison. That is why it is the one word that gets the third rung. It is not competing with the others for the top of the scale. It *is* the top of the scale, applied to all of them at once.

This is what the old word actually reaches toward. To be *holy*, *qodesh*, is at root to be *set apart*, cut off from the common, belonging to a category by yourself. It is the word for the thing you may not treat as ordinary. And when you triple it, you are saying He is set apart even from the set-apart things. Not the holiest of the holy things, as though on a shelf with them. Off the shelf entirely. In His own class, of which He is the only member.

## They were not singing to Him. They were singing to each other.

Now notice something about *how* they say it, because this is where the grammar of worship gets personal.

Isaiah tells us that one called *to another*. The burning ones are not each singing a private solo up at the throne. They are turned toward one another, tossing the line back and forth, one lifts *holy*, another answers *holy*, the room fills with the third. It is antiphonal. It is a call and a response. The oldest worship in the universe is not a soloist performing for God. It is a congregation reminding *each other* of what is true, and the reminding is so glorious the doorposts shake and the house fills with smoke.

Read that into your Sunday and it rearranges everything. When you sing *holy* across a room full of other people, you are not merely addressing God, though you are. You are doing what the seraphim do, turning to the person beside you and answering their *holy* with your own, building the truth up between you until it fills the room. Praise was designed to bounce. The line is meant to be caught and thrown back. You were never supposed to sing it alone, and heaven doesn't.

And it does not stop. John, given the same window centuries later, hears the four living creatures around the throne, and they say the same three words, *holy, holy, holy*, and he tells us they do not cease, day or night. The antiphon Isaiah walked into was not a one-time event he happened to catch. It has been going the whole time. It is going right now. When you lift that word on a Sunday, you are not starting a song. You are joining one already in progress, adding your voice to a chorus that has never once paused since before the foundations of the earth.

## So sing the third holy on purpose

Here is what I want you to carry into worship, the next time that word comes up in a song, as it always does.

Do not sing *holy* the way you would sing any other adjective. It is not any other adjective. It is the one word Scripture lifts to a degree the language cannot hold, the top of every scale, the *set-apart* even from the set-apart, the only attribute the burning ones cannot say enough times. When the song gives you that word, sing it like you know the grammar. Let the first *holy* be the true thing. Let the second be the most. And when the song reaches for a third, and the great ones always do, let that be the moment you walk with the seraphim off the edge of what words can carry, and mean it.

You are not exaggerating when you triple it. You are finally telling the truth. Everything smaller than three *holys* is an understatement.

And when you sing it across the room to the people beside you, and they answer it back, know what you are standing inside of. You have joined the oldest antiphon there is. The burning ones are still throwing that line back and forth above the throne, and they have not run out of reasons, and they never will.

Sing your third *holy*, and hold it, and know: the whole earth really is full of His glory. You are just early to a song that will never end.
