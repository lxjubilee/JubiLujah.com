We have a picture of when worship begins, and I think the picture is wrong by one crucial step.

The picture is this: the band starts, the room stands, we open our mouths, and *now* worship is underway. Worship, in our imagination, is something that begins at the mouth. It is a thing we produce, a song sent up, a sound we make, an offering that starts the moment our voices do. And so the whole preparation for worship, in our minds, is preparation to *speak*: warm up the voice, learn the words, get ready to sing.

But go to the sentence Yeshua Himself called the greatest of all commands, the confession at the very center of the faith, the line His people have carried for three thousand years, and notice what its first word is. It is not a command to sing. It is not a command to the mouth at all.

The first word is *Shema*. **Hear.**

*Hear, O Israel: Yahuah is our God, Yahuah is one.*

The foundational sentence of all worship does not open at the mouth. It opens at the ear. And that one detail, once you see it, quietly overturns the entire order of praise.

## Hearing was never done with the ears alone

Start with the word itself, because the Hebrew is doing more than our English can carry.

*Shema* does mean *hear*. But in the Hebrew world, hearing was never a thing you did only with your ears. To *shema* was to hear in a way that reached all the way down into your hands, to listen so deeply that the listening became doing. The very same word, elsewhere in the Scriptures, is the ordinary word for **obey**. There was no wall between the two. To truly hear was already to begin to yield. A person who heard and did nothing had not, in Hebrew, actually *heard*; they had merely let sound pass by their ears.

So when Israel is commanded to *shema*, it is not being asked to listen politely and then decide. It is being asked to let what it hears take hold of it, to hear with such surrender that allegiance forms in the hearing itself. *Shema* is hearing plus a bent will. It is the ear and the loyalty arriving together.

> Before worship is ever a mouth, it is an ear. And before it is even an ear, it is an allegiance.

This is why the greatest commandment opens the way it does. Watch the order of the sentence carefully, because the order is the doctrine. First comes *Hear*, the ear. Then comes the confession of *who* is being heard: *Yahuah is our God, Yahuah is one.* And only *then*, only after the hearing and the confession, comes the command to *love*, to love Him with all your heart, all your soul, all your strength. The love comes third. The mouth, the song, the offering, all of it is downstream. Upstream of every act of praise there is first a hearing, and a yielding to the One heard.

## Praise is an answer, and an answer requires you heard something first

Here is why this matters more than a point of grammar, and why I would build a whole doctrine of worship on it.

Praise, at its root, is a **response**. It is an answer. And an answer, by its very nature, comes second. You cannot answer a voice you have not heard. You cannot reply to a word that never reached you. The moment you call praise a *response*, you have admitted that something came *before* it, that God spoke, or acted, or revealed Himself, and the praise is our answering back. Worship is not us initiating a conversation with heaven. It is us answering a God who has already spoken.

Which means the mouth is always the *second* half of worship. The first half belongs to the ear. And a mouth that opens without an ear having first been opened is not truly answering anyone. It is only making sound into the air, a reply to a voice it never actually heard. This is the quiet emptiness underneath so much of our singing. Not that the songs are bad. That the ear was never opened before the mouth was, and so the praise, however loud, is not an answer to anything. It is a monologue we have mistaken for a dialogue.

You cannot truly praise what you have not first heard and yielded to. Say it the other way and it lands harder: everything you genuinely praise, you praise because you first *heard* it, heard who He is, heard what He has done, and let the hearing bend you. Take away the hearing and the praise has nothing underneath it. The song becomes a roof with no foundation. *Shema* is the foundation. It is the ground the whole house of praise is built on.

## One God heard means one allegiance given

Now look at what the confession actually says once the ear is open, because it is not a neutral fact. It is a claim that demands a whole self.

*Yahuah is one.* The Hebrew is *echad*, one, single, undivided. And this is not merely arithmetic, a statement that there happens to be one God rather than several. It is a claim on the shape of your loyalty. If the One you have heard is *one*, undivided, then the only fitting response is a love that is *also* undivided, which is exactly why the command that follows asks for *all* the heart, *all* the soul, *all* the strength. Not a portion. Not a slice reserved for Sunday. The oneness of God heard rightly produces the wholeness of a life given back. You heard *One*; you answer with *all*.

This is what the ear was for. It was never meant to gather information you could take or leave. It was meant to hear the One, and in the hearing, to gather up the whole divided self and hand it over in a single undivided allegiance. The Shema is the confession that founds all praise precisely because it fuses the two things praise cannot live without: a God clearly *heard*, and a self wholly *yielded*. Get those, and the song writes itself. Skip them, and no amount of music can supply what is missing.

## So come as a listener first

Here is what I want you to carry into worship, and it will change what you do before you ever open your mouth.

Come as a *listener* first. Before you think of what you will sing, ask what you have *heard*, who God has shown Himself to be this week, what He has spoken, what He has done that your praise would be the answer *to*. Let the ear open before the mouth does. Let the hearing bend the will. Then, and only then, sing, and your song will not be sound flung into the air. It will be an *answer*, the second half of a real conversation with a God who spoke first.

This is why the greatest commandment begins with *Hear* and not with *Sing*. God knew the order. He knew that a mouth opened before an ear is opened produces only noise, but a mouth opened *after*, after the hearing, after the yielding, after the whole self has been gathered and given, produces worship that is actually worth the name.

So the next time you stand to worship, do not rush to the mouth. Go to the ear first. *Shema.* Hear Him. Yield to the One you hear. And then let the praise come up out of you as what it was always meant to be, not the beginning of worship, but its answer.

Worship begins with an ear. The mouth only finishes what the ear starts.
