Somewhere in the world, right now, the sun is coming up over a small room where a woman is singing before anyone else in her house is awake.

She is doing it quietly so as not to wake the children. She has a cup of something warm going cold beside her. The words are in a language that, statistically, you almost certainly do not speak, one of thousands of human tongues, most of which you will never hear in your life. And she is singing praise to the same One you would call on, in the same posture of a heart lifted upward, at the exact moment that on the other side of the planet a man is doing the same thing at midnight because he cannot sleep and the only thing that quiets him is the old song his grandmother taught him.

They will never meet. They could not understand a word the other said. And they are, at this instant, part of a single sound.

I want you to feel the scale of this, because I do not think we let ourselves feel it nearly often enough. The praise of Yahuah does not stop. Not for a second. It cannot, because the earth is round and it turns, and there is always a place where the light is arriving and a place where it is leaving, and in every one of those places, in every hour of every day, someone is singing. The anthem has no gaps. It has no intermission. Since long before you were born and continuing long after you fall asleep tonight, the song has been rolling around the world on the back of the turning earth, following the sunrise, never once falling silent.

## An anthem no single room could hold

We are so used to thinking of worship as something that happens in a building, on a morning, for an hour, that we shrink the whole thing down to the size of a room. And a room is a beautiful thing. But it is not the scale of what is actually happening.

Think about what a national anthem is. It is a song that binds a whole people together, sung at borders and stadiums and ceremonies, a sound that says we are one, we belong to each other, we are part of something larger than the person standing next to us. Now stretch that past every border. Imagine an anthem with no nation, or rather an anthem for a people gathered out of every nation, every tribe, every tongue, every language under heaven, a people so vast and so scattered that no field could hold them, no stadium could seat them, no single language could carry all their voices. That is the church. That is the redeemed. And the astonishing thing is that this people, who could never all fit in one place, are nonetheless singing one song.

Not the same words. The words are in Hindi and Japanese and Romanian and Russian and English and ten thousand tongues besides, and that is not a problem to be solved. It is the glory of the thing. Difference is not the enemy of the anthem. Difference is what makes it an anthem for the whole earth instead of the private song of one people. The unity was never going to be everyone forced into one language. The unity is one Name, sung in every language at once, so that the sound going up is richer and stranger and more beautiful than any single tongue could ever produce alone.

## You are never singing alone

Here is why this matters for the woman with the cold cup of tea, and for you.

She thinks she is singing alone. It is dark, or barely light, and the house is quiet, and by every appearance she is one small voice in one small room and the world is not listening. And she is right that the world is not listening. But she is wrong that she is alone. She has joined a sound that was already in progress when she opened her mouth and will still be going when she closes it, a sound so large that her voice is genuinely part of it, the way a single instrument is genuinely part of an orchestra even though no one in the audience could pick it out.

This is one of the kindest truths I know, and I want to hand it to you directly, because you have almost certainly felt small in your worship. You have stood in a half-empty room, or sat alone in a car, or sung under your breath in a hospital corridor, and it has felt like a tiny sound going nowhere. It is not a tiny sound. The moment you begin to praise, you are not starting something. You are joining something. You are adding your one voice to an anthem that has been rolling around the world without pause since before you were born, and your voice, small as it feels, makes the whole thing fuller. The orchestra is not complete without your instrument. That is not flattery. That is how a body works.

## The rehearsal for a gathering we cannot yet imagine

And all of it, every dawn service and midnight whisper, every language and every scattered room, is a rehearsal.

There is a scene near the end of the Scriptures that I cannot get to the bottom of no matter how many times I return to it. John sees a crowd that no one could count, from every nation and tribe and people and language, standing together at last, and they are crying out praise with one great voice. Everything that is scattered now is gathered there. The woman with the tea and the man who could not sleep, who never met and never could have understood each other, are standing close enough to touch, and finally, finally. They hear what they were part of the whole time. The song that circled the earth for all those ages, always in progress, always just out of earshot of the singers themselves, resolves into one room at last, and the room is large enough to hold everyone.

That gathering has not happened yet. But the song already has. This is the thing I most want you to carry: the anthem of that final day is not a new composition. It is the same song the woman is singing right now in the sunrise, the same song you are invited to join tonight. We are not waiting to begin. We began long ago. We are only waiting for the moment the scattered voices are finally brought into one room and get to hear how enormous the thing we were part of always was.

So when you sing today, in whatever language is yours, in whatever small room you are standing in, at whatever hour the light happens to be doing wherever you are, do not believe the lie that it is a small and lonely sound. Somewhere the sun is rising on someone lifting the same praise. Somewhere it is setting on someone else. And your voice, added now, keeps the anthem unbroken for the stretch of earth that is yours to cover.

The sun never sets on this song. It has been waiting all along for your voice to be part of the reason.
