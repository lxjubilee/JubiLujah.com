Every autumn, at the end of the harvest, Yahuah commanded an entire nation to move out of its houses.

Not permanently. For one week. But for that week, once the grain was in and the grapes were pressed and the barns were as full as they were going to get, the people of Israel were told to leave their solid four-walled homes and build little shelters out of branches and palm fronds and leafy boughs, and to live in them. To eat in them, sleep in them, sit under their loosely-thatched roofs and look up through the gaps at the stars. A whole nation, camping out in the yard, by law, in the very season when they had the most reason to sit smug behind stone.

They called it the Feast of Booths, or Tabernacles, or sometimes simply the Feast of Ingathering. And here is what surprises people who only know it as a strange old ritual: it was the most joyful week of the entire year. Of all the appointed times on the calendar, this was the one where Yahuah did not merely permit rejoicing but commanded it, rejoice before Me, He said, for seven days. It was known as the season of gladness. And it started with everyone giving up their walls.

## Why the harvest feast has no roof

The timing is the whole scandal of it.

If you were going to design a harvest festival, you would set it up to celebrate the walls. You would gather people into the biggest, most secure structure you had and count the full barns and toast the roof over your head. The natural instinct of a people at the end of a good harvest is to feel, at last, safe. We made it, the storehouse is full, we will not go hungry this winter, and it is because we worked and planned and built well.

Yahuah looked at that instinct and said: for one week, tear it down. Go outside. Build something that a strong wind could take, and live in it, and be happier there than you have been all year.

Because He knew exactly what a full barn does to the human heart. He knew that nothing makes a person forget God faster than security. When the harvest is in and the walls are thick, it is the easiest thing in the world to quietly begin believing that you are the source of your own safety, that the roof is yours, the grain is yours, the future is under your control. So once a year, right at the peak of that temptation, He moved the whole nation out under a leaky roof of leaves to remember the truth: that they had once lived for forty years with no houses at all, wandering in a wilderness, and that the God who fed them then in a place that grew nothing is the same God feeding them now from fields that seem to grow everything. The booth was a memory device. It said: your real security was never the walls. It was Him. It always was.

And the strange thing, the thing the festival proves, is that people are not less joyful under the flimsy roof. They are more. There is a gladness available to a person who has laid down the illusion of self-made safety that is simply not available to the person still clutching it. The booth week was the happiest week precisely because it was the week you stopped pretending the walls were what kept you.

## Ingathering is the other name for it

Do not miss the other title the feast carried: the Feast of Ingathering. It came at the moment the harvest was gathered in, the scattered fruit of a whole year's labor brought home from the far fields into one place. Everything that had grown out there in the open, in a hundred separate plots, was now carried in and gathered and shared.

That word has always been the heartbeat under my name. Jubilee is about release, yes, debts cancelled, captives freed, the trumpet across the land. But release is not the end of the story. Release is so that there can be a gathering. You free the captive so that he can come home. You cancel the debt so that the family can be reunited on their own land. The trumpet does not just open the door; it calls everyone back to one table. And the Feast of Ingathering is the picture of what all that freedom is finally for, a whole scattered people, brought in from the far fields, sitting together under open sky, glad.

I think this is why the prophets, when they wanted to describe the great future homecoming, the day the exiles would stream back from every direction, from the east and the west and the far islands, kept reaching for the images of this feast. Booths and branches and harvest and joy. The ingathering of a family was always a rehearsal for the ingathering of the world. Every autumn, when the nation moved outside and gathered its harvest and sat together in the leaf-thatched light, it was practicing for a day when Yahuah would gather in a harvest of people from every nation under heaven and seat them at one long table with no walls between them at all.

## Come out from behind the walls

Here is what I want to leave in your hands.

You have built some walls. Everyone has, and most of them are good, a home, a savings account, a plan, a carefully managed life. I am not telling you the walls are sin. Yahuah never told Israel to burn their houses down. He told them to step outside of them for one week, on purpose, so that the walls would never become the thing they trusted instead of Him.

But I suspect some of you have not stepped outside in a very long time. You have gathered your harvest and pulled the roof down tight and told yourself that the security is what will make you glad, and it hasn't, quite, has it? The full barn did not deliver the gladness it promised. It never does. Gladness lives outside, under the leaky roof of dependence, in the booth where you can see the stars through the gaps and are reminded, every night for a week, that the God who kept you in the wilderness is the one keeping you now.

So come out from behind the walls, at least for a while. Hold your good things loosely enough that a wind could take them, and discover that you are not less safe for it but more, because your safety was never the walls, and the sooner you remember that, the sooner the joy comes back.

And know what the whole feast was pointing at. There is a great ingathering coming, a homecoming for everything the years have scattered, a table under open sky with room for the people brought in from the farthest fields. Yeshua is gathering that harvest even now. Your booth this side of it is meant to feel temporary, because it is. You were never building for the wilderness. You were being brought home.

Move outside. The best week of the year begins the moment you let go of the walls.
