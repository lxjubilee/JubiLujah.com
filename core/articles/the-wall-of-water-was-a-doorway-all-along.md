## Pinned Between the Army and the Ocean

Picture the worst position a rescued people ever stood in. Behind them, six hundred chariots throwing up dust. In front of them, the Red Sea, no boats, no bridge, no shallows. On both flanks, wilderness. Israel had walked out of Egypt free on Friday and by the next morning looked utterly trapped. The math said they were finished.

I have stood in versions of that spot, and so have you. The diagnosis on the counter with the army of what-ifs behind it. The courtroom date. The bills stacked against a wall of water you cannot cross. And the cruelest lie in that moment is the one that says the obstacle in front of you is the end of your story.

I made a whole album about the night that lie drowned. Because what Israel could not see is the thing I most want you to see: **the wall of water was a doorway all along.**

## The Strange Command in the Panic

Before the sea ever moved, Moses said something that makes no sense to a cornered heart. Exodus 14 records it: *Do not be afraid. Stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD. The LORD will fight for you, and you have only to be silent.*

Stand still. Of all the instructions to give people with an army bearing down, stand still.

> The Lord will fight. You hold your peace. Salvation's near, the chains release.

That is the hinge of the whole record, and it upended how I understand deliverance. We are trained to believe rescue is something we muscle our way into. But at the sea, **Yahuah** did not hand Israel swords. He handed them stillness. The one job of the delivered was to stop striving long enough to watch the Deliverer work. The pillar of cloud slid between them and Pharaoh, and the same God who heard their groaning in Egypt became the wall at their back while He opened the wall in front.

### What's Behind You Stays Behind You

There is a song on the record about the rearview mirror, because deliverance is never only about what is ahead. Israel could still hear the chariots. Six hundred of them, wheels loud, dust rising, the entire machinery of their old slavery bearing down. And the pillar of cloud slid in between the army and the people and simply *stood there*, a wall of glory holding the past off the redeemed.

The chorus names it plainly: "What's behind stays behind us. We are walking through the night."

I wrote that verse for the addict on the highway out of last year's house, for the survivor with the paperwork finally settled, for the person who keeps flinching at a life they already left. Pharaoh's army looked enormous in the rearview, but a drowning army cannot drive. The same **Yahuah** who opens the water ahead of you posts Himself between you and everything that used to own you. You do not have to keep turning around. What is behind you stays behind you, because He is standing in the gap you keep trying to guard yourself.

## The Warrior Who Does the Fighting

There is a line in the Song of Moses that I built a centerpiece around. After the sea closed over the chariots, Israel sang, *The LORD is a warrior; the LORD is His name*, Exodus 15, verse three. Not *we are warriors.* Not *look what we pulled off.* The LORD is a warrior.

The centerpiece song shouts it back over and over, "He fights the fight we cannot fight; Faithful Father, He wins the night", until the truth sinks past the panic.

I need you to hear the relief in that. So much of religious exhaustion comes from believing the war is ours to win. But the testimony of the sea is that the horse and rider went into the water by a hand that was not human. Later, when King Jehoshaphat faced three armies with no strategy, the prophet told him the same ancient truth, the battle is not yours, but God's, and Judah marched in singing instead of swinging. Praise became the weapon precisely because the fighting belonged to Someone stronger. That is not passivity. That is knowing whose arm actually parts seas.

## The Obstacle Was the Exit

Here is the revelation the whole album is built to arrive at, and it still undoes me.

Pharaoh looked at the sea and saw a trap. Israel looked at the sea and saw a wall. But **Yahuah** looked at the very same water and saw a *gate.* The obstacle that was supposed to end them became the road that saved them. The thing in their way was the way out.

> What looked like the wall was the way. He made the sea a doorway. He knew the day.

Sit with that against your own impossible water. The counter where the diagnosis sits. The wall of a marriage that looks past saving. The account that will not stretch. What if the barrier you are staring at, convinced it is the terminus of your story, is the exact place God intends to make a doorway? He does not always take the sea away. Sometimes He splits it and walks you through the middle of it on dry ground, so that the very thing that terrified you becomes the testimony you tell forever. He did not trap Israel against the water. He delivered them *to* the door.

## Miriam Already Had Her Tambourine

I could not end this record on the far shore without a woman I love. Exodus 15 says that when Israel came up out of the sea, Miriam took a tambourine in her hand, and all the women followed her out with dancing.

She did not wait to process the trauma quietly. She did not schedule the celebration for a more composed season. Sand still wet on her feet, Egypt's smoke still hanging in the air, she picked up the tambourine early and led the women in the shout.

The song leans in with the line I most want you to keep: "Every breakthrough has a tambourine, pick it up, sister; the women are waiting."

That is the posture I want to leave you in. You may be mid-crossing right now, walls of water on your right and your left, heart still pounding from the army behind. Stand still long enough to watch the Warrior work. And when your feet touch the far shore, even before the sand dries, reach for the tambourine. Because the God who split the sea still reigns, forever and ever, and the doorway He made in the impossible was never the exception. It is exactly who He is.
