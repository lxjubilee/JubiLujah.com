Two-fourteen in the morning, the phone is face-down on the nightstand, and you cannot sleep. The list of names you are carrying is longer than the hours left in the dark. That is where I wanted this record to find you, not on Sunday with your good clothes on, but on the kitchen floor at the exact minute you decided nobody was coming.

Because somebody is. And you can sing before the chains come off.

That is the whole confession of *Earthquake at Midnight*. Twelve songs that start in the hush of a sleepless house and end with the sun climbing a prison wall. I did not want to hand you a victory song and pretend I skipped the night to get there. I wanted to walk the night with you, minute by minute, and show you what I have learned on that floor: the praise comes first, and then the ground moves.

## Why the Album Is Named for a Prison

There is a story in Acts 16 I have never been able to leave alone. Paul and Silas, beaten, backs torn open, feet locked in wooden stocks in the innermost cell of a Philippian jail. Every reason to be silent. And Scripture says, *"And at midnight Paul and Silas prayed, and sang praises unto God: and the prisoners heard them"* (Acts 16:25).

Read the order slowly, because the order is the sermon. They sang **while bound**. The song went up before a single lock had moved. And then, *"suddenly there was a great earthquake, so that the foundations of the prison were shaken: and immediately all the doors were opened, and every one's bands were loosed"* (Acts 16:26).

The earthquake did not come to *start* the worship. It came to *answer* it.

That is why the album is named for an earthquake and not for the morning. I did not want to name it after the outcome. I wanted to name it after the moment your faith is tested hardest, the moment the room is still dark, the stocks are still on, and you decide to open your mouth anyway. **Yahuah** is not moved by the volume of the crowd. He is moved by the saint who will worship in the inner cell.

## Walking the Night, Track by Track

I built this one as a real night, in three acts, so you would know exactly where you are standing.

It opens in the tarrying. *"Midnight Finds Me Praying"* meets you at your own 2:14 AM and tells you the truth the enemy hides: midnight is not where the prayer goes to die, midnight is where the Helper meets your cry. *"The 3 AM Watch"* takes you to the hospital corridor and the cold coffee and the hand you are holding that will not warm up, and it stands on Psalm 63:6, *"when I remember thee upon my bed, and meditate on thee in the night watches."* Then *"Tarry With Me Holy Ghost"* plants both knees on the linoleum and refuses to leave, because **Yeshua** Himself told the disciples to *tarry* in the city until they were endued with power from on high (Luke 24:49).

The middle act is the long haul, and I did not make it pretty. *"Persistent Widow Prayer"* is the woman in Luke 18 who would not stop knocking, and the whole point of her, Yeshua said, is *that men ought always to pray, and not to faint* (Luke 18:1). *"Groanings Too Deep for Words"* is for the night your chest is heaving and no sentence will come, and Romans 8:26 promises that the Spirit Himself makes intercession for us *with groanings which cannot be uttered.* You do not have to have the words. He is praying the ones you cannot say.

Then comes the hinge of the whole record: *"Sing Hymns At Midnight."* This is the model. This is Paul and Silas. Sing even when the chains are on. The praise will be the dawn.

> The bound singing until the bound come alive, that is not a strategy. That is the oldest weapon the church ever carried into a dark room.

*"The Watchman Does Not Sleep"* holds the last tension, Habakkuk on his tower saying *"I will stand upon my watch"* (Habakkuk 2:1), and then the floor drops out. *"Earthquake (At Midnight)"* is forty-five seconds of held breath and then the whole room breaks: *Earthquake! At midnight! Every chain falls off!* I wrote it so the music itself would be the quake, so your body would feel the *suddenly* before your mind caught up.

## Who I Made This For

I made it for the intercessor nobody sees. The one on the floor at 3 AM with a list of names and a chest that will not breathe. The mother praying for a child who has not called in a year. The chaplain on the night shift. The saint who has held a wall so long she has started to wonder if the morning is a rumor.

And I made it because the breakthrough is bigger than your one prayer. Watch how Acts 16 ends. The chains fall off, and then the jailer, the very man who locked them in, falls down trembling and cries, *"Sirs, what must I do to be saved?"* And Paul says, *"Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house"* (Acts 16:30–31). That whole household came home that night. *"Jailer Saved Tonight"* on this record is me telling you: you thought you were praying for your one thing. **Yahuah** was setting a whole house free while you sang.

The album does not end at the earthquake. It ends at sunrise. *"Morning Has Broken Through"* names the real content of the breakthrough, not just that He answered, but that He *stayed.* He did not leave when the words ran out at three. And *"Sunrise on the Prison Wall"* lifts the very first line of the album, *midnight found me praying*, into celebration, because the woman who walked in on the bedroom floor walks out at the open door, and the other prisoners walk out with her.

Here is what I need you to hear tonight, wherever the dark has you. The chains are real. I am not going to sugar it. But you were never meant to wait for the morning to start the song. Open your mouth in the inner cell. Sing before anything moves. The foundations of that prison are closer to shaking than they have ever been, and the God who answered at midnight in Philippi has not aged a day, has not cooled, and has not run out of *suddenly.*

Sing, beloved. The morning is already on its way to you.
