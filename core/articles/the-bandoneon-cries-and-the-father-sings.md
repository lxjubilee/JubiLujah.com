Eleven thirty-three at night. The kettle not on yet. The chair pulled out from the table but nobody sitting in it. The silence in a dark kitchen is a heavy thing, and if you have ever sat in it, waiting on a phone call, or a test result, or a paycheck, or just for the fear to loosen its grip, then you already know the sound this album is trying to make.

I set **Cristo No Se Va** to Argentine tango, to the bandoneón, because the bandoneón is the one instrument I know that cries like a person. It opens like a window, it breathes in and out, and it sighs the exact sigh you sigh at three in the morning when you cannot name what is wrong. I wanted an instrument that would not pretend the night is fine. And then, over top of every one of those cries, I put the answer the whole record exists to give: the Father, singing back.

## What the title means

*Cristo No Se Va* means "Christ Does Not Leave." That is the whole thesis, and I built it on a single verse the way you build a house on a single rock. **Yeshua** made a promise and He did not soften it: *"I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee"* (Hebrews 13:5). The title track sings it plain:

> He's not the friend who shows up on the easy day. He's the One who never goes away.

Tango is usually the music of the one who *left*, the lover gone, the door closed, the empty room. I took that grieving, gorgeous music and turned it inside out. On this record the tango is not about who walked away. It is about the One who *stayed.* The bandoneón weeps the way grief actually weeps, and the Father does not tell it to stop. He just refuses to leave the room while it does.

## Why I had to make it

Because most of the real crises of a life happen at night, with no audience, and the church does not always have a song for that hour.

We have plenty of songs for Sunday morning with the lights up. But the diagnosis comes at 11:47 PM in a hospital corridor. The panic comes at 3 AM at the kitchen table. The loneliness comes when the phone sits silent on the counter and does not ring. I needed to make a record for *those* hours, not to explain the pain away, but to sit inside it and testify to the one thing I have found to be true every single time: He was already there before I said a word.

The opening song, "El Bandoneón Abre," says it over and over until you believe it, *the Father was in the window before the prayer. The night arrived, but He arrived before it.* That is the good news for the dark: you are not waking Him up with your 3 AM cry. He was already awake. He was already standing there. Scripture says even the dark is not dark to Him, *"the night shineth as the day: the darkness and the light are both alike to thee"* (Psalm 139:12). The night that terrifies you does not so much as dim His sight of you.

## The songs, and the long night

The album walks through the specific nights, one at a time, and refuses to be vague about any of them.

"El Corredor del Hospital", *The Hospital Corridor*, is the fluorescent-lit walk at almost midnight, and its subtitle is the whole comfort: *Cristo Camina Conmigo,* Christ walks with me. Down that hallway you are never actually walking alone.

"Cuando el Dinero No Llega", *When the Money Doesn't Come*, is for the believer who is ashamed to bring their empty bank account to God, as if He only handles spiritual things. He handles the rent too. He is Lord of the corridor and the kitchen and the unpaid bill.

"El Teléfono Que No Suena", *The Phone That Doesn't Ring*, is the loneliest one, the silent phone on the kitchen table at 9:47 PM. And its subtitle is the turn: *El Padre Llama,* the Father calls. When the call you were waiting on never comes, there is Another who is already calling your name.

Every one of these songs does the same thing. It names the night honestly, no fake cheer, no rushing past the ache, and then it points to the Presence that was already in the room before the ache started.

## The mercy that shows up at dawn

Then the record turns, and it turns exactly where the Bible turns: at the morning.

"El Padre Es La Luz" is built on Lamentations 3, a book of *tears,* which is the point. Right in the middle of the saddest scroll in Scripture, the prophet writes: *"It is of the LORD's mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness"* (Lamentations 3:22-23). The Scriptures open at three-twenty-two, the kettle goes on again at 6:14 AM, and the mercies that got you through the night are already fresh for the day. **Yahuah** does not ration His mercy. He bakes a new batch every single morning.

> Weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning (Psalm 30:5). The night is real, and it is not the last word.

And "Cuenta Las Estrellas", *Count the Stars*, reaches back to the night **Yahuah** took Abram outside and said, *"Look now toward heaven, and tell the stars, if thou be able to number them… So shall thy seed be"* (Genesis 15:5). Notice the timing: God gave His biggest promise *at night.* He did not wait for the sun. He used the dark itself, the very thing Abram was afraid of, as the canvas for the promise. Your dark sky is not empty. Count it. It is full of what He has promised.

### We danced in the dark anyway

There is a song near the end I could not make this record without: "Bailamos en la Noche," *We Dance in the Night.* Because here is what I have learned as a man who leads worship in a culture that dances at every wedding and every wake. You do not have to wait for the morning to dance. The joy of **Yahuah** shows up *before* the sunrise, in the dark, and you can dance right there in it while the bandoneón is still weeping. The album's last track is called "El Amanecer", *The Dawn*, and its subtitle is the testimony of the whole night: *We Never Stopped Dancing.* The morning did not start the joy. The joy was there in the dark the whole time, because He was there in the dark the whole time.

## Who this album is for

This one is for the 3 AM kitchen and the almost-midnight corridor. For the person staring at a phone that will not ring, or a bill that will not shrink, or a chair that will not be filled again. For everyone who has decided, quietly, that the night is proof God left.

He didn't. He was at the window before you prayed. He walks the corridor beside you. His mercies are already rising for the morning you cannot see yet. **Cristo no se va**, Christ does not leave, and I would stake my whole life on that sentence, because I already have.

Let the bandoneón cry. It's allowed. Just listen underneath it, and you will hear the Father singing back. And if you have any strength left at all tonight, get up, in the dark, and dance. The dawn is coming, and He never once left the room.
