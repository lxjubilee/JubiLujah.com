Try to hand me a song.

Not the recording. Not the sheet music, not the file, not the vinyl. The song itself, the thing that made you cry in the car, the thing that got a room of strangers to lift their hands at the same moment. Put *that* in my palm.

You can't. Nobody can. It has no weight. It has no color. It has no location. You cannot point to where the song is in the room, only to where the air is moving. It has no edges. And most unsettling of all, it does not even fully exist in any single instant. A song only *is* while it is going. Freeze it, stop time on one note, and you don't have a song, you have a pressure. The song lives only in motion, only across time, dying continuously into the note behind it and being reborn continuously into the note ahead. It is never all there at once. And the moment it stops moving, it is simply gone.

By almost every test we use for whether a thing is *real*, can you hold it, weigh it, keep it, point to it, stop it and still have it, a song fails completely.

And yet.

And yet a song can take you apart. A song can reach a grief you had bricked up for twenty years and open it in four bars. A song can walk a terrified person through a night they were sure they would not survive. A song sung by people long dead can arrive, intact, in a room they never imagined, and do its full work on hearts that were not born when they sang it. The most weightless, un-holdable, cannot-be-frozen thing in your whole day may also be the thing that changed you most.

I think about this more than is probably reasonable, because I make my living inside it, and because I have come to suspect that music is not just beautiful. I think it is *telling us something*, quietly, constantly, about the whole architecture of what is real. And the thing it is telling us runs exactly opposite to what our hands believe.

Our hands are convinced that real means holdable. Solid. Weighable. Ownable. The whole grabbing logic of a human life runs on it: get your hands around enough solid things, money, property, security, the visible and the verifiable, and you will have gotten your hands around reality itself. We stake our peace on what we can grip.

But a song will not be gripped, and a song is undeniably real, and once you have really noticed that, a crack opens in the whole assumption. Because a song is not the only thing like this. Neither is love. You cannot hand me a pound of it. Neither is courage, or forgiveness, or joy, or grief. None of them can be held, weighed, or frozen. All of them exist, like music, only *in the motion*, love only while it is being given, forgiveness only while it is being extended, joy only while it is being lived. And every one of them is more real, and more powerful, and more permanent than the solid things we spend our lives clutching. The most world-changing forces you will ever encounter all share the song's strange nature: weightless, un-holdable, alive only in motion, and truer than the floor under your feet.

The old Scriptures were saying this long before anyone measured a sound wave. *The things that are seen are temporary,* they insist, *but the things that are unseen are eternal.* We read it as a poetic flourish and go back to trusting our hands. But it is not a flourish. It is the plainest description of reality on offer, and music has been demonstrating it in every worship set, every lullaby, every song at every funeral, the entire time. The holdable things are the passing ones. The un-holdable ones are the ones that last.

Which is why I have come to love, rather than resent, that you cannot hold a song. It is training. It is God teaching the grabbing creature, gently and over a lifetime, that the realest things were never meant to be gripped. They were meant to be *received while they move,* and then passed on while they are still warm.

And this is where it turns from philosophy into something I would kneel down and say to you softly, because it is the whole point.

The God the Scriptures describe is, Himself, exactly this kind of real. You cannot hold Him. You cannot weigh Him or freeze Him or set Him on a shelf and own Him. Every attempt in the ancient story to reduce Him to a holdable object, to cast Him in gold, to nail Him down to one mountain, to get a grip on Him and keep Him, ends in ruin, not because He is less real than the idol, but because He is *more* real, in precisely the way a song is more real than the speaker it comes out of. He will not be held. But He can be *received while He moves*, met in the going, encountered in the living current, known the way you know a song: not by seizing it, but by letting it move through you and take you somewhere you could not have carried yourself.

I find that unbearably hopeful, especially for the people who are certain they have missed Him because they cannot point to Him. If you have ever felt disqualified from faith because you could not produce God as a holdable fact, could not weigh the evidence in your palm and be done, then hear this: you were trying to hold a song. That is not how songs are known, and it was never how He was meant to be known. He is not less real for being un-holdable. He is real the way the truest things are always real, the way the music is real, undeniable in its effect and impossible in your grip.

So here is what I would leave in your open, empty hands, which is the only honest place to leave it.

Stop trying to hold the song. Let it move. Stop demanding that the realest things become solid before you will trust them. The song was weightless and it healed you anyway. Love has no edges and it is the strongest force in your life. And the God you cannot get your hands around is not far away because you cannot grip Him. He is near in exactly the way music is near, filling the room you are standing in, moving through the very air, asking only that you stop grasping long enough to let Him wash over you and carry you where you could not go alone.

The realest thing you will ever know is the one thing you will never hold. Open your hands anyway. That was always how it was going to be received.
