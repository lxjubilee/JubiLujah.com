Somebody taught you that Pentecost was a Sunday on the calendar. A red banner, a memorial service, a nice sermon about something that happened a long time ago to people you will never meet. I made *Pentecost Fire* to walk into that room and turn the lights all the way up.

Because Acts 2 is not a museum. It is an address. And the wind that filled that upper room is not a memory. It is weather that is still moving through buildings, and kitchens, and bus stops, and this exact room.

I built this record fast on purpose. Double-time, the Hammond Leslie spinning at the top of its speed, the kick on eighth-notes, tambourine in sixteenths, brass stabbing every "Holy Ghost." I wanted the *tempo* to preach. Pentecost was not a slow, cautious thing. When the fire fell, they came out of that room spinning so hard the crowd thought they were drunk. I wanted your feet to feel that before your head could argue with it.

## What the Title Is Really About

*Pentecost Fire.* Two of the most misread words in the whole church.

Here is what actually happened, straight from the text. The one hundred and twenty waited in the upper room, just like **Yeshua** told them to, *tarry ye in the city of Jerusalem, until ye be endued with power from on high* (Luke 24:49). And then: *"suddenly there came a sound from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind... And there appeared unto them cloven tongues like as of fire, and it sat upon each of them. And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost"* (Acts 2:2–4).

Read that last part again, because it is the whole album. The fire *divided.* It took a shape you could see. And *it sat upon each of them.* Not on the leaders only. Not on the ordained, the polished, the ones with the right last name. Each of them. The fire did not skip a single head.

That is the line I could not get past, and it became the beating heart of the whole record. On *"Fall on Me (Fall on Me!)"* I say it plain, the fire didn't skip a head. It fell on the daughter in the third row who has never felt it. It fell on the first-time visitor. It fell fresh on the grandmother who has been singing about the fire for forty years. **Yahuah** does not hand out His Spirit by seniority.

## The Wind Was Already Moving

The first thing I wanted you to know is that you are not begging a closed heaven to crack open. On *"The Wind Is Already Moving"* I lean all the way into Acts 2:2 and connect it back to the very beginning, the same Spirit that *moved upon the face of the waters* in Genesis 1:2 is the wind in the upper room. It is not a different Spirit than the beginning. The air pressure drops before the fire falls. He announces Himself like weather.

Then the fire itself. *"Tongues of Fire"* stands in Acts 2 and marvels at the thing the crowd could not explain, Parthians, Medes, Elamites, the visitor from Cappadocia, every one of them hearing the wonderful works of God in his own mother tongue. The Spirit does not translate through the brain. He speaks from love, in a language you never studied.

And when the room caught, they *danced.* On *"Dance Till the Room Spins"* I put David in the frame, *David danced before the Lord with all his might*, and Michal despised him from the window (2 Samuel 6:14–16). The world will always call the Spirit-dance foolish. Peter stood up on that first morning and told the mockers exactly what it was: *this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel* (Acts 2:16).

> The dance the world thinks is drunk is the oldest evidence in the book. Let them look out the window. Keep spinning.

## The Daughters Were Moved to the Front

I could not make a Pentecost record and leave out the line that changed everything for women like me. Peter, quoting Joel, preached it over the whole crowd: *"And it shall come to pass in the last days, saith God, I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh: and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy"* (Acts 2:17).

*"Your Sons and Daughters"* is my favorite corner of this album. The synagogue used to seat the women behind. The upper room sat them in the front. The fire fell on Mary the mother. It fell on the back-row daughter who was told to be quiet, and on the grandmother who has been dreaming for thirty years. The prophecy does not check your gender at the door. If you have breath, you have a portion.

And then the cinematic center of the whole thing, *"Acts 2 Right Now"*, where the record refuses to keep the story in the past tense. The Spirit is falling in the kitchen, the parking lot, the kid's bedroom, the dorm room, the hospital chapel, the sanctuary at midnight. I built that song so you would hear your own kitchen on the list. That is not poetry. That is Acts 2:39, *the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off.* That is *you.*

## Who I Poured This Out For

I made this for the believer who thinks the fire has an expiration date, and it ran out before she was born. For the visitor in the back who is sure the encounter is for everybody but her. For the young ones the church keeps telling to wait their turn. For the tired saint who has been to a thousand services and never once let herself believe the flame could fall on her own head, fresh, tonight.

The album does not end in the upper room. It never could. *"The Church Was Born Today"* remembers that three thousand were added in a single afternoon (Acts 2:41), the same crowd that had screamed *crucify* seven weeks before became the harvest field. And *"Fire in the Streets"* opens the door, because the upper room *had* a door. Peter lifted his voice in the street (Acts 2:14). The fire that fell was never meant to stay in the room. It was meant to walk out onto your commute, your cubicle, your corner store, carried in the pocket of an ordinary saint who finally believed it was hers.

So let me say to you what the fire said to that whole room, all at once, skipping no one: *fall on me.* You are not too late, too ordinary, too far back, or too far gone. The same wind is in the building. Lift your head. It has always been the heads He was looking for, and He still does not skip a single one.
