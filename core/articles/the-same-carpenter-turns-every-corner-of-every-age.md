Eleven thirty-eight at night, the 149th Street and Grand Concourse platform, MetroCard down to two dollars and ninety cents, the last of the pay, and the landlord already called. That is where I opened *Miracle Worker (Subway Funk)*, not in a cathedral, but on a Bronx platform under the fluorescent light, with a clavinet funk-stab and a Hammond punch and a bass walking deep in the pocket.

Because I got tired of hearing the miracles of the Gospels talked about like they closed with the first century. They did not close. The same Carpenter who worked Galilee is working the 149th Street line, and I built a whole funk record to say it in the language of the block.

## Same Carpenter, First Century to This One

The title track puts the thesis right in your ear: *same Jesus, first century, same Jesus, twenty-first century.* And the anchor is one dismissive little question the hometown crowd threw at Him in Mark. *"Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary?"* (Mark 6:3). They meant it as an insult, just a tradesman, just a working man's boy. But that is exactly the glory of it. The hands that built tables also raised the dead. The Carpenter turns the corner of *every* age.

And He handed the works forward. This is the promise the church keeps flinching away from: *"He that believeth on me, the works that I do shall he do also"* (John 14:12). So on *"Miracle Worker (149th Street)"* the city becomes the choir, shouting back *yeah, He works!*, because the works were never retired. **Yeshua** stopped for blind Bartimaeus at the Jericho gate; He stops for the corner you navigate. Same stopping. Same mercy. Different pavement.

> The works of the Carpenter did not end at the edge of the New Testament. They walked out of it, straight onto the rush-hour block.

## The Miracles Have a Bronx Address

I took the actual miracle stories and gave each one a real address, because that is how you know a God is present-tense: He shows up in a specific place, at a specific hour, for a specific person.

*"Walk (Gate Beautiful Now)"* is Acts 3, Peter and John at the temple gate, and the lame man asking for change. Peter says the line that reorganized the early church: *"Silver and gold have I none; but such as I have give I thee: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk"* (Acts 3:6). I moved the Gate Beautiful to the 149th subway gate, because the man on that bench is the same man, and the Name is the same Name. *"Open My Eyes (Lincoln Hospital)"* takes Bartimaeus, *Jesus, thou son of David, have mercy on me* (Mark 10:47), into a fourth-floor corridor at 11:18 at night, where **Yeshua** deals not in fifty-fifty odds but in opened eyes.

And *"Lazarus Come Forth (Project Tower)"* is the one I could not sing without weeping. John 11, the tomb at Bethany, and the shortest, heaviest verse in the Bible: *Jesus wept* (John 11:35). He wept *first,* and then He spoke, and *Lazarus, come forth* emptied a grave (John 11:43). I mapped it to a project-tower funeral on the fourteenth floor with the elevator broken, because the same Voice that broke the bonds in Bethany climbs the stairs of the Bronx. He is not embarrassed by your human tears. He honors the grief, and then He speaks.

## The Miracle Rides the Rush-Hour Train

There is a track on this record called *"Praise Break on the 6 Train,"* and it carries the whole idea further than any of the others. Friday, 7:43 in the evening, the doors sliding back at Union Square, and the Holy Ghost steps onto the car with the rush-hour crowd. The lady with the shopping bag starts humming. The brother in the corner has his Bible on his knee. And by the time the train passes Twenty-Third and Twenty-Eighth and Thirty-Third, the whole car is standing with hands lifted.

I wrote it that way on purpose, because the church has quietly agreed that the encounter belongs to the sanctuary and the sanctioned hour. It does not. The Carpenter does not keep office hours. If the works were handed to *believers*, then the believer at the bus stop, the cubicle, the corridor, the moving train is carrying a portable miracle. The 6 train becomes a moving sanctuary the moment somebody on it believes He is present, because He is.

Do not wait for Sunday to expect Him. He is on the train.

## The City Itself Becomes the Testimony

The tenderest thing on the record is a track called *"What God Has Done,"* built around a storefront church on White Plains Road and a mother named in the song who stood at the altar rail and passed the microphone to her four children, one by one: *tell what God has done.* And they did. *He healed my mama. He fed us when the cupboard was bare. He stopped the eviction at the door. He brought my brother home.* A whole family line reading its receipts out loud.

That is the architecture of the whole album, provision on *"Ravens at My Window,"* where the church-lady's grocery bag becomes the literal fulfillment of *I have commanded the ravens to feed thee* (1 Kings 17:4–6); restoration on *"Years the Locust Ate,"* standing on the mercy of *I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten* (Joel 2:25); and the commission of *"Signs Shall Follow,"* where **Yeshua** promises *these signs shall follow them that believe* (Mark 16:17), and the signs are not for the platform alone, but for the believer in the church and the home.

## Who I Made This Record For

I made it for the one on the late platform with $2.90 and a landlord's voicemail, sure that the dramatic miracles happen to other people in other centuries. For the family in the corridor being handed odds instead of hope. For the recovering one counting the years the locust ate and wondering if any of them can come back. For everybody who reads the Gospels like a closed museum and never once believed the Carpenter would turn *their* corner.

Because the record ends where it began. *"The Streets Still Sing"* reprises that first platform cry, *I needed a miracle on track one*, and answers it: the works ain't done, the Carpenter is still walking, and the whole city is the choir now. And underneath all of it stands the sentence Gabriel spoke to a teenage girl in Nazareth, the one I built an entire song around: *"For with God nothing shall be impossible"* (Luke 1:37).

Nothing. Not the rent, not the diagnosis, not the years you lost, not the grave you are standing over. The platform is a chapel when the chest is tight, the fluorescent is the chandelier, and the same Carpenter who worked first-century Galilee is turning the corner of your century right now. Make the call, beloved. He still works.
