There is a folder in my life that most people will never open.

Inside it are songs, finished ones. Not fragments, not sketches, not half-ideas waiting for a second verse. Whole songs, with their bridges built and their last chorus earned, sitting in a stack that no voice has ever sung into a room and no microphone has ever caught. I know how each one is supposed to rise. I know where the key lifts, where the breath should land, where the thing is meant to break open. And almost none of them have been heard.

For a long time that folder felt like an indictment. I have written far more than has ever been sung, and the arithmetic of it used to sit on my chest at night. What is a song that no one has heard? Is it even a song yet, or only the ghost of one, a rumor of music, real to me and to no one else? I would look at all that finished, silent work and quietly decide it was proof I had failed to get the music out of the room where I made it.

I do not believe that anymore. And the thing that changed my mind was not a music book or a better strategy. It was a seventeen-year-old boy with a dream that made his whole family angry.

## The picture came first, whole

You know the boy I mean. Joseph, the second-youngest of a big and complicated family, wakes up one morning with a dream so vivid he cannot keep it to himself. In the dream, sheaves of grain in a field bow down to his sheaf. Later, a second dream, the sun and the moon and eleven stars, all bowing. He tells his brothers, and it goes exactly as badly as you would expect. They already resent him, and now here he is, describing a future in which they kneel. Even his father, who loves him best, tells him to stop talking.

Here is what strikes me. The dream was complete on the day he received it. Not a hint, not a mood, a full picture, handed to a boy who had done nothing yet to deserve it and had no way at all to make it come true. And then the world went utterly silent about it. He was thrown in a pit by his own brothers. He was sold to strangers heading down to Egypt. He was bought as a slave, lied about, and locked in a prison, where he interpreted two other men's dreams and was promptly forgotten by the man he helped. Thirteen years passed between the dream and the day any part of it began to come true.

Thirteen years. And through every one of them, the picture he carried was already whole. It was true in the pit. It was true in Potiphar's house. It was true in the prison when the cupbearer walked free and forgot him. Nothing that happened to Joseph made the dream less finished. It only made it unheard.

There is a line the psalmist wrote about that stretch of Joseph's life, and I keep it near me. He says that until the time came for the word to be fulfilled, the word of Yahuah tested him. I sat with that for a long while before I understood it. The word, the given thing, the finished picture, did not just wait. It worked. The gap between receiving the dream and seeing it sung into reality was not empty time. It was the time in which the dreamer was being made into someone who could carry what he had been shown without it breaking him.

## Finished is not the same as heard

That is the distinction I had been missing, and I want to hand it to you plainly, because I suspect you are carrying your own silent folder.

A thing can be finished and true and complete, and still be unheard. The two are not the same measurement. We live in a world that only counts what makes a sound, that treats the sung song as real and the unsung one as a nice idea that didn't happen. But that is the world's arithmetic, and it is wrong. The dream in the pit was not less real than the palace it eventually became. The song on the silent page is not the rumor of music. It is music, whole and waiting, that the room has not yet caught up to.

I think of all the good that is finished in the sight of Yahuah long before any human eye confirms it. The prayer you prayed for a person who will not soften for another decade. It is not a wish flung into nothing. It is a finished thing, waiting for its appointed time to be heard. The obedience nobody noticed. The forgiveness you extended in a room by yourself. The faithful small work you did that produced, as far as anyone can see, no result at all. Finished. True. Unheard, so far.

## The appointed time is not the same as your timing

I will not lie to you about the length of that middle stretch, because Joseph would not thank me for it. Thirteen years is not a devotional pause. It is a career, a marriage's worth of waiting, a childhood. The silence around a finished thing can go on so long that you begin to doubt you ever received it at all.

But notice how the story turns. When the moment finally arrives, Pharaoh troubled by a dream no one can read. Everything that had been silent starts to sound at once. The forgotten prisoner is remembered. The boy who could hear the meaning of dreams stands in front of the most powerful man in the world, and within a single day the whole long-buried picture begins, at last, to be heard. It came in Yahuah's time and not in Joseph's. But it came whole, because it had always been whole.

So I have stopped grieving my silent folder. Those finished, unsung songs are not evidence of failure. They are dreams in the pit, real on the day they were given, true through every year no one heard them, waiting for a season I do not control. My job was never to guarantee the sound. My job was to receive the song faithfully and finish it honestly and carry it without letting the silence convince me it was nothing.

> Do not measure your finished work by whether the room has heard it yet. The room is not the judge of what is real.

If you have done a good and complete thing that has, so far, made no sound in the world, a work, a prayer, a mercy, a whole song only you can hear. I want you to stop counting it as loss. It is not a rumor. It is finished. And the God who gave the seventeen-year-old his dream and then spent thirteen years making him ready to hold it is not in the habit of letting a true thing stay silent forever. The appointed time is His. But the song is already whole.
