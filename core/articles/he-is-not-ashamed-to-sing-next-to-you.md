Ask most believers where Yeshua is during worship and they will point up.

He is on the throne. He is the audience of One. He is the King we sing *to*, seated above us, receiving the praise. And all of that is gloriously true. But it is not the only place the Scriptures put Him, and the other place is so tender it can undo you if you actually stop and picture it.

There is a line, quoted in the New Testament and lifted straight out of an ancient psalm, that puts Yeshua not above the congregation but *inside* it. Not receiving the song from a distance, but *starting* it from the middle of the crowd. Not the One we sing to, the One singing *next to us*.

## The verse the letter puts in His mouth

The letter to the Hebrews is building a breathtaking case: that the Son who made the worlds was not too proud to become one of us, bone of our bone, brother of our brothers. And to prove He is unashamed to call us family, the writer does something remarkable. He reaches back into the Psalms, takes an old line, and puts it directly into Yeshua's mouth.

The line is this: *I will declare Your name to My brothers; in the midst of the congregation I will sing Your praise.*

Sit with the setting of that quotation, because it is almost too much. It comes from Psalm 22, the very psalm that begins with the cry Yeshua screamed from the cross, *My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?* The song that opens in total abandonment does not end there. It climbs. And by the time it reaches the line the letter quotes, the forsaken One has come through to the other side and is doing something with His rescued breath: He is turning to a *crowd* and starting a song.

The One who was forsaken becomes the One who leads the worship. And the first word out of His mouth on the far side of the cross is *brothers*.

## Where Hebrew puts Him: in the middle

Look hard at the geography of that sentence, because English rushes past the most important word in it.

*In the midst of the congregation.* The Hebrew is *b'toch qahal*, in the *middle*, the very center, of the *qahal*. And *qahal* is the great gathering-word of Israel: the assembly, the called-out ones, the whole company summoned together before God. When the ancient translators carried the Hebrew into Greek, the word they reached for to render *qahal* was *ekklesia*, the exact word that would later come to mean *the church*.

So read the verse again with that in your ear. Yeshua says: *in the midst of the ekklesia, in the dead center of the gathered church. I will sing.* And the verb the New Testament uses is not a distant, kingly word. It is *hymneo*, to sing a hymn, to lift the song. The King does not merely accept the worship of the assembly from above it. He steps into the middle of the assembly and *sings*.

He is not the guest of honor watching the choir. He is *in* the choir. He is the voice that starts the line you follow. Where we imagined Him seated at the front receiving, He is standing in the crowd *leading*, the firstborn among many brothers, and He has taken the lead part.

> The One worthy of every song has chosen to sing one with us, from inside the huddle, close enough to hear.

## Unashamed, and you need to feel the weight of that

Here is the word the whole thing turns on. The letter says He is *not ashamed* to call us brothers.

Feel how much that word is carrying. To be *unashamed* to stand with someone is only remarkable when there would be every reason to be ashamed. And there is every reason. He is the sinless Son; we are the ones who denied Him at the fire, ran in the garden, wasted the years, showed up to the gathering with our failures barely hidden under our sleeves. If anyone in the history of the universe had grounds to keep His distance, to stand apart, to be worshipped from a careful, dignified separation. It is Him.

And He will not do it. He steps down off the platform of pure majesty, walks into the middle of a crowd of forgiven failures, throws His arm around us, calls us *family* in front of everyone, and *starts the song*. He is not embarrassed to be found singing next to the recovering addict. He is not too holy to lead the tune standing beside the doubter, the divorced, the barely-holding-on. He put Himself in the *midst* of exactly those people on purpose, and He is not ashamed of a single one of them.

That is the God we actually have. Not one who tolerates our worship from a safe height, but one who came all the way down into the assembly to *worship with us*, because worship, done right, is not a performance we offer to a distant King. It is a family lifting its voice together, and the eldest Brother has taken His place in the middle of the room.

## So look around. He is in the room

I want to change how you walk into your next gathering forever.

You have always looked *up* to find Yeshua in the worship, and you should. He reigns. But start also looking *around*. Because the same Scripture that seats Him on the throne also plants Him in the middle of the congregation, leading the song, unashamed of the company He is keeping. When the room lifts its voice, He is not only receiving it from above. He is *in it*, at the center, His voice under yours, His breath in the line, carrying the part when yours falters.

And notice how this completes the whole psalm. Psalm 22 does not end with one Man singing. It ends with the song spreading outward until *all the ends of the earth* remember and turn to Yahuah, until families of every nation bow, until a people not yet born is told what He has done. The lead voice in the middle of the qahal was always meant to become a *global* chorus, the one Brother starting a song that every tribe and tongue would eventually join.

That is the gathering you were made for. Not a room of individuals performing for a faraway King, but a family singing shoulder to shoulder with the risen Yeshua Himself standing unashamed in the middle of them, calling every last one *brother*, *sister*, and leading the tune home.

So sing like He is beside you. Because He said He would be, right there in the midst of the congregation, one voice among the ransomed, not ashamed to be found singing next to you.

He took the lead part on purpose. All you have to do is come in on His line.
