They told me the pan could not preach. I want to tell you where that lie goes to die.

I have sat in the pew of the second-generation child. You know the one, accent for the office in the morning, accent for granny on the phone at night, and somewhere in between, the growing suspicion that you have to choose. That the serious faith is the one with the Greek words and the systematic outlines, and the island is just the seasoning you sprinkle on top before the real meal gets served somewhere more respectable. I believed a version of that for years. Then I grew up enough to see what had actually raised me, and I made a whole album to repent of it.

## The Sound Somebody Said Was Too Shallow

The opening line of **Theology on the Pan** is not poetry. It is a receipt:

> They said our island was too shallow. Said the pan could never preach. Said the verandah ain't a pulpit. Said the doctrine's out of reach.

Every diaspora believer I know has heard some form of that sentence, even if no one ever said it plainly. And here is what I have come to know, standing as a teacher of the Word: **Yahuah** has been preaching on the steelpan for generations, whether or not anyone credentialed it. The pan is not decoration laid over the doctrine. On this record, the pan *is* how the doctrine sings.

Because a pulpit is not a piece of furniture. A pulpit is any place the truth of God is handled with reverence and handed to His people. I have watched more sound theology travel across a panyard at dusk than across some rooms with far better lighting.

## Where I Actually Learned the Faith

I did not first learn the character of God from a lecture. I learned it in a kitchen on a Sunday afternoon, rice-and-peas pot on the stove, an uncle in the doorway quoting Romans 8 to the cousins, a grandmother's hand resting on every child's head. That is the whole of **Kitchen Sunday Catechism**, the plain claim that *doctrine gets passed in the pot,* that the family table is a seminary, that a question asked over gravy is a question **Yahuah** honours.

This is not sentiment. It is Scripture's own strategy. **Yahuah** told Israel to teach His words *diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way* (Deuteronomy 6:6–7). Notice where the teaching happens, not in a temple annex, but in the house, on the road, at the table, at bedtime. The verandah catechism is not a lesser version of discipleship. It is the exact method the Lord commanded, and the diaspora has been keeping it faithfully while everyone else looked for it in a building.

Think of how much theology you actually carry that arrived this way, not in a footnote, but in an auntie's warning, an uncle's story, a grandmother's proverb dropped over the washing-up. That is not folk religion waiting to be corrected by the real thing. For many of us, it *was* the real thing, handed down intact.

That is why **Doctrine in the Children** sits near the end of the album, grandmother's hand on a grandchild's head, a bedtime prayer, a Sunday-school flannelgraph, the whole unbroken chain of faith handed down. *Grandmother to grandchild, the gospel within reach.*

## The Verandah Has Its Own Creed

One of the songs I am most protective of is **Verandah Creed**, and it is simply the Apostles' Creed confessed the way my family has always confessed it, a grandmother starting the line at the evening hour and the whole household answering.

> I believe in God Almighty, Maker of the heaven and the earth. Jesus Christ His only Son here, Holy Spirit's holy birth.

Now let me be careful and precise, because I am a teacher and I owe you accuracy: the Creed is not Scripture. It is the church's oldest summary *of* Scripture, a confession the body of Christ has held for well over a thousand years. But the *content* of it is straight from the Word, one God, Maker of all; **Yeshua** crucified, buried, risen, ascended, returning; the Spirit; the communion of the saints; the life everlasting. When my grandmother led that confession on the verandah, she was not doing something quaint. She was standing in a line of witnesses that stretches back to the apostles, and she was passing me a faith that had already survived empires.

## Sound Doctrine Never Softens on a Danceable Bed

People sometimes assume that if you make the music warm and danceable, you must have watered down the theology. I built **Atonement on the One-Drop** to prove the opposite. It puts the fiercest doctrine in the book, substitution and victory at the cross, on a reggae one-drop. *By His stripes we are healed* (1 Peter 2:24). *He spoiled principalities and powers, triumphing over them* at the cross (Colossians 2:15). **Yeshua** paid in full and disarmed every power that ever held the diaspora. The riddim does not soften that; it carries it into the body, where doctrine is meant to live.

And the reach of it is worldwide. The heart of the album is the reframe in **Sovereign on the Sea** and the finale, **Pulpit on the Pan.** Revelation shows us the end of the story, *a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues,* standing before the throne (Revelation 7:9). Every tongue. The Caribbean tongue is not waiting in a lobby for permission to enter that room; the pan is already in the New Jerusalem, one unbroken hymn from the Brooklyn pew to the throne. It was always meant to be there.

## Who I Made This For

I made *Island Theology* for the second-generation child who thinks they have to leave home to find depth. For the auntie whose kitchen has been a discipleship room for forty years without a single certificate on the wall. For the young believer choosing between the seminary tongue and the praise life, when **Yahuah** never once asked them to choose.

Hear me as your teacher and your sister: the vernacular you were half-ashamed of is a vessel the Lord has already honoured. He filled it. He preached in it. He is not embarrassed by the pan, the pot, or the verandah. He has been using all three to keep His truth alive in you. So stop apologizing for the sound of home. Pick it up and preach.

The pan can preach. It has been preaching the whole time. Some of us were just finally quiet enough to hear the sermon.
