The bravest thing I did all year took about four seconds, and no one saw it.

I was in the car. The key was still in the ignition, eight-forty-seven on the clock, coffee going cold in the cup. There was half a text I never sent glowing on my phone and a whole day I did not feel ready for. And instead of waiting to feel brave, because I have learned that feeling never arrives on schedule. I just did the small thing. I stood it up. I sent the text. That was it. That was the entire act of courage, and it was over before the coffee got any colder.

I used to think that didn't count. I thought bravery had to be a leap off a cliff, a big cinematic yes with a swelling soundtrack, and because I almost never felt *that*, I concluded I was mostly a coward. This album is my repentance from that lie. I wrote the thesis into the very first line of the first song: **a small brave is a brave, and a tiny little yes is the kind of yes I save.**

## What "Small Brave Moments" Actually Means

The title is a quiet act of defiance against the way we usually talk about courage.

We picture one enormous moment of nerve, and so we spend our lives waiting for a version of ourselves who will show up fully assembled and unafraid. She never comes. What comes instead, if you let it, is a string of four-second decisions: the mirror at 6:42, the raised hand in the meeting, the friend you finally call after months of silence, the extra twenty the cashier handed you by mistake that you turn around and give back. None of those are leaps. All of them are brave. And here is the mercy in it, a small brave is *available*. You can't summon a heroic leap on a Tuesday morning, but you can do the small thing. You can always do the small thing.

> Brave isn't a leap you're waiting to feel ready for. It's a four-second yes you make while your hands are still shaking.

## The Album Is a Year of Tiny Yeses

I laid the record out like a single year, from the first shaky yes to the harvest of them all.

"Morning Mirror" is the pep talk you finally give the girl in the glass instead of the list of shoulds, *tell her she's the keeper of her own brave heart.* "Call the Friend" is the number you've had saved with a heart emoji since a younger year, and the voicemail from October you never let yourself hear, and the courage to dial anyway *even if your voice shakes.* "Say the Thing" is the sentence you've been swallowing at a table of eight until you finally let the truth ring. "Ask for Help" is the hand half-raised under the table and the apricot-sized lump in your throat, and the discovery that *you don't have to carry it alone.*

The peak of the album is a song called "Walk In." It is just the twenty-five seconds outside a door, hands like ice on the handle, knees a little candle, almost turning around, and a countdown: *five, four, three, two, one.* And then the pivot the whole record is built toward: *I'm already in. The brave one's begun.* But my favorite might be the tender one, "Quiet Brave," about the night the bravest thing you can do is not charge forward at all. It's to stay on the bathroom floor and sit with the feeling instead of running. *The bravest thing today might be the breath I save.* Some days brave is loud. Some days brave is just not fleeing the room.

## The Verse Behind the Small Things

Here is the Scripture underneath all of it, and it reframes everything we think we know about how heaven measures a life.

We assume the Kingdom keeps score the way the world does, by the size of the moment, the bigness of the yes. But Scripture says the opposite. It tells us not to despise the day of small things, as though the tiny beginning were beneath notice. It says the one who is faithful in very little is faithful in much, that the small yes is not the warm-up act *before* the real thing, it *is* the real thing, the actual raw material a whole character gets built from. Every four-second brave you make is a vertebra of spine, and **Yahuah** is counting them even when no one else is in the car.

And there's the quiet brave, too. When Elijah was worn down to nothing, waiting for God in the wind and the earthquake and the fire, the Lord was in none of the loud things. He came in a gentle whisper, a still small voice. That has always undone me. The God who could announce Himself in an earthquake chooses the whisper, which means He is not impressed by our noise and not disappointed by our smallness. **Yeshua** met people at the exact size of the faith they had, even when it was mustard-seed small, and called it enough to move a mountain. Your trembling yes is not too small for Him. It is precisely the size He works with.

> One brave breath, one brave step, every brave a win. The small ones aren't the practice. They're the whole thing.

## Who I Made This For

I made *Small Brave Moments* for the person sitting in a parked car right now, key in the ignition, not feeling ready.

For the one waiting to feel brave before they act, who is about to learn the order is reversed. For anyone with a text they can't send, a friend they can't call, a hand they can't raise, a truth stuck behind their teeth. For the person on the bathroom floor who needs to hear that staying is a brave too. For everyone who has quietly decided they are not a courageous person because they have never once felt like one.

You don't need the leap. You need the four seconds. Do the small thing while your hands are still shaking, send it, say it, raise it, dial it, or just breathe and stay. At the end of the year you will open the shoebox and find three hundred and sixty-five receipts of brave, and you'll realize the whole tall life was built out of moments you didn't think were anything. The small brave is a brave. It always counted. And the One keeping the tally has been cheering the whole time.

Walk it out, walk it slow. One small brave is all you owe.
