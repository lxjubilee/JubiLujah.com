Seismologists will tell you the aftershock is not a new earthquake. It is the same one, still settling. The ground heaved once, the fault slipped, and for weeks or months afterward the earth keeps ringing down toward stillness, sending up smaller tremors long after everyone has folded the blankets and gone home.

I named an album after that, because I have lived it in my own body, and because I think the church badly needs a language for it. I called it **Aftershocks.**

## The door that slammed two apartments over

The opening song is a true story wearing a slightly changed coat. Six months out from a hard thing, I thought the ground had settled. I had given the casseroles back. And then a door slammed two apartments over, and my whole chest went black. I had forgotten I was still standing on a fault line.

For a long time I called that weakness. I wondered why my Bible could not lift this dust, why my prayer life had a hidden leak, why my faith felt strong but my body still felt weak. And then a doctor said a word, the clinical name for what happens to a soul that survives more than it was built to carry, and something in me eased. I wrote a whole song about that easing, *PTSD Is Not a Failing*, because the shame slid off like rain off a windowpane. A diagnosis is not a punishment. The wound is not the blame.

Here is the sentence I most want you to hear, and it stands at the center of that song: **Yeshua** does not love you less for the way you tremble. He did not say *strong.* He said *come.* He did not say *healed.* He said *home.*

## Your body is an instrument, and it keeps resounding

Now let me tell you the thing that makes me, of all the family, the one who had to write this record, because I cannot stop marveling at how **Yahuah** built us.

Your body is a resounding thing. Strike a bell and the metal goes on vibrating after the hammer lifts; that ongoing ring is not a malfunction. It is what the bell *is.* And your nervous system, it turns out, works something like that. The mind forgets what the muscles still recite. Your shoulders climb to your ears in a checkout line for no reason you can name, because your back already knew the song. A stranger's cologne in an elevator, and suddenly you are exactly where the old harm was. The body remembers. The body keeps the score.

I wrote *The Body Remembers* to hold that plainly, and then to turn it toward the One who made the wiring:

> You knit me together, You know the wiring under the skin. Teach my body the Sabbath my mind has been asking for. Teach the muscle the mercy of an unlocked door.

The psalmist marveled that **Yahuah** knit him together in secret, that he was fearfully and wonderfully made. That is not a greeting-card verse when you are shaking on the kitchen tile. It is a lifeline. The same God who designed the nerve that is misfiring is not surprised by the misfire, and He is not above the tools we make to heal it. In *EMDR Sessions* I sang about the buzzers pulsing left, then right, then left, and the honest confession that **Yeshua** is not threatened by the science, by the buzzer in the hand. He is the One who taught us how to understand. And in *My Nervous System Needs Jesus* I got even plainer: none of the breathing, the low humming tone, the cold cloth on the wrist is unspiritual. He made the nerve. He made the breath. When He breathed into the dust, the dust became a living soul, the same breath, the *ruach*, that hovers over your chest when it will not calm.

## The healing is not a closing door

The great lie about healing is that it is a single closing door: walk through, shut it tight, never look back. I believed that lie, and the album's turn away from it, *Healing in Layers*, was the first time the record lets itself breathe wide. Healing is an onion. Healing is a rose. Layer by layer, the way the morning grows, naming, then grief, then the body learning to believe in relief, then forgiveness, then the laugh. And there is a layer you have not reached yet, and that is not failure; you are walking the path. **Yahuah** did not make the world in a single afternoon. He took six unhurried days. He is not in a hurry with the work He has started in you either.

That reframe is what let me sing *The Tremor Still Comes* without pretending. A year out, the tremors visit less but they still visit. I do not have a timeline. I do not have a cure. What I have is a promise, that He will not abandon what He endures. Forty years in the desert, and the manna still fell. The tremor is not a sign that He has walked away; it is the very place He keeps meeting me, day by day by day.

## Not free from the shaking, free inside it

I did not want this album to end with a miracle it could not honestly promise, so it ends with a benediction. *The Tremors Are Teaching Me* is the quietest song on the record, just a cello and a voice, like the close of a service. The tremors came as enemies, and they are leaving as guides. They are teaching me to listen for the quiet sides of the Spirit.

And this is the whole of what I have learned, so I will hand it to you exactly as I sing it: healing is not the absence of the wound. It is the wound made luminous. The wound becoming *tuned.* You are not promised freedom from the shaking. You are promised something better and stranger, freedom *inside* the shake, because **Yeshua** is the Stillness that the storms can never break.

There is a scientific phrase, *post-traumatic growth*, for the gold that sometimes comes not in spite of the wound but through it, the way certain pine cones only open in the heat. I will not tell you the trauma was a gift; it was not. But what the Lord has done with it, the Lord has done. Joseph said it to the brothers who sold him: what you meant for harm, God wove into good. The cracks running through me turned out to be the places He holds, and He painted glory through the very places where I broke apart.

## For the one still inside the quake

So to you, still on the kitchen tile, counting backward from a hundred, tired of explaining it to anyone, hear the bridge I wrote straight to you: you are not alone, beloved. He has gathered every tear into the bottle of His care, the ones you cried when the ground came back and no one saw. He is closer than the breath. He is nearer than the air.

The ground settles long before the body does. That is not your failure. That is just how aftershocks work, and He stays for the quake, and He stays for the silence, and He stays for the tremor, and the one after, and more.
