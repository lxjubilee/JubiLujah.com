There was a curtain in the temple, and it was not a decoration. It was a verdict.

It hung between the room where people could go and the room where no one could, a woven wall thick as a man's hand, tall as a house, and stitched into its face were the same guardians that stood at the gate of Eden with a blade of fire. Every thread of it said the same thing the flaming sword had said at the beginning: *not yet, not you, not here.* The nearness a person was made for was on the other side of that cloth, and the cloth did not move.

So hold the scene in the Gospels a moment longer than you usually do. Yeshua, Jesus, hangs on a Roman cross outside the city. He cries out. He gives up His breath. And in the same hour, a mile away, inside a building He is not touching, that curtain rips. The men who wrote it down were not interested in the fabric. They were interested in *which way it went.* From the top. To the bottom.

## The Direction Was the Whole Message

Think about how you tear cloth that will not tear. You reach as high as your arms go, you seize it, and you drag *down* with everything your weight can lend you. Human strength tears from where the hands can reach. It tears from the middle up or the middle down, and it starts where a person can grip.

This tore from a height no hand was near. It started at the top, where only Heaven has fingers.

That is why the writers slowed down for it. They were telling you *whose hand.* The barrier that had stood for the entire history of the nation, the cloth that turned back kings and silenced the curious and cost a man his life to cross uninvited, was not pulled aside by a priest who finally decided the rules were cruel. It was opened from the inside of the room no one could enter, by the only One who was ever in there. Yahuah tore His own veil. He reached down the full height of the separation and split it end to end, and He did it *from His side.*

We spend most of our gratitude on being forgiven, and we should. But notice what the torn curtain is telling you. It is not mainly a picture of a debt marked *paid.* It is a picture of a **door**. Forgiveness clears the account. The veil opens the house. And the gospel was never satisfied to leave you outside with a clean record. It came to bring you *in.*

## What the Separation Actually Cost You

Trace the wound back to its beginning and you find it is older than any temple.

In the garden, the trouble was not first a list of broken rules. The trouble was that the man who used to walk with Elohim in the cool of the day heard His footsteps and *hid.* Sin did that. Its first and deepest effect was never the guilt alone. It was the hiding the guilt produced. A conscience that flinches. A nearness turned into a nakedness you cannot stand to have seen. And then the gate, and the guardians, and the sword, and the long ache of a creature built for the Presence, kept out of it.

Everything the curtain did, it did to teach that ache. Once a year, *once*, one man could pass through, and only carrying blood, and only after being made ceremonially clean, and even then his heart was pounding, because the room on the other side did not tolerate what was unholy. The system was not cruelty. It was *honesty.* It told the truth about how holy Yahuah is and how unclean we had become, and it refused to pretend the distance was smaller than it was.

So when you feel the low, wordless conviction that you are not fit to come close, that if He really saw you He would turn away, understand that this instinct is not paranoia. It is the memory of the veil. It is theologically accurate about the old arrangement. The gospel does not answer it by saying the distance never mattered. The gospel answers it by saying *the distance has been removed by the One it protected.*

## A New and Living Way, and It Is a Person

The letter to the Hebrews takes this exact image and does something breathtaking with it. It says there is now a fresh way opened into the holy place, and that the way *is the torn curtain*, and the torn curtain is His flesh. The body of Yeshua, broken open, *is* the opening. He did not merely unlock the door. He became it.

And so the same letter dares to say the words that would have been unthinkable at that curtain for a thousand years: *draw near.* Come. With a true heart. In full assurance. With a conscience that has been sprinkled clean.

> The blood of bulls kept covering the same wound over and over because it could only reach the record, never the conscience. What Yeshua did reached the conscience. It did not sponge the ledger and leave the flinch. It cleansed the *flinch.*

That is the piece we most often leave on the table. A cleansed conscience is not a legal fiction you must talk yourself into believing on the days you feel unworthy. It is the actual work of the cross applied to the deepest interior room of you, the room where the hiding lives. He did not only remove what stands *against* you in Heaven's court. He removed what stands *inside* you and keeps you at the threshold. The sword that guarded the way is sheathed. The Presence you were exiled from is the Presence you are now summoned into. And you come not creeping and not covering, but with your face lifted, *boldly,* the letter says, which is the most staggering adverb in the whole book.

## Come In With Your Face Up

Here is what I want to press into your hands, and I press it with trembling, because I am talking about the nearness of a holy Yahuah and that is not a small country to speak of.

The same tremble the seraphim carry, the covered faces, the *holy, holy, holy*, is real, and it is not being cancelled. But it is no longer a tremble that hides. It is a tremble that *draws near.* Awe and access are not enemies now. They arrive together, hand in hand, through a curtain torn from the top. You can be undone by His holiness and welcomed into it in the same breath, and that pairing, reverence that runs *toward* Him instead of away, is one of the surest signs the veil is truly gone from your own heart.

So do not stand in the outer court of your own life, rehearsing the reasons you are not fit to come further. That court was for the old cloth, and the old cloth is on the floor. If your rescue had ended at *not condemned,* it would already be a mercy beyond telling. But it did not end there. He tore the veil so that *not condemned* could become *come home.* The room is open. The Presence is uncovered. The way in is a Person, and He is holding it open with His own body.

Lift your face. You were not saved merely from something. You were saved *into* Someone. Walk in.

Inspection passed. Go, before He finds a reason you never had to give.
