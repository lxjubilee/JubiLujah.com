Ask most believers where the Kingdom of God is, and you'll get a direction: *up there*, and a time: *later*. A distant throne, a someday sky, a coronation scheduled for the far end of history. We file the whole thing under "eventually."

I made *King Yeshua Reigns* to hold two things at the same time that only sound like a contradiction. The King is enthroned in cosmic, blazing, every-knee-will-bow glory. And that same King walks into an ordinary kitchen on an ordinary weekday afternoon, pulls out the empty chair, and sits down. The album refuses to choose between the throne room and the kitchen table, because the gospel doesn't.

## Not far off, not later, here

The second track, *The Kingdom Is Here*, plants the flag. **Yeshua** did not preach a Kingdom you could only find in the future or the past. He said, "the kingdom of God is in the midst of you" (Luke 17:21), and He opened His ministry announcing that it was "at hand" (Mark 1:15). The song hammers the present tense until you can't miss it:

> Not far off, not later, here. Not someday, not maybe, here.

That is not wishful thinking. That is the plain grammar of how the King spoke about His own reign. It has arrived. The **Ruach** has fallen. The work has begun. Whatever else is still coming, the Kingdom is not only a "will be." It is a "here."

## The day it walked into a kitchen

The emotional center of the record is track seven, *The First Time The Kingdom Walked Into My House*, and it's why I chose it as this album's anchor song.

Picture it, a Tuesday in November, a messy kitchen, unopened mail, yesterday's news, a Bible on the table you keep meaning to read. And a daughter asks a small, enormous question: "who is **Yeshua**?" And the quiet in the room gets loud. The King doesn't knock. He walks in like He always belonged there, takes the chair across the way, and the house you built for yourself quietly becomes a home that belongs to **Yahuah**.

That's the whole point of the song, and it says it as tenderly as anything I've written:

> He came home to me, and I came home to Him.

This is what "the Kingdom of God is at hand" feels like on a weekday. Not a parade. A presence. The most cosmic reality in existence, walking through your kitchen door without a sound.

## But make no mistake. It is a coronation

Now, if the album only did the kitchen, it would be sentimental. So the record surrounds that intimate moment with the full, blazing coronation, because the King who sits at your table is the King before whom the universe bends.

*Every Knee Bows* takes Paul's great hymn: because **Yeshua** humbled Himself and died, "God has highly exalted him... so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow... and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord" (Philippians 2:9-11). *Lift The Gates* sings Psalm 24, "Lift up your heads, O gates... that the King of glory may come in... the LORD of hosts, he is the King of glory" (Psalm 24:7-10). And *Melech HaMashiach* stands in the throne room of Revelation, where twenty-four elders fall down and the Lamb takes the scroll no other hand could open (Revelation 5). *He Reigns From Zion* draws on Psalm 2, where the nations rage and "he who sits in the heavens laughs," having set His King on His holy hill (Psalm 2:4-6). This is not a small King who fits in your kitchen because He's modest. This is the King of kings who fits in your kitchen because He wants to.

## The crown ended up on the wounded One

There's a track I have to point you to, because it holds the strangest and best thing about this King: *The Crown Is On The Lamb*.

In the throne room John saw, the One worthy to take the scroll and receive the crown was "a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain" (Revelation 5:6). Read that again. The universe-crown does not sit on a warrior with an unmarked face. It sits on the Wounded One, the Lamb who was despised and killed, now wearing the marks of slaughter *and* the crown of everything. Heaven's anthem is "Worthy is the Lamb who was slain, to receive power" (Revelation 5:12). The King who conquers wears the scars of the cross He conquered by. That is the only throne in all of literature where losing everything is how you win the crown.

And it means the Kingdom that has come is not a Kingdom of raw force. It's a Kingdom of a King who bled for the people He now reigns over.

## He shares the throne

The album refuses to end with us as spectators, and this is the part I most want a listener to hear.

*Daniel Saw The Saints Receive The Kingdom* takes Daniel's night vision, the Son of Man coming on the clouds, given everlasting dominion by the Ancient of Days, and then the astonishing turn: "the saints of the Most High shall receive the kingdom" (Daniel 7:14, 7:18). And the finale, *We Reign With The King*, stands on **Yeshua**'s own promise: "The one who conquers, I will grant him to sit with me on my throne, as I also conquered and sat down with my Father" (Revelation 3:21). He made us "a kingdom, priests to our God" who will reign on the earth (Revelation 5:10). The King who walked into your kitchen is preparing to hand you a seat beside Him. Not because you earned it. Because that is the kind of King He is.

## Who I made this for

I made *King Yeshua Reigns* for the believer who has quietly relegated the Kingdom to the far horizon, who prays "your kingdom come" as if it's entirely a future weather report. Some of it is future, yes. But the King is at hand. He is at your door on a Tuesday. Stop waiting for a someday sky and set an extra chair.

And I made it for the one who cannot imagine that a King that big would want a table that small. Hear the album's whole argument: the Lamb who wears the universe-crown is the same One who walks into a messy kitchen and blesses the coffee like it's Sabbath. He does not visit your life as a formality. He comes home to you, so that you can come home to Him.

The Kingdom isn't only a someday. It walked in on a Tuesday. And the King who took the empty chair intends to share His throne.
