There is a decision every commander makes before a battle, and it is always the same decision: you put your strongest at the front. The shields go first. The armor goes first. Whatever can absorb the first blow, that is what you send to meet it.

So imagine the messenger's face when the order finally comes down, and the order is this: put the singers in front.

Not the shields. The choir. Send the ones with no weapon but a melody out ahead of the whole army, out where the arrows land first, and let them walk toward the enemy, singing.

You would think it was a mistake. You would think someone had lost their mind. And I want to sit with you in that exact disbelief, because I think you are near it right now. Something is coming toward you that is bigger than you. A diagnosis. A number at the bottom of a page. A door that closed. A person you cannot fix and cannot leave. Something with more soldiers than you have, and it is not on the horizon anymore. It is close. And the last thing on earth that feels reasonable is to open your mouth and worship before a single thing has changed.

So let me tell you where that order came from, because it is not a slogan. It happened.

A king named Jehoshaphat got word that three armies had joined against him and were already on the march. The account does not hide his reaction, and I love it for that. He was afraid. Genuinely, humanly afraid. He did not perform calm he did not have. What he did was turn the whole nation toward Yahuah and pray one of the most honest prayers in all of Scripture, the prayer of a man at the end of his own resources: we have no might against this great company, and we do not know what to do, but our eyes are on You.

And that is the sentence I want you to underline. Not we have a plan. Not we have found the strength. We do not know what to do. Our eyes are on You. That is not the prayer of a weak faith. That is the prayer of a faith that has finally stopped pretending, and it is the exact place power is given.

Because the word came back, and the word was staggering: this battle is not yours. Stand still. You will not even need to fight it. Just go out and watch.

So they went out. And here is the part that stops me every time, as they went, the king did the unthinkable thing. He appointed singers. He put worshipers at the very front of the army, ahead of the men with swords, and sent them marching toward three armies with nothing in their hands but praise, thanking Yahuah for a mercy that had not visibly arrived yet, for a victory not one of them had seen.

And the record is plain about the timing, so do not rush past it. It was in the moment they began to sing, not after the win, in the moment they began, that Yahuah set ambushes against the enemy, and the great army that was coming to swallow them turned on itself and was gone. By the time the singers crested the hill, there was nothing left to fight. Only the aftermath to gather.

Now catch what actually happened there, because everything hangs on it.

The singing was not the celebration. The singing was the strategy.

We have the order of it backwards, almost all of us. We think worship is what you do after, the party at the end, the thank-you once the check clears and the scan comes back clean and the door reopens. Worship as the victory lap. And so we hold our praise hostage to our outcome, and we stand there white-knuckled and silent, waiting to see if God earns our song.

But look again at where the singers were standing. In front. Ahead of the army. Before the battle. Their praise did not commemorate a victory. Their praise walked out into the space where the victory had not happened yet and declared it real anyway, and Heaven met them in the declaring.

Here is why that works, and it is not magic. When you worship before the outcome, you are doing something in the unseen world that your fear cannot do. Fear stares at the size of the thing and takes its measurements and hands you the total, and the total is always bigger than you. Fear is not lying about the enemy's numbers. The three armies were real. Your thing is real too. But worship refuses to let the size of the enemy be the biggest fact in the room. Worship names a bigger One. It says, out loud, before anything shifts: this is not mine to win, and my eyes are not on it, my eyes are on You. And something in the unseen turns.

You will feel the resistance in your own chest as you read this, and I understand it completely. I have stood in the silence where praise felt like a lie I could not make my mouth tell. So hear me carefully, because this is the tender part and I will not sell you something cheap: worshiping first is not pretending the enemy is small. It is not painting a smile over dread. The king was afraid and worshiped in the same week, and both were true. This is not denial. It is redirection. It is turning your face, trembling, honest, undefended, away from the size of the threat and toward the size of the One who already called the outcome before you marched.

You do not sing because you have stopped being afraid. You sing so that your fear is no longer standing at the front.

So make the choice the king made. Whatever is coming toward you that is bigger than you, do not wait at the back of your own life for permission to worship. Do not hold your song until the report is good. Put the singers in front. Let the first move you make toward the thing be praise, not because you have seen the deliverance, but because you have seen the Deliverer, and He does not lose.

And here is the only question left, the one that decides whether tomorrow you march out fighting or march out watching: what would you sing right now, before one thing changes, if you truly believed the battle was not yours?

Find that song. Get it in your mouth tonight. And put it out in front of you where your fear used to stand, because the ambush is set the moment you begin.
