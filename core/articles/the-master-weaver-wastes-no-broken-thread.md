My grandmother could not pass a loose thread without reaching for it. A frayed hem, a pulled stitch, a quilt corner coming apart, her fingers found it before her eyes did. And she taught me something at her sewing basket that took me fifty years to understand was theology: a broken thread is not a ruined thread. It is an *unfinished* one. The weaver does not throw it out. She works it back in.

That is the whole of **Hands That Mend**. I made an album about weaving because I had run out of gentler ways to say the truest thing I know about **Yahuah**, that He is a Weaver, that nothing torn is beyond His loom, and that He is patient enough to work a whole life back into the cloth one strand at a time.

## We are threads, and He is the loom

Start with the picture Scripture itself hands us. Before you were anything you could name, *"you knit me together in my mother's womb... I am fearfully and wonderfully made"* (Psalm 139:13-14). Knit. Woven. You did not assemble yourself; you were *made* by hands that knew the pattern before the first row. The song at the center of this record is "The Master Weaver," and its confession is exactly that: *the Master Weaver knows the pattern from the start, knows the secrets of my heart... surrendering to the weaving of His hand and of His will.* That is not a passive resignation. It is the deep rest of a thread that has stopped fighting the hands that are trying to place it.

And here is why the picture matters so much for the broken among us. A single thread is a fragile, useless thing. It snaps at the lightest tug. But **Yahuah** does not leave any of us as single threads. Ecclesiastes 4:12 says *"a threefold cord is not quickly broken."* The opening song, "The Tapestry Begins," is built on that verse and on Dorcas, Tabitha, the seamstress of Joppa, *"full of good works,"* who sewed garments for the widows until the whole grieving town stood holding up the coats she had made (Acts 9:36-39). A woman with a needle preaching the gospel in thread. The tapestry begins, the song says, *with a single thread of faith*, but it never stays single. Faith woven to faith is what holds.

## The unraveling is not the end of the weaving

I will not sell you a soft record. The song on this album I most needed to write is track six, "The Unraveling and Re-weaving," and it is honest about the thing every one of us dreads: *sometimes the tapestry unravels. Plans fall through.* My grandmother lost much across her years, more than I knew as a child. But here is the line that carries the whole album: *she lost much, but she never lost her faith. In the unraveling, she found grace and grace.*

This is doctrine, and it is precise, so hear me as a teacher. **Yahuah** is not only the One who wove you in the beginning; He is the One who *re-weaves* what has come apart. Psalm 147:3, *"He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds."* Binds. That is a weaver's word, a mender's word. And Isaiah 61:1, the very passage **Yeshua** stood up in the synagogue and claimed as His own mission, says He was sent *"to bind up the brokenhearted."* Your unraveling is not proof the pattern failed. In the hands of the Master Weaver, the unraveling is the part of the work where the loose strand gets picked back up. The song says it plainly: *in the breaking and the mending, we discover what we're made of.* You do not learn what a cloth is made of until a thread is pulled, and neither do you.

> Sometimes the tapestry unravels, plans fall through, but in the unraveling we find what's true. The unraveling and re-weaving is how we learn to love.

## Many colors, one cloth

Now the picture opens outward, because a tapestry is never one color, and this is where the album turns from the private ache to the whole household of God.

We are, the second song says, "Many Colors", *beautiful and broken*, every one of us a different strand, and the church has too often tried to sort us by shade before letting us into the weave. But that is not how the Master works. First Corinthians 12 is the loom laid bare: *"the body is one and has many members... the eye cannot say to the hand, I have no need of you"* (1 Corinthians 12:12, 21). Different threads, one cloth, and *"individually members one of another"* (Romans 12:5). That is why the songs on this record are called "Patterns of Belonging" and "We Are One Cloth", because belonging is not something you earn by matching the threads around you. It is what happens when the Weaver runs your strand *through* theirs. "Through and Through," I called one of them. That is how cloth is made: over and under, through and through, until you cannot pull one life out without pulling on all the others.

And what binds the whole thing so it does not fall apart? Not our compatibility. Paul tells us the binding thread by name: *"above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony"* (Colossians 3:14). Love is the strong cross-thread. The knots are not flaws in the cloth, the song "Knots and Strength" is exactly right, the knots are where two lives got tied and the whole thing got *stronger*. We spend so much energy trying to hide the knots in our story. The Weaver builds the cloth's strength out of them.

## Who this is for

This album is for the person who has looked at the underside of a life, theirs, or someone they love, and seen nothing but a chaos of loose ends and cut threads and thought, *there is no pattern here, only a mess.* I wrote every song on this record to say: you are looking at the back of the tapestry. Of course it looks tangled from there. Only the Weaver sees the front, and He *"knows the pattern from the start."* He does not make a mistake, and He will not let you break.

It is for the woman walking through a divorce, a grief, a diagnosis, anyone whose plans just fell through, and it is for the one in the pew who feels like the wrong color thread in a cloth that never wanted her. And it is for the mender: the grandmother, the quilter, the caregiver, the one whose whole calling is picking up loose threads in other people's lives and working them gently back in. There was a woman who was sure she was too unraveled to be worth the Healer's time, so she only reached for *the fringe of His garment*, the very edge of the weaver's cloth, and He turned and said, *"take heart, daughter; your faith has made you well"* (Matthew 9:20-22). Even the hem of Him mends.

The last song is "Forever Woven," and it looks all the way down the loom to the finished piece: *"a great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, tribe, people and language,"* standing together before the throne (Revelation 7:9). That is the cloth He has been weaving the whole time, every color, every broken strand, every re-worked thread, whole at last and never coming apart again.

So bring Him the frayed edge. Bring Him the part that came undone. The Master Weaver wastes not a single broken thread. He picks it back up, and He works it into the whole.
