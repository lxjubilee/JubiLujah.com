Anybody can rejoice on carnival Sunday. The brass is blazing, the whole street is moving, the steelpan is ringing off the buildings, joy on a day like that costs you nothing. I love those days. I made a whole loud, foot-stomping album for them. But that is not actually where I learned what joy is.

I learned it on the Tuesday. *Island Joy* is a celebration record, but its true center is the quietest song on it, and it is about the morning after, when the costumes are packed away, the parade is over, and the heart goes flat.

## When the Carnival Heart Goes Quiet

I put that exact feeling into a song called **Carnival Heart Quiet**, and I did not dress it up:

> Carnival heart quiet now, Father, where did the joy go? Brooklyn parade is over, Tuesday morning, the diaspora slow.

Every one of us knows that Tuesday. The high recedes and something underneath it is exposed, the burnout, the loneliness, the ordinary grey of a life that cannot stay at parade pitch. And here is the trap so many believers fall into: we decide that the flat Tuesday is the real us and the joyful Sunday was the performance. We start to think joy was a mood we borrowed for a weekend, and now the bill has come due.

That is exactly the moment Nehemiah was written for.

## What They Learned on Their Worst Day

Picture the scene in Nehemiah 8. The people of God have gathered, the law is read aloud for the first time in generations, and they understand it, and they break down weeping, because they realize how far they have fallen. It is a low, exposed, Tuesday-morning kind of moment. And their leaders say something that sounds almost impossible: *Mourn not, nor weep... neither be ye sorry; for the joy of the LORD is your strength* (Nehemiah 8:9–10).

Sit with the order of that sentence, because I built the album's peak, **Joy Is My Strength**, entirely on it. We assume you need strength first, and then, if things go well, joy shows up as the reward. Nehemiah says the reverse. *The joy of the LORD is your strength.* The joy is not the payoff at the end of a strong week. It is the fuel the strong week runs on. Joy is upstream of strength, not downstream.

> Not strength first and joy as the reward, joy of the LORD is the source. Strength flowing from the rejoicing.

That is why it can come to you on the flat Tuesday. If joy depended on the parade, the empty morning would be the end of it. But *the joy of the LORD* does not depend on your circumstances. It depends on **Yahuah**, who does not pack up and go home when the celebration ends.

## Gladness Is a Command, Which Is Good News

Here is where I have to teach as a pastor, because "be joyful" can sound like pressure. It is not pressure; it is permission, and it is provision. Psalm 100 does not suggest gladness, it commands it: *Serve the LORD with gladness: come before his presence with singing* (Psalm 100:2). I built **Serve the Lord With Gladness** on that, and on the freeing truth two verses later, *it is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture* (Psalm 100:3).

Do you feel the relief in that? The reason you can be commanded into joy is that the joy was never something you had to manufacture out of your own flat heart. You did not make yourself. You are His, the sheep of His pasture, as I sing on **Sheep of His Pasture**, which means the source of the gladness is *outside* you, in the Shepherd, and it does not run dry when your energy does.

And notice what those weeping people were told to do next: to eat the fat, drink the sweet, and *send portions unto them for whom nothing is prepared* (Nehemiah 8:10). The very first act of Nehemiah's joy was generosity toward the ones with nothing. That is how you know this joy is not a private high. It overflows. Real gladness in **Yahuah** always reaches for the person beside you who has less. The parade was never the point; the caring was.

## Even the Foot-Stomp Belongs to Him

Now, do not hear me diminishing the loud days. The parade belongs to God too. When *David danced before the LORD with all his might* (2 Samuel 6:14), stripped of his royal dignity in the street, he showed us that the whole body, feet, hands, hips, breath, was made for praise. I reclaim all of it on **Wine to the King** and **Foot-Stomp Glory**: the soca, the carnival road, the shout-drums, every bit of it aimed back at the King who gave us bodies that know how to move. Psalm 47 opens with it: *O clap your hands, all ye people; shout unto God with the voice of triumph* (Psalm 47:1). And Psalm 150 closes the whole songbook with *let every thing that hath breath praise the LORD.*

The point is not to trade the loud joy for a quiet, serious faith. The point is that the loud Sunday and the flat Tuesday are both held by the same source, so the joy survives the trip between them.

## Who I Made This For

I made *Island Joy* for the one on the Tuesday. For the person who felt God at the parade, at the conference, at the retreat, and now sits in an ordinary week wondering if it was ever real. For anyone running on empty, trying to grind up enough strength to feel joyful again, when the whole thing works the other way around.

Stop trying to build strength so you can afford joy. Go to **Yahuah** for the joy first, the joy that is *His,* not yours to generate, and let the strength flow out of it, the way Nehemiah said it would. You do not have to wait for a good day to rejoice. The joy of the Lord is your strength on the flat Tuesday exactly as much as on the loud Sunday, because its source never once depended on the parade.

Pack away the costumes. The joy was never in them. It is in Him, and He is still here on Tuesday morning.
