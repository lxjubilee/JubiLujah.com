I built a whole record around a campfire, and I want to tell you why a fire and not a cathedral.

Because a fire is where **Yeshua** sat with His men. After everything, after the cross, after the empty tomb, after Peter had denied Him three times, the risen Lord met His disciples on a beach at dawn, and John chapter twenty-one says there was a fire of coals there, and fish on it, and bread, and He said, "Come and dine." That is the picture I could not get out of my head. Not a throne room. A campfire. The King of glory cooking breakfast for the men who had failed Him, gathering them back around the flame.

So *Campfire Covenant* is my record about that fire, about covenant belonging, about the circle of the tired and the wandering drawn in out of the dark. And the deepest thing it says is right there in the title of this piece: the Fire is older than our names.

## Why I had to make it

I have watched a lot of lonely believers. People who are technically in the church but have never once felt like they *belonged* to anyone. And I have watched us try to fix that with programs and small groups and name tags, gathering people around each other and wondering why the loneliness does not lift.

The record makes a quiet correction that I believe is the whole cure. In the opening song the circle sings, "We don't need another idol, we don't need another shrine, we just need the One who sat with men at the fire, by the sea, that time." We do not gather around each other. We gather around Him. Belonging is not something a circle of people manufactures. It is something that happens to a circle of people when they all turn and face the same Fire. Two men on the road to Emmaus felt it without even knowing who they were sitting with, their hearts burned inside them while He talked, long before they recognized His face. That is the secret of every true fellowship. The warmth was never coming from the circle. It was coming from the One in the middle of it.

## What the title means

The Fire in this album is a Person, and the Scripture is not shy about it. Hebrews chapter twelve says plainly, "our God is a consuming fire." He met Israel that way at Sinai, in Exodus chapter nineteen, the whole mountain wrapped in smoke because **Yahuah** had come down in fire. He is not a warm feeling we generate. He is a holy flame that was burning long before we showed up cold and looking for somewhere to sit.

That is why the bridge of the opener sings straight to the Father: "the Fire is older than our names, but our names, in You, have come home." I wrote that line, and then I sat with it a long while, because it holds the whole gospel. You did not start the Fire. You did not earn a chair by it. But the Fire has been burning since before you had a name, and when you finally come in out of the dark, you discover it was keeping a place for you the entire time.

> You do not build the belonging. You come in out of the cold and find that the Fire was already burning, and your chair was already there.

## The heart under the songs

A covenant, in the Scripture, is a bond cut in blood, and this record never lets you forget that the warm campfire and the costly covenant are the same thing.

In Genesis chapter fifteen, when **Yahuah** made covenant with Abraham, He passed between the pieces as a smoking furnace and a burning lamp, binding Himself to His promise by fire. The album stands on that: the covenant You cut in Your own blood is the One we are here about. And it reaches its fulfillment in *Jesus, the Covenant Word* and *Jesus, You Are the Promise*, because Hebrews chapter nine calls **Yeshua** the mediator of a new covenant, the One in whom every old promise to Abraham, Moses, and David finally comes true. Jeremiah chapter thirty-one had prophesied it centuries before: a new covenant, no longer written on stone but written on the heart.

But the tenderest tracks are the ones about the men around the fire who almost were not there. *Jesus, I Broke the Covenant* is Peter's song, the disciple who denied his Lord at one fire of coals, restored by his Lord at another. That is not an accident in John's Gospel; the man who failed at the fire is remade at the fire. It preaches the whole hope of the record: the campfire is where the covenant-breaker gets a chair again. And *Belonging to Jesus* and *Jesus, You Keep Forever* land the promise Paul makes in Romans chapter eight, that nothing, not height nor depth nor anything else, shall be able to separate us from the love of God which is in Christ **Yeshua**. Once you are in that circle, the night can be as long as it wants. You are not going anywhere.

## Who I made this for

I made *Campfire Covenant* for the ones out on the highway in the dark who are not sure anybody saved them a seat.

For the believer who serves and shows up and still goes home feeling like a stranger. Hear the bridge of the very first song: "no man here is a stranger, no chair here sits alone." For the one who broke faith and is sure the circle would never take them back, when the whole record is proof that the fire is exactly where **Yeshua** restores the one who failed. And for the tired soul who has been trying to earn belonging, who needs to lay that burden down and simply come in out of the cold and sit, because the Fire is older than your name and it has been waiting.

Hebrews chapter thirteen says **Yeshua** is the same yesterday, today, and forever. The Fire that warmed the shepherds and burned on Carmel and cooked breakfast on that beach is the same Fire burning tonight. It has not gone out. It is not going to.

So come on in. The coffee is hot, the wood is dry, the stars are coming slow. There is a chair by the flame that has had your name on it since before you were born.

And the Man tending the Fire, the One who cooked breakfast for the ones who let Him down, is looking up, right now, glad you finally came home.
