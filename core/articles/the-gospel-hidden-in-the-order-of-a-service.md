A man told me once, half embarrassed, that he only came to church for the music. He did not mean it cynically. He meant that the sermon washed over him, the announcements bored him, but the songs did something, moved him, settled him, sent him home lighter, and he could not explain why, so he had decided it was just the music. Just the melodies working on his mood, the way any good song does in the car.

I did not have the heart to tell him at the time, but he was wrong about what was happening to him. It was not just the music. He was being walked somewhere. And the reason he went home lighter was that the songs, in the order they came, had taken him on the exact journey a soul needs to make, and set him down at the end of it, whether he noticed the terrain or not.

Because here is the thing almost no one thinks about: the songs do not come in a random order. Someone chose the sequence. And a wise sequence is not a playlist. It is a path. It is the gospel itself, laid out as a road you walk with your feet instead of a lecture you hear with your ears.

## The pattern was carved in a throne room

To see the shape of the road, we have to watch one man make the whole journey in a single vision, because his experience is the template every good service is quietly copying.

The prophet Isaiah went into the temple in a year of grief, his king had died, and he was given a vision of Yahuah high and lifted up, the train of His robe filling the house, and the burning ones calling to one another, *holy, holy, holy.* And notice the *order* of what happens to Isaiah, because it is everything.

First, he sees God's holiness. He does not begin with himself. He begins with the sheer weight and worth of God filling the room until it shakes. That is the first movement. Adoration. You lift your eyes and you behold Him.

Then, only *then*, comes the second movement, and it comes as a direct result of the first. Seeing God's holiness, Isaiah suddenly sees himself, and he is undone. *Woe is me,* he cries, *for I am a man of unclean lips.* Confession. You cannot manufacture true confession by staring at your own sin; it curdles into shame or despair. Real confession is born from the vision of holiness. You see how bright He is, and only against that brightness do you finally see the truth about yourself. The order matters. Adoration first, then confession, and never the reverse.

Then comes the third movement, and it is pure mercy. A burning coal is brought from the altar and touched to his lips, and the word comes: *your guilt is taken away, your sin atoned for.* Cleansing. Assurance. The undoing does not have the last word; grace does. He is not left face-down in his uncleanness. He is lifted, forgiven, made able to stand.

And out of *that*, cleansed, assured, standing, comes the fourth and final movement. He hears the voice of the Lord asking *whom shall I send?* and he answers, *here am I, send me.* Commission. Sending. A soul that has beheld God, been undone, and then been cleansed does not stay in the room. It goes out with something to do.

Adoration. Confession. Assurance. Sending. Watch that pattern, because you have walked it a hundred times without knowing its name.

## Your Sunday is Isaiah's throne room, slowed down

Now go back to the man who came for the music, and follow the order of the songs he loved, and you will find Isaiah's vision hidden inside it, stretched out over an hour.

A well-built service almost always opens with the big songs, the ones about who God is, His greatness, His worth, His majesty. That is not an accident of energy or crowd-warming. That is the first movement. Adoration. Before anything else, the room lifts its eyes and beholds Him, exactly as Isaiah did. You start with God, not with yourself, because everything true downstream depends on seeing Him first.

Then, so often, the set turns quieter and more searching. The songs get honest. They speak of need, of failure, of a heart that wanders, of hands that come empty. That is the second movement, and it arrives right on schedule, confession, born from the adoration that came before it. You saw how holy He is, and now, in the quiet song, you see yourself, and you tell the truth.

And then the sequence turns again, and this is the hinge the whole hour is built on. The songs of grace come, the cross, the blood, the finished work, the mercy that reaches all the way down. Assurance. The room that just confessed is now told, in melody, that the guilt is taken away, the sin atoned for. Nobody is left face-down. The coal touches every set of lips in the room.

And the service closes, how? With songs of *going.* Of being sent, of carrying His name out the doors, of light into the world. The commission. The same *here am I, send me* that ended Isaiah's vision ends the service, so that a people who have adored and confessed and been cleansed do not simply disperse. They are *sent.*

That is what was happening to the man who came for the music. He was being walked through his own rescue every single week, beholding, breaking, being cleansed, being sent, and the songs were the stepping stones. He felt lighter at the end because a person who has traveled that whole road is lighter. The gospel was preached to him after all. It was just preached in the order of the songs, with his own feet, before the sermon ever began.

## So build the road, and walk it awake

Here is what I want you to carry out of this, whether you choose the songs or only ever stand in the back and sing them.

If you ever have a hand in the order of a service, even choosing three songs for a small gathering, understand that you are not making a playlist. You are building a road, and the road can lead somewhere or lead nowhere. Sequence matters as much as selection. Start with Him, not with us. Let the honesty come after the beholding. Do not leave the room in its confession; always turn it toward grace before the end. And send it out. A service that adores but never confesses stays shallow; one that confesses but never assures leaves people in the pit; one that assures but never sends breeds a people who receive and never carry. The whole road, in order, is the gospel. Build the whole road.

And if you only ever sing the songs someone else chose, walk the road awake. When the opening song lifts your eyes to His worth, let it. When the quiet song turns honest, tell Him the truth. When the song of grace comes, receive the coal; do not sing it as a spectator. And when the last song sends you out, go, actually go, carrying what you were given. You were never just there for the music.

You were being walked home, one song at a time, along a road that Isaiah walked first, that Yeshua opened, and that the whole church has walked every week since, and the destination, every single time, is a people who have seen Him, been cleansed by Him, and been sent out lighter than they came.
