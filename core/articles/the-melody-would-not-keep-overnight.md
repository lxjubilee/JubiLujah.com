Every writer I know has tried to cheat the same way, and it never works. We try to live off yesterday's melody.

It goes like this. Last month, a song arrived, a real one, the kind that came from where the wind comes from, that made the room go still. And it was so good that some quiet part of me decided I was done. I had proof now. I had the thing that showed I could do this. And so I coasted. I stopped showing up empty-handed to the quiet morning, because showing up empty-handed is frightening and coasting on the memory of a triumph feels safe. Why risk the blank page today when I have a masterpiece from three weeks ago to keep me warm?

And every single time, the same thing happens. The old song, hoarded, starts to go foul. Not the song itself, the song is fine, out there doing its work. What goes foul is *me*. Living off the memory of the last one, I get stale and self-satisfied and slightly rotten, protective of a past triumph instead of open to a present one. The very thing I was clutching to feel secure becomes the thing spoiling in my hands. And I do not get free of it until I go back to the frightening blank morning and hold out empty hands for something new.

For a long time I could not understand why it works this way, why inspiration will not be stockpiled, why yesterday's provision refuses to cover today. Then I went back to the story of the manna, and there it was, the rule I had forgotten, the strangest rule in the whole account.

## The bread that would not be hoarded

When Yahuah fed the people in the wilderness, He did it in a way designed to drive a hoarder out of his mind.

Every morning there was bread on the ground, a fine, flaky thing they had never seen, which is why they called it manna, from a question that means roughly *what is it?* And the instruction was simple and maddening: gather what you need for today. Just today. And of course some of them did not trust that. Some of them gathered extra and kept it overnight, the sensible thing, the prudent thing, the thing any careful person does. You store against tomorrow, you do not live hand to mouth if you can help it. And in the morning the hoarded portion had bred worms and gone rank. It could not be kept. The only manna that stayed good was the manna you trusted enough to gather fresh.

There was one exception, and it is beautiful: on the day before the Sabbath they gathered a double portion, and that portion, and only that one, kept overnight so they could rest. So it was not that God was against provision or against rest. It was that He would not let the manna become a substitute for the relationship. He was teaching them, through their stomachs, to come back to Him every single morning. He gave them enough for today precisely so they would have to show up tomorrow.

I read that and I understood my own foulness. The melody would not keep overnight for the same reason the manna would not. Not because inspiration is scarce, but because I am prone to hoard the gift and skip the Giver. The daily-ness is not God being stingy. It is God refusing to let yesterday's provision become the thing I trust instead of Him.

## Why the writing has to be daily

This changed how I understand the discipline of my own work, and I think it reaches far past writing.

We imagine that the ideal would be to *arrive*, to accumulate enough skill, enough proof, enough past triumphs that we no longer have to face the frightening empty morning. We treat the blank page, the daily return with nothing in our hands, as a beginner's problem we are meant to grow out of. But the manna says the opposite. The empty-handed morning is not a stage you graduate from. It is the whole arrangement. It is designed to be permanent, because its purpose was never merely to feed you. Its purpose was to keep bringing you back.

So when I sit down today with nothing, no melody in my hands, last month's success now foul and useless for today's work. I have stopped reading that as failure. I read it as the manna doing exactly what it was made to do. It has emptied my hands so that I will show up and hold them out again. The daily fear of the blank page is not a flaw in the creative life. It is the creative life keeping me honest, keeping me dependent, keeping me coming back to the only source that actually has anything.

And the same is true of walking with Yahuah, which is why He taught it through bread. You cannot live this year on the strength of a spiritual experience you had five years ago. You cannot store last summer's sense of His nearness against this winter's silence. People try, constantly. They hoard an old encounter, a season when God felt close, and try to make it cover the long ordinary stretch, and it goes foul in their hands, and they wonder why their faith feels rank and stale. It is the manna rule. Yesterday's portion was never meant to feed today. He gives you enough for today so that tomorrow you will come back to Him and gather again.

## Come back empty-handed

So here is what I do now, and what I would hand to you if you are living off an old portion that has quietly gone foul.

I come back every morning with empty hands. I stop trying to coast on the last good thing. I treat the blank page and the silent room not as evidence that the gift has left me, but as the daily invitation it has always been, the ground scattered again with what I did not make and could not have stored, waiting to be gathered fresh. Some mornings there is a feast. Some mornings there is just enough, an omer, barely a song, only enough for today. But it is always there, and it is always new, and it is never the same as what I gathered yesterday.

The melody would not keep overnight. I used to grieve that. Now I am grateful for it, because a melody that could be hoarded is a melody I would have used to stop needing the Giver. Instead I am kept poor in the best way, sent out every single morning, empty-handed and expectant, to gather what has fallen fresh in the night.

Do not try to live off yesterday's bread. Let it go foul if it has to. Get up, hold out your hands, and come back to the ground where the new thing is already lying, waiting for you, glowing in the early light. There is enough for today. There will be enough tomorrow. There always has been.
