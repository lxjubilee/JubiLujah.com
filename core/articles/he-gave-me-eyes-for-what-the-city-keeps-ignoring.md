Four forty-seven in the morning, the blinds still on the wall, and I sat up in the dark with the Hammond from somebody's radio already in the hall and a vision already in my chest. Before I lit the air, the Lord was showing me pictures. And the strangest part was not the vision. It was the brass band that woke up with it, the tambourine coming on slow, the whole procession assembling in my spirit while the streetlamp was still the only light on the corner.

That is where *Eyes Wide (The Seer Walks)* was born. And I set it to a New Orleans second-line, trumpet, trombone, sousaphone, the zydeco accordion, the frottoir rubboard scraping the backbeat, because I needed you to understand one thing the church keeps getting wrong: the seer is not a person who *sits.* The seer *walks.*

## The Old Word for Prophet Was "Seer"

There is a little verse most people read right past. *"Beforetime in Israel, when a man went to enquire of God, thus he spake, Come, and let us go to the seer: for he that is now called a Prophet was beforetime called a Seer"* (1 Samuel 9:9). Seer. One who *sees.* Before we had the word prophet, we had a word about the eyes.

And the eyes have a rule. The seer does not manufacture the vision. She receives it, and then she has to *wait* for it, and then she has to *walk* it. That is Habakkuk's whole assignment: *"I will stand upon my watch, and set me upon the tower... And the LORD answered me, and said, Write the vision, and make it plain... For the vision is yet for an appointed time... though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come"* (Habakkuk 2:1–3).

So the album opens on the watch. *"Eyes Wide Open"* is the seer the Lord wakes at dawn, and the line I could not stop singing became the title of everything I want to say here: *He gave me eyes for what the city has been ignoring.* Then *"What I See Is Real"* is the seer at the kitchen table at eleven at night, Habakkuk open, the coffee gone cold, second-guessing, until the validation comes in slow. **Yahuah** does not hand out visions just to make you feel something. He shows you what He shows you so you will not shut up about it.

## The Procession Is the Prophecy

Here is why I built the whole thing as a second-line and not a solo. In the culture that raised this music, the procession *is* the message. You do not read the prophecy off a page. You walk it down the street, and the whole block falls in behind the brass.

So on *"The Procession Has Begun,"* the seer lifts a handkerchief pressed white the night before, and steps off the sanctuary floor with the trumpet on the lead-note and the sousaphone on the back. The middle of the record is one long walk, and the seer's eyes sweep across everything she loves. *Over the sanctuary*. I see the wind, I see the wave, the glory getting ready to fall on Sunday. *Over the marriage*. I see the husband on his knees and the wife finally letting go of the key she carried too long, and the Lord loosing what was bound *at the speed of slow.* *Over the next generation*, the teenagers at the altar, the prophets in their twenties already being built.

> The seer does not walk to keep the vision. She walks so the whole street can catch what heaven has been trying to show it.

And then *"Over the City (NOLA Walk)"*, Treme to the Lower Ninth, handkerchief in the air, the intercessors behind the brass, the sister at the storefront setting her broom against the chair to watch the procession pass. This is where the prophetic stops being private. The seer walks the actual blocks and calls out over each one what the Lord let her track.

## What the Seer Is Really Looking For

Do not miss where the eyes finally land, because it tells you the heart of the whole gift. After the city and the nation, the vision narrows down to the ones nobody else is looking at. *"Eyes for the Hidden"*, the orphan in the system, the widow at the back, the forsaken on the corner the family stopped tracking. The Lord opens the book on the overlooked.

That is what prophetic sight is *for.* Not to impress a room. Not to guard secrets in a tower. **Yeshua** Himself walked into every crowd and saw the one, the woman in the press, the man up the tree, the blind beggar the disciples wanted to hush. Real seeing always ends up at the person the world has agreed to ignore.

And the burden is not meant to be carried alone. On *"Over the Nation,"* the vision gets heavier than one set of shoulders can hold, the weight of a whole country at dawn, and the seer does the only faithful thing: she lays it at the prophetic throne and calls the whole community in. *We are the seers, and the burden is the Lord's to be known.* Sight that stays private curdles into pride. Sight that gets shared becomes intercession, and intercession is what a procession was always for.

## Who I Made This Walk For

I made it for the one who woke at 4:47 with something in her chest she could not explain and no category to put it in. For the seer at the kitchen table second-guessing the vision because the coffee is cold and the doubt is loud. For everybody who was taught that "prophetic" belongs to a special few standing on a stage, when the last-days promise was poured out on all of us.

Because the record does not end with one seer. *"We All Are Seers Now"* stands right on the ground Joel gave us: *your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, your young men shall see visions* (Joel 2:28). I stepped back from the front line, pointed at the whole congregation, and watched the aunties see the vision and the teenagers in the back row name the brave. And then *"The Whole City Will See"* lifts it to the end of the story, *behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him* (Revelation 1:7). Twelve songs that started with one seer at dawn end with a city, and then a world, whose eyes are all about to open at once.

So if He has been showing you pictures in the dark, if you have felt something over your sanctuary, your marriage, your children, your city, do not file it away and go back to sleep. Lift the handkerchief. Fall in behind the brass. The seer was never meant to keep the vision. She was meant to walk it down the street until the whole city sees what He gave her eyes to see. And beloved, He gave you those eyes on purpose.
