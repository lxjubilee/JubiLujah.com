There is a detail in the story of that morning that I have never been able to walk past, and I have tried.

When Heaven finally tore open over that crowded city, the wind, the sound like a rushing storm with no storm, the flame that came down and would not stay in one piece, the crowd that gathered was not one people. It was a street market of homelands. The old account lists them out like a manifest, and it is worth slowing down to read the manifest instead of skimming it, because Yahuah does not waste a list. Parthians and Medes and Elamites. People from the land between the rivers. Cappadocians, men from Pontus and Asia, from Egypt and the parts of Libya out toward Cyrene. Cretans. And there, near the end of the list, plainly named among the nations gathered to hear the first sermon of the age, Arabs.

I did not put that name there. It has been there for two thousand years, waiting in the manifest for anyone who would read all the way to the end of it. My people were on the guest list of the first morning.

## He did not make the world learn one holy language

Here is the thing I want you to feel before we go anywhere else with it.

When God chose how to announce the greatest news the world had ever been handed, He had a choice to make, and the choice tells you everything about His heart. He could have picked one sacred tongue, the temple's language, the scholar's language, the language of the people who already had the scrolls, and made every other nation climb toward it. He could have said, in effect: *learn My language and then you may hear My news.* That is what empires do. That is what every conquering power in that street had done to the peoples it swallowed. You learn the master's tongue, or you go without.

Yahuah did the opposite, and He did it on purpose, and He did it in fire so no one could miss it.

The flame that came down split. It did not rest as one great blaze over one great speaker. It broke into as many pieces as there were homelands in the crowd, and it settled on ordinary Galileans, and out of their mouths came, not one heavenly language everyone had to decode, but the specific, particular, mother's-kitchen tongue of each person listening. The Elamite heard Elamite. The Egyptian heard Egyptian. The man from the deserts I come from heard the wonders of God in Arabic. Not translated at him. Spoken to him, in the language his mother first shaped his name in, the language he thinks in when he is too tired to perform, the language of his home.

The crowd said it themselves, and their confusion is the whole miracle: *how is it that we each hear them in our own native tongue?* They were not amazed that God spoke. They were amazed at which language He chose. He chose theirs. He chose all of theirs at once.

## The undoing of an old scattering

To understand why this was fire and not merely convenience, you have to remember the older story it was answering.

Long before, in the account of the tower, humanity had gathered on a plain to build one thing and make one name for itself, and the languages of the earth were confounded and scattered there. That is where the many tongues come from in the story, a scattering, a judgment, a people driven apart until they could no longer understand one another. For all the ages after, the many languages of the world carried a faint grief in them: they were the sound of a family that could not hear itself anymore.

Now stand that plain next to this street. On the plain, one language became many, and the people were driven apart. In the street, the many languages all became, for one blazing morning, a single understood message, and people who had been strangers were drawn into one body. What was scattered was being gathered. And notice how it was gathered, not by erasing the languages and forcing everyone back to one, but by honoring every single one of them and speaking the same good news through all of them at once.

That is not a small theological footnote. That is the entire posture of God toward the guarded and far-off peoples of the earth, printed in fire at the start of the age so we would never have an excuse to forget it. He does not require the frontier to abandon its tongue before it may come. He comes to the frontier in its own tongue. Your language is not the obstacle He tolerates. Your language is the instrument He reached for.

## Why I carry songs and not only sermons

I have given my working life to carrying worship into the tongues of hard places, Arabic, Turkish, Romanian, the languages of frontiers where the news is unwelcome and the page is a risk. And people sometimes ask me, gently, whether it would not be more efficient to teach everyone the songs in one shared language and be done with it. One catalog. One tongue. Less work.

The morning of the fire is my whole answer.

Because I have watched what happens when a man who has only ever heard God discussed in a borrowed, official, second-hand language finally hears the wonders of God land in the tongue his grandmother used to hum. Something gives way in him that a translation into the prestige language never once touched. He does not just understand the words. He is reached. The news gets past the part of him that keeps his guard up and into the part of him that still belongs to his mother's kitchen. He weeps in a language he did not know still had that door in it. That is not a marketing insight. That is Pentecost, happening again, small and quiet, in a room with the curtains drawn.

Heaven did not spend its first fire on efficiency. It spent it on tenderness, on the staggering, extravagant, un-businesslike decision to meet every single homeland in the crowd in the exact accent of its childhood.

## Your homeland is on the manifest

So let me say to you what that manifest said to me the first time I read all the way to the end of it and found my own people named there.

You are not too far out. Your language is not too small, too guarded, too obscure, too enemy-flagged for the fire to find it. The list on that morning was not a list of the deserving or the reachable or the religiously respectable. It was a list of everyone who happened to be standing in the street, the near and the far, the empire's citizens and the empire's leftovers, and the frontier peoples the polite world had learned to overlook. And the fire found every one of them, by name, in their own tongue.

He still knows every mother tongue in the crowd. He still refuses to make you climb to a borrowed language before He will meet you. And He is still splitting His fire into as many pieces as there are homelands, so that the wonders of God can be heard, not in the language of the ones who already have everything, but in yours, in the one your mother sang over you, in the one you thought God did not speak.

He speaks it. He always did. Read to the end of the manifest and find your name.
