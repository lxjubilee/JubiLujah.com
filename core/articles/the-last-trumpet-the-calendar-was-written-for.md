There is a trumpet still to sound.

The Scriptures speak of it plainly, and once you have really heard the promise, it lodges somewhere deep and it does not leave. There is coming a moment, the apostle calls it the *last trumpet*, at which everything changes in an instant, in the twinkling of an eye. At that blast, he says, the dead are raised imperishable, and those still living are changed, and mortal puts on immortality. Elsewhere he tells it again: the Lord Himself will descend with a shout and the trumpet of God, and the dead in the Messiah will rise first, and the living will be gathered up to be with Him, and so we will always be with Him.

One trumpet. The dead raised. A scattered people gathered home. And I have come to believe that the entire Hebrew calendar has been quietly tuning toward that one note from the very beginning.

## The calendar was always music

I hear the world as sound; I cannot help it. And when I look at the feasts of Yahuah laid out across the year, I do not see a list of holidays. I see a piece of music. A composition with movements, with rests, with a shape that builds, spring festivals that open the piece, a long summer rest, and then the great autumn festivals gathering toward a finale.

And here is the astonishing thing that so many faithful teachers have noticed. The spring feasts were *filled*, completed, brought to their meaning, at Yeshua's first coming, and they were filled right on their appointed days. He was offered as the Passover lamb at Passover. He rose as the firstfruits of the resurrection at the Feast of Firstfruits. The Spirit was poured out on the gathered believers at the feast fifty days on, exactly when the calendar said. Movement after movement of the spring music resolved, precisely on time, in Him.

Which leaves the autumn feasts still leaning forward. Still unresolved. Still ringing an unfinished chord that the ear is waiting to hear completed. And the first of those great autumn appointments is the Day of the Blast, the feast of the trumpet.

> The whole calendar is a song. And a song is not finished until its last note lands.

## The note the whole piece has been reaching for

Anyone who has loved music knows the feeling of a piece that has been building toward a note you can sense coming before it arrives. The harmony strains upward. The tension gathers. Everything in the composition has been leaning, phrase by phrase, toward a single resolving sound, and when it finally lands, the whole piece suddenly makes sense backward. Every earlier bar was preparing your ear for *that.*

That is what the last trumpet is to the calendar of Yahuah.

Every trumpet blast that ever sounded over that ancient people was a rehearsal for this one. The horn that shook the air at the mountain when Yahuah came down. The horns that circled the doomed walls until they fell. The blast that broke over the camp and told a wandering people to strike their tents and *move.* The yearly cry of the ram's horn on the Day of the Blast, waking a drowsy nation to attention. Every one of them was the same note, sounded early, a foretaste, a tuning, a getting-ready of the ear for the blast that has not come yet. The whole history of that horn has been an orchestra tuning before the piece begins.

And the last trump is the downbeat. The note the entire composition was written to reach.

## What the last note does

I want to tell you what this note *does,* because it is not a note of ending. It is the most alive sound in all of Scripture.

At the trumpet, the dead are raised. Think of what that means as a piece of music. The composition does not merely conclude. It *resurrects.* Everyone the song ever included, everyone who ever went silent, every voice that dropped out of the choir in grief and loss and death, comes back in at the final chord. The people you have buried. The saints of every century. The great scattered choir of the faithful, separated by continents and by centuries and by the grave itself, all of them gathered into one sound at last, on the same beat, in the same breath. It is the largest reunion in the history of the world, and its downbeat is a trumpet.

And this is why I cannot hear the last trump as a sound of dread, though it will surely be dreadful to a rebellion that never wanted the King to come. For those who belong to Yeshua, it is the sound we have been leaning toward our whole lives without always knowing it, the note under every homesickness, every grief at a graveside, every ache for the friend who is gone, every longing for a scattered family to be whole. The calendar has been promising, year after appointed year, that the piece is *not* finished, that the rest in the music is not the end of the music, that there is a final blast coming at which everything that fell silent will sing again.

## Tuned toward a coming sound

So here is how I have come to live, and how I would love for you to live too.

A musician who knows the final note is coming does not slump in the rests. She stays tuned. She keeps her instrument ready, her ear leaning forward, her heart already reaching toward the resolution she knows is written into the score. She plays her small part faithfully in the long middle of the piece, precisely *because* she trusts the ending, because she has read ahead and she knows the last chord raises the dead.

That is the whole posture of a people living between the spring feasts and the last trumpet. We are in the long movement, the part of the song where much is unresolved and the grief is real and the friends are truly missing from the choir. But the score is written. The last note is composed. And it is not silence. It is a trumpet, and it is a resurrection, and it is a gathering home.

The calendar has been tuning toward this from the fourth day of creation. Every horn that ever sounded was preparing the ear. And one day, on a day the watching heart has been leaning toward all along, the last trumpet will finally land, and the whole piece will make sense backward, and the choir the world thought was scattered forever will come in all at once, alive, on the downbeat, and be with Him, and never fall silent again.

Stay tuned. The best note is still coming. And it raises the dead.
