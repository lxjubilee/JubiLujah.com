We have sanitized this story, and I want to un-sanitize it, gently, because the softened version robs you of the very thing that could set you free.

The way it usually gets told, death came for the Egyptians and passed over the Israelites, and the Hebrews woke up the next morning untouched, unbothered, their households exactly as full as the night before. Clean deliverance. No cost on our side of the street.

But that is not what happened. Read it slowly. On that tenth and final blow against Egypt, the death of the firstborn, a firstborn died in *every* house in the land that night. Every single one. Egyptian and Hebrew alike. The difference between the homes was never *whether* death came. Death came to all of them. The only difference, the whole difference, was *what* death found when it arrived.

## In the unmarked house, the child

In the homes without the blood, death took the firstborn son. From the palace of Pharaoh down to the prisoner in the dungeon, from the highest to the lowest, the firstborn of Egypt fell. It was the most terrible night in that nation's history, and I will not pretend it was anything less, because Yahuah does not ask us to look away from the weight of judgment. There was a great cry in Egypt that night, the Scriptures say, for there was not a house where someone was not dead.

That is the sentence that ought to arrest us: *not a house where someone was not dead.* And it is easy to read that as meaning only the Egyptian houses. But hold on. Walk across the street to a Hebrew home, a home with the blood on the posts and the lintel. Was there a death in that house too?

Yes. There was. It was just lying on the table.

## In the marked house, the lamb

The firstborn *bekhor* of that Hebrew family was alive and breathing behind the door, but only because something else had already died in his place. The lamb. The lamb the family had brought into the house four days earlier, the lamb the children had come to love, the lamb whose blood was now drying on the frame of the door. In the marked house, death still collected a firstborn. It just collected the *substitute* instead of the son.

Do you see it now? Every house in Egypt lost a firstborn that night. In the house under judgment, it was the child. In the house under mercy, it was the lamb. Judgment and mercy were not separated by geography, or by ethnicity, or by whether these were good people and those were bad people. They were separated by one thing and one thing only.

Blood.

> There was no house that escaped death that night. There were only houses where the son died, and houses where the lamb died in his place. Mercy was never the absence of death. Mercy was a substitute.

This is why I cannot let you keep the sanitized version. If you believe the Israelites simply got skipped, you will believe deliverance is God overlooking the problem, winking at it, deciding it does not matter, waving you through without a cost. But that is not what the blood on your door means. The blood means the cost was *paid.* It means death was real, and judgment was real, and instead of falling on you it fell on the one you brought inside. You were not overlooked. You were *ransomed.* Something died so you would not have to. That is a far more expensive mercy, and a far more solid one, than being skipped.

## The firstborn who did not have a substitute

Now I have to take you somewhere the first Passover was always pointing, and I want you to brace, because it is glorious and it is heavy.

Centuries later, there was a firstborn Son who came into a marked and unmarked world, and when the night of judgment fell on Him, there was no lamb on the table to die in His place. He *was* the Lamb. Yeshua, the firstborn of Yahuah, walked into the house of judgment on purpose and let death collect Him, not as a victim caught without the blood, but as the substitute Himself, the one whose blood would go on every doorway that would receive it.

Every house lost a firstborn on Passover night. On the hill outside Jerusalem, so did heaven. The Father gave up His firstborn so that yours would live. The substitute Israel found in a lamb, Yahuah provided in His own Son, so that the death which is coming for every house, and beloved, it is coming for every house, would find, at your door, that it had already been paid.

## Which death is on your table tonight

So let me bring this all the way home, to your doorway, tonight.

Death is not a possibility you might avoid by being good enough or careful enough. That is the Egyptian lie, the one that let people sleep soundly in unmarked houses because they assumed the disaster was for someone else. Death is coming to every house. The only question, the same question it was three and a half thousand years ago, is *what it will find when it gets to yours.* Will it find an unmarked door and collect the firstborn? Or will it find the blood of the Lamb, and see that the price has already been paid, and pass over?

You do not get to choose whether death visits. You get to choose whose blood is on your door. And I am telling you with everything in me. There is a Lamb already slain, His blood already available, His substitution already accomplished. You do not have to find your own lamb. He offered Himself. All that is left for you is to take the blood and mark the door of your life, and stop trying to survive the night on your own goodness.

Every house lost something that Passover night. In the houses that grieve, it was a son. In the houses that rejoiced, it was a lamb. Make sure that when death reaches your door, what it finds already lying down in your place is the Lamb, and then step into the morning as one of the ransomed, awed and unashamed, alive because Another was not.
