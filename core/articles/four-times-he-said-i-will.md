Let me tell you about a table where nobody drinks just once.

At the Passover meal, the *Seder*, which simply means "order," because the whole night follows a set path like a song follows a melody. There are four cups. Not one. Four. And here is the thing that took my breath away when I first understood it: those four cups are not four refills of the same happy toast. Each one has a name. Each one has a job. And when you line the four names up in a row, you find that the family gathered around that table is drinking its way, cup by cup, straight through a promise God made to a people who had nothing.

The four cups are tradition, mi gente. Let me say that plainly so nobody accuses me of smuggling. You will not find "drink exactly four cups" written as a command in the Torah. What you *will* find is a sentence, spoken to a nation of slaves who had forgotten how to hope, and the tradition of the four cups is built right on top of that sentence, one cup for each promise inside it. So this is a case where the custom is faithful. It took a word of God and turned it into something you could hold in your hand and lift to your mouth.

## The sentence the cups are made of

Go back to Egypt. The people are crushed. The bricks never stop. And Yahuah sends word to them through Moses, and inside that word there are four things He swears He will do. *I will bring you out.* *I will deliver you.* *I will redeem you.* *I will take you to be My own people.*

Four verbs. Four "I will" promises, stacked one on top of the next like a man building a staircase up out of a pit. And the tradition took each of those promises and gave it a cup.

The first cup they call the cup of **sanctification**, *I will bring you out.* This is the cup that sets the night apart, that says: something holy is starting here, we are not who we were an hour ago.

The second is the cup of **deliverance**, *I will deliver you from their bondage.* This is the cup you lift over the memory of the plagues, over the night the whole machinery of slavery finally cracked.

The third is the cup of **redemption**, *I will redeem you with an outstretched arm.* And that word, redeem, is *ga'al*, the word for a kinsman buying back what his family lost. This is not a rescue by a stranger. This is family reaching into the slave market and paying to bring you home.

And the fourth is the cup of **praise**, *I will take you to be My people.* This is the cup where the story stops being about escape and becomes about belonging. You did not just get out. You got *taken in.*

Four cups. Four promises. One staircase out of Egypt. And every family that lifts them is rehearsing, without always knowing it, the exact shape of how God saves.

## The cup He lifted

Now come with me to a different upper room, on a different Passover night, generations and generations later.

Yeshua, Jesus, if that is the name you learned Him by first, is at this same table, keeping this same order, lifting these same cups. His friends have done this every year of their lives. They know which cup comes when. They could do it half-asleep. And that is exactly why what He does next lands so hard.

The tradition holds that it was at the **third cup**, the cup of redemption, the *ga'al* cup, the kinsman-buys-you-back cup, the one lifted after the meal, that He picked it up, and instead of saying the words they had all expected, He said something no one at any Passover table had ever said before.

> This cup is My blood. Poured out for you.

Think about *which* cup. Not the first. Not the second. The **redemption** cup. The cup that for a thousand years had meant *I will redeem you with an outstretched arm*. He lifted that one, and He said: this. This is how the arm gets outstretched. This is the family member reaching into the slave market. The price is not going to be gold. The price is going to be Me.

Every year they had been drinking a promise. That night He told them the promise had a body, and it was sitting at their table, and it was about to be poured out.

## The cup He would not finish

But there is one more thing, and this is the part I cannot get over, the part that makes me want to stand up from my chair.

There are four cups. He lifted the third and made it His blood. But the tradition, and the accounts of that night agree, is that after that cup, Yeshua said something strange about the *next* one. He said He would **not drink of the fruit of the vine again** until He drank it new in His Father's Kingdom.

The fourth cup. The cup of **praise**. The cup of *I will take you to be My people.* He would not finish it.

Do you feel the weight of that? The story of salvation has four movements, and He drank three of them to the bottom, the sanctifying, the delivering, the redeeming, all of it, He drained the cup of what it cost to buy you back. But the fourth cup, the cup of the family fully gathered, the cup of *you are finally, wholly Mine*. He set that one down. He left it unfinished on the table. On purpose.

Because that cup is not for an upper room. That cup is for a wedding feast that has not started yet. He is saving it. He looked His friends in the eye and told them, in effect: I am going to drink the whole bitter price now, alone, but the cup of joy, the cup of belonging, the cup where the whole family is finally home, *I will not lift that one without you.* I will wait. I will hold it, untouched, until the day we can raise it together in the Kingdom.

Mi gente, He is still holding it.

## Drink like the promise is coming true

So here is why I love this table with my whole heart.

Most of the world thinks Passover is a history lesson, a sad, solemn remembering of something that happened to somebody else a long time ago. But that is not what those four cups are. Those four cups are a promise you drink. They are Yahuah's own "I will," turned into something warm and red that you lift with people you love, so that the saving does not stay an idea in your head but gets down into your body, into the muscle of your arm, into the taste on your tongue.

You were brought out. Lift the cup. You were delivered. Lift it again. You were redeemed, bought back by family that came looking for you in the market, lift it a third time and let it be His very blood. And the fourth cup, the one He set down and would not finish?

That one is still on the table. It has your name near it. He is not going to drink it alone. And on the day the whole familia is finally gathered, when there is not one empty chair left, He is going to reach for that fourth cup at last, the cup of *you are Mine and you always will be*, and we are going to lift it with Him, and we are going to drain it to the bottom, and the joy of that toast is going to shake the whole house of God.

¡Salud, familia! Keep your cup ready. The best one is still to come.
