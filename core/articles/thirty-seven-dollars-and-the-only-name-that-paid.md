Let me tell you about a kitchen drawer.

When my grandmother passed, we opened the drawer by the stove where she kept her important things, and inside there were thirty-seven dollars, a folded church bulletin, and a pencil worn down to almost nothing. That was the accounting of her estate. Thirty-seven dollars. And here is what undid me as I stood in that kitchen: she did not owe a single soul a single cent. Every bill that had come to that house across a long life had been paid, and not because she had the money, because most seasons she plainly did not. It was paid because when the letters came and the balance was more than the drawer could cover, she did the only thing she knew to do. She called on a Name.

That is the whole album. I called it **The Only Name You'll Ever Need**, and I made it because I have spent my life watching people build backup plans out of everything except the one thing that actually holds.

## The world will sell you a Name and a hundred fallbacks

We are taught, almost from the cradle, to keep a Plan B in our pocket. Better have a savings account. Better have a contingency, a network, an exit door, a fallback in the wallet, in case the prayer doesn't reach and you're left with nothing to do. I wrote a song about exactly this and called it "I Don't Need a Backup Plan," because somewhere along the way my grandfather leaned back in his rocker, shook his head at all of it, and told me the truth: *the Name of Jesus has never needed a backup plan, and neither do you.*

I want to be careful here, because I am a teacher and not a fool. Wisdom saves and plans are good; Scripture praises the ant that stores in summer. But there is a difference between stewarding a resource and worshiping a fallback. The heart will always try to lean its full weight on *something*, and the tragedy is how many believers lean on the backup and merely tip their hat to the Lord. Psalm 20:7 puts the sorting line right down the middle of the human heart: *"Some trust in chariots and some in horses, but we trust in the name of the LORD our God."* Chariots were the backup plan of the ancient world, the fastest, surest thing a nation could buy. The psalm doesn't mock them for being weak. It exposes them for not being **Yeshua**.

## Why a Name, and not just a feeling

Here is the doctrine underneath the whole record, and I made twelve songs about it because it took me twelve songs to say it plainly.

A whisper can comfort you for an evening. A Name can carry a family for a hundred years. The opening song on the album is called "Whispers Don't Work Like His Name Does," and it came from my grandmother handing me sweet tea on the porch and saying, *the world will give you whispers, soft and sweet, but when the storm comes rolling through the valley of your living, only the Name of Jesus is the rock beneath your feet.* That is not sentiment. That is Proverbs 18:10, *"The name of the LORD is a strong tower; the righteous run into it and are safe."* A tower is not a mood. You cannot lean your weight on a mood at three in the morning. You can run into a tower.

And the reason His Name works is not magic; it is identity. Matthew 1:21 tells us why He was even given the Name: *"you shall call his name Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins."* The Name is a description of a rescue already accomplished. So when Acts 4:12 says *"there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved,"* it is not being narrow to be cruel. It is being honest about the only door that actually opens. Philippians 2:9-11 says **Yahuah** exalted Him and gave Him *the name that is above every name*, above every diagnosis, every deadline, every debt in every drawer.

> A whisper fades with the evening breeze on the windowsill. A Name has been carrying my grandmother's grandmothers, and it is the strong tower where the family begins.

## The songs are a map of when you'll need it

I built the record as a journey through the hours a person actually reaches for the Name, because doctrine you can't find in the dark isn't doing its job.

There's "I Called on Jesus (And Everything Changed)", a Tuesday in October, a doctor's letter in the mailbox by the gate, and a woman sitting on the kitchen step with her mother's Bible. The letter still said what it said. But she called, and the Name walked into the kitchen and stood by the bed. That is Psalm 50:15 lived out in a farmhouse: *"call upon me in the day of trouble; I will deliver you."* Notice the song does not pretend the letter changed. It says the *room* changed. The Name doesn't always rewrite the report; it rearranges who is standing in the room with you while you read it.

There's "Power in the Middle of Nowhere," out on a two-lane road where there's no cell tower and no neighbor close enough to holler, and my grandfather cutting the engine to say, *this is where the Name has the most power, where there ain't a single soul but Jesus and the trees.* There's "That Name Found Me First," a cradle song built on the astonishing truth that before you ever called on Him, He was already calling, that the Name searched you out before you could spell your own. And there's "The Devil Flinches When I Say It," which I will defend to anyone: it is not about naming the enemy, it is about naming the One who emptied his tomb. The devil flinches at the Name because the Name has the keys.

## Who this album is for

It is for the woman with the letter in her hand. It is for the man at the kitchen table with the bills he can't quite pay. It is for the one in the hallway with the phone call she's been dreading, which is why the tenderest song on the record is "Just Say the Name (Even If You're Broken)." You don't need a script. You don't need a steeple or a prayer-book or a good Sunday hat. Psalm 34:18 promises *"the LORD is near to the brokenhearted,"* and Romans 10:13 makes it as plain as a Name can be: *"everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved."* Everyone. Even if your voice is shaking. Even if your knees won't hold.

My grandmother left thirty-seven dollars. But she left me the drawer's real inheritance, the thing that had quietly paid for everything: she left me the Name. And I have spent a life discovering she was right. It is the only Name you'll ever need, because it is the one that's still standing in the room after every backup plan you ever built has run clean out.

Say it again, even if you're broken. He is right there at that.
