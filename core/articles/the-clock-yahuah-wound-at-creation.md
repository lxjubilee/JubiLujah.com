I want to take you back to the fourth day of creation, because there is a phrase tucked into it that most of us have read straight past our whole lives, and once you catch it, you cannot look at the night sky the same way again.

On that day, the account says, Yahuah set lights in the expanse of the heavens. And He gave them a job. Not one job, several. To separate day from night. To give light on the earth. To mark days and years. And then this: to be *for signs and for appointed times.*

That phrase, *appointed times*, is the Hebrew word *moadim.* The very same word used, all through the Torah, for the *feasts.* The festivals. The sacred appointments Yahuah would later write into the year for His people.

Do you see what that means? The feasts were not invented at Sinai. Their clock was hung in the sky on the fourth day of the world.

## A calendar with no paper

We are so used to time living on paper, on a grid of numbered boxes, a phone that pings, a page you tear off at month's end, that we forget the ancients had none of that. And they did not need it. Because Yahuah had already installed the calendar overhead, in lights that could not be lost, could not be edited, could not be forgotten, and would keep perfect time whether or not anyone was watching.

The sun rules the *year.* Its long journey through the seasons is what ripens the barley in the spring and the grapes and olives in the autumn, and the feasts are pinned to exactly those harvests. The spring festivals cluster around the first grain coming in; the great autumn festivals fall as the final harvest is gathered. The agricultural year and the sacred year are the same year, because both are governed by the same burning clock-hand crossing the sky.

The moon rules the *months.* Every new month opens with the thin returning crescent, that first sliver of light coming back from the dark. So the feasts that fall at the new moon or the full moon are set by the moon's own faithful cycle, a cycle you do not schedule, you simply *observe,* because it keeps itself.

And even the stars had their place in this, the fixed points against which the ancients read the turning of the seasons, the reliable markings on the great dial of heaven.

> Yahuah did not hand His people a calendar. He handed them a *sky,* and told them the appointments were already written in light.

## The wonder of a clock that keeps itself

Now let me put on, for a moment, the part of me that cannot stop marveling at how things actually work, because the real mechanics here are more beautiful than any story I could invent.

The moon takes roughly twenty-nine and a half days to go from one new crescent to the next. That is a true and stubborn number, and it is why the Hebrew months run twenty-nine or thirty days, never a tidy round figure, the calendar is honestly tracking a real object in real motion, not rounding it off for convenience. And because twelve of those lunar months add up to a little *less* than one full solar year, a purely lunar calendar would slowly drift, the spring feast would creep backward into winter, the harvest feast would wander out of the harvest.

So how was it kept in step? An extra month was added in certain years, a leap month, to gently nudge the lunar count back into alignment with the sun, so that the spring feast always landed in spring and the harvest feast always landed at harvest. (The formal system for calculating this came later; in the earliest days it was watched and declared by observation.) Two clocks, sun and moon, running at their own God-given speeds, patiently held in harmony so that the appointments never slid off their seasons.

I find this almost too lovely to bear. The feasts of Yahuah run on a timepiece with two hands moving at different rates, wound at the foundation of the world, self-correcting across the centuries, never once needing to be reset. When His people gathered for an appointed time, they were not consulting a human invention. They were keeping an appointment written into the physics of the heavens.

## The appointments were always about a Person

But here is where I have to lift my eyes from the mechanism to the meaning, because a clock is only ever *for* something. And these appointments were never really appointments with a season. They were appointments with the God who set the seasons, and, it turns out, appointments that pointed to a Person.

The word *moed* means a *meeting*, a set time when two parties agree, in advance, to come together. Yahuah wound a clock into the sky so that year after year His people would keep showing up to meet Him at the times He had chosen. And the whole arrangement was building toward a keeping of the appointments that no one saw coming.

Because when Yeshua came, He did not arrive on a random Tuesday. He filled the appointments. He gave Himself as the Passover lamb at Passover. The Spirit fell upon the gathered believers at the feast that falls fifty days later, right on the appointed day. The great harvest feasts of the autumn, the trumpet, the atonement, the tabernacling of God with us, lean forward still, toward the appointments He is coming back to keep. The cosmic clock Yahuah wound on the fourth day was, all along, ticking toward Him. Every feast was a hand on a dial, and every hand was pointing at the Son.

## Look up, and keep the appointment

So the next time the night is clear, go outside and look up. Find the moon, wherever it is in its cycle, waxing or waning, full or nearly gone. And remember that you are not looking at decoration. You are looking at the *appointment book of Yahuah,* opened over your head on the fourth day of the world and never once closed.

He is a God of set times. He does not drift. He does not lose track of the season you are in or forget the meeting He made with you. The same faithfulness that has kept the moon returning from the dark for the entire history of the world, never late, never skipped, never once failing to come back, is the faithfulness holding the appointment He has made with you.

The clock is still running. It has never lost a second. And every tick of it is carrying you toward the One who keeps every appointment He makes.

Look up. He is right on time.
