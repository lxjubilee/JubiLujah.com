The hardest hour of the night is not midnight. Midnight is easy; everyone agrees it is dark at midnight. The hardest hour is the one just before dawn, and the hardest job in that hour belongs to the watchman on the wall.

Picture him. The city sleeps below, wrapped in a dark it has stopped questioning. He alone is awake, and he has been awake for hours, and his eyes ache from staring at a horizon that has given him nothing all night. His whole assignment comes down to one impossible task: he has to know that morning is coming *before he can see it.* By the time the sun is obviously up, no one needs a watchman. His entire worth is in the stretch of time when the dark is still total and he must, against all the evidence in front of his eyes, believe in the light and call it out.

There is an exchange in the old prophets that has haunted me for years, because it is so short and so unresolved. A voice calls up to the watchman out of the dark, *Watchman, what of the night? How much of it is left?* And the watchman answers, and his answer is strange and honest all at once: *Morning is coming. And also the night.* Morning is coming, but the night is not over yet. He will not lie and say the sun is up. He also will not surrender to the dark and say it never ends. He holds both, on the wall, alone, and keeps watching.

That, I have come to believe, is the exact job of prophetic worship. And it may be the most important sound a frontier ever hears.

## Singing the dawn before it arrives

We have mostly reduced worship to a response. Something good happens, and we sing about it. The morning arrives, and we celebrate the light. And that is real and good, but it is not the watchman's song. The watchman does not sing *because* the sun is up. He sings *toward* a sun that is still below the horizon, on nothing but the word of the One who set the sun in motion and has never once failed to bring it back.

That is a different kind of singing entirely, and it is the kind the hard places need most.

Anyone can praise in the light. That is the easy shift, the one that shows up when the danger has passed and the evidence is in and the outcome is safe to celebrate. But the watchman is posted for the other shift, the long, dark, evidence-free hours when the whole city is asleep in its despair and there is no visible reason yet to hope. His praise is not a reaction to the dawn. It is a declaration of it. He sings the sun up. Not because his voice moves the sun. It does not, but because his voice tells the truth about the sun while the truth is still invisible, and somebody on that wall has to.

The old songbook knows this ache exactly. One of its writers, worn thin, says that his soul waits for Yahuah more than watchmen wait for the morning, more than watchmen wait for the morning. He says it twice, the way you repeat a thing when it is the only thing holding you up. He is not waiting like a man idly wondering when the sun might come. He is waiting like a man on a wall whose entire body is leaning toward a horizon, certain the light is coming precisely because it always has, and unable to make it come one minute sooner.

That is what it is to worship on the frontier. To lean your whole self toward a dawn you cannot yet see, and to sing as though you can.

## Why the frontier needs a voice, not just a survivor

Here is what I have learned carrying songs into the guarded places, the lands where the night has gone on so long that a whole people can forget mornings are even a category.

The deepest thing the dark does is not hurt you. It is convince you the dark is all there is. Stay in a long enough night and the memory of light starts to feel like a rumor, a thing children believe, a soft lie the strong have outgrown. Whole regions live under that spell, told from every direction that there is no hope for them, no dawn coming, that they should make their peace with the dark and stop embarrassing themselves by expecting anything else. The night does not need to keep them chained. It only needs them to stop watching the horizon.

And that is why the frontier does not only need survivors. It needs watchmen. It needs a voice on the wall that will say the outrageous thing out loud, *morning is coming*, into a darkness that has no evidence for it yet. Because a people that has stopped believing in dawn will not lift its eyes on its own. Someone has to stand up where they can hear and sing toward the horizon until, one by one, they turn and look at what the singer is looking at.

That is worship as witness. Not a private comfort. A public announcement, made in the dark, of a morning the singer stakes everything on. The evangelist in me will not soften it: this is the whole assignment. Not to explain the night. Not merely to endure it. To stand on the wall of a place everyone has left for lost and sing the sun up over it, until the ones sleeping below begin to stir.

## The morning star rises in the chest first

Now here is the mercy hidden inside the hard job, and it is the thing I most want you to carry away.

There is a promise in the last letters of the Scriptures that the day star, the morning star, will rise, and it says it will rise *in your hearts.* Read that again, because the order is everything. The dawn does not begin in the sky and then reach you. It begins in you and then reaches the sky. The light shows up first inside the watchman, a certainty, a warmth, a song rising before there is one ray on the horizon, and *that* interior sunrise is what makes it possible for him to announce the outer one before it comes.

So if you are the one keeping watch through a night that will not end, over a nation, over a family, over your own long dark, and you feel a stubborn, unreasonable song rising in you when everything outside still says midnight, do not distrust it. That is not denial. That is the morning star doing exactly what it promised, rising in the one place dawn always rises first. The light in your chest is not you fooling yourself. It is the true sunrise, arriving early, in the only place it can reach before the sky.

And the prophets promise where it is all going. For the ones who fear His name, they say, the sun of righteousness will rise with healing in its wings, and they will go out leaping like calves let loose from the stall. The night that feels permanent has a documented end. The One who has brought every morning that has ever come is bringing this one too.

## So take the wall

You may be the only one awake where you are. That is not a punishment. That is a post.

Do not wait for the horizon to give you permission before you sing. The whole point of the watchman is that he goes first, that his voice reaches the sleeping city while the sky is still dark, so that they are already turning toward the east when the first line of gold breaks. Sing toward the dawn you cannot see yet, on the strength of the One who has never failed to bring it. Announce the morning into the darkest hour, over the ground everyone said would stay in shadow forever.

Morning is coming. The watchman does not lie about the night. It is real, and it is not over. But he leans his whole life toward the light, and he sings the sun up, and one day, over a frontier that everyone had left for lost, the horizon finally answers him in gold.

Take the wall. It is nearly dawn.
