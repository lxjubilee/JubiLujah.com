The first time I really *saw* a cajón, not just heard it, but saw what it was, an old man was sitting on one in a doorway, playing it with his bare hands, and I could not for the life of me figure out why a grown man was drumming on a wooden crate when there were real drums in the world.

It looks like nothing. That is the first thing you have to understand about it. A cajón is a box. A plain wooden box, about big enough to sit on, which is exactly what you do. You straddle it like a stool and lean forward and strike the front face with your palms and your fingers, and out of this humble nothing of a box comes a whole kit's worth of sound: a deep boom near the bottom, a bright crack up top, a rattle and a slap and a heartbeat. A grown man on a wooden box, and the whole street starts to move.

I asked the old man where the box drum came from. And he told me a story I have never been able to shake, and it changed the way I understand praise itself.

## The box was never supposed to be an instrument

The cajón was born in chains.

When my brothers and sisters were carried in ships to this side of the ocean and sold, the drums came with them in their memory, the drums were the language, the drums were the prayer, the drums were how a people talked to God and to each other across the whole long dark of what was being done to them. And the ones who bought and sold human beings understood this perfectly. So they banned the drums. Outlawed them. Took them away, because a people who can still drum is a people who can still gather, still remember, still hope, and you cannot fully own a people who can still hope.

So they took the drums.

And the people sat down on the shipping crates, the very boxes that had been used to move goods and move bodies, the boxes of their captivity, and they drummed on *those.* On the fruit crates. On the dresser drawers. On whatever wooden thing was too ordinary and too useful to be forbidden. They could not stop a man from sitting on a box. And out of the box, the banned heartbeat came back. The prayer they had tried to outlaw simply changed its address and kept right on praying, from inside the very object that was meant to keep the people down.

The old man in the doorway told me this and then he hit the box once, hard, and let it ring, and he said something I have carried ever since. He said: *they gave us the crate to break us, and we made it sing.*

I sat with that for a long time. Because I realized I was looking at more than a clever instrument. I was looking at the whole shape of the Gospel, carved in wood.

## This is exactly how my God works

You do not have to look far in the old accounts to find that God is forever doing this same thing, taking the object meant for harm and turning it, from the inside, into the instrument of praise and rescue.

Think of the boy sold by his own brothers. They hated him, and they threw him in a pit, and they sold him off to strangers headed for a far country, and everything after that was a box built to break him, the false accusation, the prison, the years forgotten in the dark. Every single thing that happened to that boy was a crate of someone else's cruelty. And when he finally stood in front of the brothers who had done it, grown now, holding their lives in his hand, he did not say what they were braced to hear. He said: *you meant it for evil. But God meant it for good.* The same events. The identical crate. Meant one way by the ones who built it, and meant another way entirely by the God who was quietly turning it into the salvation of nations the whole time.

That is the cajón. That is the whole thing. The box does not stop being a box. Joseph's pit was a real pit; the chains were real chains; the ban on the drums was a real, cruel, bitter ban. My God does not pretend the harm away. He does something stranger and far greater than pretending. He reaches into the very instrument of the harm and pulls praise out of it. He lets the thing that was meant to silence a people become the thing that carries their song farther than the old drums ever could.

And the cross itself. I have to say it, because it is the same story one more time and the largest one. The cross was a crate built to break a man. It was the empire's box, its instrument of shame and silence, designed for exactly one purpose: to end you publicly and make sure everyone got the message. And God sat down on it. And made it sing. The most terrible object the world could build became the most joyful symbol the world has ever known, and we hang it in our homes and lift it over our dancing, because our God specializes in exactly this, taking the crate that was meant to break the people, and turning it into the heartbeat of their praise.

## Whatever they gave you to break you, make it sing

So here is what I want to leave with you, and I want to say it gently, because I do not know what box you are sitting on tonight.

There is something in your life right now that was built to break you. Maybe someone handed it to you on purpose, the way the brothers handed Joseph the pit. Maybe it was just the crate that life ships everyone in, the loss, the poverty, the wound that will not close, the years you feel you have lost in some prison nobody remembers you are in. And the whole world's counsel is the same: get rid of it. Escape it. Wait for it to be gone before you dare to make any joyful sound.

But my people learned something better in chains, and I am handing it to you. You do not always get to throw the crate away. Sometimes the box is the only wood you have. So do not wait for the drums to come back. Sit down on the very thing that was meant to keep you down, and drum on it. Let the God who meant it for good pull a heartbeat out of it. Let the object of your captivity become the instrument of your praise, because that is the exact miracle He is best at, and He has been practicing it since a boy in a pit and a man on a cross.

*Le quitaron los tambores, y él hizo cantar la caja.* They took the drums, and he made the box sing. That is not just the story of an instrument. It is the story of everyone my God has ever gotten His hands on. And it can be your story too. Sit down on the box. And play.
