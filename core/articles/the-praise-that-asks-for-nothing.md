I want you to try something the next time you pray, and I already know it will be harder than it sounds. Try to pray for one full minute without asking for a single thing. No *help me,* no *heal her,* no *provide,* no *fix,* no *forgive,* no *give me.* Just tell Yahuah who He is and how good He is, and stop there. Most people cannot do it for thirty seconds before a request slips in. We are so trained toward the ask that a prayer with no ask in it feels almost incomplete, like a sentence with no verb.

That is worth sitting with, because it reveals how much of our life with God has quietly become transactional. We come to Him the way we come to a counter, with a need, a list, an expectation of exchange. And He is endlessly patient with that; He tells us to bring Him our requests, and He means it. But there is another kind of song entirely, older and rarer and higher, and the church has a word for it that we say without hearing: *praise*. Not thanks, thanks is still, in a sense, about what He did for us. Praise is different. Praise asks for nothing. Praise wants nothing back. Praise is the love that has stopped negotiating.

## The song the sun sings

There is a psalm that lays this out on a cosmic scale, and once you have really read it you cannot un-see what it is doing. It calls the whole of creation to praise Yahuah, and listen to the roll call. Sun and moon. Shining stars. The highest heavens. Sea creatures and all the deeps. Lightning and hail, snow and clouds, the stormy wind. Mountains and every hill. Fruit trees and every cedar. Wild animals and cattle, small creatures and flying birds. Kings and all peoples, young men and young women, old and young together. Everything that exists is summoned to praise.

Now notice what none of them gain by it. The sun is not paid to blaze. The mountains are not bargaining for anything; they will still be mountains whether they praise or not. The sea creatures in the dark deeps receive no favor in exchange for their glory. When Scripture calls the stars and the storm and the cedar to praise, it is not describing a transaction. It is describing creation being *most fully itself.* A thing praises Yahuah simply by being gloriously what He made it to be, out in the open, holding nothing back, expecting nothing in return. The sun does not shine to get. It shines because it is a sun, and shining is its praise.

And then the psalm turns to us, kings and peoples, the old and the young, and puts us in the same list, as though the most natural thing a human being could do is join the sun and the sea and the storm in that great, unpaid, unpurchased outpouring. Praise that asks for nothing is not a strange or advanced spiritual discipline. It is us finally doing what the whole created order was already doing without us.

## Why the highest love wants nothing

Think about the loves you have known. There is a love that is mostly need, the love a small child has for the parent who feeds him, real and sweet but bound up with dependence and asking. And then there is a love that has moved past need into sheer delight, the love where you no longer want anything *from* the person; you simply want *them*, and being near them is its own reward, and you would cross a room just to look at them with no agenda at all. We all know which of those is the higher love. The love that has stopped needing in order to keep loving is the love that has grown up.

Praise is that love, aimed at God. And this is why I think it matters so much and why we should not let it stay rare in us. As long as all our songs are requests, our worship, however sincere, is still partly about us, still oriented toward our own supply. But when you sing a song that asks for nothing, something comes loose. For the length of that song you are not managing your life, not lobbying heaven, not tending your list. You are just beholding Him and saying so. And the strange gift is that it is *freeing* in a way the asking never quite is. The request keeps you leaning forward, tense, waiting on an answer. The praise lets you lean back into who He already is, settled, needing nothing in the moment because the One you are looking at is enough.

## A rehearsal for forever

Here is the thing that finally undoes me about it. In the end, when all is made right, when the tears are wiped away and the last enemy is gone. We will have nothing left to ask for. Every request will have been answered or made unnecessary. There will be no more *heal her,* because she will be whole. No more *provide,* because the table will be full. No more *forgive,* because it will all be forgiven and finished. And so the one form of prayer that will survive into eternity, the only music left when every need has been met, is the praise that asks for nothing. Heaven is not a place of endless requests. It is a place of endless praise, because there will be nothing to want except Him, and Him you will have.

Which means that every time you sing a song here that asks for nothing, you are not doing something small or ornamental. You are rehearsing the language of forever. You are practicing, in a body still full of needs, the love you will love Him with when you have no needs left at all.

> Thanksgiving looks back at what He did. Petition looks ahead at what you need. Praise just looks at Him, and finds that enough.

## Sing one that asks for nothing

So this week, sing one song that requests absolutely nothing. Choose it on purpose. Do not let a single *help me* or *give me* slip in. Just tell Yahuah what He is like, holy, faithful, kind, mighty, unbegun and unending, and refuse to turn it into a transaction. It will feel strange at first, the way an empty hand feels strange when it is used to holding a list.

But stay in it, and something will loosen. You will find that you are loving Him for a moment the way you will love Him always: not for what is in it for you, but simply because He is worthy, and because you were made, like the sun and the storm and the singing stars, to say so out loud, and ask for nothing back.
