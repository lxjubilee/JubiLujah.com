There is a psalm, the hundred-and-third, that begins with a man giving an order. Not to an army. Not to a congregation. Not even to Yahuah. He turns and gives the order to himself.

*Bless Yahuah, O my soul.*

Sit with how strange that is for a second. He does not say "I bless You, Lord." He does not describe how he feels. He turns to his own insides and commands them, like a man clapping his hands at a room that has gone quiet. **Bless. Get up. Do the thing you were made for.** And when you notice him doing it, you realize something that ought to change the way you show up to God on your hardest mornings: sometimes the soul does not lead. Sometimes the soul has to be led.

## The word he used was bigger than "soul"

The word underneath is *nephesh*. We translate it "soul," and that is not wrong, but it is thin. In the old Hebrew way of seeing a person, the *nephesh* is not some ghostly religious part of you tucked away behind your ribs. It is the whole living you, your breath, your throat, your hunger, your thirst, your wanting. When the Scriptures say a hungry man's *nephesh* craves food, they mean his gut is growling. When they say the *nephesh* pants for God like a deer for water, they mean a thirst you feel in your body. The *nephesh* is you at the level where you are tired, where you are hungry, where you either want to get out of bed or you do not.

So when this man says *bless Yahuah, O my nephesh,* he is not talking to a halo. He is talking to the part of himself that is worn out. He is talking to his own appetite, his own weariness, the flat and heavy thing you carry into a room when you have nothing left. And he is telling it to praise anyway.

This is what makes the psalm a gift to the tired rather than a rebuke. He is not a man overflowing so full he cannot help but sing. He is a man who has to *rally* himself. And he shows us there is no shame in that. Some days worship is a flood you get swept up in. And some days worship is a decision you make to your own reluctant heart before your heart agrees.

## The strange, holy act of preaching to yourself

We think of preaching as something one person does to a crowd from up front. But the oldest and most personal kind of preaching is the kind you do to yourself, quietly, with nobody watching.

Watch what he does across that psalm. He does not wait to feel grateful. He starts listing. He tells his own soul what is true whether it feels true or not, that Yahuah forgives, that Yahuah heals, that Yahuah lifts you up out of the pit, that His mercy is not measured out in teaspoons but poured out like the sky is high over the earth. He is not informing God of any of this. God knows. He is informing *himself.* He is a man standing in front of the mirror of his own soul, preaching the gospel to the one person in the room who forgot it, him.

I want to tell you plainly: this is allowed. In fact it may be one of the most faithful things a weary believer ever does.

> You do not have to wait until your heart is singing to open your mouth. You are allowed to talk your own soul into it, one true thing at a time.

Because your feelings are not the boss of your worship. On the mornings when the *nephesh* is flat and heavy and says *not today,* you do not have to obey it. You can turn around and preach to it. You can say, out loud if you need to, the way this man did: *Bless Yahuah, O my soul.* Remember what He did. Remember who He is. And you will often find the feeling follows the command, not always in the first sentence, but somewhere down the list, the heavy thing in your chest begins to lift, because it has finally been reminded of what it forgot.

## "And forget not"

There is one small phrase in that psalm that I think about more than almost any other. Right after he commands his soul to bless, he adds a caution so gentle it is easy to miss: *and forget not all His benefits.*

Forget not. He knows the danger. The danger to a tired soul is not usually rebellion. It is amnesia. You do not stop worshiping because you decided God is not good. You stop because you *forgot*, forgot the last rescue, forgot the answered prayer, forgot the pit you were lifted out of that you now walk past like it was always level ground. The weary heart is a forgetful heart. It remembers every fear and misplaces every mercy.

So the whole psalm is really one man fighting his own forgetting. *Bless, and forget not.* He is stacking up the memories like stones so his soul cannot slide back into the amnesia that would rob him of joy. He forgives all your sins. He heals all your diseases. He crowns you with love. He satisfies you with good. Stone after stone, benefit after benefit, until the soul that woke up empty has been talked back into fullness by nothing more than the truth it already knew and had simply let slip.

## The lowest bar, again

Here is the rest I want to leave in your hands this morning.

If you woke up today and your soul is not singing, you have not failed. You are exactly the person this psalm was written for. It was not written by a man on his best day, brimming over. It was written, at least in part, for a man who had to command his own insides to remember God was good, which means it was written for you, on the day you least feel like worshiping and most need to.

You do not have to manufacture a feeling. You do not have to climb your way back up to some spiritual high before you are allowed to praise. You only have to do the small, stubborn, holy thing this man did: turn to your own tired soul and tell it the truth. *Bless Yahuah, O my soul.* He forgave you. He healed you. He lifted you out of the pit. Forget not.

And if all you can manage today is to say those words without feeling them, say them anyway. The feeling is not the offering. The turning is the offering. You turned your weary self back toward the One who has been good to you the whole time, and somewhere in the listing, quietly, when you were not watching for it, your soul will lean in and begin to remember. It always does. That is what it was made for.

Rest here a moment before you go, and let your soul catch up to the truth you just told it.
