I made this record for the people in the rubble. The ones the system already filed under "no hope." The imprisoned, the addicted, the fallen, the ones who fell into a place so low that respectable folks stopped reaching for them.

And I built the whole thing on the plainest, most stubborn fact in all of Scripture: **Yahuah** has never once built anything out of anything but dust. That is not a limitation. That is the good news. Because if dust is His raw material, then the very place you think disqualifies you is the exact place He does His best work.

## Why I had to make it

I have sat with too many people who believe their bottom is the end of their story. The cell, the relapse, the wreckage of a life that came apart in public. And I noticed that the church sometimes agrees with them by accident. We celebrate the polished testimony and quietly step around the ones still covered in the dust of the fall. We like our resurrections clean. We prefer the part where the man is already standing. But Scripture never skips the dust, and neither should we, because the dust is where the miracle starts.

So the opener just says it, flat and unashamed: "I am dust, dust and ashes, dust and breaking, but dust knows its Creator." I wanted the first thing you heard on this record to be the thing the world tries hardest to make you forget, that dust is not garbage to God. Dust is the stuff He reaches for when He is about to make something alive.

## What the title means

Go back to the beginning. Genesis chapter two says **Yahuah** formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life. The first human being was dirt with God's breath in it. That is the whole species. Dust that got loved into life.

And then Genesis chapter three, after everything breaks, He says the hard word too: "dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return." We spend our lives running from that sentence. But the record turns it over and finds the hope hiding underneath it, because the God who started with dust the first time is not the least bit intimidated by dust the second time.

> Your lowest place is not a disqualification. It is raw material. God has been building glory out of dust since the first morning of the world.

That is the arc of the album, right there in the title. *Dust and Resurrection.* Not dust *or* resurrection, dust *and* resurrection, because in His hands the one becomes the doorway to the other. The bridge of the opening song says it as clear as I know how: out of dust God made humanity, out of dust **Yeshua** rose, out of the dust the gospel is alive.

## The heart under the songs

This record is a gallery of people the world buried, and every one of them gets up.

*Prison Cell Prayer* is for the man behind the bars, praying in the one place the system swears is the end of the line, and I wrote it remembering Paul and Silas, who at midnight in a Philippian jail prayed and sang until the foundations shook and the doors flew open. *The Prodigal Returns* is Luke chapter fifteen: the son who spent it all, hit bottom in a pigpen, and found that the father had been watching the road the whole time and ran to meet him while he was yet a great way off. *The Gospel in Recovery* and *Rising From the Ashes* are for the ones clawing their way back one day at a time, who need to know that the climb itself is holy ground.

And then there is *Scars as Resurrection*, which may be the truest thing on the album. When **Yeshua** rose from the grave, He did not come back unmarked. He kept the scars. In John chapter twenty He shows Thomas the holes in His hands and His side, the risen Lord wearing the wounds of the worst day of His life, now glorified. That is the whole promise of this record in one image: resurrection does not erase where you have been. It transfigures it. The scar becomes the proof. The very mark of your breaking becomes the evidence of your rising.

I anchored the sound of the album in one of the strangest, most hopeful passages the prophets ever wrote, Ezekiel chapter thirty-seven, the valley of dry bones. **Yahuah** sets the prophet down in a valley full of the deadest things imaginable and asks, "Can these bones live?" And then He tells him to prophesy, and the bones come together, and the breath comes into them, and they stand up, an exceeding great army. That is what I wanted this record to do, to prophesy to whatever the world has buried in you and call it back to its feet.

## Who I made this for

I made *Dust and Resurrection* for anyone who has been written off, most of all by themselves.

For the one reading this from a place they are ashamed to name. For the one who has relapsed so many times they have stopped believing the next morning could be different. For the one whose life fell apart where everyone could see it, and who is convinced that the dust they are sitting in is the final chapter. It is not. First Corinthians chapter fifteen promises a body sown in corruption, raised in incorruption; sown in weakness, raised in power. The sowing is real. But so is the raising, and the raising gets the last word.

Hear me on this, because it is the reason the record exists. The tomb was not the end of **Yeshua**, and the rubble is not the end of you. He came out of the ground, and He came out still wearing the wounds, which means He is not embarrassed by yours. He specializes in exactly the thing you are most afraid He will turn away from.

So if you are in the dust tonight, do not despise it. It is the one material the Maker has always used. Let Him breathe on it. Let the breath come into the dry bones. Let the scar become the testimony. Stop apologizing for the ground you are lying in and start listening for the voice that asks whether these bones can live, because when He asks it, He is not mocking you. He is about to answer His own question with your name.

Because the God who has only ever built with dust is not finished building you, and everything He has ever made out of the dust, He has made to rise.
