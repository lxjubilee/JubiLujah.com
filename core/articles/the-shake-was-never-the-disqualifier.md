The microphone was taller than I was that night. My sermon notes were shaking in my hand. I had underlined His name nine times and still could not get my mouth open. *Faithful Witness* is the album I wrote out of that exact terror, and out of the truth I learned on the other side of it: the shake was never the thing that disqualified me.

This is a dobro-slide, harmonica, brushed-kit record, front-porch music, because the kind of witness I most believe in does not happen on a stage. It happens across a kitchen table, on the steps at dusk, in a diner over coffee gone cold.

## The mic and the knees are both His

The opening song is "The Mic and the Knees," and it does not hide the fear. It consecrates it. *The mic and the knees are both Yours, Lord. Father, let the shake be the sermon tonight.* I built it on the great crowd in Hebrews, *since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us run with endurance, looking to Jesus* (Hebrews 12:1–2), and on Peter's charge to be *prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you, yet with gentleness and respect* (1 Peter 3:15). The shake is not the disqualifier. The shake is the altar.

And the second song says the thing I most want a nervous believer to hear: you do not have to wait for a feeling that may never come. "Tell It Anyway" is a man who was waiting on the wind and got an elder's hand on his shoulder instead, *son, the witness doesn't wait on the wind; He'll meet you in the telling.* When **Yeshua** healed the man among the tombs, He did not send him to seminary; He said, *go home to your friends and tell them how much the Lord has done for you* (Mark 5:19). And the apostles, hauled in and ordered to stop, could only answer, *we cannot but speak of what we have seen and heard* (Acts 4:20). The testimony does not need a stage to land. It just needs a mouth willing to shake.

> The shake in the voice is the song. Jesus, You'll do the work in the silence between the lines.

## Front porch gospel

The heart of the album is a picture I would put over every anxious evangelist's desk. "Front Porch Gospel" is a screen door slapping at six, a neighbor sitting down with his coffee and his griefs, and a man who *did not preach*. He poured him a cup, and **Yahuah** did the rest. The porch became the pulpit, the cup became the chalice, and the gospel walked from chair to chair. *Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden* (Matthew 11:28) handed across a step. Because *we are ambassadors for Christ* (2 Corinthians 5:20), and an ambassador does not need a sound check. He needs a doorway and a willingness to sit in it.

Then the record does something I needed it to do: it honors the quiet, unglamorous witness that never trends. "The Long Haul Honesty" is about the testimony that ages out of the spotlight, my grandfather preached forty-one years in the same small town, no album, no platform, just the elder's hand on every shoulder, and *forty-one years of front-pew faithfulness sing louder than the stadium.* That is the witness Scripture actually crowns: *I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith* (2 Timothy 4:7). Not the flash. The kept candle.

## Faithful through repetition

The album's peak is a song called "Faithful Through Repetition," and it may be the least glamorous thesis I have ever built a track around, and the truest. The same sermon notes for the four-hundredth Sunday. The joy not new today, and the witness getting up anyway. *Faithfulness is a kind of repetition, the same Name on my tongue until the tongue forgets to question it.* The Father's own word in the bridge is the whole permission I wish someone had given me at twenty-three: *son, you don't have to dazzle the room to witness for Me. Just say My name today. Say it again tomorrow. That's the witness.* Paul closes his resurrection chapter exactly there: *be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord* (1 Corinthians 15:58); *let us not grow weary of doing good* (Galatians 6:9).

And it multiplies down a bloodline. "Teach Them the Telling" is a man sitting his daughter down with her coffee and saying *the testimony doesn't skip a generation in this house*, teaching her to tell the very part she was tempted to hide, because *you shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house and when you walk by the way* (Deuteronomy 6:7). The testimony is a torch, not a trophy.

## Who this album is for

It is for the believer whose hands go cold at the thought of saying His name out loud. For the person convinced they need a platform, a title, or a certain confidence they do not have. For the one who has been outsourcing their own story to polished men in suits, when the rough draft they lived is the very gospel God wants to preach through them.

The finale, "Witness Forever," names the listener as the next faithful witness and sends them out, because *they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony* (Revelation 12:11), and *let the redeemed of the Lord say so* (Psalm 107:2). So say it, wherever you are. You do not need a stage; you need a screen door and a cup of coffee and the nerve to tell the part you want to hide. Let the shake be the sermon. He will meet you in the telling. He always does.
