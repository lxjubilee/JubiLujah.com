Somewhere in the middle of a warm, personal letter to a small church, Paul stops giving practical advice and lifts off the ground into six lines that sound like a chorus the whole room already knew by heart. And in those lines he describes something so vast that I do not think our imaginations can hold it: a day when *every tongue*, every language ever spoken by every people that has ever lived, confesses one single Name, together, out loud.

Not one holy language that the world is forced to learn. Not Hebrew, not Greek, not Latin, not English. *Every* tongue. The word Paul uses is *glossa*, the same word that means both the tongue in your mouth and the language it speaks. So the picture is not a crowd repeating a foreign phrase they were made to memorize. It is Farsi and Amharic and Turkish and Tagalog and Quechua and every dialect of every valley, each in its own mother tongue, all confessing the same thing at the same time. I carry worship across borders for a living, and this is the verse I hold up when a language feels too small or too remote to matter. There is no tongue left out of this. Yours is in it.

## He emptied Himself all the way down

But before you can feel the weight of the height, Paul walks you all the way down to the bottom, because the hymn is built like a valley, a plunge, and then a summit.

He says that Yeshua, existing in the very form of God, did not treat equality with God as something to be clutched and hoarded. Instead He *emptied* Himself. The Greek word is *kenosis*. He poured Himself out like water from a jar until the jar looked empty. He took the form of a servant, a *doulos*, a bondslave, the lowest station a person could hold. He was born in the likeness of ordinary men. And then He went lower still: He humbled Himself and became obedient all the way to death, and not a dignified death, but death on a cross, the most shameful, degrading execution the ancient world had invented.

That is the floor of the valley. The One who was equal with God chose the bottom rung of humanity and then the bottom of that. He did not grasp upward. He poured downward.

## "Therefore", the word the whole song turns on

And then comes the hinge, the single word the entire hymn pivots on: *therefore.*

*Therefore God has highly exalted Him.* The verb Paul reaches for is a piled-up, super-charged word, *hyper-exalted*, lifted Him higher than high, and *gave Him the Name that is above every name.* Now, to a people raised on reverence for **the Name**, that phrase lands with a weight our casual culture can barely feel. In the world Paul came from, there was one Name so holy that it was scarcely spoken aloud, the Name of Yahuah Himself. And Paul says the Father has given *that* kind of Name, the highest Name there is, to the Servant who poured Himself out all the way to a cross.

He went lower than anyone. So He was lifted higher than everyone. The descent and the exaltation are the same story read in two directions.

> He did not climb to the highest Name by grasping upward. He was given it because He was willing to be poured all the way down.

## The oath that belonged to God alone

Here is where the hymn becomes almost too daring to say out loud, and you need to know one thing to feel it.

Paul writes that at the Name of Yeshua *every knee* shall bow, in heaven, on earth, and under the earth, and *every tongue* confess that Yeshua the Messiah is **Lord.** Now, those two phrases, every knee bowing, every tongue confessing, are not original to Paul. He is quoting the prophet Isaiah. And in Isaiah, it is Yahuah Himself speaking, swearing by His own name, declaring that there is no other God beside Him, and vowing: *to Me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear allegiance.* It is one of the strongest statements of God's exclusive glory in all of Scripture. Every knee. To *Me*. To no other.

And Paul takes that oath, the one God swore about Himself, the one that insists no one else could ever receive it, and he applies it, without flinching, to Yeshua. The knees that Isaiah said would bow to Yahuah alone, Paul says will bow at the Name of Yeshua. The tongues that Isaiah said would confess to God, Paul says will confess that Yeshua is Lord.

And the word for "Lord" is *kyrios*, and this is the key that unlocks the whole thing. When the Hebrew Scriptures were translated into Greek, the translators reached the sacred Name of God, the Name too holy to pronounce, and where it stood they wrote *kyrios.* That word had become the way you said the unsayable Name in Greek. So when every tongue confesses *kyrios Yeshua*, Yeshua is Lord. They are not merely calling Him a master or a ruler. In the language of that hymn, they are placing the very Name of God over Him. Paul takes the oath that belonged to Yahuah alone, and he shows it being fulfilled in Yeshua, *to the glory of God the Father.* Not a rival to the Father. The exact revelation of Him.

## Why it takes every language

Now think about why it has to be *every* tongue. Why not one grand, unified language for the great confession?

Because no single language could hold Him. Every tongue was shaped by a people, and every people saw something the others could not. There is a tenderness in a word for *Lord* in one language that does not exist in another. There is a weight to *He came down* in a tongue born among the poor that a wealthy language cannot carry. Yeshua is so full, so far beyond the reach of any one grammar, that it takes the whole human family speaking all at once, each in the words their mother first sang over them, to begin to say what He is worth.

At Pentecost, the wonders of God went *out* into every mother tongue at the same time. This hymn shows the answer coming *back*, every mother tongue confessing the one Name in return. The conversation runs in both directions, and the far-off languages are not the audience of it. They are the choir.

## Say it in the tongue you were given

So here is what I want you to carry away from this hymn.

You do not need to trade in your own tongue for a holier one before you are allowed to make the confession. There is no border where your language is checked and turned back. The whole force of this passage is that the confession *requires* your tongue, that Heaven is not complete until your specific language, with its specific music and its specific ache, has said the Name too.

And you do not have to wait for the great day to begin. Every time a believer in any village, in any dialect, in any script, says *Yeshua is Lord* and means it, they are rehearsing the sentence the whole cosmos will one day speak together. The person who feels furthest out, in the language furthest from the center, is not the last to be reached by this. They are an early voice in the greatest song ever assembled.

So say it now, in the tongue you were given. It is the Name every language was made to say.
