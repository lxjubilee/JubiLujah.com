She came from the ends of the earth on the strength of a rumor. That is the detail I cannot get over, that a queen would load her camels and cross a killing desert because of something she had only *heard*.

The record calls her the queen of Sheba, from the far south of Arabia, the edge of the trade routes, about as far from Jerusalem as the known world got before it ran out of map. She had heard reports of Solomon, of his wisdom, yes, but the text is careful to say his fame was tied *to the name of the LORD*. The rumor that reached her was not merely that a king down north was clever. It was that his wisdom, his kingdom, his whole glory was somehow bound up with the God he served. That is the report that got into her, and it would not let her rest at home.

So she did what almost no one does with a rumor of glory. She went to see for herself. She assembled a caravan, camels bearing spices and gold and precious stones, and she crossed the wilderness with hard questions in her hand, riddles and problems she intended to test him with, because she was a serious woman and she was not going to travel that far to be flattered. She wanted to know if the report was true or if it was, like most reports of glory, bigger than the thing itself.

## The half had not been told

When she arrives, Solomon answers everything. There is no question too hard, nothing hidden from him that he cannot open up for her. And then the account slows down and lingers over what she *sees*, and it reads like a person's senses being overwhelmed one after another.

She saw the wisdom. She saw the house he had built. She saw the food of his table, and the way his officials were seated, and his servants standing in their places, and their clothing, and the cupbearers, and the ascent by which he went up to the house of the LORD. Course after course, sight after sight, the whole ordered splendor of a kingdom arranged around the worship of God, and the record gives us the line that all of it built toward: *there was no more spirit in her.*

The breath went out of her. She had come with hard questions and a skeptic's caution, braced against exaggeration, and the reality knocked the wind clean out of her lungs. And when she could speak again, she said the truest thing a traveler ever says at the end of a long pilgrimage: *the report I heard in my own country was true, but I did not believe it until I came and saw with my own eyes, and behold, the half was not told me.* The half was not told. The rumor that had been big enough to move her across a desert turned out to be an understatement.

Then she blessed his God. This Arabian queen, standing in a foreign court, said, *blessed be the LORD your God, who has delighted in you.* The glory she had crossed the desert to test had done to her exactly what real glory does. It did not win an argument. It emptied her of breath and filled her mouth with praise to a God who, until that journey, had not been hers.

## Something greater than Solomon

Now here is why I, a man who carries worship into countries most people have written off, cannot leave this story in the past.

Centuries later Yeshua reaches back and picks this queen up out of the history and holds her in front of a crowd that had every religious advantage and was using none of it. He says that the queen of the South will rise up at the judgment and condemn that generation, *because she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and behold, something greater than Solomon is here.*

Read what He is doing. He is taking a pagan queen from the far frontier of the world and holding her up as the standard. She traveled to the ends of her endurance on a rumor of Solomon's glory. And here, Yeshua says, is a glory greater than Solomon's, standing in the street, free of charge, no desert to cross, and the people with the scrolls in their laps will not walk across the room for it. The outsider who chased a rumor across a wilderness will shame the insiders who would not turn their heads for the real thing.

The frontier queen is not the cautionary tale in that sentence. She is the hero of it. She is the one who took the report of God's glory seriously enough to spend everything getting to it. And Yeshua tells us she will stand at the last day as a witness, that the ends of the earth were reachable, that the guarded and distant places *do* respond to a true rumor of glory, and that the tragedy is never the far country's hardness. The tragedy is the near country's boredom.

## The report is still traveling

I give my life to carrying songs into the guarded countries, Arabic, Turkish, Romanian, the tongues of places official reports call closed. And the queen of Sheba is one of my great encouragements, because she proves something about how the glory of God actually moves. It moves as a rumor first. It travels ahead of any missionary as a report, a story someone heard, a song that leaked across a border, a whisper that there is a God whose worship is unlike anything, and that the half has not been told.

We underestimate what a true rumor of glory can do in a place we have given up on. We assume the far countries need the full apparatus to arrive before anyone will move. But Sheba moved on a *report*. She heard something true about a distant worship, and it lodged in her, and it would not let her stay comfortable, and eventually it put her on a camel headed for a God she did not yet know. There are people, right now, at the far edges of the map and the far edges of your own life, in whom a true rumor of glory is quietly doing its work, making them restless, loading their camels, aiming them across a wilderness toward a sound they have only half heard.

Which means our worship is never only for the room it is sung in. Every honest song of praise is also a rumor being released, a report going out over the borders, into the trade routes, toward the ends of the earth, of a glory so real that when someone finally crosses the desert to stand inside it, the breath will go out of them and they will say what the queen said: the half was not told me.

## Do not be out-traveled by the frontier

So let this old queen ask you her question. She spent a fortune and risked a desert on a *rumor* of glory that turned out to be an understatement. You live inside the fuller report. The greater-than-Solomon she never got to see, you have been told His name. Do not let a pagan queen out-hunger you for the glory of God.

And when you worship, remember that you are not only singing in a room. You are releasing a rumor toward the ends of the earth, true, and still traveling, and still, somewhere, loading a camel.
