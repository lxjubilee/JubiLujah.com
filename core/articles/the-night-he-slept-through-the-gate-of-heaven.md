Jacob was running for his life when it happened, which is worth remembering before we make the story too golden. He had swindled his brother, deceived his blind father, and fled the tent with Esau's fury behind him. He was not on a pilgrimage. He was a fugitive with the sun going down and no roof anywhere, and when the light failed he did what a tired man does. He found a place, took one of the stones lying around, put it under his head, and slept.

There was nothing sacred about the spot, as far as he could tell. It was just a place. Somewhere to stop because his body had run out of daylight. He was not looking for God. If anything, God was one of the things he was running from. And he lay down on a rock in the middle of nowhere and closed his eyes on the loneliest night of his life.

Then heaven opened over his head.

## He did not choose the holy ground; he stumbled onto it

In the dream a stairway stood on the earth with its top reaching into heaven, and messengers of God were going up and coming down on it, and Yahuah Himself stood above it and spoke. And what He spoke was not judgment on the schemer at the bottom of the stairs. It was covenant. I am the God of your father, He said. The ground you are lying on I will give to you and your children. Your family will bless every family of the earth. And then the line that must have gone through Jacob like light: I am with you, and I will keep you wherever you go, and I will not leave you until I have done what I promised.

To a man who had just burned every bridge behind him, Yahuah said, I am coming with you. Not because Jacob had earned it. He plainly had not, but because Yahuah is the kind of God who meets fugitives on rocks and hands them a future they did not deserve.

And here is the sentence I cannot get past. Jacob woke up and said, Surely Yahuah is in this place, and I did not know it. Then fear came over him and he said, How awesome is this place. This is none other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven.

He did not know it. He had walked into the gate of heaven, lain down, and gone to sleep on it. The most sacred threshold he would ever cross in his life, and he nearly missed it entirely because it did not announce itself. There was no temple there. No pillar of fire. No sign. Just a rock, a tired man, and a holiness that had been present the whole time, waiting to be noticed.

## We sleep through more glory than we know

I think about that more than almost anything in the Scriptures, because I am certain it is not only Jacob's story. We move through far more holy ground than we ever register. Yahuah is present in places that give us no dramatic signal, the ordinary evening, the hard road we are only on because something went wrong, the stretch of life we would never have chosen and cannot wait to leave. We lie down on it exhausted, treating it as nowhere, a place to endure until the sun comes up. And all the while it may be the very gate of heaven, and we do not know it.

The reason we do not know it is rarely that God is hiding. It is that we have grown numb. We have trained ourselves to expect glory to arrive with fanfare, and so we sleep straight through the quiet, unannounced presence of the living God on an ordinary rock. Reverence, at its root, is not a special intensity we work up in a worship service. It is a waking up. It is the moment Jacob's eyes open and the whole landscape he thought was empty turns out to have been full of God the entire time.

Notice what fear did to him, and notice it was the good fear, the awe that steadies rather than the terror that scatters. It did not make him run. It made him build. He took the stone he had used for a pillow, the plainest object at hand, set it up as a pillar, and poured oil over the top of it. That is the first recorded act of worship in his life, and it is beautifully simple. He marked the spot. He said, in effect: something happened here, God was here, and I refuse to walk away pretending I did not notice. He gave the place a name, Bethel, the house of God, where before it had been called Luz and had meant nothing to anyone.

## The stairway turned out to be a person

The gate of heaven has moved since that night, or rather it has become someone you can know. Long after, Yeshua told a man He had just met that he would see heaven opened and the messengers of God ascending and descending, and then He said the words that break the whole thing open, upon the Son of Man. He was telling us what Jacob's stairway had always been reaching toward. The ladder between earth and heaven is not a place on a map you might stumble onto by accident. It is Yeshua Himself. He is the gate. He is the ground where heaven touches down and where a fugitive is met with covenant instead of the judgment he expected.

Which means the holy ground is no longer somewhere you have to be lucky enough to lie down on. It is a Person you can wake up to anywhere. The numbness the whole story diagnoses has a cure, and the cure is not a better location. It is opened eyes. Surely Yahuah is in this place is a sentence you could say over the room you are sitting in right now, over the hard season you are running through, over the rock you have been treating as nowhere.

So take the plainest thing at hand and pour worship over it. Wake up to where you have been standing. You have almost certainly slept through more of the presence of God than you have ever noticed, and the invitation of this whole story is not guilt about the nights you missed. It is the gasp Jacob gasped at dawn, the awe that finally sees, and the small holy stubbornness that says: God was here, and I will not walk away from it unnamed.
