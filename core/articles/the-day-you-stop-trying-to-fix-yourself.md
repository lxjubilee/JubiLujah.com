The most solemn day on the entire Hebrew calendar is also, according to the law that commands it, a day of rest. And almost no one notices, because the two things seem like they could not possibly belong together.

It is the Day of Atonement, *Yom Kippur*, the gravest, most searching day of the whole year, the day set aside for the sin of a nation to be dealt with. It is a fast day. God tells His people to *afflict their souls*, an old phrase long understood to mean going without food, laying aside comfort, humbling themselves low before Him for one solemn stretch. If you were told nothing else about it, you would imagine the heaviest, most striving day imaginable, a nation straining with everything it has to somehow get right with God.

And then you read the actual command, and there is a phrase in it that stops you cold. God calls this day a *Shabbat Shabbaton*, a Sabbath of complete rest. The very same word He used for the weekly day of stopping, doubled, intensified, laid right into the middle of the most solemn fast of the year. On the day the people most needed to be made right, God did not say *strive harder.* He said: *rest.* Do no work. Cease. Stop.

I have turned that over for years, and I have come to believe it holds one of the most freeing truths in all of Scripture, one the tired and the guilt-ridden desperately need to hear.

## What the word underneath it means

The name *kippur* comes from a Hebrew root that carries the sense of covering, of wiping clean, of atonement, the setting-right of what has gone wrong between a people and their God. And the whole shape of the day tells you *who* does that setting-right, and it is not the people.

On this one day of the year, the high priest carried out the great ceremony of atonement on behalf of the whole nation. The people did not perform it. They could not. They stood outside while one man went in on their behalf and did what none of them could do for themselves. The blood, the covering, the cleansing. It was all done *for* them, by another, while they waited. Their assignment on the holiest day of the year was not to fix themselves. It was to stop, and humble themselves, and let the atonement be made for them.

Do you feel the weight lifting already? On the day most concerned with sin, the people were the most *passive* participants in the whole calendar. Everyone else worked. The priest worked. But the nation rested, because the one thing they absolutely could not do was atone for themselves, and God, in His mercy, did not ask them to try. He asked them to cease trying and receive.

## The rest that is hidden in the solemn day

Let me be careful about what is Scripture and what is the tradition around it. That the Day of Atonement is called a Sabbath of complete rest, that it is a fast, that the atonement was made by another on the people's behalf, that is all in the text. The many customs that grew around the day across the centuries are the practice of God's people, and I name them as tradition. But the truth sitting at the center of the day is not tradition; it is bedrock, and it is this: *you cannot make yourself right, and you were never asked to.*

This is the rest that hides inside the heaviest day. It is not the rest of a nap or a vacation. It is a far deeper rest, the rest of stopping the one labor that never, ever ends: the exhausting, grinding, lifelong project of trying to make yourself acceptable. Of trying to be good enough. Of trying to earn, by sheer effort and enough guilt and enough resolve, a cleanness you can never manufacture no matter how hard you strain.

> The strangest rest in Scripture is the one hidden in the solemnest day: the day the whole nation stopped trying to fix itself and let itself be fixed.

I pastor so many people worn ragged by exactly this labor. They are not lazy sinners. They are the opposite. They are wearing themselves out trying to be clean, trying to earn back God's smile, treating their own souls like a broken thing they have to repair before God will have them. And it never ends, and it never satisfies, because self-atonement is a machine with no off switch. The Day of Atonement is God reaching in to switch it off. He built a day into the very calendar of His people that says: *on this day you do not work at fixing yourself. You afflict your soul, you humble yourself low, and then you rest, because the fixing is being done for you, by another, and your striving was never going to accomplish it anyway.*

## What the day was pointing at

And of course all of it was pointing forward. The whole aching, annual repetition of that day, one man going in on behalf of the many, blood carried into the holy place, the sins of a nation covered for another year, was a shadow leaning toward a substance. Every year it had to be done again, which quietly told the people it had not yet been finished. The atonement kept needing to be remade because the true and final one had not yet come.

Then Yeshua came, and He did what the day had always pointed to. He went in on behalf of the many, not with the blood of animals but with His own life, and He made the atonement that never has to be made again. The exhausting annual repetition found its end in Him. What the Day of Atonement rehearsed once a year, one going in for the many, the people resting while the work was done for them. He accomplished once, for all, forever. Which means the rest that was hidden in that solemn day is now simply *offered*, permanently, to anyone who will stop striving and receive it.

## Put down the tools you were never meant to hold

So here is the honest word your pastor most wants you to carry away.

If you are worn out, and I mean the deep kind of worn out, the soul-tired that comes from years of trying to make yourself good enough for God, then the oldest and most solemn day on His calendar has a word aimed straight at you, and the word is: *stop.* Stop trying to atone for yourself. Stop treating your own soul as a repair project you have to complete before God will love you. That labor was never yours to carry, and it has been quietly crushing you, and God has been waiting to take it off your back.

The most searching day of the year turned out to be a day of rest, because the thing you most need done is the one thing you cannot do, and it has already been done for you by Another. You do not have to fix yourself. You were never able to, and you were never asked to. You were asked to humble yourself, to come with empty and open hands, and to *rest* while the atonement is made on your behalf.

So set the tools down. All of them. The endless self-improvement, the guilt you keep paying and paying and never finishing, the exhausting effort to earn a cleanness that is being handed to you as a gift. Let it be done *for* you. That is the rest hidden inside the heaviest day of the year, and it is the truest rest there is. Stop striving. You are being made right by hands far more capable than your own. Rest, and let it be finished.
