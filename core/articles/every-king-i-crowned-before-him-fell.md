I tried every name on the radio play. I chased every name the street ever sold, the money, the fame, the sneaker parade, the whole line of kings the world hands you and tells you to bow to. And I need you to hear the testimony plainly, because I paid for it: *nothing ever filled me. No one could hold.* Every king I crowned collapsed under the weight of the crown.

Then one Friday night, His Name walked in, and the dembow itself seemed to surrender to the Light. **Solo Tú, Jesús**, *Only You, Jesus*, is the reggaeton record I made the morning after every counterfeit fell and only one Name was left standing.

## What the title means

*Solo Tú, Jesús* means "Only You, Jesus." It is the most exclusive claim a person can make, and I make it on purpose, over an 808 and a dembow kick, because the barrio needs to hear that the gospel is not shy. There is a track literally called "Reyes Que Caen", *Kings That Fall*, and it is the honest one, the inventory of every throne I built for something that could not sit on it.

The world will offer you a thousand names to worship. Isaiah heard **Yahuah** answer all thousand of them at once: *"I am the first, and I am the last; and beside me there is no God"* (Isaiah 44:6). And Peter, standing in front of the whole council, refused to soften it: *"Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved"* (Acts 4:12). That is not narrow. That is *rescue.* When you are drowning, one rope is not narrow-minded. One rope is everything.

> There's no other Path, no other Way. There's no other Name, no other day. Only You, Jesús, only You can save.

## Why I had to make it

Because I watched a generation crown TikTok kings and come away empty, and I have been that generation.

There is a verse in the opening track about a kid on the block with the airpods in, chasing kings on the timeline all week, and then the real King walks in *through the algorithm's bend.* **Yeshua** on the feed. That line makes people laugh and then it makes them quiet, because they know exactly what it describes. We are the most-entertained, most-marketed-to, least-satisfied generation in history. We have crowned more idols than any people who ever lived, and every one of them has fallen.

I made this album to say: stop rebuilding thrones for things that keep collapsing. There is a Name that does not break. *Every other name will break, only Yours can hold.*

## The songs, and the "I AM" that answers everything

This whole record is really a tour through the great **Yeshua** self-declarations, the "I AM" statements, set to reggaeton so the barrio can chant them back.

"Camino, Verdad, Vida" is John 14:6 turned into a hook you cannot get out of your head, *"I am the way, the truth, and the life."* "Una Sola Puerta" and "La Puerta del Pastor" plant the flag on John 10, *"I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved"* (John 10:9), and *"I am the good shepherd"* (John 10:11). The door and the Shepherd are the same Person, and He is the only way into the fold and the only One inside it who will lay His life down for you.

"Nombre Sobre Todo Nombre", *Name Above Every Name*, takes the crown off every fallen king and puts it where it belongs. "En el Barrio Tu Nombre," *Your Name in the Barrio,* plants that one Name on the block I am from, on the corners where all the other names were sold to me first. And "Altar en la Calle," *Altar in the Street,* refuses to keep any of this indoors. The barrio corner is an altar. **Yeshua** is *"the light of the world"* (John 8:12), and light does not stay in the building. It goes out to the very street where the counterfeit kings are still being advertised, and it names the real One.

"Lleva Tu Nombre Conmigo", *I Carry Your Name With Me*, is the commissioning song, and it settles the difference between an idol and the Lord in one line. You carry an idol; it never carries you, and eventually the weight of it breaks your back. But His Name carries *you.* You do not prop Him up. He holds you up. Every king I ever crowned needed me to keep it standing. **Yeshua** is the first one who kept *me* standing instead.

### The most intimate song on a loud record

There is one track that pulls the whole party down to a whisper: "Solo Tú Me Conoces", *Only You Know Me.* Under all the ad-libs and the 808s, this is the quietest confession on the album, and it leans on Psalm 139: *"O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me."* The world crowned a version of me it invented, the image, the followers, the highlight reel. But only **Yeshua** knows the real one, the one behind the phone. Only He can be Lord of a person He actually knows. Every other king was ruling a stranger.

That is why He is the only One worth the crown. The idols demanded I perform for them. He already knows me all the way down and calls me His anyway.

## Where it's all headed

The album does not end with me. It ends with the world. "El Mundo Te Conocerá", *The World Will Know You*, reaches back to Paul standing in Athens in front of an altar marked *TO THE UNKNOWN GOD,* saying, *"Whom therefore ye ignorantly worship, him declare I unto you"* (Acts 17:23). Every city is full of altars to unknown gods, kings that will fall. My job, our job, is to walk up to those altars and finally name the One they were reaching for the whole time.

## Who this album is for

This one is for the kid with the airpods in, chasing kings on the timeline. For the person who has crowned money, or status, or a relationship, or their own image, and watched the crown crush the thing it sat on. For everyone exhausted from bowing to names that keep breaking.

Here is the good news, loud, over the dembow: there is a Name that does not fall. Every king I crowned before Him collapsed, and then **Yeshua** walked in, and He is still standing, and He always will be. Take the crown off the counterfeit. Put it where it holds.

*Solo Tú, Jesús.* Only You. Let His Name lead.
