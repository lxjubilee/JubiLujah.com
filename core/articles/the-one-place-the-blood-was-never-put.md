Let me ask you a question that sounds simple and is not. On that first Passover night, *where* exactly did the blood go?

Most people, if you press them, will say "on the door." True, but not precise, and the imprecision costs you the whole picture. The instruction was exact. Take the blood of the lamb, and put it on the two side posts and on the upper beam, the lintel, of the houses. In Hebrew, the side posts are the *mezuzot* and the top beam is the *mashqof.* Two uprights and a crossbeam. Three sides of the doorway, marked in blood.

Now hold that shape in your mind, two posts and a beam over the top, because the geometry of that doorway is not random. It is a door of blood built around a family that had done nothing to earn its own rescue.

## They painted it with a fistful of weeds

Before I tell you the one place the blood did not go, look at *how* it got there, because even the applicator is preaching.

They did not use a fine brush or a golden vessel. The command was to take a bunch of *ezov*, hyssop, a common, lowly, scrubby little plant, the kind of thing that grows in the cracks of a wall, dip it in the blood caught in a basin, and strike the posts and the lintel with it. The most important act of that entire night, the one thing standing between a household and death, was done with a handful of humble weeds dripping red.

I love that Yahuah chose hyssop. He did not make salvation depend on having the right tools or the right eloquence or the right pedigree. He made it depend on the blood, applied by whatever ordinary trembling hand was willing to reach up and mark the door. If you have ever felt too common to be the one who carries deliverance into your house, remember the hyssop. He has always worked through the lowly thing.

## The threshold stayed clean

Here is the detail that will not let me go. The blood went on the two posts. The blood went on the lintel over the top. But the blood was never commanded onto the *threshold*, the bottom, the sill, the part you step over to go in and out.

Sit in that, because there is a reason so tender it aches.

The people were told that night not to go out the door of the house until morning. When the destroyer passed through the land, Yahuah Himself would stand over the marked doorway and not permit death to enter. So picture the geometry now: blood on the right post, blood on the left post, blood on the beam above, but the ground you stand on, the threshold you cross, was to stay *clean.* You do not walk over the blood. You do not tread the blood of the lamb under your feet. The one line the blood was kept from was the one line your foot would touch.

> The blood was placed everywhere except where you would step on it. Even in the terror of that night, Yahuah was teaching them that the blood is holy. You shelter under it, you never trample it.

I want to be careful and honest with you here, the way I would want someone to be with me. The text gives the command about the posts and the lintel plainly; the reverence about not treading the threshold is a truth the practice preaches rather than a sentence spelled out for us. I will not dress a faithful reading up as a chapter and verse it is not. But look at what the whole night insists on. The blood is not a magic paint you smear wherever. It is a boundary of mercy, and the God who commanded it counts it precious. The renewed covenant later warns, in the sharpest terms, against the person who treats the blood of the covenant as a common thing, something to be walked on. That first doorway was already whispering it: this blood is over you and beside you, but never *under* you.

## A frame of blood around the ones inside

Now step back and see what the three marks make together. Two posts and a beam. A doorway of blood surrounding a family who could not save themselves, who had done nothing that night but obey and stay put and trust the mark.

That is the shape of the gospel before the gospel had words. Deliverance was not something the family generated by their courage or cleaned up by their goodness. Deliverance was a covering they stepped *inside* of. The blood did the standing-between. All they had to do was be under it and not wander out from beneath it before the morning came.

And this is where I need you to see Yeshua, because the pattern is not subtle. He is the Lamb whose blood is applied, not with hyssop to a wooden frame now, but to the doorposts of a human heart. When death, the last destroyer, sweeps through, it is looking for one thing: the mark. Not your record. Not your résumé of good behavior. The blood. Are you *under* it or not.

## Stay under the covering

So let me speak to the one who is lying awake tonight while something dark moves through your life, wondering if it is coming for your house.

Get under the blood and stay there. That is the whole instruction, and it is enough. You do not defeat the destroyer by being strong enough to fight it at the door. You are delivered because the blood of the Lamb is over you and beside you, and the One who sees it will not permit death to cross that marked frame. Your job on the night of dread is not to go out and wrestle the darkness in the street. Your job is to trust the mark and not step out from under it before the morning.

And never, *never*, let anyone teach you to treat that blood as common. Do not tread it underfoot with a casual heart, as though it were cheap, as though your covering cost nothing. It cost a spotless Lamb His life. Keep it holy. Keep it over you and beside you. Keep your foot off it.

Two posts, a beam, and a clean threshold you were never meant to trample. That doorway has been preaching for three and a half thousand years, and it is still saying the same thing to you tonight: you are surrounded by the blood, and death has no way in. Stay inside until the morning. The morning is coming.
