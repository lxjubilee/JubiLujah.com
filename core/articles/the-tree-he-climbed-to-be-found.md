He was too short to see over the crowd, and too hated for anyone to let him through.

That is the picture I want you to start with, because both halves of it matter. Zacchaeus was a chief tax collector in Jericho, which meant two things about him: he was rich, and he was despised. He had grown wealthy by collecting the occupying empire's taxes off his own people and skimming whatever extra he could squeeze out of them, and everyone in town knew it. He was a traitor with a full purse. When he walked down the street, the crowd did not part for him out of respect. If anything they closed ranks. And so on the day the traveling teacher everyone was talking about came through Jericho, little Zacchaeus found himself at the back of a wall of shoulders that would not move for him, unable to see a thing.

So he did something undignified. This wealthy, powerful, grown man ran ahead of the crowd and climbed a tree.

## Just to see

I love that he climbed. There is something in that detail I cannot get past. He had money and status and every reason to protect his dignity, and he threw all of it away for one thing: he wanted to see. He did not have a theology. He did not have a plan. He did not, as far as we can tell, even want to be noticed, a man who is hated by a whole town does not go looking to be noticed. He just wanted to catch a glimpse of this Yeshua over the heads of people who would never have let him near the front. So he tucked himself up into the branches of a sycamore where he could look down on the road unseen, a small hidden man hoping only to watch something good pass by.

He got so much more than a glimpse.

Because when Yeshua reached that spot, He stopped. And He looked up. Into the tree. At the one person in Jericho nobody else was looking at except to sneer. And He called him by name.

Zacchaeus. Come down. I need to stay at your house today.

## The scandal of the chosen house

Understand what that did to the crowd. Of every house in Jericho, of every righteous and respectable and generous person who would have been honored to host Him, Yeshua chose the traitor's. The whole crowd started to grumble, the same crowd that had just been pressing in to see Him now muttering that He had gone to be the guest of a sinner. Of *that* sinner. The worst one. The one who had gotten rich off their backs.

And here is the thing they missed, standing there with their arms crossed. They thought Zacchaeus climbed the tree to find Yeshua. But it was the other way around. Yeshua walked into Jericho already looking up into the branches for a small lost man. Zacchaeus thought he was doing the seeing. He was the one being sought. He climbed a tree to catch a glimpse and discovered he had been the object of the whole search the entire time.

## Everything, times four

What happened next is the part that makes it a Jubilee story, and it happened fast, and it happened out loud.

Zacchaeus stood up, in his own house now, with Yeshua under his roof, and he made an announcement. Half of everything he owned, he was giving to the poor, right now. And anyone he had cheated, anyone he had defrauded, and there were many. He would pay back four times over. Not the amount. Four times the amount.

Nobody made him do this. There was no sermon about money, no demand, no condition attached to the visit. Yeshua had simply come into his house, and something in the man broke open, and out of it poured restitution on a scale the law had never even required of an ordinary thief. The old law of restoration said that a man who stole and was caught made it right, sometimes double, sometimes more for certain thefts. Zacchaeus reached past the requirement and grabbed the most extravagant number in the book and applied it to himself, gladly, laughing probably, a rich man cheerfully emptying his own vaults because the joy that had walked in the door was worth more than everything in them.

That is what the presence of the Redeemer does to stolen things. It does not merely forgive them. It sends them home. The money Zacchaeus had pulled out of his neighbors' lives came flowing back out of his house four times as fast as it had come in, debts he had caused, cancelled and overpaid, families restored, the whole crooked account of a lifetime unwound in an afternoon.

## Today

And Yeshua said the word over that house that I would build my whole life around hearing said over mine. He said: *Today* salvation has come to this house. Not someday. Not after a probation period, after Zacchaeus proved himself, after the town forgave him. Today. He is a son of Abraham too, one of the family, all along, however far he had wandered from it. And then Yeshua named His own mission in one clean line: the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost.

Not the found. The lost. The ones up trees. The ones the crowd will not let through. The ones with too much stolen money and too few friends, who only dared to hope for a glimpse and never imagined being called down by name.

## The sound of a house set right

I think there was music in that house by evening. I cannot prove it, Scripture does not tell us, but I know what a homecoming sounds like, and I know what happens in a house when a debt gets cancelled and joy moves in, and I do not believe that afternoon ended in silence. A man had been found. His whole crooked history was being made right, out loud, with laughter. The poor of Jericho were about to have a very good week. And the one person the whole town had written off was, that day, more free than any of them.

The Jubilee was never only about the calendar or the trumpet. It was always about this: the moment the Redeemer walks into the house nobody expected Him to choose, and the stolen years come flooding back, and a lost man discovers he was a son the whole time.

If you have been hanging back at the edge of the crowd, too compromised to push to the front, too aware of what you have taken to expect to be wanted, climb the tree. Just to see. And do not be surprised if the One passing by stops under your branches, looks straight up at you, calls you by the name you did not think He knew, and says the word that changes the whole day.

Today.
