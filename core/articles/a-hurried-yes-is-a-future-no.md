I have buried what hurried leaders broke. I do not say that to be dramatic; I say it because I have lived long enough to attend the funerals of good ministries, good marriages, and good churches that did not die of scandal. They died of speed. Somebody lifted the gavel before the Lord had spoken, made a fast yes to look decisive, and the whole house paid for it years later. So when I sat down to make **Leadership Wisdom**, I did not build it around vision or charisma or strategy. I built it around the one discipline almost no leadership book teaches: how to *wait* before you decide.

## The first work of the faithful is not deciding. It is kneeling

The opening song is called "Deciding Well," and it does not start with a decision. It starts on the knees: *Jesus, which chair, which word, which meeting, before the voices are heard. I will not decide before I have knelt. Father, speak the weight before the gavel is dealt.* That is the whole thesis of the record in one verse. Leadership, in the kingdom, begins as surrender, not initiative.

James gives the reason, and it is astonishingly practical: *"If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach, and it will be given him"* (James 1:5). Notice, wisdom is not primarily something you develop by experience. It is something you *ask for*, from a God who gives it generously and without shaming you for needing it. And Paul locates that wisdom in a Person, not a principle: *"Christ Jesus, who became to us wisdom from God"* (1 Corinthians 1:30). This is why the song's refrain is not *I will be wise* but *Jesus, You decide in me.* The wise leader is not the one with the best judgment in the room. She is the one most surrendered to the One whose judgment is perfect.

> A hurried yes without You is a future no in disguise, but a leader yoked to the Lord will not outrun His eyes. I have sat with the yes till You softened it to a no; I have sat with the no till You told me where to go.

## Listening is a decision you make before all the others

The third song is called "Listening First," and I put it early on purpose, because most bad leadership is not a failure of courage. It is a failure of hearing. We decide loudly and listen last.

The model is the boy Samuel in the dark, taught by old Eli to answer the voice with the only posture that ever produces wisdom: *"Speak, LORD, for your servant hears"* (1 Samuel 3:10). Servant first, decision second. On this record I even let the music preach it. There is a moment where a single fiddle note holds while everything else falls away, twenty seconds of enacted silence, because I wanted the listener to *feel* how uncomfortable and how necessary the pause is. We are so frightened of the quiet before a decision that we fill it with noise and call the noise leadership. But *"trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding; in all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make straight your paths"* (Proverbs 3:5-6). The straight path is on the far side of the listening, not the far side of the hurry.

## Wisdom wears two faces: truth and kindness

I called the fifth song "Truth and Kindness," and it corrects the two ditches leaders fall into, because wisdom is not just *knowing the right thing*. It is knowing how to carry it.

Some leaders have truth and no tenderness; they are right and they wound. Others have kindness and no spine; they are warm and they mislead. But the Lord we follow *"came... full of grace and truth"* (John 1:14), both, at full strength, in one Person, with no dilution of either. And the wisdom that comes down from Him is described exactly this way: *"the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy"* (James 3:17). Pure *and* peaceable. That is the whole art of it. This is why the album's picture of authority is not a raised fist but the King's own self-description: *"I am gentle and lowly in heart"* (Matthew 11:29). "Authority Gentle," I named that song, because the strongest leaders I have ever known held the most power with the softest hands.

There is also a song on this record called "Breaking Barriers," and it stands at the altar-peak of the whole album for a reason. Because there come decisions no amount of careful waiting can resolve by human means, walls that will not move, doors that will not open, situations where the wise thing and the possible thing seem to be two different things. And **Yeshua** says of exactly those moments, *"I am the door. If anyone enters by me, he will be saved and will go in and out and find pasture"* (John 10:9). The wise leader does not batter down the barrier with her own force. She waits at the Door, and lets Him open what no committee, no budget, and no clever strategy ever could. Some of the most important yeses of my life were doors I could not have forced in a hundred years, the Lord simply opened them, in His time, after the waiting.

And real wisdom multiplies itself. The record does not end with the leader deciding well alone; it ends with "Commissioning Others" and "Teaching Leaders," because a wise leader who trains no one has misunderstood the job. **Yeshua** promised, *"where two or three are gathered in my name, there am I among them"* (Matthew 18:20), leadership in the kingdom was always meant to be shared, done in the midst of others, never hoarded by one gifted person at the top.

## Who this is for

This is for the pastor with a hard meeting on the calendar and a decision she is dreading. It is for the ministry leader, the business owner, the mother of a household, anyone who holds a gavel over other people's lives, which is nearly all of us. It is for the young leader in a hurry to prove herself, and for the tired one who has been burned by her own fast decisions and needs to be reminded that the pause is not weakness; it is the first work of the faithful.

Here is the sentence I want you to carry into your next decision: the room will wait if the One speaking is the Lord. You do not have to outrun His eyes, and you do not have to lift the gavel before He has spoken. Sit with the yes until He confirms it or softens it to a no. Because a hurried yes is only a future no wearing today's clothes, and a leader yoked to **Yeshua** has all the time in the world to decide well.
