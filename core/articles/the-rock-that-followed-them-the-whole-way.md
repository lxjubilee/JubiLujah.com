There is a place in the wilderness story where the people are not gently thirsty. They are desperate. The water is gone, the children are crying for it, the animals are failing, and a whole nation is standing on ground where there is, by every honest measure, no water and no way to make any. And in that place they do the thing thirst always tempts a soul to do. They turn on the God who brought them there.

*Why did you bring us up out of Egypt to kill us and our children with thirst?* They are one bad hour from stoning Moses. And this is the moment Yahuah chooses to do something no desert has ever done before or since. He tells Moses to take his staff, to stand before a rock, and to strike it, and out of the dry heart of solid stone, water comes. Enough for a nation. Enough for the animals. A river out of a boulder, in a place that had none.

## They named the place after the fight

What Israel called that place tells you everything about what really happened there.

They named it two things at once: **Massah** and **Meribah**. *Massah* means *testing*, or *proving*, the place where they put Yahuah to the test, where they demanded He prove Himself or be found a fraud. *Meribah* means *quarreling*, *strife*, *contention*, the place where they contended with Him, where they made their thirst into an accusation. Testing-and-Quarreling. That is the name they hung on the very spot where He split a rock open to save their lives.

Sit with that. He did not name the place. *They* did. And they named it after their own faithlessness, not after His faithfulness. They could have called it *The-Lord-Provides.* They could have called it *Rock-of-Rescue.* Instead the name that stuck was the record of their own grumbling, a monument, forever, to the fact that they doubted Him in the exact moment before He rescued them anyway.

And that is a mercy hidden in the naming, if you have eyes for it. He answered the quarreling with water. He met the test not with judgment but with a flood from stone. The people wrote their worst hour into the map, and Yahuah wrote His kindness right on top of it.

> He gave them water at the place they named after their unbelief. That is who He is. He does not wait for you to deserve the rescue before He splits the rock.

## Water where there is no water

Do not rush past the sheer impossibility of the picture, because the impossibility *is* the point.

A rock is the last place on earth you would go looking for water. It is the very symbol of dryness, of hardness, of the place where life cannot get in. When Scripture wants to describe utter desolation it speaks of flint and stone and drought. And that is precisely what Yahuah reaches for. He does not lead them to a hidden oasis. He does not open a spring in soft ground. He brings the water out of the *hardest, driest, most hopeless thing in the landscape*, so that no one, not one soul in that whole camp, could ever mistake where it came from.

An oasis you could credit to luck, to good scouting, to the land. A river out of a rock you can only credit to God. He chose the impossible source on purpose, because provision that could be explained any other way would have taught them nothing. This water had one signature on it, and everyone drinking it knew the hand.

That is worth holding onto if you are standing on hard ground yourself right now, the kind of situation everyone around you has already declared a dead end, the stone that everyone agrees will never give. The wilderness God has a particular habit. He likes to bring the water out of exactly the place the world has written off. He is not limited to soft ground and easy springs. He splits rocks.

## The Rock that followed

Now for the sentence that turns the whole story inside out.

Centuries later, the apostle Paul is writing to a young church, reminding them that their ancestors all passed through the sea and all ate the same spiritual food and all drank the same spiritual drink, *for*, he says, *they drank from the spiritual Rock that followed them.* And then he adds the line that should stop you cold: **and that Rock was Messiah.**

The Rock followed them. Not a boulder rolling across the sand, Paul is telling us that the source of the water, the One behind the stone, was not stationary and was not left behind at Massah. He went with them. The whole way. Every dry stage of the forty years, the true Rock traveled with the camp, and every time the water came, it came from Him. (There is an old tradition among the teachers of a literal well that rolled along after Israel through the desert; that is a tradition of the teachers, not a claim of the Torah, but Paul's point stands on its own and is far greater. The living Source was always present, always moving with them, always ready to give.)

Think of what that means for the naming. They called the place Testing-and-Quarreling, and the One they were testing was the very Rock He would one day identify as His own Son. They struck at Him with their words, and He answered by being struck for their thirst.

## Struck once, for a river that never runs dry

And that word, *struck*, carries the last and deepest thing.

At Massah, the rock had to be struck before the water would come. Moses lifted the staff and hit the stone, and only then did the river break loose. It is a hard, violent image for a gift so kind. And it points, the way all of it points, straight at Yeshua. Paul says the Rock was Messiah, and Messiah was struck. On a hill, in the sight of a jeering crowd not so different from the one at Meribah, the true Rock was struck once for the thirst of the whole world, and out of Him came the water that does not run dry.

Yeshua Himself made the promise in almost these words. He stood up and cried out that anyone who was thirsty should come to Him and drink, and that out of the one who believes would flow rivers of living water. The Rock that followed Israel through the wilderness had come to stand in front of a thirsty people in person, and to be struck, once, so that the river would never have to be summoned again.

So if you are at your own Massah tonight, parched, tempted to turn on the God who led you into the dry place, ready to name this whole season after your doubt, hear the older story underneath yours. The Rock is not behind you at some place you failed. He followed you here. He is with you on the hard ground. And He has already been struck, so that the water you are dying for is not something you have to earn, or deserve, or wait to be worthy of.

Come to the Rock. Drink. He has been traveling with you the whole way, and He is not out of water yet.
