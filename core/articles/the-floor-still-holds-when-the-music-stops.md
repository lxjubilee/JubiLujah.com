There is a word I have spent years trying to get people to feel in their feet, not just understand in their heads.

The word is **hesed**. Your English Bible translates it a dozen ways, steadfast love, lovingkindness, mercy, faithfulness, because no single English word can carry it. Hesed is covenant-love. It's the love that has signed something. It is loyalty that has made a promise and cannot, will not, walk it back. Hesed is the love that never learned how to quit.

And when I finally understood that hesed doesn't quit, I couldn't sit still. So *Hesed That Never Stops* is a dance record, oud, darbuka, a hora pulse, hands clapping. But right in the middle of all that celebration I hid the quietest song on the album, because I wanted you to know the floor holds even after the band goes home.

## Why a doctrine had to become a dance

The opening track states the thesis with your body before your mind can argue. *Hesed That Never Stops* calls hesed a floor, the covenant floor you dance on, the ground that does not fall below. That image is the spine of the whole album.

Because most people treat God's love like weather. Sunny today, storms tomorrow, better keep an umbrella of good behavior handy. But hesed is not weather. Hesed is the floor. It was already there before your morning and before your failing, and it will still be there after. The chorus says it the way I'd want it shouted in a full room: His love does not let go, the covenant floor doesn't fall below.

And I can say that with a straight face because of *why* it's true, which the second track spells out. Deuteronomy calls **Yahuah** "the faithful God who keeps covenant and steadfast love with those who love him... to a thousand generations" (Deuteronomy 7:9). A thousand generations. That's the song *Faithful, Faithful, Faithful*, and the whole point is that His faithfulness is not a mood He's in. It is who He is. He cannot stop, because to stop would be to stop being Himself.

## The most personal line on the record

Track three is where the doctrine gets its knees dirty. It's called *Your Hesed Never Stopped on Me*, and the hinge of it is a small grammatical flip that I think is the most honest sentence on the album:

> I stopped on You, but You did not stop on me.

Read that twice. Every one of us has a season where our prayer got tired, our faith got thin, our song went silent. We stopped. And the temptation is to assume God stopped too, that He matched our distance, that the moment we drifted He drifted. Hesed says no. His love kept flowing down a corridor I wasn't even walking anymore. The covenant kept what I could not see. That is not sentiment. That is the difference between a love that responds to you and a love that has sworn something over you.

## The king who danced away his dignity

Now, if hesed is a floor you can dance on, the album needed a dancer. It has one. His name is David.

Track five is *Leap Before the Ark*, and it's built on that scene in 2 Samuel 6 where David, bringing the Ark into Jerusalem, dances "with all his might", leaping and spinning in a linen ephod, so undignified that his own wife despises him for it (2 Samuel 6:14-16). And David's answer is essentially: I will be even more undignified than this, before **Yahuah**. I love that scene because it settles an argument a lot of quiet believers are having with themselves. **Yahuah** does not despise the dancing heart. When covenant-love is that faithful, a leaping body is the only reasonable response. Hesed deserves a dancing kid.

The album then widens the shot. *Hesed Storm Across the Generations* sweeps from Abraham under the stars, to Isaac, to Jacob wrestling through the dark, to David in the courts, to the disciples at the Passover table, the same steadfast love rolling down through every one of them like a storm that never runs out of rain. One family tree, one unbroken covenant, one love that reaches all the way down to the person pressing play.

## The night the music stops

And then, the quiet one.

Track nine is *When the Music Stopped*, and I need to tell you why it exists, because it's the reason for the whole album. It would be a cheap record if it only worked at the party. Anyone can believe in God's love when the harvest is in and the band is loud. The test comes on the night the music actually stops in your house, the diagnosis, the empty chair, the grief that walks in with the morning.

So I anchored that song in the prophet Habakkuk, in one of the bravest paragraphs in Scripture. He looks at total loss, no blossom on the fig tree, no fruit on the vine, no flock in the fold, no herd in the stall, and then he says the sentence I built the song around: "yet I will rejoice in **Yahuah**; I will take joy in the God of my salvation" (Habakkuk 3:17-18). Yet. The music stopped, but the hesed didn't. The harvest failed, but the floor was solid where I slipped.

That is the whole promise of the album, stated in its most stripped-down form. The dance songs are true because *this* song is true. The floor that you dance on when everything is good is the same floor that holds you up when everything is gone.

## Then dance it back out the door

The album refuses to end in the quiet, though, because hesed doesn't stay in the sanctuary either. *Dance the Hesed Into the Street* takes the celebration out to the corner store, the laundromat, the bus stop, the lonely at dawn. Covenant-love is portable. It's meant to be carried to the wounded, not hoarded by the healed. And the finale, *Dance Us Home Tomorrow*, hands the whole thing forward, a prayer that **Yahuah** would lead the dance line into the next morning, and the morning after that. Because His mercies, Lamentations tells us, are "new every morning; great is your faithfulness" (Lamentations 3:22-23).

## Who I made this for

I made *Hesed That Never Stops* for the person who is quietly exhausted from trying to earn a love that was never on the auction block. You cannot behave your way onto the floor. It's a covenant floor. **Yahuah** laid it down at cost, sealed, the album says over and over, in **Yeshua**'s own blood, and He did not build it so you could stand nervously at the edge.

And I made it for the one whose music has stopped. If the harvest failed and the house went silent and you are sure, in the dark, that the love stopped too. I wrote you a whole song to say it didn't. Put your weight down. The floor is solid where you slipped. Hesed never stops, and it is dancing you home.
