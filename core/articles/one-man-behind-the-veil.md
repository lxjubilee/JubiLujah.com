There was one room in all of Israel that the whole nation believed in and almost none of them would ever lay eyes on. Behind a heavy woven curtain, in the deepest heart of the tent, sat the place where the presence of Yahuah met the earth, and the rule about that room was simple and terrifying: you do not come in.

Not the priests who served daily just outside it. Not the Levites who carried its furniture wrapped so their eyes never fell on it. Not the king. One man could enter. One day a year. And even he came in the way a man walks toward something that could kill him.

We say the words *Holy of Holies* so easily now that they have gone soft in our mouths. On the Day of Atonement they were the sharpest words in the language.

## The one who was allowed, and the way he was allowed

The high priest did not simply stride behind the curtain because the office was his. Before he crossed that threshold he bathed his whole body. He set aside the golden garments of his glory and dressed in plain linen. He prepared blood, first for himself and his own household, because the man carrying the nation's atonement had first to be atoned for, and then for the people.

And he did not go in empty-handed or clear-eyed. He took a censer full of burning coals from the altar and two handfuls of fragrant incense, and Scripture is explicit about why: he was to put the incense on the fire **before** the presence, so that a cloud of smoke would cover the mercy seat, *lest he die*. The text says it plainly. The cloud was not atmosphere. It was mercy. He was to enter God's presence veiled even from the thing he had come to serve, because unshielded nearness to that glory is not survivable for a son of Adam.

Then, into that fragrant darkness, he brought the blood, and he sprinkled it toward the cover of the ark and before it, the place the Hebrew calls the **kapporet**, the seat of covering, where atonement was made not in theory but at a specific spot on the earth, once, for a whole people who waited outside.

> A curtain between the nation and the presence. A single man permitted through it. A single day it could happen. A cloud of incense so the mercy would not consume the one bringing it. This is the machinery of nearness in a fallen world, and every part of it is a locked door with one narrow key.

## The weight no one else could feel

Try to stand where he stood. Outside the curtain, the whole congregation is waiting, not casually, but in the fast and the silence of the holiest day, every soul in the land aware that at this hour one man is doing the one thing on which their standing before God depends. Inside, alone, he is closer to the untamed glory of Yahuah than any living person, with nothing between him and it but a rising cloud of smoke and the blood in his hand.

There is a well-known story that a rope was tied to the high priest's ankle so that, if the holiness struck him dead inside, his body could be dragged out without another man daring to enter. I want to be honest with you the way the Word requires: **that is a later tradition, not Scripture.** The Torah says nothing of a rope. But the legend survived because it tells the truth about the fear even where it invents the detail, the truth that going in was a matter of life and death, and everyone knew it.

That is the point I do not want you to miss under the drama. The system worked, and the system was also a standing confession that the door was still, fundamentally, *shut*. One man could go in, which meant the rest could not. Once a year, which meant the other days it was closed. And even he came out again, and next year the whole trembling procedure had to be done over, because a covering that must be renewed is a covering that has not yet finished the job. The genius of the Day of Atonement was that it opened the room a crack and, in the very same motion, showed you how firmly it was ordinarily sealed.

## The One who went in and stayed in

Now hear the astonishment of what the followers of Yeshua came to understand, and why they could not stop marveling at it.

Yeshua did not enter a tent made by human hands, a copy of the true thing. He entered the reality the tent had only ever sketched, the actual presence of Yahuah, heaven itself. He did not bring the blood of goats and bulls, offered again and again because it could never quite finish. He brought His own blood, offered a single time. And He did not slip in behind a cloud of incense to shield Himself from a glory that might consume Him, because He is Himself the radiance of that glory. He walked in as *both* the priest who carries the blood and the offering whose blood is carried, and He did not come back out to repeat it the next year, because **once was enough, and once was forever.**

The Day of Atonement said: one man, one day, one narrow permitted moment, and then the door swings shut again. Yeshua said: one Man, one death, one entrance, and the door does not swing shut again.

I will not retell here the great tearing of the temple curtain the hour He died; that wonder deserves its own telling and it has one. But stand back and see the shape of the whole thing. For century upon century a nation had watched a lone figure disappear behind a curtain to do for them what they could not do for themselves, and had held its breath until he emerged alive. In Yeshua the figure went behind the final curtain, into the true and unshielded presence, carrying a covering that never needs renewing, and He is *there still*, not to hide from the glory but to hold the door open in His own body, so that the people who once could only wait outside are now invited to come near.

## What the open door asks of you

Here is what all of this means for you, and it is not a fine point of ancient ritual. It is your permission to approach.

You were not built to earn your way into that room. Neither was the high priest; he bathed and bled and shielded himself precisely because worthiness was never on offer. What is on offer now is a nearness he could only taste one day a year and you are invited to live inside, not because the glory got smaller or the holiness got safer, but because Someone went in ahead of you and made a way that stays open.

So when you come to worship, do not come as one hoping the door might crack for a moment. Come as one for whom it has already been thrown wide at a cost you will spend eternity understanding. Come with reverence, yes, the room is no less holy for being open. But come. That is the whole point of the one Man who went behind the veil and never had to come out again.

The curtain is not the barrier anymore. It is the doorway, and He is standing in it, and He is not going to move.
