Every year in early November, my people go to the cemetery. We bring flowers the color of fire, *cempasúchil,* the marigold, and we lay the petals in a path, because the old belief is that the scent guides the dead back home for one night. We set out photographs. We light candles in a semicircle. We tell the stories again. We laugh and we cry at the same table, and it is one of the most beautiful and most aching things my culture does.

I did not want to argue my people out of that ache. The ache is *love,* and love is holy. But I could not stand to leave it unanswered either, because I know something about where the dead in **Yeshua** actually are, and it is so much better than a path of petals could ever promise.

**Vivos en Cristo**, *Alive in Christ*, is the album I made to answer the ache. I set it to full mariachi, violins and vihuela and guitarrón and the trumpet, because if I am going to sing you the resurrection I am not going to whisper it. I am going to blow the horn.

## What the title means

*Vivos en Cristo* means "Alive in Christ." The subtitle of the closing song says it even plainer, *Los Santos Viven,* the saints *live.* Present tense. Right now.

The whole record is built as a turn, the Day of the Dead becoming the Day of the Living. There is literally a song in the middle called "El Día de los Vivos," the Day of the Living, and everything before it is walking toward that pivot and everything after it is dancing away from it. Because for the believer, death is not the end of the story. Paul said it without flinching: *"For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain"* (Philippians 1:21). *Gain.* Not loss. The song that carries that verse, "Morir es Ganancia," has the whole congregation shouting the phrase back, because it is the most defiant good news a grieving people can sing.

## Why I had to make it

Because I have stood at too many gravesides where the only thing on offer was a memory.

A memory is precious, but a memory cannot hold you up at 4:18 in the morning when the grief comes back. I needed to sing something *truer* than memory. So this album leans its full weight on the thing **Yeshua** said standing outside a tomb, four days too late by human reckoning: *"I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live"* (John 11:25). The song "Yo Soy la Resurrección" puts that sentence in His mouth and lets the congregation answer *"vivirá, vivirá"*. He shall live, he shall live, until you believe it in your chest.

I made this record for the aunt who cannot stop crying, and for the grandchild standing in front of the ofrenda looking at a black-and-white photo of an abuelo in his white guayabera. There is a song for exactly that, "Abuelo's Photo on the Ofrenda", and the testimony under it is that Cristo held that old man's hand as he crossed over. The photo is not all that is left of him. He himself is more alive right now than the grandchild staring at his picture.

## The songs, and the shape of the hope

The album starts in restraint. The opener, "El Cementerio en Noviembre," deliberately holds the trumpet back, a mariachi record with *no trumpet* on track one, because there is a reverence you owe the graveside before you are allowed to celebrate. "Los Cempasúchiles" walks the marigold path. "Te Recordamos" says *we remember*, but it adds the two words that change everything: *with hope.*

Then the turn comes, and the trumpet is finally allowed in, and the whole thing lifts.

"Lejos del Cuerpo, Cerca del Señor" takes its title straight from Paul, *"absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord"* (2 Corinthians 5:8). That is the actual address of the believing dead. Not wandering. Not waiting by a petal path for one night a year. *Present with the Lord.* Home already.

"La Gran Nube de Testigos" pulls in Hebrews 12:1, that *"great cloud of witnesses"*, and I staged it like a stadium, because that is what the passage describes: a packed arena of the faithful who ran their race and are now cheering ours. Your abuela is not gone. She is in the stands. She is *loud.*

And then the two songs I wrote to be shouted at a funeral until the funeral changes its mind. "La Trompeta Sonará", the trumpet shall sound, from Paul's promise that *"the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible"* (1 Corinthians 15:52). And "Dónde Está, Muerte, Tu Aguijón," where the whole congregation taunts the grave with Paul's own words: *"O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory?"* (1 Corinthians 15:55). I gave the trumpets a call-and-response on that taunt on purpose. Death does not get the last word on this record. It gets *mocked.*

> We do not gather to say goodbye. We gather to say *hasta luego*, until later, because in Christ there is a later, and the later never ends.

## What I am not saying

Let me be honest and careful, because I love the truth more than I love the tradition. I am not telling you the marigold petals bring anyone back. I am not telling you to pray to the dead. The dead in **Yeshua** do not need our petals to find their way. They are already home, already in the presence of **Yahuah**, already more alive than we are. What I *am* doing is taking the beautiful, aching instinct of my people to remember and honor their loved ones, and pointing it at the only place that instinct can finally rest: the empty tomb.

Remember them. Light the candle. Tell the stories. But remember them *forward*, toward the resurrection, not backward toward a grave that Christ has already emptied of its power.

## Who this album is for

This one is for the family at the cemetery in November. For the grandchild in front of the ofrenda. For the aunt whose grief is still raw. For every believer who has buried someone they cannot wait to see again.

Play it at the wake. Play it at the graveside. Play it loud. Let the violins mourn for a while, and then let the trumpet come in and tell the truth: the ones we remember are not behind us. They are ahead of us, in the great cloud, cheering, waiting, *alive.* And one day the trumpet on this record is going to be a small echo of a real one, and the graves are going to open, and *los santos*, the saints, are going to stand up and dance.

Until later, then. Not goodbye. *Hasta luego.* We will finish the song together.
