I grew up aware of a wall I couldn't quite see.

It ran between two families who were supposed to be cousins, the people of the ancient covenant and the people of the church that grew out of it. Built up over centuries out of liturgies and lies, silences and last-supper goodbyes, it got so tall that both sides forgot it was ever supposed to have a door. I spent years thinking my job as a teacher was to be a mason, to build, brick by careful brick, a nice bridge over the top of it.

Then I read Ephesians 2 slowly, and it wrecked my whole plan. Because Paul says the bridge isn't something you build alongside the wall. The bridge is a Person, and He already came. *Bridge of Yahuah's Love* is the album that came out of that discovery.

## The wall was real, and so was the demolition

Paul is blunt about the wall. He tells the Gentile believers they were once "separated from Christ, alienated... strangers to the covenants," far off, without hope (Ephesians 2:12). That's a real distance. He doesn't pretend it away. And then he says **Yeshua** "is our peace, who has made us both one and has broken down in his flesh the dividing wall of hostility" (Ephesians 2:14).

Read that again, "in his flesh." Not with a memo. Not by softening the rules. With His body.

The opening track, *Walk the Bridge*, states the thesis in the plainest words I could find: **Yahuah** built it, and **Yeshua** *is* the bridge. Not a bridge He commissioned. The bridge He became. The second track, *Gap Closed in Yeshua*, drives it home from the other side, the blood closed the distance that history left open. Once you see that, you stop trying to be the mason. The demolition already happened. Your only job is to walk across.

## The image I could not shake

The cinematic peak of the record is track eight, *Wall Came Down on the Carpenter*, and it holds the single picture that the whole album was built to deliver.

We usually imagine reconciliation as a wall being *lifted*, hoisted up and out of the way by some triumphant force. But that's not what the text describes, and it's not what the cross was. **Yeshua** didn't lift the wall off to the side. He let it fall, and He got underneath it. He bore it across the gap on His own back and laid it flat. Paul says He did this "through the cross, thereby killing the hostility" (Ephesians 2:16). The hostility didn't evaporate. It was executed, and the place it died was His shoulders.

So the chorus of that song is almost unbearable to me every time:

> Wall came down on the Carpenter. He bore it across the gap, He laid it flat, the bridge is His body.

That's why I kept calling Him the Carpenter throughout the record. The One who worked wood in Nazareth is the same One who let the heaviest structure in human history come down on Him so two estranged families could finally hold hands on the rubble.

## The tree, and the warning that comes with it

If the album only celebrated, it would be dishonest, because a newly opened door tempts the newcomer to swagger. So I put a teaching ballad at the center of it: *Olive Root and Grafted Branch*, straight out of Romans 11.

Paul's image there is precise and humbling. There's an ancient olive tree, older than any of our countries, whose root drinks from a promise **Yahuah** made to Abraham. Some natural branches were broken off, and wild branches were grafted in. And then Paul turns and warns the grafted branch directly: "do not be arrogant toward the branches... it is not you who support the root, but the root that supports you" (Romans 11:18). The song lives entirely in that warning. The bridge of the chorus is the whole point:

> The root holds me up. I do not hold the root.

The grafted-in were not brought near to climb above anyone. They were brought near out of sheer mercy, to share the sap they never seeded. Being a bridge people means holding that humility in your body, not just your theology.

## What one new humanity looks like on a Tuesday

The thing I most wanted to avoid was leaving all this at the level of a nice idea. So the album keeps landing the vision in kitchens and doorways and around tables.

*Echad on the Chorus* takes the Shema, "Hear, O Israel: **Yahuah** our God, **Yahuah** is one" (Deuteronomy 6:4), and reasons from it that if the God we worship is One, then His body has no business staying two. *Stand on Each Other's Holy Ground* pictures it concretely: a Gentile believer lighting a Shabbat candle on Friday evening, a Messianic grandmother lifting the doxology on Sunday morning, each honoring the other's holy ground because **Yeshua** walks both rooms with the same feet. *Two-Made-One Communion* sits at the table where the Passover lamb and the broken bread turn out to be the same meal, "because there is one bread, we who are many are one body" (1 Corinthians 10:17). And *One Father's House* reaches Paul's conclusion: "you are no longer strangers and aliens, but... members of the household of God" (Ephesians 2:19).

Then the record refuses to let you sit down. *Cross It With the Stranger* is a commissioning song, the bridge is never finished with one walker. You crossed it once; now go take a hand and lead somebody else across. And the finale, *We Walk the Bridge Every Morning*, makes the whole thing a daily habit rather than a one-time event. He walked us across once, and now we walk it every morning, over coffee and breakfast and the ordinary business of a shared house.

## Who I made this for

I made *Bridge of Yahuah's Love* for the believer who feels the ache of that old wall, who senses that the two families were never meant to be strangers and doesn't know what to do about twenty centuries of distance. Here is what to do: stop trying to build the bridge. Walk the One who is it.

And I made it for the one carrying an old quarrel in the walls of their own body, the inherited silence, the shame no one ever named. There's a tender song near the end, *The Echo of Old Quarrels*, for exactly that person. It doesn't pretend the wound was weightless. It says the Carpenter walks the corridors where the lamps went out, honoring the silence, naming the shame, and lighting every lamp as He goes.

That's the God this record is about. He did not stand at a safe distance and issue a peace treaty. He came to the seam between us with His arms wide open and His shoulders to the sky, and He let the whole wall come down on His own back. The bridge is not built. The bridge is **Yeshua**. Walk His name, and take somebody with you.
