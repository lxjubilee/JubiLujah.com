Before you had a face anyone could recognize, you had ears that worked.

This is one of the tenderest facts I know about how we are made. Deep in the warm dark, months before birth, long before your eyes had anything to open toward, your hearing came online. And it was not idle. A baby in the womb learns the particular music of its mother's voice, the rhythm of her speech, the songs sung near her, learns them so well that newborns, only hours old, will turn toward the voice they heard through the water, and calm to a melody they were played before they had a name. You were listening before you were looking. Hearing was your first window onto the fact that there was a world, and that you were not alone in it.

And here is the fact that sits at the other end of your life, and I say it gently because it has stood beside me in rooms I will not forget: hearing is very often the last sense to leave. When the body is shutting its windows one by one, when sight has gone, when speech has gone, when the person seems already half-departed, the ear frequently stays open to the very end. This is why the people who tend the dying will tell you, quietly, *keep talking. Keep singing. She can still hear you.* The last thread connecting a leaving soul to the people who love it is very often a sound.

So the sense that opened first is the one that closes last. Hearing brackets the whole of you. It was there before you could do anything at all, and it lingers after everything else has been laid down. Whatever your life turns out to be, it begins and ends inside a world of sound, held open by an ear that would not shut.

I have not been able to hold that fact without trembling a little, because of what the Scriptures say arrives through exactly that door.

## Hearing is touch you cannot feel

Stop and consider what hearing even *is,* because we treat it as ordinary and it is not.

Sight happens at a distance; light lands on you but does not move you. But sound is different. Sound is a physical wave, actual moving air, pressing in and pulling back, pressing and pulling, and when it reaches you it does not politely inform your ear. It *pushes* it. It sets a delicate membrane trembling, and that trembling shivers three of the smallest bones in your entire body, and those bones stir a coil of fluid, and standing in that fluid are thousands of impossibly fine hairs that *bend* under the motion, and the bending is the hearing. Which means every sound you have ever heard, you did not merely receive. You were *touched* by it. Sound is the one thing that reaches across a room and physically moves the inside of your body without ever seeming to touch you at all. Hearing is touch at a distance. It is the sense through which the world gets *inside* you without your permission and stirs something at your very center.

Now hold that next to the strangest claim the Scriptures make about how faith comes to a person.

They do not say faith comes by *seeing.* We assume it should. We are forever wishing God would show us something, produce some sight that would settle it. But that is not the door the old writers name. They say faith comes by *hearing.* It arrives through the sense that opened first and closes last, the sense that reaches in and physically moves you at your center, not the one that keeps everything at a safe, observing distance. Faith is not, first, something you look at and evaluate. It is something that *reaches* you, the way sound reaches you: from outside yourself, moving toward you, pressing gently in until something deep and previously still begins to tremble in response.

I find that unbearably fitting. Of course faith comes by hearing. Hearing is how you first knew you were not alone in the dark. Hearing is the last light left on when all the others fail. It would make perfect sense that it is also the way the One who made you keeps reaching in.

## Hear, O Israel

And this is why the most important sentence the Hebrew people ever carried does not begin with *look.* It begins with *hear.*

*Hear, O Israel*, that is how it opens, the confession they were told to bind to their hands and speak over their children and say lying down and rising up. Not *see, O Israel.* Not *understand, O Israel.* *Hear.* Before you are asked to grasp anything, before you are asked to see anything, you are asked to open the one sense that was open before you could do anything else, and to let a word reach in and move you. Yeshua Himself kept pressing the same door. Again and again He would finish a hard and wonderful thing by saying, *whoever has ears to hear, let them hear*, as though the whole matter came down not to how clever you were or how much you could see, but to whether you would keep the oldest window in you open, and let the sound in.

Because it is possible to have working ears and hear nothing. That is the ache underneath all of it. You can be present at every service and let the whole thing hum by like a refrigerator. Hearing that reaches the soul is not automatic; it is a kind of consent, a decision to stop merely processing the air and to let a word actually *touch* the center of you, the way sound was always designed to.

## Keep the window open

So here is what I want to hand you, tenderly, especially if you are in a season where God has felt far and silent.

You may be straining to *see* Him. Waiting for a sign, a proof, a sight that would finally settle the matter, and finding only more distance, because sight keeps everything at arm's length and that is exactly what it is for. But you were not built to meet Him first with your eyes. You were built to meet Him through the door that opened before your eyes ever did, the one that reaches in and moves you from within. Stop straining to see, for a moment, and simply *listen.* Not for something loud. For something that reaches. A word read slowly until it stops being ink and starts being a voice. A song that presses gently in and stirs the thing at your center that has been still too long. That trembling is not nothing. That is the sense that opened first, doing again what it was made to do: telling you that you are not alone in the dark, and that Someone is near enough to reach you.

And if you are keeping watch beside someone who is leaving, if the doctors have gone quiet and the room has gone still, hold onto this. The window is very likely still open. So do the thing love does when words have run out and hearing is the last thread left. Sing over them. Say the old true things. Let sound do the one work it has done since the warm dark before any of us were born, and will do until the very last door closes: reach in, across the distance, and let a soul know it is held.

You began your life inside a sound you did not choose, that told you you were not alone. It is fitting beyond words that you might end it the same way, and that in between, the God who made the ear would come to you through the one window He made sure would open first and close last.

Keep it open. Faith comes by hearing. It always has.
