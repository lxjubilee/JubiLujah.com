Picture a man at the kitchen sink, hands in the dishwater, humming.

He is not thinking about the song. He could not tell you its name. He did not decide to play it. It started itself, somewhere behind his eyes, and now his mouth is following along to words he never chose to memorize but somehow knows by heart.

This happens to him most days. It happens to you too. And it is not nothing.

## The teacher you never enrolled with

There is a track playing on repeat in your head right now, or there was an hour ago, and there will be again before the day is out. You did not pick most of them. They arrived. They lodged. They loop.

Here is the uncomfortable part: that loop is not idle. It is teaching you something. Every time it comes around it rehearses a small claim about the world, about love, about worth, about what you are here for, about whether anyone is coming to save you, and it slips that claim past you while your guard is down and your hands are busy.

You have a teacher you never enrolled with. It teaches you in your own voice. And most people never once stop to ask what it is saying.

## Belief is not built the way you think

We imagine we arrive at what we believe by thinking. We weigh the arguments, examine the evidence, reach a conclusion, and there, a belief, reasoned neatly into place.

Almost none of your real beliefs got there that way.

The convictions you actually live by were not argued into you. They were worn into you, the way a path is worn across a field, not by one dramatic crossing, but by the same feet taking the same line, day after day, until the grass gives up and the trail is simply there.

Repetition is how a belief stops being an idea and becomes a reflex. And nothing on earth repeats inside you like a song.

A fact you hear once, you forget by evening. A chorus you have sung a few hundred times is laid down in you like track. You will lose your keys, your passwords, the name of someone you met last week, and still, unbidden, produce every word of a song you have not deliberately thought about in twenty years. Music does not knock on the door of your memory. It moves in.

That is a staggering power. And it is entirely neutral about what it carries.

## What you rehearse, you become

This is the turn, so slow down here.

Whatever you put on repeat, you are rehearsing. Whatever you rehearse, you are slowly becoming. The loop is not describing you. It is forming you.

Sing nothing matters and no one is coming enough times, in a melody gorgeous enough to bury the meaning, and one ordinary afternoon you will find that you believe it, not because anyone convinced you, but because you practiced it. Sing I am held, and I am not alone enough times, and that grows roots too. The line does not have to be true to take root. It only has to be repeated.

## Yahuah knew this before there were kitchens or sinks

Long before anyone studied how memory works, the Maker of memory used it on purpose.

Near the end of the Torah, Yahuah tells Moshe to do something strange. Not write down a law. Not carve a monument. Write a song. Teach it to the people, He says, and put it in their mouths, because this song will not be forgotten out of the mouths of their children.

Read that again. He chose a song because it would outlast their forgetting. He knew they would drift from the commandments. He knew they would not drift from the tune. So He gave them the truth in the one container that survives, the one that installs itself and hums back on its own.

Later, the followers of Yeshua were told to let His word live in them richly by teaching one another in psalms and hymns and songs. Not only by lecturing each other. By singing to each other. Because they understood what you now understand at your own sink: the melody is not decoration on the message. The melody is how the message gets in, and stays.

## Choose the loop

Here is what I want to hand you, and it is smaller and more doable than a whole new life.

You cannot stop the humming. You are not built with an off switch, and you would not want one, the same wiring that traps a shallow song traps a saving one. You cannot control that your mind loops.

But you can choose what gets in.

You can notice, maybe for the first time, what has been on repeat, and ask, plainly, is that true? Is that what I want worn into me until it becomes a reflex I mistake for my own soul? And if it is not, you can, gently and deliberately, give the loop something better to chew on. Put a truer song within reach. Play it on purpose until it starts playing itself.

> You are not what you believe on your best day. You are what you hum without thinking.

The man at the sink will hum again tomorrow. So will you. The only question, the one almost no one asks, is whether the song doing the teaching is one you would have chosen, had you known it was teaching at all.

Now you know.

So the next time a melody surfaces on its own and you catch yourself following along to words you never chose, stop for just a second and actually listen. Not to the tune. To the claim underneath it. Ask what it has been quietly rehearsing into you.

And if you don't like the answer, you are not stuck with it. Choose the next loop on purpose. Hand your incorrigible, faithful, repeating heart something worth repeating, and let it disciple you toward the truth for a change, the way it was always going to disciple you toward something.

It was never going to stay quiet. It was only ever going to teach.

Make it teach you something true.
