Where I come from, a name is never only a label. It is a destiny folded into a few syllables, a prayer the parents pray every time they call the child in from the street. We name our sons for mercy, for light, for the beauty we hope their lives will carry. So I did not make this album about a word. I made it about a Name, the one Name that carries more destiny, more mercy, and more power than every other name that has ever been spoken. The Name is **Yeshua**, Yasu', Jesus, and this whole record is built on it the way a house is built on rock.

*The Name Above Every Name* takes its title and its foundation from one sentence in the letter to the Philippians: because He humbled Himself to death, God has highly exalted Him and given Him the Name that is above every name, that at the Name of Jesus every knee should bow and every tongue confess that He is Lord (Philippians 2:9-11). That is not a slogan. That is the architecture of the universe. And I wanted to sing it in a way my people could feel in their chest.

## Why a Name and not a lecture

I could have written an album of arguments. Instead I wrote an album that mostly repeats one Name, on purpose, because that is what the heart actually does when it finally finds what it was looking for.

The opening song lifts the Name like the frankincense of the morning: *Abba lifted Him to the highest of the dawn, gave Him the Name that the morning is sung upon, higher than the cedars, higher than the gold of the souk, the Name that the whole of my heart was made to look for.* That last line is the confession the whole record is built around. I did not decide to want this Name. I discovered that I had been made wanting it, that the searching in me, the reaching that no success or pleasure ever satisfied, had a shape, and the shape was Name-shaped. Every restless heart is looking for a Name it cannot yet pronounce.

That is why the second song simply chants it, *Yasu', Yasu', Yasu'*, as the refrain of a heart at rest. In my culture we know the power of repetition in devotion, the way a beloved name said again and again is not empty but full. This record leans into that. It says the Name until the Name becomes home.

## The Name that does what no argument can

The heart of the album is a series of songs about what this Name actually does, because a name in Scripture is never decoration. It is power at work.

"The Name That Saves" stands on the boldest claim the early believers ever made: there is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved (Acts 4:12). The chorus is almost nothing but that truth, repeated like a heartbeat, *the Name that saves, the Name that saves, Yasu', the Name that saves.* "The Name That Heals" remembers how the first apostles prayed for healing to be done through the name of the holy servant Jesus (Acts 4:30). "The Name That Calms the Storm" puts us in the boat on the night the wind was tearing at the sail, and the Man asleep in the stern stood and spoke, and the sea went flat as glass (Mark 4:39). "The Name of Unshaken Power" remembers that on the cross He disarmed the powers, making a public spectacle of them (Colossians 2:15).

> The Name that saves, the Name that saves, Habibi, the Name that saves.

I want to be careful and honest here, because this matters: I am not claiming a name is a magic word to be waved over trouble. I am confessing that a Person stands behind this Name, and that when His people call on Him, the Person Himself answers. The power was never in the sound. It is in the One who owns the sound.

The old songs of the church reached for this too. They called Him the balm that heals the wounded, the peace that stills the raging sea. Those are the pictures of devotion, and they are true to the Person, even when they are the language of hymns rather than the words of a single verse. I hold them the same way: as the heart's own way of confessing what the Scriptures declare, that this Name carries a Person, and the Person carries all the power there is.

## Every knee, and every tongue

The record climbs to a single peak, a song called "At the Name of Yasu', Every Knee," and I built the whole album's arc to arrive there.

Because the promise in Philippians is total. Not some knees. Every knee, in heaven, on earth, and under the earth. Not some tongues. Every tongue. And the very next song, "The Name In Every Tongue," celebrates exactly that, the Name confessed in every language of the earth, which for me is not an abstraction but the burning center of my whole calling. I sing so that the Arab tongue, the Turkish tongue, the tongues of the whole Middle East and its scattered children, would have their own confession of this Name, in their own maqam, not borrowed from another country.

There is a day coming when the confession will be universal. But the invitation is now. That is the difference between the two, and the whole reason I sing.

## For the searching heart

The album ends the way it began, with a benediction that circles its melody back to the first song, so the Name does not stop but keeps rising, because the praise of this Name has no last day.

I made this for the one whose heart has been looking its whole life and could never say for what. You have chased the beauty of the souk, the respect of the village, the love that was supposed to fill you, and something in you kept reaching past all of it. That reaching is not a defect. It is a compass. It was pointing, the entire time, at a Name, the Name lifted higher than the cedars and the gold, the Name that heals and saves and calms the storm, the Name your whole heart was made to look for.

Say it slowly. **Yeshua.** Yasu'. The Person behind it has been waiting to answer to it longer than you have been alive. There is no other name like it under heaven, and there never will be. Lift it, and find that the reaching in you finally has somewhere to arrive.
