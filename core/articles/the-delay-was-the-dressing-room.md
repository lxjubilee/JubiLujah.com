I put the return of the King on a steelpan and a drill beat, and I would do it again. *Any Day Now* is a record about the second coming, and I refused to make it sound like a warning siren. Because the church has handed my generation the blessed hope wrapped in dread, and I wanted to hand it back the way the New Testament actually gives it: as the single best piece of news a frightened world will ever hear.

He's coming back. And when I finally understood what that meant for all the waiting I had been calling wasted, it undid me. So I made twelve songs to say it. The delay you have been resenting was never a punishment. It was the dressing room.

## Any Day Now, Hope, Not a Countdown to Fear

The whole album lives in three words: *any day now.* That is not date-setting, and I was careful to say so right in the songs, *I don't need a date, I just keep my lamp aglow; Father, You set the hour.* **Yeshua** was plain that the timing is not ours to calculate: *"Watch therefore: for ye know not what hour your Lord doth come"* (Matthew 24:42).

But listen to how He framed the not-knowing. Not *be terrified,* but *watch*, the way you watch for someone you love to come up the walk. Paul called it *"that blessed hope, and the glorious appearing of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ"* (Titus 2:13). The *blessed* hope. And when **Yeshua** left, the two men in white did not tell the disciples to brace for doom; they told them *"this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go"* (Acts 1:11). Same Jesus. Same face. Coming back.

That is why the opener is a dance and not a dirge. *Any day now, You're coming back*, eyes on the seam where the gold breaks the ground, lacing my shoes for glory before the morning even yawns. He told me to watch. So I'm dancing through the night.

## The Waiting Was the Wedding

The turn that broke the record open, the song I put in the frontmatter, is *"The Waiting Was the Wedding."*

Picture it: the dress in the garment bag on the door, the ring turned and turned on a nervous finger, the calendar crossed out in red, and a heart that had started to believe the long wait was a punishment for every night it prayed. That was me. And then the Spirit whispered something that flipped the whole thing over, *every day of longing was a getting-ready-for-You.*

That is the theology under the whole album, and it comes straight from the wedding at the center of the Bible's last chapters: *"Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb"* (Revelation 19:9). If the end of the story is a wedding, then the waiting was never dead time. It was bridal preparation. The tears were the fitting. The longing was the veil being sewn.

> I was counting the days as empty. He was calling them a feast. Not one day I waited fell forgotten to the floor.

That is why I titled this the way I did. The delay was the dressing room. And **Yeshua** told us plainly why He was gone so long: *"I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself"* (John 14:2–3). He is not late. He is *preparing.* And so, it turns out, are we.

## Trim the Lamp, Lace Your Shoes, Watch the Sky

Ready is a posture, not a panic, and the middle of the record teaches it three ways.

*"Trim the Lamp"* is the ten virgins of Matthew 25. Five kept oil and five leaned on the sand, and at the midnight cry only the burning lamps went in. So the song is not anxious; it is *tending*, feeding the flame with worship till the morning breaks in white, because **Yeshua** said *"Watch therefore, for ye know neither the day nor the hour"* (Matthew 25:13). *"Lace Up Your Shoes"* is the get-up song for everybody stuck in *someday, maybe, soon*, double-knot them tight, the Bridegroom is on the doorstep. And *"Watch the Sky"* is for the whole scrolling generation with their faces to the floor: *me, I'm looking up, where the angels made it plain, same way that He left is the same way He'll reign.*

But readiness is not only trumpets and rooftops. The tenderest track, *"Every Ordinary Morning,"* is where the record does its quietest work. Soap bubbles catching gold on the window, the 7:42 train door, a toddler's lost sock under the chair, and the confession that if He came right now, He'd find me *right there.* The mother nobody sees, folding little laundry like she's folding it for Him. He does not measure by the loud; He measures by the true. So the way you get ready for the King is not to quit your Tuesday. It is to be *faithful in the small* until He comes, a citizen already, because *"our conversation is in heaven; from whence also we look for the Saviour"* (Philippians 3:20).

## Who I Made This Record For

I made it for the ones who were taught to be scared of His coming, who flinch at the word *rapture* because somebody used it to frighten them as a child. For the tired saint who is sure the wait has been wasted. For the generation staring down at cracked screens, drowning in headlines, who has never once been told that the best news in the universe is that this is not the end of the story.

Because the record does not stay in the waiting room. *"Trumpet on the Downbeat"* erupts on the promise that undoes death itself: *"In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump... the dead shall be raised incorruptible"* (1 Corinthians 15:52), when *"the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout... and the dead in Christ shall rise first"* and the living are caught up to meet Him (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17). Then *"Even So, Come"* becomes the whole church's final prayer, one candle passed hand to hand until the room is on fire, *"The Spirit and the bride say, Come... Even so, come, Lord Jesus"* (Revelation 22:17, 20). And the finale, *"The King Returns,"* opens the sky like a sheet of golden paper and seats us at a banquet table running further than the eye can see, every tear I cried turned to a diamond in the atmosphere.

So lift your head, beloved. The One who promised *"Surely I come quickly"* has never broken a word. The dread was never yours to carry. If you are still waiting, for the promise, for the healing, for the day it all makes sense, hear the good news in the groove: the waiting is not wasted, the delay is the dressing room, and the King is coming for a bride He has spent the whole long season getting ready. Keep your lamp burning. Watch the road with joy. He could come any day now, and when He does, may He find you dancing.
