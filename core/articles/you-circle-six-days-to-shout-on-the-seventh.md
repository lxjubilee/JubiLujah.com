Put a go-go pocket under it, congas, cowbell, the rototom, the pocket bass walking deep on the sixteenth-note ghost notes, the Hammond spinning on a fast Leslie, the horns answering every line, and then hand the oldest deliverance story in the Bible back to the people it was written for. That is what I made *Go-Go Praise Break* to do.

Because Jericho was never a story about engineering. There was no battering ram. No siege tower. No ladder against the wall. The most fortified city in the land came down to a *sound*, and I wanted the whole DMV to feel that in their feet before they ever argued it in their heads.

## The Wall Did Not Fall on Day One

Here is the part we skip when we want the miracle without the marching. **Yahuah** told Joshua to take the whole army around the city once a day, in silence, for six days. Six days of walking a perimeter and watching nothing happen. Trumpets in the front, the ark on the shoulders of the men, and not one brick moving.

*"And it came to pass on the seventh day, that they rose early about the dawning of the day, and compassed the city after the same manner seven times... And the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat"* (Joshua 6:15, 20).

I wrote a whole song just for the waiting, *"Seven Days Around the Wall"*, and I built the DMV day-count right into the bridge, because that is the part that breaks people. *Day six in your waiting, child, the answer's almost here.* Don't quit on day six. The silence of the first six days is not **Yahuah** ignoring you. It is the discipline that makes the seventh-day shout mean something. Most folks abandon the march one lap short of the wall.

And notice the marching order Israel kept. The trumpet went in the *front*, the ark of the covenant on the shoulders of the men, the presence of God carried right into the perimeter of a city that had not budged. They were not circling a wall. They were carrying the presence around it, six days running, trusting a promise they could not yet see keeping. That is the real work of the silent days, not proving your patience, but keeping His presence in the center of the march while nothing visible changes.

## The Shout Is a Weapon, Not a Mood

Now hear me carefully, because the culture has trained us to think a shout is just excitement. It is not. At Jericho the shout was the *weapon*. It was the thing **Yahuah** chose, on purpose, to bring down what no army could.

So on *"When We Shout, The Walls Fall Down"* I take the seventh-day shout and I aim it at your living room. *What you been staring at for thirteen years can fall tonight.* The name of **Yeshua** carries more authority than any obstacle's might. That is not hype. That is the standing pattern of a God who prefers to topple the impossible with a sound His children make in faith.

> Concrete crumbling all around, the same God who walked into Joshua's story walks into a praise break, and the walls have never learned to tell the difference.

And then Act Two of the record turns the shout loose on the real walls. *"Shout the Anxiety Out"* stands on Philippians 4:6, *be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God*, and answers the 3 AM phone call from worry with an eviction notice. *"Shout the Addiction Out"* takes the twelve-step circle of folding chairs and puts **Yeshua** in the middle of the room, on the word He gave in John 8:36: *if the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.* *"Shout the Old Story Down"* hands the abuse survivor a new page, because *if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature; old things are passed away* (2 Corinthians 5:17). Every wall in the deliverance act falls to the same weapon that dropped Jericho.

## Why I Took It to the Parking Lot

The center of gravity on this album is a track called *"Holy Ghost Block Party."* Watch Night, hour seven, and the Spirit says: *take it out, the parking lot needs the light.* The deacon runs a cable from the truck to the bass. Somebody rolls the Hammond out the storefront's side door. The neighborhood comes out in their pajamas under the streetlight, and the brother who spent years too afraid of all the tears to come inside gets touched on the shoulder on the asphalt, and the chains break in his own words.

That is the whole reason for the go-go. Go-go is DMV street music, call-and-response, the congregation *is* the band, the pocket never stops. And that is exactly what a Holy Ghost people are. So on *"Where My Holy Ghost People At?"* I stop being the caller and I turn the microphone around: I have called for six straight tracks, now the sanctuary answers back. The deliverance was never meant to stay on the platform. Once you have shouted a wall down, *you* are the deliverance team.

## Who I Made the Shout For

I made it for the one still marching on day six, convinced the wall is permanent because it did not move on the first lap. For the one who has been staring at the same obstacle for thirteen years. For the survivor carrying an old story in somebody else's voice. For the brother on the corner who has watched the church for years and is sure the tears would swallow him if he stepped inside.

And I made it because the shout does not stop at the wall. Listen to how the record ends. *"The Shout Is Our Address"* claims the whole identity, the world wants a denomination and a street number, and we tell them we are *from the praise break, where the chains come undone.* Then *"The Shout Don't Stop"* lifts the eyes all the way up, because Revelation 7:9 shows a great multitude *which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues* standing before the throne with a shout that has no end. Jericho was just the opening lap. The same shout that dropped the wall is already rising around the throne, and it is on the map from here to eternity.

So circle your six days, beloved. Keep marching when nothing is moving. Hold the silence without quitting. And when the seventh dawn comes and the trumpet finally sounds, open your mouth with everything in you. The wall in front of you was built by men and reinforced by years, but it has never once survived the sound of a people who believe **Yahuah**. Shout, and deliver. The wall is closer to falling than it has ever been.
