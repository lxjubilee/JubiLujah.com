I once spent an entire recording session on one word.

Not one song. One word. Twelve tracks, an oud, a darbuka, a room full of singers from four countries, and the whole apparatus was built to carry a single Hebrew syllable across the finish line: **echad**. One. I know how that sounds. Who writes an album to say a word a toddler could learn in an afternoon? But I had been teaching for years, and I kept running into the same quiet emergency: people who could sing loud, dense, wonderful worship, and had somehow never let the smallest true thing land on them. That the God they were praising is not one god among a shelf of options. He is One. And that changes the size of a life.

So I made *Echad, the Name Is One* to say the word properly, from the top of my lungs and from the edge of a pillow.

## The word that is also a confession

The album opens where every faithful Hebrew day has opened for thousands of years, with the Shema. "Hear, O Israel: **Yahuah** our God, **Yahuah** is one" (Deuteronomy 6:4). The first track is called *Shema Yisrael*, and it does the thing I wanted the whole record to do: it starts as an unaccompanied voice, then the drum enters, and suddenly a confession becomes a body moving. Because that is what the Shema actually is. It is not a doctrine you file away. It is a summons you answer with your feet.

Here is the part I never want a listener to miss. In Hebrew, *shema* does not only mean "hear." It means hear-and-do, to listen in a way that reorganizes your life around what you heard. So the very first word of the confession is already asking for everything. And the next verse tells you how much everything is: "You shall love **Yahuah** your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your strength" (Deuteronomy 6:5). Track three carries that line, *All My Heart, All My Soul, All My Strength*, and I wrote it as a checklist you can't get out of. Heart, soul, strength. Nothing held back. The One asks for the whole.

When **Yeshua** was asked which command was greatest, He did not invent a new one. He reached back and quoted the Shema, "Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one" (Mark 12:29). The Messiah's answer to the biggest question was the oldest sentence His people knew. That is not a small thing to build an album on.

## Why "One" is bigger than a number

There's a track in the middle I fought hardest for. It's called *Compound Unity*, and it's the teaching heart of the record. Because the moment you sing "the Name is One," an honest person asks: then what do we do with Father, Son, and Spirit?

Here is where I have to be a careful teacher and not a sloppy one. The Hebrew *echad* is a rich word. When Genesis says a man and his wife become "one flesh," the word is *echad* (Genesis 2:24). It is a word that can hold a togetherness inside its oneness. Now. I want to be plain: the Bible does not hand us a tidy sentence that says "echad proves the Trinity." That reading is a teaching tradition, and I'll name it as one. But I'll also tell you what Scripture shows me directly. At the Jordan, when **Yeshua** came up from the water, the Father spoke, the Spirit descended like a dove, and the Son stood dripping in the river (Matthew 3:16-17). Three, present, in one scene, and no crack in the oneness. So the song does not lecture. It simply sings:

> The mystery is not a math, the mystery is a path.

I am not asking you to solve the One. I am asking you to love Him.

## The shout and the whisper

If the album has a secret, it's the eighth track, *Echad, Hear Me Whisper His Name*. I built the whole record to arrive there.

Because I had watched something over the years that undid me. The Shema is a shout when a congregation stands together. I've heard rooms roar it. But there is an old, tender Hebrew custom of making the Shema the *last* words a person speaks before sleep, and the last words breathed at the end of a life. That's tradition, not commandment, but it is a holy tradition, and it taught me something the loud rooms never could. I have sat beside people at the end and sung *echad* slowly while a hand went still. I have watched a man who spent his whole life chasing a dozen different names finally weep over the discovery that there is only One.

So the song strips down to a single held note, a voice whispering the Name that holds the world, and then it rises back into full sound. Shout and whisper, the same word. That is the whole Christian life in one track. The God who is loud enough to shake a festival is close enough to be your last breath.

## The Name on the doorpost, the Name on the children

The Shema doesn't stay in the sanctuary. Deuteronomy 6 tells the household to bind these words on the hand, set them between the eyes, write them on the doorpost, and teach them to the children when you sit at home and walk by the way (Deuteronomy 6:7-9). Two tracks live right there in the kitchen and the hallway, *Mezuzah on the Doorpost, Mezuzah on the Heart* and *Teach the Children His Name*.

I put those songs on the record on purpose. Because a confession that only works in a crowd is not yet yours. The Name has to make it onto your doorframe and into your bedtime and into the small ears at your table, or it stays a performance. The album closes with a lullaby, *Bedtime Shema*, and then a benediction, *Adonai Echad*, that carries the Name back out the door into an ordinary Tuesday. The first track and the last track are the same confession, the bookends of a whole life. You open your eyes with the Name and you close them with the Name, and the day in between is just the space you get to love Him in.

## Who I made this for

I made *Echad* for the person who is tired of a faith that feels like a menu. Who suspects, correctly, that being pulled in twelve directions is not the same as being whole. The Shema is the cure for a divided life, because a God who is One asks for a heart that is one, undivided, unhedged, unashamed.

And I made it for the person at the bedside. The one holding a hand that is going still, reaching for words holy enough for the moment and finding only one. There is one. It is the smallest true thing and the largest. **Yahuah** is one, and **Yeshua** is His salvation, and the Name is close when the room is quiet.

So yes. I wrote a whole album to say one word. I'd do it again tomorrow. Some words are worth a lifetime of saying, and this one you can carry all the way home.
