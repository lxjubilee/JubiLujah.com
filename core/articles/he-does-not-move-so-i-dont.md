There is a sentence God pressed into the middle of my chest a long time ago, and I have swallowed it so many times I nearly forgot it was there. Every meeting, I rehearsed it and said nothing. Every hallway, I revised it down to something safer. And I told myself that swallowing the word was humility, when the honest name for it was fear. **Courage and Conviction** is the album I made when I finally understood that a called woman holding her silence is simply a woman not yet heard, and that the word in my bones was never mine to keep down.

## The fire that will not stay swallowed

The record opens with a song called "Speak Your Word in Me," and it does not begin with boldness. It begins with a confession: *Jesus, You put a sentence in the middle of my chest, and I have swallowed it so often I had forgotten it was pressed.* Anyone who has ever been given something true to say and then talked themselves out of saying it knows exactly what that feels like.

The prophet Jeremiah knew it best of all. He tried to quit, decided he would not speak in the Lord's name again, and found he could not: *"there is in my heart as it were a burning fire shut up in my bones, and I am weary with holding it in, and I cannot"* (Jeremiah 20:9). That is the engine of this whole album. Conviction is not, first of all, a personality trait. It is a fire. And a fire that is fed and covered does not go out; it just burns the one holding it in. The courage the record is after is not the courage to manufacture something bold. It is the courage to stop suffocating what **Yahuah** already lit. As the opening song prays it: *the mouth is mine; the message, Lord, is the message You made.*

> A called woman holding silence is a woman not yet heard. Jesus, You are the Word made flesh, come and loose the word. I am only a vessel for the voice of the only Lord I follow.

## Courage is not the absence of fear. It is a different foundation

Now let me teach, because there is a lie about courage that keeps timid believers timid: the idea that brave people simply do not feel afraid. That is not the Bible's picture at all. Scripture never tells the fearful to feel differently; it tells them *whose they are* and *who goes with them.*

When the Lord commissioned Joshua to lead a nation into everything that could go wrong, He did not say *feel confident*. He said, *"Be strong and courageous. Do not be frightened, and do not be dismayed, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go"* (Joshua 1:9). The command to courage is welded to a promise of Presence. And Paul says the same to a young, frightened pastor: *"God gave us a spirit not of fear but of power and love and self-control"* (2 Timothy 1:7). Power *and* love *and* a sound mind, which is why the courage on this album is never reckless or harsh. The chorus of the opening song asks for a word that is *"clear and kind, undiluted, unhedged, unafraid."* Kindness and clarity are not opposites. The bravest thing is usually also the most loving thing said plainly.

Look at how the first believers were described. When the council saw *"the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were uneducated, common men, they were astonished. And they recognized that they had been with Jesus"* (Acts 4:13). Their credential was not training or temperament. It was proximity. They had been *with Him*, and the boldness came off Him onto them. Courage, in the kingdom, is contagious. You catch it by staying close.

## You do not move because the Rock does not move

The hit single at the heart of this record is "You, Lord, Do Not Move," and it holds the whole thesis in one line: *You, Lord, do not move, so I don't. Christ the Rock beneath me, and the Rock is where I stand.* I want to be exact about why that order matters, because we get it backwards constantly. We try to summon steadiness *from ourselves*, grit our teeth, stiffen our spines, resolve to be unshakable. And we shake anyway, because a person standing on their own resolve is standing on sand.

The steadiness the song describes is borrowed. *"No one can lay a foundation other than that which is laid, which is Jesus Christ"* (1 Corinthians 3:11). **Yeshua** told us the wise builder digs down and builds on rock, *"and the rain fell, and the floods came... but it did not fall, because it had been founded on the rock"* (Matthew 7:24-25). The house does not hold because it is a strong house. It holds because of what is under it. So when the weather comes and the letter comes and the room goes silent and the phone goes cold, the very costs the song names. You are not asked to be immovable by willpower. You are asked to stand on the One who is immovable, and let His steadiness become yours. Hebrews calls that hope *"a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul"* (Hebrews 6:19). An anchored ship still feels the storm. It just does not drift.

And there is a cost, which this album refuses to hide. The song's bridge weighs it out loud, *if it costs me platform, title, friendship.* Conviction that has never cost anything has never really been tested. The world will always offer the softer door: *you could be a little softer, and they'd let you through.* But Paul drew the line where it has to be drawn: *"Am I now seeking the approval of man, or of God? ... If I were still trying to please man, I would not be a servant of Christ"* (Galatians 1:10). You cannot serve two audiences. At some point every faithful believer has to choose whose face she is living toward.

## Unashamed, and building the next brave one

The song that lands hardest for me is "Unashamed of Jesus," because shame is the quiet thing that keeps more good people silent than outright fear ever does. *A daughter does not apologize for the One who set her free.* That is Romans 1:16 with dirt under its nails: *"I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes."* Not ashamed of the message, the call, the Cross, or the blood-bought claim, the shame, the song says, goes in the grave where it belongs.

But courage was never meant to end with the one brave person, and neither does the album. It closes with "Teaching Them Jesus" and "Strong in the Lord Forever," because the whole point of finding your voice is to help the next frightened believer find hers. The armor Paul describes, *"be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might... put on the whole armor of God... that you may be able to stand"* (Ephesians 6:10-13), is issued to a whole army standing shoulder to shoulder, not a lone hero. And at the end of it all, the goal is Paul's own last words: *"I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith"* (2 Timothy 4:7). Kept it. Handed it on. Still standing.

## Who this is for

This is for the believer with a true thing to say who has swallowed it one too many times. It is for the one being told she would go further if she were softer about **Yeshua**, sister, the Door you are standing for is the only Door worth losing the other doors over. It is for the tired, the outnumbered, the ones who feel the fire in their bones and are weary of holding it in.

Hear the whole record in one sentence: you do not have to generate your own steadiness. He does not move, so, standing on Him, neither do you. Let the fire out. Say it clear and kind and unafraid. The first time always shakes. But He is not shaken, and He is what you are standing on.
