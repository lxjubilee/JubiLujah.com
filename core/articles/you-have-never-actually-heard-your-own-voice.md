Almost everyone has had the small, specific horror of hearing themselves played back.

A voicemail you didn't know was recording. A video someone took at a birthday. A voice memo you meant to delete. And there it is, your voice, coming out of the speaker, and it is *wrong.* Too high, or too thin, or too nasal, some quality you do not recognize and immediately dislike. *That's not what I sound like,* you think, with a flush of embarrassment. *Do I really sound like that?*

Yes. You do. That is exactly what you sound like, to everyone. To your mother, your best friend, the person you married, the strangers on the bus. That cringe-inducing recording is the true one. The voice you have loved and lived inside your entire life, the one in your head, the one you think of as *yours*, that is the one no one else has ever heard, and never will.

I find this quietly astonishing, and the older I get the more I think it is a mercy dressed as an embarrassment.

## Two roads into one ear

Here is why the recording sounds like a stranger.

When you speak, the sound reaches your own ears by two completely different roads at once. Some of it travels the ordinary way, out of your mouth, into the air, around and back into your ear, exactly the path everyone else's version of your voice takes. But a great deal of it never goes into the air at all. It travels *through you*, through the bone of your jaw and your skull, conducted directly to the inner ear as a trembling in the very structure of your head. And bone carries sound differently than air does. It favors the low, warm, resonant frequencies. It adds a richness, a depth, a fullness that is not in the sound anyone else receives.

So the voice you hear when you speak is a blend of two things: the air version everyone hears, *plus* a private, bone-deep, low-end fullness that is for you alone and can never leave your own head. Strip that hidden layer away, record just the part that made it into the air, and what is left sounds thinner and higher and stranger than the voice you know, because you have never once heard your voice without the secret warmth your own skull was adding to it.

Which leads to a conclusion I cannot stop turning over. Of every single person on earth, *you* are the only one who has never heard your real voice. Everyone else knows it. You are the one it is hidden from. You are, by the plain architecture of your own head, the least reliable judge of your own sound in all the world.

## The eye that cannot see itself

I want to sit with that, because we build so much of our lives on the opposite assumption.

We assume we are the experts on ourselves. We assume the voice in our own head, the running self-assessment, the verdict we have reached about our own worth, our own gifts, our own likeability, is the accurate one, the inside track, the true report that outsiders only glimpse. And so we walk around trusting our own most private estimate of ourselves as if it were the last word.

But the body is telling on us. The one voice you can never hear correctly is your own. The one face you can never see except backwards in a mirror or frozen in a photo is your own. You are, structurally, the person with the worst seat in the house for the show that is *you.* And I think that goes far deeper than acoustics, because the harshest, most confident, most authoritative judge most of us ever listen to is the voice in our own head passing sentence on ourselves, and that judge is sitting in the exact seat that cannot hear the true sound.

The person who refuses to sing because they *hate their voice* is trusting a judgment made from the one position that cannot hear it. The person convinced they are unlovable, or useless, or too far gone, has reached that verdict from inside the skull that distorts everything before it arrives. We are so sure our self-assessment is the accurate one. It is the one measurement we are structurally least equipped to take.

## The Lord looks at the heart

The Scriptures say this in a way I did not connect to any of it until recently.

There is a moment when a prophet named Samuel is sent to find a new king among a father's sons, and one by one the impressive ones are paraded before him, tall, strong, the obvious choices. And each time Samuel is sure he has found the one, God stops him. Do not look at his appearance or his height, He says, for I have refused him. And then the line that has outlived three thousand years: *man looks at the outward appearance, but Yahuah looks at the heart.* The one they finally send for is the youngest, still out with the sheep, the one no one in the room thought worth calling in, and he is the one God had chosen all along.

Hear what that is actually saying. There is a way you are seen and heard by others, and it is partial. There is a way you see and hear *yourself,* and it is distorted, filtered through a skull that adds and subtracts before you ever get the sound. And then there is the way *God* perceives you, which is the only undistorted hearing in the universe. He is not fooled by the air version the way your neighbors are. He is not fooled by the bone-deep private version the way *you* are. He hears the actual sound, the true voice, the true worth, the real self underneath every distortion your own perception laid over it. He looks past the outward, and past your own crooked report of your inward, straight to the heart.

## Sing anyway

So here is what I want to leave with you, tenderly, especially if you have gone silent because of a verdict you passed on yourself from the one seat that cannot hear.

You may have decided your voice is not worth lifting, that it is too thin, too broken, too unlovely to add to the song. I want you to notice *where that judgment was made.* It was made from inside the one head that has never once heard your voice as it truly is. It is the least trustworthy assessment available anywhere, delivered with total confidence by the one witness structurally guaranteed to be wrong. You are not the authority on your own sound. You never were. The body made sure of it.

The voice that makes you cringe on a recording may be the very voice that steadies someone else in a room. The self you have quietly written off may be the one God looked past every taller, stronger option to choose. Do not let the distorted judge have the last word. Sing the true thing anyway, out an open window, into an ordinary morning, badly if you must, and let the One who hears you without distortion do what He has always done with voices the singer thought were nothing.

You have never actually heard your own voice. But He has. And He is not embarrassed by it. He made it, He hears it true, and He has been waiting for you to stop trusting the cringe and start trusting the One who has loved the sound of you from the beginning.
