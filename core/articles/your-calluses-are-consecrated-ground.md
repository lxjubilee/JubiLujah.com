The heartbeat of this record is a hammer.

Not a kick drum dressed up to sound like one, a real hammer on a real downbeat, an anvil, a mill bell, the percussion of people who make their living with their hands. I built *Working Hands, Faithful Heart* on that rhythm because I got tired of a church that treats the calloused as a lower kind of saint.

## Why I had to make it

Somewhere we got the idea that there are holy jobs and ordinary jobs. That the man behind the pulpit is doing the Lord's work and the man behind the plow is just paying his bills until Sunday. I have stood in too many rooms where the farm worker, the miner, the millhand, the woman raising kids on two jobs, all got the quiet message that their real life happens elsewhere, that the sacred is somewhere they visit, not something their hands already touch.

That is a lie, and it is old, and I wanted to burn it down with a record.

Because the very first thing **Yahuah** did with the man He made was give him work. Genesis chapter two. He put him in the garden to dress it and to keep it, before the fall, before sin, in the middle of paradise. Labor was not the curse. Labor was the assignment, handed out in Eden while everything was still very good. The sweat got hard later. The dignity was there from the beginning.

## What the title means

*Working Hands, Faithful Heart.* I put those two together because the world keeps trying to pull them apart.

It will tell you the hands are for the marketplace and the heart is for the sanctuary. But the second track on the record says the thing I most believe: *Working Hands Are Holy.* Not holy someday, when you finally get into ministry. Holy now, in the field, at the rig, on the line. The opener, *These Hands Know the Way*, traces it back through a family, my father's hands, my mother's hands, all of them knowing the work, all of them knowing God through it, because faith in my country got handed down calloused palm to calloused palm.

Moses prayed it in Psalm ninety: "establish thou the work of our hands upon us; yea, the work of our hands establish thou it." He asked twice in one breath. He was not asking **Yahuah** to bless his church service. He was asking Him to make the daily labor count for something that lasts. And think about who prayed it, a man who had spent forty years on the backside of the desert keeping another man's sheep before God ever spoke to him from the bush. He knew what it was to labor in obscurity, to work hard at something nobody was watching. When he finally asked heaven to make the work of his hands stand, he was asking for every calloused, unnoticed year of it.

> The question was never whether your work is holy. The question is whether you will believe Him when He says it already is.

## The heart under the songs

This record has a lot of faces on it, and I put them there on purpose.

There is *Farm Worker's Prayer* and *The Migrant's Song*, because Scripture is not shy about the ones who stoop in other people's fields. *Coal Dust and Gospel*, for my own mountains, where men went down into the dark and came up singing. *Single Mother's Blessing*, because raising children alone is some of the hardest and most invisible labor there is, and heaven sees every hour of it. *The Mill Bell Rings* and *Hands That Build*, for the ones who make the things the rest of us use without a second thought.

And then there is *Wage Theft and Justice*, and I will not apologize for it. James chapter five says the wages of the laborers, kept back by fraud, cry out, and the cries have reached the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth. That is not my opinion. That is the Word raising its voice on behalf of the underpaid. A record about the dignity of work that stayed silent about the theft of a paycheck would be a coward's record. So I named it, and I set it to the same hammer as the rest, because justice for the worker is not a distraction from worship. It is the shape love takes when it has skin in the field.

The whole thing rests on one verse more than any other. Colossians chapter three: "whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men." Paul wrote that, remarkably, to slaves, to people whose labor was stolen from them by definition, and he told them their work could be aimed straight past their masters at **Yeshua** Himself. If the most exploited workers in the ancient world could hand their labor up to the Lord and have Him receive it as worship, then so can you, at whatever bench you are standing at tomorrow morning.

## Who I made this for

I made *Working Hands, Faithful Heart* for the ones the church forgot to disciple because they showed up tired.

For the man who comes home with dirt he cannot fully scrub out and wonders if God notices a life spent that way. He does. *Faithful Hands Forever* closes the album for exactly him. For the woman who has never once felt like the "spiritual" type because her days are spent doing and lifting and cleaning and providing, and who needs to hear that her doing is her devotion. For every saint who suspected the real Christians were the ones who got to sit in a study all day, and quietly counted themselves out.

Hear me. Hebrews chapter six says **Yahuah** is not unrighteous to forget your work and the love you have shown toward His name. He is not unrighteous. He does not forget. Not the field, not the shift, not the double, not the night feeding, not the thing nobody thanked you for. He keeps a record calloused hands never see, and He keeps it faithfully.

The first man the Bible ever says was filled with the Spirit of God was not a preacher. He was a craftsman, Bezalel, filled to work gold and stone and wood for the tabernacle. The Spirit's first named skill was the skill of the hands. That has never stopped being true.

So set the hammer down for one minute and look at your own palms. The lines in them, the calluses, the wear, that is not evidence of a lesser life. That is consecrated ground. And the God who worked the world into being with a word is not the least bit embarrassed to call your labor holy.
