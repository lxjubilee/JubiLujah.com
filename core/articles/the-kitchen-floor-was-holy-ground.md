One morning I took my shoes off at the doorway of my own kitchen, and I could not have told you why. No one had asked me to. But I felt the floorboards hum under my feet, and I understood, all at once, that I had been standing on holy ground for forty years and calling it linoleum.

That is where **Homemaking Faith** came from. Because somewhere I had absorbed a lie so common we do not even notice we believe it: that the church building is the holy place, and the house is merely where I rest *from* God until Sunday brings me back into His presence. This album is my repentance from that lie, sung twelve times over. **Yeshua** never left the kitchen. I just had not been paying attention.

## Take your shoes off in the kitchen

The opening song is called "Jesus, You Are Home," and it says the thing straight out: *I used to think the church was the holy place, and the house was where I rested from You. Now I know. You never left the kitchen.* The doctrine under it is one of the oldest scenes in Scripture. When **Yahuah** met Moses, the first word out of the fire was not a command about worship. It was, *"Take your sandals off your feet, for the place on which you are standing is holy ground"* (Exodus 3:5). The ground was not holy because of the geography. It was holy because He was on it. And that changes everything about your house, because the same Presence that made a patch of desert holy is standing in your kitchen while the coffee brews.

The first believers understood this in a way we have nearly lost. They had no cathedrals, no steeples, no sanctuaries. What they had, Luke tells us, was this: they were *"breaking bread in their homes"* and *"day by day, attending the temple together and... received their food with glad and generous hearts"* (Acts 2:46). The church was a house with His table in it. Paul greets *"the church that meets in their house"* over and over (Romans 16:5). So when my song says *the first Christians had no steeples. They had Your table, they had Your loaf, they had rooms with You in them*, it is not poetry. It is the actual floor plan of the early church.

> Before I hang a single picture, before I light a single lamp, before I bake a single loaf, Jesus, let me say it out loud: You are the holy ground. You are the sanctuary I am standing in. And You built this house, not me.

## He is in the ordinary rhythms, not just the crisis

I built the middle of the record to walk a person through an ordinary day, because that is where most of a life is actually lived, and I refuse to let the ordinary be treated as the un-spiritual.

There is "Jesus in the Kitchen," because He called Himself *the Bread of Life* (John 6:35) and then handed real bread to tired people again and again. There is "The Lord Tends the Garden," because He said *"I am the vine... my Father is the vinedresser"* (John 15:1), and a woman on her knees in the dirt is closer to that parable than she knows. There is "Jesus in the Daily Rhythms," because the holiness of a home is not built in dramatic moments but in ten thousand small faithful ones. Remember Mary and Martha: Martha was *"anxious and troubled about many things,"* and **Yeshua** did not scold her for keeping a home. He gently called her to keep it *at His feet*, where *"one thing is necessary"* (Luke 10:38-42). The homemaking is holy the moment He is the center of it and not the competition for it.

And I will not pretend a home is only warmth, so the record has its hard song too. "Jesus Outlasts the Roof" sits on the hardest confession a household ever makes, Job's: *"The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD"* (Job 1:21). Roofs fail. Homes are lost. Families scatter. But Hebrews reminds us *"here we have no lasting city, but we seek the city that is to come"* (Hebrews 13:14). The house you love is a rehearsal, not the destination, which is exactly why you can love it freely and still hold it with open hands.

## Who is really building the house

Here is the turn that keeps the whole album from becoming a hymn to domesticity for its own sake. A home is not made holy by the woman who works herself to the bone keeping it. It is made holy by the Lord who dwells in it.

Psalm 127:1 is blunt about this: *"Unless the LORD builds the house, those who build it labor in vain."* I have watched good women exhaust themselves trying to *manufacture* the peace of a home by sheer effort, and it cannot be done, because peace is a Presence, not an achievement. Your body, Paul says, is already *"a temple of the Holy Spirit within you"* (1 Corinthians 6:19), so you carry the sanctuary into every room you enter. You are not building God a house from scratch each morning. You are keeping house *with* the One who already lives there.

And the door of such a home swings outward. "Jesus Sets One More Chair," I called one of the songs, because a house where **Yeshua** is truly at home cannot stay closed. He said, *"I was a stranger and you welcomed me"* (Matthew 25:35). The holiest kitchens I have ever stood in were the ones with a place always ready for one more.

## Who this is for

This is for the homemaker who suspects her real work is invisible and un-spiritual, sister, you have been ministering on holy ground the whole time. It is for the single person in a small apartment who wonders if a home even counts without a crowd in it; it does, because the Presence does not require a crowd. It is for the family that lost the house, and for the one just moving into a new one wondering how to make it a place where God dwells. You do not make Him welcome. You wake up to the fact that He is already there.

So take your shoes off in your own kitchen. The floorboards are humming. The ground you have been calling ordinary was holy the entire time, because **Yeshua** is home, and He never once left it.
