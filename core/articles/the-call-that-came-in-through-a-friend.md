There is a kind of rescue that happens through a telephone at three in the morning, and I have made a whole album about it.

*Rescue Story* is twelve songs for the person who has been drawn up out of a pit and is still standing on the shore, still shaking, still doing the math on how close it actually was. I did not write it from the high ground. I wrote it from the low ground, from the basement folding chair, the guardrail, the waiting room, the kitchen table with divorce papers on it, because those are the rooms where **Yahuah** does His most stubborn work, and I refuse to sing about rescue like a man who has never needed one.

## The whole record stands on one sentence

David wrote it, and I have never gotten over it: *He drew me up from the pit of destruction, out of the miry bog, and set my feet upon a rock, making my steps secure, and he put a new song in my mouth* (Psalm 40:1–3). Read that slowly. The man does not climb out. He is *drawn* out. The verb belongs to God. That single reversal, from "I saved myself" to "I learned the language of the saved", is the spine of the entire album, and it is the difference between religion and rescue.

So the opener, "The Call That Saved Me," is exactly what it sounds like. A voice on the line at 3 a.m., *"brother, talk to me instead"*, a bottle in one hand, a name I would not say. And the truth the song keeps circling back to: **Yeshua** came in through a phone, through a voice that was not His, and He was His the whole time. That is the miracle I most wanted people to see. God is not too proud to arrive wearing a friend's voice. He sent a man, and His Son was on the line.

## The pit has a Person already sitting in it

The album refuses to make the rescue distant or clean. On "From the Brink" it is a cold guardrail and a whispered name, and a nail-marked hand already pressed into the metal where mine had been. On "Rock Bottom Found You" it is the burned-coffee smell of a recovery room and the shock of realizing the chair you dreaded was holy ground: *what I thought was the floor was the front pew of Your love.* That song leans on the most audacious line in the Psalms, *if I make my bed in Sheol, behold, You are there* (Psalm 139:8). You cannot dig low enough to get below God. He is already at the bottom of the pit, sleeves rolled up, dirt on His hands.

Because that is the image I keep returning to on "The God Who Digs Me Out", a Rescuer who does not shout instructions from above but comes *down*, with a shovel and dirt on His sleeves. The Carpenter has soil on Him from a Garden and a Cross. He is not afraid of yours.

> I did not climb out. I was lifted out. I did not save myself. I learned the verb of the saved.

## Rescue is not always the news you wanted

I could not write an honest rescue record without the song that admits the news sometimes goes dark. "Rescue in the Waiting Room" is the album's peak, and it is set in those chairs you cannot sleep in, the styrofoam coffee left over from shift change. Sometimes the rescue was not the surgeon's report, sometimes the rescue was the company that waited with you. Whether the doors opened onto joy or onto grief, He did not stand up and leave. *Wait for the Lord; be strong, and let your heart take courage* (Psalm 27:14). The Comforter stays through both endings. That is a harder gospel than the one that promises only good outcomes, and it is the only one strong enough to hold real people.

And then the album turns outward, because rescued people are the loudest witnesses there are. "Testimony Over Trauma" is built on Revelation 12:11, *they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony*, sung in a folding-chair circle where saying it out loud is the beginning of not being held by it anymore. "My Scars Tell Your Story" takes the marks I used to hide under long sleeves in July and calls them a sermon, because **Yeshua** wears His own scars on the throne and does not hide them either. The scar is not a shame. It is the signature of the Surgeon on the skin of the saved.

## Who this album is for

It is for the one holding a number they have not let ring in years. For the person on the guardrail who needs to stay one more hour. For the father who almost gave it all away and got interrupted by grace wearing a four-year-old's pajamas at the door. For anyone who has quietly believed that grace is for the weak and is finding out that the strong need pulling out too.

I built the finale, "Still Being Rescued," to answer the very first song, because the 3 a.m. call was the door, not the destination. Salvation is not a single night you can frame and hang up. It is a Person who keeps showing up. *He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion* (Philippians 1:6). The Rescuer who drew you up the first time is not done, and His hand does not get tired, and the road does not run out.

So take the chip. Take the chair. Take the call. If you are on the line tonight and you can barely get the name out, get it out anyway, one word, almost a whisper, almost not at all. He drew me up. He will draw you up too. The story is not over, and the One who is writing it has never once let go.
