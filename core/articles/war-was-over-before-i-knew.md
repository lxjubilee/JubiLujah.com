I spent years fighting a war that had already been won, and nobody had told me the news.

That is the confession this whole album grew out of. I would wake up bracing for a battle, against the fear, against the old chain, against the dark that whispered my verdict before I even walked into the room, as if the outcome were still up for grabs. And then one morning it broke over me like the sun over a ridge: the war was over. It was finished on a cross and sealed at an empty tomb, and it had been finished long before I ever laced up for the fight. So I made the loudest record I have ever made, full arena worship-rock, a wall of electric guitar, drums like thunder, because a truth this settled deserves to be shouted off the top of a mountain.

## Why I Made a Victory Record

Most of us do not doubt that Christ is powerful. We doubt that the matter is *settled.* We live like the verdict is still pending, like our worrying is somehow load-bearing, like if we stop straining the whole thing might come apart.

I know that exhaustion from the inside. So I built *Christ Already Won* to preach the difference between striving *for* a victory and standing *in* one:

> What is finished cannot be unfinished.
> What is won cannot be unwon.
> I'm not fighting for the victory, 
> I'm standing in the victory already done.

**Yeshua** did not say "I'll try." From the cross He said *it is finished* (John 19:30). Scripture piles the proof high: through death He *destroyed the one who has the power of death* (Hebrews 2:14); He *disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame, triumphing over them* (Colossians 2:15); *the reason the Son of God appeared was to destroy the works of the devil* (1 John 3:8). The battle was over before I knew there was a war. That is not denial. That is the gospel finally read in the right tense.

## What the Title Means

*Light on the Mountain.* I am named for a mountain, Tahoma, the great snow-capped one, and I have watched a thousand sunrises catch its face pink and gold before a single climber was awake to see it.

That is the whole picture. On the opener, I sing: *long before the cameras, long before the climbers, the Maker's light was already on the mountain. Long before the dark could whisper my name, the Light of the World had already come.* Scripture says God *has delivered us from the domain of darkness and transferred us to the kingdom of his beloved Son* (Colossians 1:13), and John promises *the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it* (John 1:5). The mountain has been preaching the finished victory since before I opened my eyes. Every ridge a pulpit. Every dawn a hymn.

The high places have always been where **Yahuah** shows His glory, Sinai wrapped in fire, the mount where **Yeshua** was transfigured until His face shone like the sun, the ridges where He withdrew alone to pray. I stand in that long line when I say the light on the summit is not a metaphor. It is a witness.

## The Heart Behind the Songs

Let me take you up the trail, because the album climbs on purpose.

*Fear Has No Throne* is the song I most needed to write. For years I let fear sit at the head of my table, served him like he was the ruler, let him deliver the verdict before I walked into any room. And then the Lord flipped the chair. The verse I built it on is Paul's word to a frightened young pastor: *God gave us a spirit not of fear but of power and love and self-control* (2 Timothy 1:7). Fear gets evicted. Fear gets the door. And nothing, *neither death nor life, nor angels nor rulers, nor height nor depth*, can separate me from the love that is seated on the throne (Romans 8:38-39).

*Lamp for Every Step* is the quieter climb. Up the trail in the dark, the headlamp only throws a small circle, *I cannot see the summit, I cannot see the bend; I can see the next stone where my boot will land.* The Lord does not hand you a floodlight for the whole road. He gives *a lamp to my feet and a light to my path* (Psalm 119:105), and *the path of the righteous is like the light of dawn, shining brighter and brighter until full day* (Proverbs 4:18). You do not need the whole map. You need the next lit stone.

And *Sunrise Over Tahoma* lets the mountain preach without a single human word, *the heavens declare the glory of God* (Psalm 19:1), *his invisible attributes... clearly perceived in the things that have been made* (Romans 1:20). The marmots and the eagles already know who the Maker is. The forest holds its breath until the first ray comes. Creation has been testifying to the finished glory all along.

## The Dawn After the Longest Night

I built this record with a cinematic turn at its center, because the finished victory has to be able to reach the darkest night.

*Dawn Breaks the Darkness* opens stripped bare, a single soft heartbeat in the dark, and it holds the vigil honestly: *we prayed through the long night, we sang into the dark, we did not know when the morning would come.* But it stands on the promise of Psalm 30: *weeping may tarry for the night, but joy comes with the morning* (Psalm 30:5). And then the whole band explodes into the sunrise, because the morning turned out to be a Person: *the morning was the Lord standing in our place.*

*Glory on the Ridge* lifts my eyes off my own summit to the whole earth, *the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord as the waters cover the sea* (Habakkuk 2:14), and *Light to the Nations* sends it out, because the Servant was given *as a light for the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth* (Isaiah 49:6; Acts 13:47). The light does not stay on my mountain. It runs.

## Who This Is For

This is for the worn-down believer who is exhausted from fighting a war they were never told was already won.

For the one who wakes up bracing, who lets fear render the verdict before the day even starts, who has been straining as if the outcome depended on the strength of their grip. Hear me: loosen your hands. *The Day He Found Me* is my own testimony on this record. I did not find Him; He was the Shepherd who left the ninety-nine and came into the dark of my disgrace, and *the one who comes to me I will never cast out* (John 6:37). And *Sons and Daughters of Day* is the identity the finished victory hands you: *you are all children of light, children of the day; we are not of the night or of the darkness* (1 Thessalonians 5:5). The dark has no claim on you anymore.

The album ends where every climb ends, *Morning on the Summit*, the whole family gathered at the top, the air thin, the light on the snow, realizing the Lord was the One who taught every step of the trail. We climbed all night. But the war was over before we started. Come up the mountain and see the light that was already there.
