There was a December I lived behind my own curtains.

I kept them pulled through the middle of the day. I wore the long sleeves like a wall I could hide behind, counted the cracks where the light tried to slip in, and held my breath every morning like the whole day might tip over if I moved wrong. I told myself the world had closed me out. That was the story I was living in, and I believed it the way you believe weather.

Then one ordinary morning I put my hand on the window and felt the latch. It was small and cold and right under my thumb. And I understood, all at once, that the world had not locked me in from the outside. The lock was on my side of the glass. It had been the whole time. I could open it in one second, whenever I decided to. That was the moment the whole album found me. I wrote the first line almost immediately: *the latch is right here under my thumb, and my chest is so done being numb.*

## What "Hearts in Bloom" Actually Means

I did not name it that because flowers are pretty. I named it that because of the timing.

A bloom is not a decoration a plant adds to itself to look nice. A bloom is what happens when the closed thing finally decides it is safe to open, and it is almost always a little late, a little loud, a little more than the plant thought it had in it. Blooming is risk. It is the tender inside part agreeing to be seen. That is exactly what that winter asked of me, and exactly what I could not do until I noticed the latch.

So the whole record is built as one long opening. It starts with a window cracked in the first song and ends, twelve songs later, with the window thrown wide and me singing *I opened the window in track one, now I'm blooming in twelve.* I wanted the album to move the way a real heart actually thaws: not in one triumphant leap, but in a series of small, almost embarrassing permissions.

> The becoming is not a performance you finally pull off. It is a latch you finally stop guarding.

## The Album Is a Room You Get to Walk Through

The middle of the record is just a house full of the small brave seconds nobody claps for.

There is the friend who walks in at 9:14 on a night you'd already given up on, and the whole room rearranges, *friend-shaped light walked in the door, I forgot what I was sad for.* There is the morning your own body decides it is done sitting still, and the sunlight plays the drums on the kitchen floor. There is the drive home where your mom does not ask if you want coffee, she just pours it, three sugars up, because *home is who pours your coffee right before you ask.*

And there is the hardest one, the song about finally speaking the sentence you have been swallowing at the dinner table for two weeks, *say the quiet part loud.* I put a whole thesis into its bridge, because I needed to hear it myself: **brave is just a quiet thing you finally said aloud.** So much of what we call courage is not a battle. It is a whisper we stop strangling.

By the back half the record turns tender on purpose. "The Version of Me" is about the self I shut down years ago because I thought she was proof of everything I couldn't say, and the discovery that *the closed door was a doorway* somebody walked right through to reach me. Then "Mirror, We're Okay," which is the quietest song on the album and the one that says the most: two people finally at peace in a bathroom mirror at 11:54 at night.

## The Verse Hiding Under the Windowsill

Here is what I could not have told you that December, and what the album is quietly built on.

I thought the whole story was about me finding the strength to open up. And the brave second is real. You do have to reach for the latch. But underneath every one of these songs is a truth I only understood later: I did not keep myself alive through that winter. Somebody did. In "Mirror, We're Okay" the tenderest line is not a pep talk to myself. It is a discovery: *somebody's been keeping you even when you didn't know.*

That is not just a nice sentiment. Scripture says the One who watches over you does not so much as doze, the Keeper of your life never sleeps, not even in the hours you were sure you were alone in the dark. And it says something even more disarming: that we are only able to love at all because we were loved first. I did not generate my own thaw. I was being kept warm the whole time I thought I was freezing.

That is why the record refuses to end on self-congratulation. The next-to-last song, "Loud Like I Mean It," is the loudest, most confident track on the album, and its entire point is to give the credit away. *I'm not the source, I'm the song that somebody else first heard.* I wanted a bloom that knew where it came from. Scripture puts it plainly: every good and perfect gift comes down from above, from the Father of lights, the flower does not manufacture the sun. **Yahuah** is the light I finally opened the window to. I only worked the latch.

> I am not the source. I am the song. And that is the most freeing thing I know about myself.

## Who I Made This For

I made *Hearts in Bloom* for anyone living behind their own curtains right now and calling it winter.

For the one wearing long sleeves like a wall. For the person with a sentence stuck in the back of their throat and a dinner table two weeks past due. For the friend who thinks the version of themselves they shut down years ago was the shameful part, when it was actually the doorway. For everyone who has been counting their own closings and calling it realism.

I want you to know the latch is on your side. One small click really is a lot. You have been kept, all this time, by a love you did not start, and that means the opening is safe. So crack the window. Let the morning in. You were made to bloom out loud, and the One who has been keeping you is standing in the light on the other side of the glass, glad, and not going anywhere.

Open it up. I'll be right here while you do.
