We have all felt the small, uncharitable thought. The worship leader lifts a hand, the band swells, and you realize they are going to take the chorus *again*, a fourth time, a fifth, and something in you sighs. *We already sang that. We get it. Move on.* There is a whole style of complaint built around it: worship music is repetitive, it is shallow, it just says the same thing over and over, why can't it be as rich as the old hymns with their eight dense verses.

I have made that complaint myself. And then I watched it fall apart, in real time, in a real room, because of one woman I could see from where I stood.

She had come in shut. You could read it on her, arms folded, jaw set, one of those weeks. The first time through the chorus, her mouth did not move. The second time, her lips formed the words but no sound came, the way you mouth something you do not yet believe. The third time, still nothing you could hear. And then, on the fourth pass, the one I would have called excessive, the one I was tempted to resent, something in her face broke open, and she began to sing, and by the fifth time through she was singing it like it was the only true thing left in the world. If the leader had *moved on* after two, if he had been efficient, if he had respected my impatience. She would have gone home with the truth still locked outside her. It took her four times through to get in. The repetition was not for the people who got it on the first pass. It was for her. And I have been her more often than I would like to admit.

## We are not slow to be informed. We are slow to be changed.

Here is the thing the complaint gets wrong, and it is a deep thing. It assumes the purpose of singing a truth is to *inform*, to transfer the fact into your head. And by that measure, of course repetition is pointless. You understood *He is faithful* the first time. Saying it five times adds no new information. If worship were about informing you, once would do.

But worship is not trying to inform you. It is trying to *change* you. And those are entirely different jobs that run at entirely different speeds.

Information is fast. You can learn a fact in a second and never think about it again. Formation is slow, agonizingly, gloriously slow. A truth can live in your head for thirty years and never once travel the eighteen inches down into your heart where it would actually hold you up. And the road from the head to the heart is not paved by understanding harder. It is paved by *repetition.* You do not think your way from knowing God is faithful to trusting God is faithful. You get there by saying it, and saying it, and saying it, until the saying wears a groove, and the groove becomes a road, and one day the truth travels the road on its own, unbidden, at three in the morning, when you did not summon it.

That is what the chorus, sung a fifth time, is doing. It is not repeating the information. It is deepening the groove. Each pass is a spade of earth moved. The woman I watched did not need to *learn* the words on the fourth time through. She needed the words to finally break through the crust and reach the ground where belief actually grows. And that took circling the same small true thing, over and over, until it got in.

## Heaven repeats, and it is never bored

If repetition were shallow, you would expect the worship of heaven to be the richest, most varied, most intellectually dense thing imaginable, a different theological treatise sung every hour. It is not. When we are given glimpses of the worship around the throne, what do we find? The burning ones who *never cease* saying *holy, holy, holy.* Not a rotating catalog of divine attributes. One truth, said three times, and then said again, and again, without end and without exhaustion.

Sit with how strange that is by earthly standards. We assume repetition breeds boredom, that anything said too often goes stale. But that is only true of small things. Say a shallow thing a thousand times and you will hate it. Say an *infinite* thing a thousand times and you have not even begun to exhaust it, because there is always more of it than you have taken in. The reason the seraphim never tire of *holy* is that holiness is bottomless. Every repetition is not a rerun; it is a further descent into something that has no floor. Repetition is only deadening when the object is small. When the object is God, repetition is the only honest response, because you could say it forever and never say all of it.

And Scripture does not just show us this at the throne. It builds it into the songbook. There is a great psalm of thanksgiving that runs through the whole history of God's mercy, creation, rescue, provision, the long story of a people carried, and after *every single line,* the same refrain comes back: *His steadfast love endures forever.* Over and over, more than two dozen times, the same words, deliberately, relentlessly. That is not a psalmist who ran out of ideas. That is a psalmist who understood that the refrain is the point, that the mind needs to hear *His love endures* after creation, and again after rescue, and again after provision, until the phrase is worn so deep that when *your own* story turns dark, the refrain comes back on its own: *His love endures. It endures. It still endures.*

There is a caution to hold alongside this, and honesty requires it. Yeshua warned against a kind of empty repetition, heaping up words like the pagans did, thinking they would be heard for their many words, as if you could wear God down or earn His attention by sheer volume. That is real, and it is not what I am describing. The difference is the direction of the wearing. Empty repetition tries to wear down *God*, to change His mind by nagging. Faithful repetition lets the truth wear down *you*, to change your heart by grace. One is an attempt to move heaven with noise. The other is the patient labor of getting a true thing all the way inside a slow soul. Do not confuse them, and do not let the warning against the first rob you of the gift of the second.

## So the next time, do not resent the repeat

Here is what I want you to do the next time the leader takes it around again and you feel the old impatience rise.

First, remember that you are not the only person in the room. That repeat you find unnecessary may be the exact pass on which someone three rows over finally gets in. Repetition is mercy for the slow, and the church is full of the slow, and refusing to repeat for their sake because *you* already understood is a small unkindness dressed up as sophistication. Let the chorus come around again for the woman with her arms folded. She needs the fourth time. Give it to her gladly.

And second, the humbler thing, remember that the slow one is usually you. Not in the way you assume; you may grasp the words instantly. But grasping is not the same as being held. Ask yourself, on the fifth pass, whether the truth you understood on the first pass has actually reached the ground yet, or whether it is still sitting in your head where it cannot save you. And then use the repeat for what it is: another spade of earth, another turn of the groove, another chance to let *He is faithful* travel the long road down to where you will need it. Do not sing it as review. Sing it as descent.

The seraphim are not bored. The psalmist was not out of ideas. The woman was not slow to learn. They were all doing the one thing repetition is for, taking an infinite truth and going deeper into it, one more time, and one more, until it holds. Sing it again. And when it comes around once more, let it in a little further than it got last time.
