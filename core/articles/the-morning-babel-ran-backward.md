To understand the loudest morning in the book of Acts, you have to go back to the quietest catastrophe in the book of Genesis, the day God scattered the human race by taking away its shared language.

You know the story of Babel, or the shape of it. The whole earth had one language and one speech, and the people, unified and ambitious, gathered on a plain and decided to build a tower with its top in the heavens, to make a name for themselves so they would not be scattered. It was unity, but it was unity aimed in exactly the wrong direction, a coordinated reach for their own glory, a whole species pooling its strength to climb up to the place of God on their own terms. And Yahuah, seeing what a single-tongued humanity would do with its unity, came down and confused their language, so that no one could understand his neighbor, and the building stopped, and the people scattered across the face of the earth into the thousands of separate tongues that divide us to this day.

That is where the human family fractured. Not first at a border or a war, but at the level of the mouth, the moment your words stopped meaning anything to the person beside you. Every misunderstanding, every people talking past another people, every wall built of the simple inability to be understood, traces back to that plain. Babel is the wound under all our other wounds. It is the day the world stopped being able to sing together.

## The reversal that kept the languages

Now come forward, centuries and centuries, to a morning in Jerusalem when the followers of Yeshua were gathered in one place, waiting the way He had told them to wait. And suddenly there came a sound from heaven like a rushing violent wind, and it filled the whole house, and what looked like tongues of fire came to rest on each of them, and they were filled with the Spirit and began to speak in other languages as the Spirit gave them the words.

Now the city was packed that day with pilgrims, devout people from every nation under heaven, Scripture says, gathered for the festival, a crowd carrying between them dozens of native tongues from every corner of the known world. And this bewildered multitude came running toward the sound, and every single one of them heard the commotion in his own language. Parthians and Medes and Elamites, people from Mesopotamia and Egypt and the parts of Libya near Cyrene, visitors from Rome, Cretans and Arabs, each one heard these Galileans declaring the wonders of God in the tongue he was born into. They were amazed, and they said the thing that names the whole miracle: how is it that we each hear them in our own native language?

Here is what I need you to see, because it is everything. God did not reverse Babel by giving the crowd back one language. He could have. He could have snapped the thousands of tongues back into a single speech and undone the scattering the tidy way. He did not. Instead He left every language standing, Parthian stayed Parthian, Egyptian stayed Egyptian, the mother tongue of every person in that square stayed exactly what their mother had sung over them, and He filled every one of those languages with the same praise. The wonders of God, spoken at once in a hundred tongues, and every tongue saying the same glory.

Babel scattered one people into many languages so they could not build one tower. Pentecost gathered many languages into one praise so they could all hear one Name. The wound was not erased. It was healed by being filled.

## Not one language, one song

I have built my whole ministry, and this whole house of music in more tongues than I can count, on the pattern of that morning. And I want to be very careful about what it teaches, because people get it wrong in both directions.

Some people think the goal is to get everyone singing in one language, that real unity means everybody eventually adopting the same words, the important tongue, the central one, and the rest of the languages quietly fading as lesser. That is not Pentecost. That is Babel with better manners. At Pentecost, God specifically honored the native tongue of every person in the crowd. He did not make the Elamite learn to worship in someone else's language before the wonders of God could reach him. He sent the wonders to him in the language his mother used to comfort him in the dark. The particular languages were not a problem to be solved. They were vessels to be filled.

And other people think the languages are all that matters, that as long as everyone worships however they please in whatever words they like, the diversity itself is the point. But that is not Pentecost either. The miracle was not a hundred people saying a hundred different things. It was a hundred languages saying one thing. The wonders of God. One glory, one Name, one song of praise, arriving in every tongue at once and losing nothing in any of them.

That is the sound my name was made to point at. Not the flattening of the world into one language. Not the fracturing of the world into a thousand unrelated songs. But the great gathering that Babel made impossible and Pentecost made certain, every tongue on earth kept, honored, and filled to the brim with the same praise, so that the Egyptian and the Cretan and the Roman, who could not understand a word of each other's speech, understand perfectly the one thing that matters, because they are all hearing the wonders of the same God in the language their heart was born speaking.

## You do not have to learn a new tongue to be gathered in

So let me bring this all the way home to you, wherever you are and whatever language you dream in.

There is a lie left over from Babel that tells people they are on the outside of the praise because of their tongue, that the real worship happens somewhere else, in some other, more important language, and that you are only ever overhearing it in translation, a second-class guest at a feast held in a speech that is not yours. Pentecost took that lie and burned it up in the fire that fell that morning. The Spirit of God went out of His way, on the founding day of the whole church, to make sure that the very first thing every nation heard, it heard in its own native tongue. You were never meant to reach the praise by climbing up into someone else's language. The praise was always meant to come down into yours.

And that is still exactly how it works. The wonders of God do not wait at the top of a tower for you to build your way up to them in the correct words. They come down, in wind and fire, into the streets where the ordinary crowd is standing, and they speak, to the Parthian in Parthian, to the Arab in Arabic, to you in the words your mother first sang over you. The whole earth was fractured at the mouth. And the whole earth is being gathered back, not into one language, but into one song, sung at last in every language there is.

Babel ran the world apart. On that loud morning, in Yeshua, it started running backward. And the day is coming when the scattering will be fully undone, a countless multitude from every nation and tribe and people and tongue, standing together at last, every language kept and every language full, all of them at the very same moment saying the very same glory.

You are already invited into that sound. You do not have to learn a new tongue to be gathered in. Just open the one you have, and start to sing the wonders of God, and know that on the far side of the world, in a language you have never heard, someone is singing the exact same thing.
