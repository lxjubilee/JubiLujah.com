I have to tell you up front that I set out to make this album afraid.

Afraid I would say too much, or too little. Afraid that if I sang about the trains that carried children away, I would leave people in the dark with the grief and never bring them back up into the light. So before I wrote a single line I made myself a rule: every road down into this valley has to end at the Father who was standing on the platform, counting faces. I was not going to leave anyone at the depot. Not the listener. Not the child.

This is that album. It is the heaviest thing I carry, and it is the surest I am of anything I have made.

## Why I Could Not Leave This Unsung

There are wounds that families pass down like a folded blanket. You inherit it before you understand what it is. The children who were taken from home and marched into schools designed to erase their names, their language, their songs: that is a wound still living in the bones of people I love. And too often the church has handled it in one of two wrong ways. Either we look away because it is uncomfortable, or we pick at it until the wound itself becomes the only god in the room.

I refused both. I wanted to name what was done, plainly, without pretending it did not happen, and then lift my eyes to the only Shelter that did not fail. That is the exact turn of *The One Who Did Not Protect Me*: *I will not call evil good, and I will not curse him either. I will name what was done*, and look up. Psalm 27 gave me the ground to stand on: *though my father and mother forsake me, the LORD will take me up* (Psalm 27:10). That is not a slogan. On the mountain and in the valley, it is the last handhold when every human one has broken off in your grip.

## What the Title Means

*He Remembers Every Child.* Every one. The named and the unnamed.

Somewhere above the iron track, the Father of every child was leaning down, counting each small face looking back, hearing each small mouth still meaning the words they had been forbidden to speak. That image runs through the whole opener, *The Ones Who Were Sent Away*, because it is the difference between a record of despair and a record of hope. Human ledgers were partial. Some were burned. Some were never made. But **Yahuah** keeps a roll no agent ever saw and no fire ever reached.

The title track is the seal on the album: *He has the only complete list there has ever been, the file the agent did not see, the roll only His ink could light.* I hung it on the words of **Yeshua** Himself about the little ones: *their angels always behold the face of my Father* (Matthew 18:10). No child was beneath His notice. Not one.

## The Heart Behind the Songs

Let me walk you through the ache and the answer, because they are braided together on purpose.

*He Kept the Count* is about a six-year-old in a barber's chair, feet not reaching the floor, watching hair fall that a grandmother had blessed. And the Scripture that saved that song from being pure grief is small and enormous at once: *even the hairs of your head are all numbered* (Luke 12:7). What they could cut, He had already counted. The floor was full; heaven was not bereft.

*I Hid My Name* undid me. A child whispers the name they were given at sunrise into a closed palm, and holds it for three long years against the school's command. And then the reframe. There was a larger Palm, a wounded one, and the child's name was on the back of it before the child ever learned to fold his own hand.

> I was engraved on His palms before I was hidden in mine.

That is Isaiah, word for word: *Behold, I have engraved you on the palms of my hands* (Isaiah 49:16). Nothing they typed on a roll could out-hold that.

And *What the Children Did to Survive*, the smallest things: a pressed leaf folded in a handkerchief, a word whispered against the rule, a piece of bread traded bunk to bunk. I set it beside the midwives of Egypt who feared God and quietly saved the children in a system built to destroy them (Exodus 1). The Father bent above the bunks. Not one small act of survival was wasted. Not one fell.

## From the Valley Up to the Light

I promised you I would not leave you in the dark, so here is where the record turns, and it turns hard.

The centerpiece is *Christ Never Asked You to Forget*. This is the hinge of everything I believe about this history. Whatever anyone was told they had to surrender to belong to God, the word for water their grandmother taught them, the song a daughter should inherit, **Yeshua** never asked for it. *The school asked. The policy asked. The agent at the door asked. He did not.* The hands of Christ are not the hands the system used; the cross is the rebuke of every hand that abetted the forgetting. You can be fully His and fully who He made you. He never required the loss. He never has. He never will.

Then come the homecomings. *Returning to the Home I Knew* puts a survivor on the long road back with two names on him, the school's word on his papers, his grandmother's word still folded in his palm, and Christ above the two. And before he reaches the porch, the Father is already running down the road. That is the prodigal's father exactly: *while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and ran* (Luke 15:20). He did not walk. He ran.

*The Song Comes Back in His Mouth* is a grandmother of sixty-three who has not sung the old song since she was six, and a four-year-old who asks one small question, and the Spirit who had been keeping the song all along gives it back. Joel's promise made audible: *I will restore to you the years that the locust has eaten* (Joel 2:25). And *The Keepsake My Grandmother Hid* is a blanket buried behind a cottonwood for forty years, dug up the year a new daughter is born, Jeremiah's word that the children *shall come back to their own border* (Jeremiah 31:17).

## Who This Is For

This is for the survivor and the grandchild of the survivor. For the person who carries a grief they did not cause and cannot name.

It is for anyone who has ever wondered whether God was watching when the worst was done. Genesis says a brother's blood cried out *from the ground*, and the Lord heard it (Genesis 4:10), so *The Names the Records Did Not Keep* is sung slowly, with held breath between the syllables, because the God who hears blood from the ground hears every unmarked place on this land. He was not at one of the schools. He was at every one.

And it is for the tired believer who needs to know they are remembered when no one else remembers. That is why the closer, *He Remembers Every Child*, is led by children's voices, gentle, never triumphal, before the whole intergenerational family rises behind them. The roll the Father calls is whole. There is not a child He has forgotten. Not one. And the door is open for every name on it.

Come home. He counted your face on the platform, and He has been holding your place ever since.
