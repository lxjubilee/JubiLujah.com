Red dirt gets in everything. It works into the creases of your boots and the knees of your jeans and the grain of your skin until you cannot tell anymore where the land stops and you start. Out on the West Texas frontier, that is not a metaphor. That is Tuesday.

And it taught me two things I could never have learned sitting still in a pew, that I am made of the ground, and that the ground was never mine.

## Why I had to make it

I wanted to make a gospel record that smelled like actual dirt. Not a sanitized country album with a cross stamped on the cover, but something that came up out of the soil the way the people I love came up out of it, ranchers, roughnecks, border-country farmers, folks whose faith is inseparable from the land they break their backs on.

Because the land is where the whole story starts. Genesis chapter two says **Yahuah** formed man of the dust of the ground and breathed into him. We are red dirt with the breath of God in it. The opener of the record just says it flat, "red dirt, Texas, that's where I come from, red dirt in my blood, red dirt, gospel, home." I meant it as theology, not just geography. You are dust He decided to love. So is the man on the rig next to you.

## What the title means

Here is the turn the album is built on, and it took me years to make peace with it: the redemption in *Red Dirt Redemption* is not just mine. It is the land's too.

We think redemption is a private transaction, my soul, my rescue, my ticket out. But Leviticus chapter twenty-five, in the law of Jubilee, has **Yahuah** saying something that stops a landowner cold: "the land shall not be sold for ever: for the land is mine; for ye are strangers and sojourners with me." Every deed you have ever signed, every fence you have ever strung, and the ground underneath it belongs to Him. You are a guest on it. Psalm twenty-four opens the same way, the earth is the Lord's, and the fulness thereof. Even the cattle on a thousand hills are already His.

> You do not own the red dirt. You were entrusted with it. Those are very different jobs, and only one of them lets you sleep at night.

That reframes everything a working man does with land. *The Rancher's Prayer*, *The Rancher's Covenant*, *The Land Is the Lord's*, those tracks are all wrestling the same holy fact: I do not possess this place. I keep it for the One who does, and one day I hand it back.

## The heart under the songs

The center of the record is the title cut, *Red Dirt Redemption*, and I built it on the way rain comes to dry country. If you have never watched a real drought break, you have not felt the thing this album is chasing. Joel chapter two promises **Yahuah** will send the rain, the former rain and the latter rain, and restore the years the locust has eaten. Hosea says He will come to us as the rain, as the latter and former rain unto the earth. On hard land, redemption does not arrive as an idea. It arrives as weather. As mercy you can smell before you can see.

So the record moves like a rainstorm. *Oil Rig Gospel* and *Border Country Gospel* are the dry, dusty, working-hard middle, grit and diesel and heat, the long stretch where you are doing everything right and the ground gives you nothing back. *Red Dirt Repentance* is the turning, the moment a hard man finally goes to his knees in the field, and I put it there deliberately, because on real land repentance and rain tend to show up in the same week. Something in the human heart cracks open right about the time the sky does. And then the sky opens.

There is a thread through the whole thing I did not want anyone to miss. *Matthew* chapter twenty-five, "I was a stranger, and ye took me in." Border country is a place where strangers cross hard land, and I wanted a gospel record from that country to remember what **Yeshua** said about the stranger. On land that is not finally ours anyway, hospitality is not charity. It is just telling the truth about who owns the place.

And the record does not end on the dirt. It reaches past it. Romans chapter eight says the whole creation groans, waiting, the land itself is aching for the day the children of God are revealed and everything gets set free. Revelation chapter twenty-one promises a new heaven and a new earth. So *Red Dirt Forever* closes the album not with the land as it is, cracked and thirsty, but the land as it will be, redeemed, watered, restored, belonging fully and finally to the One who formed it.

## Who I made this for

I made *Red Dirt Redemption* for the ones whose faith grew up outdoors.

For the rancher who has prayed for rain until his voice gave out, and who needs to know that the God he is praying to owns the sky and the pasture and him. For the roughneck who thinks a man that dirty and that far from a church could not possibly matter to heaven, when heaven made him out of the same red dust it made kings from. For the farmer watching a drought take everything he built, who needs to hear that the years the locust ate are exactly the years **Yahuah** specializes in restoring.

And for the wanderer. Border country is full of them, and so is this record. If you have felt like a stranger on ground that will not claim you, hear the deepest thing on the album: you were always a sojourner here. Every one of us is. The dirt is on loan, the days are numbered, and the only permanent inheritance is the one **Yeshua** went ahead to secure, a land where nothing dries up and no deed ever expires.

So go on and get the red dirt in your creases. Work the ground you were given. Pray for the rain like it is coming, because on His land, it is.

And when it breaks over you, and it will, remember that the redemption running down your face was never only about you. It was the whole thirsty earth getting a taste of what its Maker has been planning to do with all of it.
