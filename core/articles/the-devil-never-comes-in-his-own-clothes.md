My grandfather used to tell a tale about a stranger who came down the mountain at dusk. The stranger walked in a fine skirt and smiled a smile like gold, but the old preacher in the story saw the snake beneath the husk. That tale is the seed of this whole record.

Because here is the thing my grandfather understood that a lot of us forget: the devil never comes in his own clothes. He does not show up ugly. He shows up beautiful, smiling, smelling like the very thing you have been praying for. And *Devil in a Skirt* is my record about the one force in all creation that can tear that costume off, the truth of **Yeshua**, and the discernment His Spirit gives a watchful heart.

## Why I had to make it

Let me say plainly what the title is and is not, because it matters. The "skirt" on this record is not a woman. It is a costume. It is the disguise the deceiver wears, the beautiful dress pulled over an ugly thing. The whole album treats it exactly the way Scripture does, as the wolf's borrowed wool, the angel-of-light glow the accuser puts on when the lamps are dim.

I had to make it because I watched too many good people get taken by something they never saw coming, precisely because it did not look like anything to fear. Sin that announced itself would be easy to refuse. It never does. Paul warned in Second Corinthians chapter eleven that Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light. **Yeshua** warned in Matthew chapter seven to beware of false prophets who come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly are ravening wolves. The danger was never the thing that looks dangerous. The danger is the honey on the lips of the lie you would happily keep.

## What the title means

The opener says it in a single line I keep coming back to: "the devil don't never come in his own true clothes, he comes in the dress that he thinks will deceive."

That is the whole warning of the record. Deception is, by definition, disguised. If you could see it plainly you would not be deceived. So the question is never "does this look good?" The question is "what is underneath it?" And you cannot answer that with your own eyes. You answer it with the Word and the Spirit. As the chorus of the opener puts it: the Lamb of God strips every disguise, the truth of His Word turns the lie to light, the Spirit of **Yeshua** exposes the counterfeit.

> The devil's whole strategy is the costume. God's whole answer is the light. You do not out-argue a disguise. You drag it into the light of Christ and watch it fall off.

That is why First John chapter four does not tell us to trust every warm feeling. It tells us to try the spirits, whether they are of God. Discernment is not suspicion and it is not fear. It is a watchful love that refuses to let a pretty lie walk off with the people **Yeshua** died for.

## The heart under the songs

The first half of this album is a frontier watch, a man learning to see.

*Wolves in the Wool* and *Angel of Light* name the disguises. *Try the Spirits* is First John four set to a fiddle. *Guard Your Heart* stands on Proverbs chapter four, keep thy heart with all diligence, for out of it are the issues of life, because the counterfeit always aims at the heart first. *Armor of Light* is Ephesians chapter six, the whole armor of God, so a man can stand against the wiles, the *schemes*, of the devil. And *The Mountain Watchman* is Ezekiel chapter thirty-three, the watchman set on the wall who will answer for the souls he fails to warn. I put a lot of vigilance on the front of this record on purpose, because you cannot resist what you refuse to recognize.

But I did not want an album that only warns. A record that leaves you scared of the dark has failed. So the whole thing turns, hard and glad, at the deliverance.

The cinematic peak is *Jesus Strips the Disguise*, and it opens with the plain words of Revelation chapter twelve: now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, for the accuser of our brethren is cast down. The song stands the deceiver at the foot of the cross and watches the costume fall. It leans on Colossians chapter two, where Paul says **Yeshua** spoiled principalities and powers and made a public show of them openly, triumphing over them in His cross. That is the frontier preacher's old refrain built right into the song: there is no disguise that can stand at the foot of the cross, no costume of the devil that can stay on his frame when the Lamb of God shines through.

And it goes back further still, all the way to the first promise in the Book. Genesis chapter three, the serpent in the garden was a disguise too, and **Yahuah** swore that the Seed of the woman would bruise the serpent's head. The bridge of the opener sings exactly that: the heel of the Seed crushed the head of his lies. Every mask in the record was already doomed the day that promise was made.

Then comes the party. *Deliverance Holler* is the morning after the long night of warfare, "the deliverance song is the song of the morning, and the praise of the Lamb sweeps the dark away." Chains falling, captives leaping, every nation gathered at the holler of grace. And the finale, *Truth Walks the Mountain*, ends the whole record not with the enemy but with the King, Truth Himself walking the high road forever, the wolves and the false lights all bowing at His feet, and word going out to the cabins and the cradles and the watchmen: the new day has come.

## Who I made this for

I made *Devil in a Skirt* for two people.

The first is the one who got fooled. The one who danced with something that smiled like gold and only later saw the snake beneath it, and who is now drowning in shame, certain they should have known better. Hear me: the whole point of a disguise is that you were not supposed to see through it alone. That is not your failure, that is what a counterfeit is. And Revelation chapter twelve says how the brethren overcame the accuser: by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony. Not by being clever enough. By the blood. The costume that fooled you has already been torn off at the cross. Come stand there and watch it fall.

The second is the watchman, the believer God is teaching to see. Maybe you have felt a check in your spirit about something everyone else is celebrating, and you have wondered if you are just being difficult. You may be a watchman on the wall. James chapter four gives you the promise to stand on: resist the devil, and he will flee from you. He flees. The disguise has no power once the light hits it.

So do not be afraid of the dark, and do not be dazzled by the pretty lie. Keep your heart. Try the spirits. Stand on the rock and hold the sword of the Word.

Because the devil never comes in his own clothes, but there is not a costume in all of hell that can stay on his frame when the Lamb of God walks up the mountain in the truth of His Name. The night of warfare ends. The deliverance holler always comes at dawn. And the last thing standing on that mountain is not the disguise. It is the King.
