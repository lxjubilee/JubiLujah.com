Imagine you found, tucked in an old drawer, a calendar that was not yours.

And every date already had your name beside it, written in another hand, long before you were born.

That is closer to the truth of the biblical feasts than anything most of us were taught. We learned to call them Jewish holidays: ancient, ethnic, a little exotic, and firmly optional. Somebody else's customs. But the Hebrew word the Scriptures actually use for them says something we were never told, and once you hear it, you cannot un-hear it.

The word is *moed* (מועד).

## A word that means an appointment

Open the great feast-chapter of Leviticus and you will find a heading that our English Bibles often render "these are the feasts of the Lord." The Hebrew says *eleh moadei Yahuah*, "these are the *appointed times* of Yahuah." Not the feasts of Israel. The appointed times of *Yahuah*. He calls them His.

*Moed* comes from the root *ya'ad* (יעד), which means to appoint, to designate, to fix a meeting between two parties. It is the language of a set rendezvous, a time and a place agreed in advance so that two people will not miss each other. A *moed* is not a mood or a season that happens to arrive. It is a **kept appointment**.

And here is the detail that undid me the first time I traced it. The tent where Elohim met Moses, the dwelling at the center of the camp, is called in Hebrew the *Ohel Moed*, the Tent of *Meeting*. Same root. The place of meeting and the times of meeting are cut from the very same word. A *moed* is a Tent of Meeting made out of hours instead of goat's hair. It is a sanctuary you enter not by walking, but by waiting until the day comes round.

So when Scripture lists the appointed times, it is not handing Israel a folk calendar. It is Yahuah saying: *Here are the days I have set aside to meet with you. Do not stand Me up.*

## He put them on the calendar before there was a nation

Watch how early this begins. On the fourth day of creation, before there is an Israel, before there is an Egypt to be freed from, Elohim makes the lights of the sky, and He says they are given for *moadim*, for appointed times. The sun and moon are hung, among other reasons, to be the great clock that tells creation when the meetings fall.

Sit with the scale of that. Before there was a single feast-keeper on the earth, the machinery for keeping the appointments was already turning overhead. The meetings were on the calendar before the guests existed. That is not the behavior of a distant God who tolerates our worship. That is the behavior of a Father who cleared His schedule for you first, and built the sky to keep the reminder.

> A *moed* is not a holiday you decide to attend. It is a meeting He decided to keep, and then invited you into.

This is why it matters so much that we recover the word. When you believe the feasts are human traditions, they become negotiable, sentimental, the kind of thing you keep if it moves you and drop if it doesn't. But an *appointment* is a different creature altogether. You do not keep an appointment because you are in the mood. You keep it because someone is waiting for you on the other end.

## What changes when you know Someone is waiting

There is a world of difference between *observing a custom* and *keeping an appointment*.

A custom asks nothing of the other side. You perform it, and no one is disappointed if you don't. But an appointment implies a face. It implies that on the far side of the date, Someone has set the time apart too, and is watching the door. The whole emotional weather of the feasts changes the moment you realize they are not you reaching up toward heaven on a nice day. They are heaven reaching down and saying, *this day, this one, I want to see you.*

Now, hear me carefully, because the Hebrew roots crowd can go strange here, and I want to keep us on the text. To recover the word *moed* is not to say your standing before Yahuah rises and falls on whether you kept a date on a calendar. The gospel is not a scheduling app. Yeshua did not die to give you a better planner. So set down any fear that you have been failing an examination no one told you about. That is not what this is.

What this *is*, is an invitation you were never shown. Most believers have lived their whole lives with a set of standing appointments they never knew were on the books, meetings Yahuah marked out from the foundation of the world, days He filled with meaning He intended you to walk into. To learn the word *moed* is simply to open the drawer and find the calendar with your name already on it.

## The One who never missed a meeting

And here is where the whole thing lifts off the page, because the appointments were never merely about attendance. They were about *Him*, the One who would come and fill each one to the brim.

Every *moed* Yahuah set was, in the end, a meeting arranged around His Son. The appointments in the spring, the Lamb, the risen grain, the fire of the Spirit, Yeshua walked into each of them and kept them to the very day, as we will trace in the pieces to come. He was the guest the calendar had been waiting for since the fourth day of creation. When the text says these are *the appointed times of Yahuah*, it turns out they were appointments to meet the Father *in the Son*.

Which means the feasts were never Israel's alone, and never merely Israel's past. They are the shape of how Yahuah has always done it: He fixes a time, He keeps it Himself, and He leaves the door standing open for anyone who will come.

So take the word home with you this week. *Moed.* An appointed time. A meeting on the calendar of heaven. Not a tradition you have to earn your way into, not a burden laid on your back, a standing appointment, set long before you drew breath, by a Father who has been keeping the sky wound like a clock so that you and He would not miss each other.

You have been invited to the oldest meetings in the world. And the astonishing thing, the thing that should make your heart lift, is not that you finally noticed the calendar.

It is that He has been at the Tent this whole time, watching the door for you.
