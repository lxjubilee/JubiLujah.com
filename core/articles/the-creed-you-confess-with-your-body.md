Watch a room worship with the sound turned off and you will still be able to read the theology. There is a woman standing very straight, chin up. Next to her a man has sunk to his knees. Across the aisle someone stands with both hands open above their head, palms up, like a child waiting to be lifted. Another has both hands pressed flat over their own chest, eyes shut tight. Nobody handed these people a diagram of postures. Nobody told them what each one means. And yet every one of them is making a confession with their body, a whole sentence of doctrine, spoken without a word, and most of them could not tell you what they just said.

I want to teach you to read it, because it is one of the ways the church keeps its faith where it cannot be lost. We store doctrine in our songs, yes. But we also store it in our bodies. And the body, it turns out, is sometimes the last part of us to forget what is true, and, on the hard days, the first part willing to confess it again.

## Standing is a resurrection claim

Start with the plainest one, the one so ordinary we never think about it: we stand up to sing. Why? A tired congregation could just as easily sit. But there is an old instinct in it, and the instinct is this, the people who worship Yahuah are people who expect to be *raised*. To stand is to put your body in the posture of the living. The dead lie down; the raised get up. Every time a room rises to its feet to sing, it is quietly rehearsing the central claim of the whole faith: that death does not get to keep us down, that the tomb was emptied, that we belong to a God who lifts the fallen and calls the buried up out of the ground. You thought you were just standing because the music started. You were confessing the resurrection with your knees.

## Kneeling tells the truth about who is God

Then there is the opposite motion, and it says the opposite half of the truth. To kneel, to bow, to go down low, is to make a confession your standing cannot make: *He is Lord, and I am not.* A psalm calls the people to come and kneel before Yahuah their Maker, and the going-down is the whole message. In a world that spends all its energy insisting on its own importance, a person lowering their body to the ground is telling a truth the mouth struggles to say plainly, that there is a God, and it is not me; that I did not make myself; that I am a creature before a Creator and it is a relief, not a defeat, to finally be small in the presence of Someone that large. Kneeling is not groveling. It is accuracy. It is your body agreeing to the actual size of things.

## Lifted hands are the posture of the empty

The one that took me longest to understand is the lifted, open hand. We treat it as the mark of an especially expressive worshiper, and so the reserved among us feel it is not for them. But look at what the hands are actually doing. Turned up and open, they are the posture of a person who is holding nothing and asking to be filled. They are the hands of a small child lifted toward a parent, *pick me up, I cannot get there myself.* They are also, in the very same shape, the hands of someone who has surrendered, a prisoner laying down every weapon. Empty hands raised say two things at once, and both are the gospel: *I have nothing to bring You,* and *I give up every claim to run my own life.* Scripture tells the people to lift up their hands in the holy place and bless Yahuah, not because the gesture is impressive, but because the empty upturned hand is the truest thing a needy creature can do in front of a giving God. It confesses poverty and surrender in one motion.

## The eyes we close, the eyes we cannot lift

Even the small things preach. When you shut your eyes to sing, you are confessing that you trust what you cannot see, that the God you are singing to is more real than the room you just closed out. And there is the opposite: Yeshua told of a man so aware of his own failure that he would not even lift his eyes toward heaven, but beat his chest and asked for mercy, and it was *that* man, Yeshua said, who went home right with God. The lowered eyes and the struck chest were a confession of need so honest that heaven answered it. Your body knows how to say *I am not worthy* and *I trust You anyway* in the same breath, if you let it.

## The body will preach when the heart has gone quiet

Now here is the reason all of this matters, and it is not academic. There are days your heart will not cooperate. You will stand in a service emptied out, unconvinced, unable to feel a single true thing. On those days the words stick in your throat, and if worship depended entirely on your heart's agreement, you would have nothing to offer at all.

But your body can go first. You can *stand* on a morning you do not feel raised, and the standing itself will start to preach resurrection at you from the outside in. You can *kneel* when your proud, frightened heart is still insisting on control, and the lowering will begin to tell your heart the truth its words could not manage. You can *lift an empty hand* when you feel you have nothing and believe less, and the raised palm will confess a surrender you have not yet felt, and often, quietly, the feeling comes up behind the posture to meet it. This is not pretending. It is letting the more obedient part of you lead the more stubborn part. The body takes the true position, and the heart, discipled by it, eventually follows.

> Sometimes faith is not the feeling that moves the body. Sometimes it is the body that moves first, and the feeling catches up on the way down, or on the way up.

## Let the posture go first

So do not think of what you do with your body in worship as decoration, or as personality, or as something for the demonstrative few. It is a creed. It is doctrine you can preach without a single word, and it is doctrine that keeps working on the days your words fail. Stand, and confess the God who raises the dead. Kneel, and tell the truth about who made whom. Open your hands, and admit you are empty and give up your grip. Close your eyes, and trust the One you cannot see.

And on the day you cannot feel any of it, especially on that day, let the posture go first. Put your body in the shape of the truth, and wait. Your heart has caught up to your knees a thousand times before. It will again.
