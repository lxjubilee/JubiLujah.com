Here is something about the Jubilee that we almost never stop to notice: it was not the kind of thing anyone could simply *decide* to have.

You could long for it. You could count the years toward it. But no debtor could convene a Jubilee for himself, no matter how badly he needed one. No slave could declare his own release. No village of the poor could gather and vote the year of favor into existence over their own heads. A Jubilee was a **proclamation**, and by its very nature a proclamation requires someone standing over the situation with the authority to make it. The freedom had to be declared, and only a certain kind of person has the right to declare freedom over a whole land.

That is why the scene I am about to take you into is so much larger than it looks. When Yeshua stood up in a small synagogue in Nazareth and read a few lines about liberty and release, the crowd was not watching a young man give a nice reading. They were watching someone step up and do the one thing only a King has the standing to do, *call the Jubilee open.*

## What the words in the scroll actually were

They handed Him the scroll of the prophet Isaiah, and He found the place and read: the Spirit of Yahuah is upon Me, because He has anointed Me to bring good news to the poor; He has sent Me to proclaim release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to set the oppressed free, and to proclaim **the acceptable year of Yahuah.**

Every ear in that room recognized that last phrase. *The acceptable year of Yahuah*, the year of His favor, His delight, His good pleasure. It was Jubilee language, the horn set to poetry. Isaiah had reached all the way back into the oldest law of liberty and lifted out its very heart: the great release, the debts undone, the captives streaming home, the fields returning to the families who first cleared them. And he had gathered all of it into a promise about *someone*, an anointed one, a Spirit-carried herald, who would one day come and stand up and *declare* that the year had arrived.

Notice how thick the language of proclamation is. To *proclaim* release. To *proclaim* the acceptable year. Isaiah did not describe a man who would explain the Jubilee or teach about it or hope for it. He described a man sent to *announce* it, to open His mouth with the authority of heaven and say the word that makes the release begin.

## A herald does not speak for himself

I know something about this in my bones, because heralding is my whole office. A herald is nothing on her own. A herald has no army, no treasury, no throne. Everything a herald says derives its weight entirely from the One who sent her. When a herald walks into the town square and cries out a decree, the words are not her opinion and not her wish. They are the will of the King, carried on a servant's voice. The power was never in the messenger. It was in the authority behind the message.

So when the anointed one of Isaiah's scroll stands and *proclaims* the acceptable year, everything depends on this question: by whose authority? A proclamation from a nobody is only noise. A proclamation of Jubilee, debts erased, captives freed, an entire nation's accounts settled, could only stand if it came from the One who owned the debts, held the captives' fates, and governed the land. In the end, only the King of the whole arrangement could ever open the year of His own favor.

And then Yeshua rolled up the scroll, handed it back, sat down, which is what a teacher did when He was about to render the weightiest word, and with every eye in the room fastened on Him, He said it. This scripture, He told them, is fulfilled today, in your hearing.

> He did not say the year of favor was coming. He said it had *arrived*, and it had arrived because He had just stood up and proclaimed it. The herald in Isaiah's scroll had a face now, and the face was His.

Feel the staggering claim folded inside that quiet sentence. He was not commenting on the text. He was *being* the text. He was taking up the office of the One authorized to open the Jubilee, and He was exercising it, right there, over that room, declaring the acceptable year not as a scholar reciting an old promise but as the King announcing His own reign had come.

## The authority behind the announcement

This is why the crowd's response ran so hot so fast. They understood, at least dimly, that no ordinary man may stand up and declare the year of favor over a nation. That is a royal act. And here was the carpenter's son, the boy they had watched grow up, whose mother they knew, claiming to be the one with the right to sound it.

But everything He went on to do proved the authority the announcement required. A King who declares debtors free had better be able to actually cancel debt, and He forgave sins, out loud, to the scandal of everyone listening, doing what they all knew belonged to God alone. A King who proclaims release to captives had better be able to open a prison, and He opened the deepest prisons there are, breaking the grip of sickness, of torment, of death itself. He did not merely *say* the acceptable year had come. He walked through the land making it come true, one released captive at a time, until the proclamation and the reality were the same thing.

That is the difference between a wish and a decree. A wish is a good feeling that changes nothing. A decree changes the world because of who stands behind it. When Yeshua proclaimed the year of favor, the blind saw and the bound walked and the dead sat up, because the authority behind the announcement was total. He was not asking heaven to grant a Jubilee. He *was* heaven's King, opening it.

## The year He proclaimed is still open

And here is the joy I cannot keep in.

That year of favor did not close when the synagogue emptied. The King who stood up and declared it open has never rescinded the proclamation. We are living, right now, inside the acceptable year, a long, generous season of release that has been running ever since the day He rolled up that scroll and said *today.* The decree still stands because the King still reigns. His authority has not lapsed, His word has not been recalled, and the debts He is willing to cancel He is still cancelling.

So hear me, wherever you are. You have perhaps been waiting for someone to give you permission to be free, waiting to feel worthy of the release, waiting for your circumstances to earn it, waiting for a Jubilee you were sure you had missed. Stop waiting for permission. The King has already stood up. The proclamation has already been made over your life, in a voice with all the authority of heaven behind it. The acceptable year is not a memory and it is not a rumor. It is a standing royal decree, and it has your name inside the words *the captives* and *the poor* and *the oppressed.*

The King has called the Jubilee. All that is left is for you to walk out of the prison He has already declared open.
