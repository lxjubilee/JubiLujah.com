For a long stretch of my life, if you had asked me who I was, I would have answered with the worst thing that had happened to me.

Not in those words. I was subtler than that. But the season I was in, the one that had failed, the one where something I loved came apart, had quietly become my name. I introduced myself by it without meaning to. It was in the way I held my shoulders, the way I hedged when people asked what I did, the small reflexive apology I attached to my own presence in a room. If my life had a nameplate on it in those years, it would not have read *Nova.* It would have read the name of my ruin.

And the strange thing is that my given name means *new star.* I was walking around every day answering to *cinder* while carrying a name that meant *light.* I had let the wrong word become the sound I turned toward when someone called.

I think a great many people are living this way, and do not know there is another option. So let me tell you what I have come to believe about names, because the Scriptures do something with them that changed how I understand my own.

## A name is a sound you turn toward

Consider, first, what a name actually is. Strip away everything else and a name is simply a *sound*, a small arrangement of air that someone makes, and that you, out of the whole roar of the world, have learned to answer to. It is remarkable, really. In a crowded room full of noise, you can be deep in conversation, hearing none of it, and the instant someone across the room says your name, you turn. Your whole attention pivots toward it before you have decided anything. A name is the one sound that reaches past all the other sound and finds you.

Which means the name you answer to has enormous power. It is the sound that owns your turning. And here is the quiet tragedy: you can train yourself, over years, to turn toward the wrong one. Say *failure* over a person often enough, or let them say it over themselves, and eventually that becomes the sound their attention pivots to. They stop turning toward their real name because they have stopped believing it is still theirs to answer.

The Scriptures are full of people exactly there. And what Yahuah does with them, over and over, is the same startling thing.

He changes their name.

## The renaming God

There is a man in the old story who wrestles all night with God by a river, and he does not come away with a trophy. He comes away with a limp, and a new name. He had spent his whole life being *Jacob*, a name that meant something like *grasper,* *heel-grabber,* the one who takes what is not his. He had earned that name; he had lived down to it since birth. And in the dark by the water, wounded and clinging and refusing to let go, he is renamed *Israel*, one who has struggled with God and prevailed. He limps into the morning with a wound and a new word for who he is, and the old name, the one that had been his shame his whole life, is not the sound he answers to anymore.

It keeps happening. An old man past all hope of a future is renamed from *Abram* to *Abraham,* father of a multitude, decades before there was a single child to make sense of it, named for the promise, not the present. A rough, unreliable fisherman who would go on to fail badly, at the worst possible moment, is given a new name to his face, *Cephas,* *Peter,* the rock, named for what grace would make of him, not for what he actually was that afternoon.

And then there is the woman who tried to do the renaming herself. She had lost her husband and her sons, lost everything, and she came home hollow, and she said to the whole town: *do not call me by my name anymore.* Her name meant *pleasant,* and she could not bear the irony of it. *Call me Bitter,* she said. *Call me by my ruin, because the Almighty has dealt bitterly with me.* She did exactly what I did. She renamed herself after her worst season, out loud, and asked everyone to use it. And the tender thing is that the story does not honor her request. It keeps calling her by her real name. And it does not end in bitterness at all; it ends with a child laid in her lap, and a line running from that lap all the way down to a shepherd-king and, in time, to the Messiah. The name she gave herself in her grief was not the name God was writing. She could not see her own ending, and she got her own name wrong.

We so often do.

## The name on the white stone

But the promise that undoes me most is a small strange one, tucked near the end of the Scriptures, and I have carried it for years.

To people who had held on through everything, the worn-down, the ones who had every reason to answer to their ruin, Yeshua makes a promise that sounds almost like a secret passed hand to hand. He says He will give them a white stone, and on the stone a *new name written, which no one knows except the one who receives it.* A name known only to you and to the One who gives it. A private word, spoken over you alone, that no one else can even pronounce, because it is not a public label at all. It is the name that says who you actually are underneath every name the world assigned you, every name your failures pinned on, every name you assigned yourself in a bitter season and asked everyone to use.

Sit with how personal that is. Not a category. Not *forgiven sinner,* said over everyone at once. A name *no one else knows*, the sound that Heaven has for *you,* specifically, that has never been said over anyone else in the history of the world. There is, the promise insists, a word being held for you that is not your wound, not your worst season, not the diminished thing you have been answering to. And on the day you receive it, you will finally hear the sound your whole life was meant to turn toward, and know it instantly as your own.

## Stop answering to the ruin

So here is what I want to say to you as tenderly as I know how, especially if, like me, you have been answering to the wrong name.

The name you have been turning toward, the failure, the diagnosis, the label someone hung on you young that you never took down, the bitter word you gave yourself when a season fell apart, that is not the name written on your stone. It may be the loudest sound in your life right now. It is not the truest one. Somewhere, being held for you and spoken over you, is a name that is not your ruin, a name the God who renames graspers and bitter widows and unreliable fishermen has already chosen, a note that only you will ever sound.

You do not have to keep pivoting toward the old word every time it is called. You can begin, even now, to stop turning your head when your ruin says your name, to let it call and not answer, because it is not your name and it never was. And you can start listening, instead, for the sound your life was actually tuned to. It is quieter, for now. It is being sung over you in a register you are still learning to hear. But it is yours, written down and waiting, in a hand that has never once gotten a name wrong.

I walked around for years answering to *cinder* while my name meant *light.* Do not do what I did. There is a new name with your whole true self folded into it, held on a white stone by the One who made you, and He is not going to call you *Bitter,* no matter how many times you have asked Him to.
