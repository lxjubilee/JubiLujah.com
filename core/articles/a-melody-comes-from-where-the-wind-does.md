I have been writing songs for most of my life, and I am going to tell you a secret that should probably worry my listeners: I still have no idea where a melody comes from.

That is not false modesty. I can teach you craft all day. I can show you why one interval aches and another resolves, how to build a line that lifts, where a chorus wants to sit, how to cut the clever bit that is strangling everything around it. Craft is real and craft can be taught. But craft is what you do *to* a melody after it arrives. It is carpentry. It shapes the wood. It does not grow the tree. And the tree, the first three notes, the thing that makes you sit up in a grocery line and reach for your phone. I cannot summon that. Nobody can. After ninety-one albums I am no closer to the machine than I was at the beginning. There is no machine.

For years this embarrassed me. It felt like a gap in my professionalism, a thing I should have solved by now. Surely someone who has written this much should be able to explain the source. Then I was reading the third chapter of John, the night conversation between Yeshua and a careful, learned man named Nicodemus, and I found the source described exactly, not for a melody, but for something even harder to hold.

## The image He reached for

Nicodemus came at night, which the Gospel writer seems to mean in more ways than one, and he could not understand what Yeshua meant about being born again, born of the Spirit. And instead of giving him a doctrine, Yeshua gave him an image. He said, in effect: the wind blows where it wants. You hear the sound of it. But you cannot tell where it came from or where it is going. That is what everyone born of the Spirit is like.

What undoes me about that is the double meaning buried in the words themselves. In the language of the Hebrews, and in the Greek the Gospel was written in, the word for *wind* and the word for *Spirit* and the word for *breath* are one and the same word. Yeshua was not making a clumsy comparison, *the Spirit is sort of like wind.* He was standing inside a word that already held both. The wind and the Spirit are the same mystery wearing two coats. And the one thing He says you can know about either of them is this: you hear the sound, and you cannot trace it home.

I read that and I thought, that is a melody. That is exactly what happens. You hear the sound. It moves through you, it moves the room, it bends the grass of your whole heart in one direction. And if you turn to catch where it came from, there is nothing there to catch. It came from where the wind does.

## Why we cannot manufacture it

I think we have a deep and modern need to own the source of things. We want the recipe. We want the pipeline, the process, the repeatable method that turns effort into inspiration on demand, because a source you cannot control is a source that might not show up when you need it, and that is unbearable to a person who has to deliver.

But some things refuse to be owned at their source, and I have come to believe those are the deepest things, and that this is not a defect. A melody you could fully manufacture would be a melody with no wind in it. You can hear the difference. There are songs assembled entirely out of craft, technically flawless, that leave the room exactly as they found it, because craft with no breath through it is furniture, not fire. And then there are the songs that arrived, that came from somewhere the writer cannot point to, and those are the ones that move people who never met the writer, in rooms the writer will never enter.

The same is true of a life turned toward Yahuah. You cannot manufacture the moment the wind actually moves. You can build the instrument, you can hollow the reed, you can show up faithfully to the quiet and keep the sails up, all of that is craft, and none of it is optional. But you cannot make the wind blow. You can only be in a posture to catch it when it does. The people I most trust in matters of the Spirit are never the ones with the airtight method for producing an experience of God. They are the ones who have felt the wind enough times to stop pretending they command it, and who keep their sails up anyway.

## You hear the sound

Here is where the image turns from mystery into hope, and it turns fast.

Because notice what Yeshua does *not* say. He does not say the wind is unreal because you cannot trace it. He does not say that a source you cannot locate is a source you should doubt. He says the opposite, and He says it to a man trained to trust only what he can define. He says: *you hear the sound.* The evidence is not in the map of where it came from. The evidence is that the grass is moving. You do not have to explain the wind to know you have been caught up in it. You only have to feel it on your face.

I find this enormously freeing, both as a writer and as a person who is trying to follow Yeshua. As a writer, it lets me stop apologizing for a source I cannot document. The melody came from where the wind does. I did not make it; I received it, and my honest job is to catch it well and pass it on, not to take credit for the wind. And as a person, it lets me stop demanding a diagram of God before I will trust the movement of God. I have felt the grass of my own heart bend in a direction I did not choose, in a room where a song was being sung. I could not tell you the mechanism. But I am not going to stand there insisting nothing happened just because I cannot trace it home.

So the next time a melody moves you and you cannot say why, the song at the funeral, the tune from childhood that arrives uninvited and buckles your knees, the three notes in a grocery store that stop you where you stand, do not rush to explain it. You are not being irrational. You are hearing the sound of something that blows where it wishes.

You cannot tell where it came from or where it is going. You only know the grass is moving. Keep your sails up. The wind is not finished with this field.
