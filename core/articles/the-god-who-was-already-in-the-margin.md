We have a way of talking in the church that I have finally decided I no longer believe.

We talk about *taking God to the margins*. Taking Him to the poor, to the barrio, to the forgotten street, to the people the world has written off. And I understand what we mean by it, and the heart behind it is often good. But there is a lie folded up inside that language, so small you can miss it your whole life, and it is this: that God is here, in the comfortable center, in the nice part of town, in our clean and well-lit rooms, *waiting for us to carry Him out* to the poor like a package we deliver.

I do not believe that anymore. I have gone looking for God in too many overlooked places, and I have found the same thing every single time. He was already there. He had been there for a long time. And more often than not, He was waiting for *me* to arrive, not so I could bring Him to the margin, but so the margin could show me a face of Him I had never seen in the center.

*Dios en el margen.* God in the margin. Not carried there. Native there.

## Where He actually kept turning up

If you read the accounts looking for where God locates Himself, you will notice something that ought to unsettle the comfortable and comfort the overlooked.

He is born, not in the capital, not in a palace, not among the people who mattered, but in a feeding trough in a nowhere town, and the first ones told are shepherds, night-shift laborers, ceremonially unclean, the sort of men no one would have chosen as the honored first witnesses of anything. Heaven throws open the greatest announcement in history, and it sends the invitation to the wrong side of the tracks first.

He grows up and does the same thing on purpose, over and over, until it becomes the most obvious pattern of His life. He eats with tax collectors, the collaborators everyone despised. He lets a woman with a reputation wash His feet in a room full of respectable men who are scandalized by it. He touches the leper the whole society had exiled. He stops the entire crowd for a blind beggar that crowd was actively shushing. He is forever wandering off toward exactly the people the center had decided did not count.

And then He tells a story that gives the whole game away. A man throws a great banquet, He says, and the invited guests, the respectable, the busy, the ones who assumed the feast was for people like them, all make their excuses and do not come. So the master is angry, and he sends his servant out into the streets and the alleys of the town, and then out along the country roads, with a strange and specific command: bring in the poor, the crippled, the blind, the lame. Fill my house with *them*. Go out to the margins and the hedges and compel them to come in, because I tell you, my house will be full, and it will be full of the ones the first list left off.

That is not a marginal story. That is the story. The banquet of God fills up from the outside in. The honored seats go to the overlooked. And the ones who assumed they were the center discover, too late, that the center of God's love was never where they thought it was.

## What the margin taught me that the center never could

I did not learn this from a book. I learned it in rooms the wider church has never heard of and never will.

I have worshiped in places with dirt for a floor and a single working light, with people who had every earthly reason to be bitter and were instead so full of joy it embarrassed me. I have watched a woman who cleaned houses all day lift her hands in praise with a freedom I have rarely seen in any beautiful sanctuary. I have heard the poor sing about the goodness of God with a conviction that the comfortable simply cannot manufacture, because they were not singing a theory. They were singing about a God they had actually needed, actually leaned their whole weight on, actually found holding them up when there was nothing else.

And every time, I came in thinking I was bringing them something, and I left knowing they had shown me the Lord. Not a smaller, poorer version of Him for people who could not afford the real thing. The real thing. A face of God, tender, near, unmistakably present among the overlooked, that I had somehow missed in all my well-lit rooms.

Because here is what the margin knows that the center keeps forgetting: God is not most at home where things are comfortable. The Scriptures say it flatly, and we keep flinching from it. He is near to the brokenhearted. He lifts the poor from the dust and seats them with princes. He has chosen the foolish things to shame the wise and the weak things to shame the strong and the things that are not, to bring to nothing the things that are. He resists the proud and gives grace to the humble. There is a bias in the heart of God, and it does not run the direction the world's does. It runs *down*, toward the low place, the last seat, the forgotten street, the ones no one counted.

## Stop delivering Him and start meeting Him

So here is what I want to say to you, whichever side of this you are standing on.

If you are one of the overlooked, if you have spent your life in the margin the world drew, assuming that the real presence of God was happening somewhere else, in someone else's nicer building, among people who matter more than you. I need you to hear the banquet story correctly. You are not on the substitute list. You are not the afterthought the master settled for. You are the ones the feast was *filled with on purpose*. The seat at the table is not a consolation prize. It has your name on it, and it was always going to, and the God who set it there has been in your margin the whole time, closer to you in your low place than He ever was to the comfortable in theirs.

And if you are in the center, comfortable, resourced, well-lit, then stop talking about delivering God to the poor like a package. He is already there. He beat you to it by a lifetime. Go, yes, go out into the streets and the alleys and the country roads, because the going is holy and the servant in the story was sent. But go as one who is arriving where God already lives, not as one who is bringing Him somewhere new. Go to *meet* Him in the face of the overlooked. Go to be shown the part of Him you have been missing. Go humble, and go ready to worship, because I promise you the singing you find out there will teach you things about the goodness of God that your comfort never could.

The margin is not the edge of God's kingdom. It is, more often than we dare admit, the center of it. He was already there. He is there now. And the only real question is whether we will finally go and join the feast that has been going on, gloriously, without us, among the very people we thought we were coming to save.
