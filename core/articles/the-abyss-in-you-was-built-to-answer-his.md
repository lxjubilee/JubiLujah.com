There is a song in the middle of the Psalter that opens with a picture so tender it has been carried in people's hearts for three thousand years, and then, a few lines later, it says something so strange that most of us skate right over it without noticing what we just read.

It begins with a deer. *As the deer pants for streams of water, so my soul pants for You, O God.* A parched animal, sides heaving, driven half-mad by thirst, searching the dry hills for the sound of water. That is what my longing for You feels like, the psalmist says. Not calm. Not settled. *Panting.*

And then, a little further down, when the same man is describing how far under he has gone, tears for food, God seeming absent, waves closing over his head. He says this: **deep calls to deep.**

In Hebrew it is *tehom el tehom qore.* And I need you to know what *tehom* is, because English hides it. *Tehom* is not just "the deep water." It is *the* deep, the primeval abyss, the vast dark formlessness that Genesis says lay over the face of the earth before there was any light at all. The unfathomable. The bottomless. The same word for the depths that burst open in the flood.

So read what he is actually saying. Drowning, undone, waves going over him. He does not say *I have lost my faith.* He says the *abyss* in his circumstances is calling out, and something just as bottomless in him is answering. And he includes the whole thing in a song of worship.

That single line rearranged how I understand longing. Let me tell you why.

## The deer is not doing anything wrong

We tend to treat spiritual longing as a symptom, a sign that something has gone wrong, a deficiency to be cleared up as fast as possible. If I still *ache* for God, we think, then I must not have enough of Him yet. The ache is the problem. Get more, and the ache should go quiet.

But look again at the deer.

The word for what the deer is doing, *ta'arog*, to pant, to long, is a rare and physical word. It is the sound of a creature that wants water *with its whole body*. And the psalmist does not present that panting as a failure. He presents it as the truest possible picture of a soul in the right relationship with its God. The deer is not broken because it is thirsty. The thirst is what proves it is *alive*, and it is what drives it, straight and unerring, toward the only thing that can save it.

> Your longing is not the evidence that God is far. It is the compass He built into you so you would keep running toward the water.

And notice the word the psalmist uses for the part of him that pants: *nephesh.* We translate it "soul," and that is not wrong, but it is thin. *Nephesh* is the whole living self, and its most physical sense is the *throat*, the seat of thirst and hunger and breath. So *my nephesh pants for You* is not a delicate, disembodied sentiment. It is a throat gone dry. It is the wanting that lives in your body, below your arguments, older than your theology. That is the part of you that longs for God, and Scripture treats that raw, bodily wanting not as a weakness to apologize for, but as worship.

## The depths were made to answer each other

Here is the line I keep returning to. *Deep calls to deep.* Abyss to abyss. *Tehom* to *tehom.*

There is a bottomlessness in God, an inexhaustible vastness we will be exploring for all of eternity and never reach the floor of. The Scriptures reach for the biggest words they own and still come up short: His understanding is unsearchable, His riches unfathomable, His depths past finding out. He is, in the truest sense, an abyss of glory. There is no bottom to Him.

And the astonishing claim buried in this psalm is that *there is a matching depth in you.* You were made in His image, and part of what that means is that He put a bottomlessness inside you too, a capacity for Him so vast that nothing smaller than God will ever fill it. You have tried to fill it with smaller things. We all have. And the ache never quite goes, because you were built with an *abyss* in you, and only *the* Abyss can answer it.

That is what *deep calls to deep* means when the waves are going over your head. The bottomless longing in you is not malfunctioning. It is doing exactly what it was made to do, *calling*, across the dark, to the only Deep that can hold it. Your emptiness is not the opposite of worship. Your emptiness, turned toward Him, *is* worship, the deepest part of you singing the one note it was tuned to sing.

## Longing itself is a form of praise

This is the thing I most want to leave with you, because I think it will set some of you free.

You do not have to wait until the ache is gone to worship. You do not have to manufacture a feeling of fullness you do not have. The longing itself, the panting, the reaching, the *God, where are You*, is already a form of praise, because it is your whole *nephesh* aimed, with everything in it, at Him.

Watch how the psalmist handles his own drowning. Three times in that song he turns and speaks to his own downcast soul: *Why are you cast down? Hope in God.* He does not scold the longing away. He does not pretend to be fine. He simply keeps aiming the ache in the right direction, *for I shall again praise Him.* He worships in the future tense, from the bottom of the present tense, while the waves are still coming. That is not a man who has stopped worshiping. That is worship in its rawest and most honest form: a deep that will not stop calling to the Deep, no matter how long the answer takes.

And here is the mercy hidden underneath the whole picture. A call implies a hearer. *Deep calls to deep* only makes sense if the other Deep is listening, if your longing is not flung out into an empty void but received by a God whose own depths rise to meet it. The panting deer is not chasing a mirage. There *is* water. The thirst was never a cruelty. It was the surest sign that the streams are real.

## So let yourself want Him

If you have been treating your longing for God as a spiritual failure, a sign you must not have enough faith, or enough of Him. I want to hand you a gentler and truer word.

That ache is holy. It is the deep in you calling out to the Deep in Him, exactly as you were designed to do. It is the deer running toward the water it can already smell on the wind. It is worship in the form of thirst.

So do not silence it. Do not be ashamed of it. Turn it, like the psalmist did, straight toward His face, and let the bottomless place in you go on calling, unashamed, across every dark, to the only One deep enough to answer.

He hears the call. He always has. And the depths in Him are already rising, at the sound of the depths in you.
