## The Worst Advice We Give Doubters

Somewhere along the way the church started handing out a piece of advice that has quietly wounded a generation: *leave your doubts at the door.* Come in clean. Sing like you are sure. Save the questions for a private crisis you will manage alone.

I made a whole album to say the exact opposite. Bring the question *in.* Carry it up the aisle. Lay it on the altar with your name still attached to it. Because the God I serve has never once been threatened by an honest question, and the altar was built to hold heavier things than your certainty.

> Bring the question, lay it at the altar. Fire is the answer. Holy Spirit's listening, every doubt a dancer.

That is the thesis of the record, and it starts where the church actually started, with a room full of people who did not have their answers yet.

## The Upper Room Was Not Full of Answers

We remember Pentecost for the fire. We forget the ten days before it. Acts tells us that after **Yeshua** ascended, about a hundred and twenty of His followers went back to an upper room and simply waited, devoting themselves to prayer. They did not have a plan. They had a promise they did not fully understand and a thousand unresolved questions about what came next.

They did not leave the room because the answer was slow. They stayed *in* the room with the questions unresolved, and that is where the fire fell.

One line keeps circling back: "Don't leave the room because the answer hasn't come, Spirit at the altar, and the altar is a drum."

I put a whole song on the waiting itself, because I think we have made an idol of the arrival and despised the wait. But the wait is not the enemy of faith; the wait is often the womb of it. The upper room is proof that **Yahuah** does some of His deepest work in the people who are willing to hold a question open long enough for Him to answer it His own way.

## Honest, Not Polished

There is a temptation, even at the altar, to dress the prayer up. To translate the rage into something churchier. To swap the real cry for the acceptable one. And the Spirit's answer to that, all through this record, is tender and blunt at once: *tell Me the truth.*

I wrote a verse about that exact reflex: "Tried to dress the prayer in language I didn't believe; the Spirit said, tell Me the truth, let the lies grieve."

Think of the woman in Mark's gospel who had bled for twelve years, twelve years of an unanswered question about her own body, and pushed through a crowd to touch the edge of **Yeshua's** cloak. He did not scold her for the desperation. He called her *daughter* and made her whole. Hebrews says we may approach the throne of grace boldly, not because we have polished ourselves, but because of the One who opened the way. Honesty is not the risk at the altar. Honesty is the whole point of it.

## The Silence That Broke Into Yes

The cinematic peak of the album is a moment I still cannot sing without a lump in my throat. It is built on a single line from Romans 8, that the Spirit Himself intercedes for us with groanings too deep for words. Every question you have ever whispered and could not finish, the Spirit was already carrying to the Father before you found the syllables.

In the song, the whole band drops out. Everything goes silent. And a whisper lands: *every question we have whispered, the Spirit has whispered yes.*

> The silence broke open in the middle of the prayer. What we have been asking, Spirit was already there.

Not yes to every demand exactly as we framed it. But yes to the deepest thing under the question, *am I heard? Are You there? Do You see me?* The Spirit's answer to that, spoken over Calvary and Pentecost and every honest altar since, is yes. And in the song, the silence detonates into a stadium of voices, because the moment a soul realizes it was heard all along, the only thing left to do is get loud.

### The Fire That Consumes but Does Not Destroy

There is a song near the end of the record about Moses and the burning bush, and I put it there on purpose, because it answers the fear underneath every honest question: *if I bring this to God, will it burn me down?*

Moses was on the back side of the desert, a man full of doubts and a stammer and a past, when he saw a bush wreathed in flame, and the strangest thing was that the fire burned and burned but the bush was not consumed. That is the exact nature of the holy fire I am inviting you into. It is not a fire that reduces you to ash. It is a fire that refines without destroying.

The song says it the way I need to hear it: "Every question consumed, but the listener remains, every doubt refined, every fire kind."

The voice from that bush said, *take your sandals off, this is holy ground*, and then it spoke the great Name: *I AM that I AM.* You can bring the hardest thing you carry into the presence of the great I AM, and the fire will burn away the lie without burning up the you. Doubt handled by God does not incinerate the doubter. It refines them. Take your sandals off. The fire is kind.

## From Kindling to Flame

There is a reason I called the record what I called it. The questions are not the opposite of the fire. The questions are the *kindling.* Peter, on the day of Pentecost, stood up and quoted the prophet Joel, that God would pour out His Spirit on sons and daughters, and they would prophesy. The same people who had huddled with their doubts in a locked room walked out and set a city ablaze. The doubt did not disqualify them. Once it was surrendered, it became fuel.

That is the turn the whole record makes: "Question becomes praise, Spirit-set on fire, every doubt is the kindling, every prayer is the choir."

So here is my invitation, and it is the one I most wish someone had given me earlier. Do not perform your certainty. Do not abandon the altar because you arrived with a fistful of hard questions. Bring them. Lay them down where the fire falls. Stand at the burning bush, where the flame consumed but did not destroy, and let **Yahuah** turn the very thing you were afraid to say out loud into the thing that finally sets you free. The altar can hold your hardest question. It was built for exactly that.
