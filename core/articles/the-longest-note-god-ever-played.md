Every musician knows a secret that most listeners never think about: the silence is written in.

Open any score and you will find rests, little marks that command the player to stop, to lift the bow, to let the air go empty for a beat, or two, or a whole measure. A novice thinks the rest is where the music isn't. A master knows the rest is where the music *breathes.* Take the silences out of a great piece and you do not get more music. You get noise. The pause is not the enemy of the song. The pause is part of it.

I learned that the slow way, in a season I would not have chosen. And out of it came the quietest record I have ever made. I called it **The Long Silence.**

## Fourteen months of a phone that would not ring

I want to be honest about where this album comes from, because pretending would help no one. There was a stretch, more than a year, when I prayed and the ceiling did not answer, when I knelt where I had always knelt and the hallway did not echo back a prayer. The first song is just the oldest question in the Bible, the one the psalmist asked and I asked after him: *how long, O Lord.* I had asked it now for fourteen months.

I built the second song, *The Phone That Doesn't Ring*, around a feeling anyone who has waited on **Yahuah** will recognize, checking the screen, telling yourself today will be the day you feel the tug, hear the name, and the screen staying dark. But the refrain that surfaced surprised me:

> The phone that doesn't ring is still the phone I love.

That was the turn. I was not going to delete the contact. I was going to be the woman by the door, the lamp left on through the night, the one who keeps the kettle warm because absence is not the same as gone, and silence is not the same as wrong.

## When worship goes quiet inside of you

Here is the part of the story I most needed to tell, because the church often will not.

There is a Sunday, maybe a year of Sundays, when the song begins, the people lift their hands, and you move your mouth and nothing in you stands. The chorus swells and you are dry. You cannot remember the last time you cried. I wrote *When Worship Goes Quiet* for that person in the cold pew, and the whole song is one stubborn decision: *I will not leave the room. I will let the choir sing what I cannot. I will let the liturgy hold what I forgot.*

Because the song is not the proof of love. The proof of love is that you came, and sat among your people again, and whispered, even, just His name. Scripture tells us not to give up meeting together, and I have come to believe that command is a mercy for exactly these seasons, when your feeling has gone silent but your body can still carry you into the room where the saints are singing your part until you can sing it again.

That is not weakness. That is *faith without feeling*, and the eighth song argues, gently, that it is the deeper kind. The warmth in the chest was never the proof that **Yahuah** was near. Love that only stays when love is felt is not yet love; it is only what was dealt. The vow that holds when the honeymoon is gone, the mother nursing through the longest dawn, the friend who shows up though the room is cold, that is the love that does not need to be told. And a faith refined past feeling is a faith that has learned to love a God it cannot, at that moment, find.

## The dark night is not the night of sin

A long time ago a Spanish contemplative named John of the Cross gave this experience a name, the dark night of the soul, and I set his idea to a slow processional in the fourth song. His insight is one the anxious heart desperately needs: there is a darkness that is *not* punishment. Not the night of sin, but the night of grace, where **Yahuah** empties the lamp to widen the place. It is the long, unlit hallway of the saints, where the eye lets go and the heart awakes.

I know how that sounds when you are in it. It does not feel like formation; it feels like abandonment. But I keep coming back to the mountain where the prophet stood waiting for God, and the wind tore the rocks and God was not in the wind, and the earthquake came and God was not in the earthquake, and the fire came and God was not in the fire, and then, after all the loud things had exhausted themselves, there was a low whisper, and *that* was Him. Sometimes the reason you cannot hear **Yahuah** is that you are still braced for thunder, and He has chosen the quiet.

## The silence was composing something

Then, slowly, the record turns toward dawn. The tenth song, *The First Word After*, is the truest thing on it. When the silence finally broke for me, it was not a thunderclap or a vision. It was a quiet sentence, on an ordinary Tuesday morning, a line I read that suddenly warmed, and I sat down on the kitchen floor and cried the way the long-bereaved cry. I had imagined trumpets. I had hoped for fire. What came was softer than desire: a simple sentence about being loved. And He did not shame the long unanswered years. He did not scold the asking through the tears. He simply spoke, and what He spoke was love, and every absence was understood in it.

Looking back, I could finally see what I titled the eleventh song, *The Silence Was Speaking.* The hush had been holding more than it withheld. The absence had been teaching me that presence cannot be earned. The years without a word had been hollowing out a chamber in me, widening the soul to hold a word too large to fit when everything was easy and bright. What I had called abandonment was a kind of school. The Master who instructs by withholding was teaching me a love that needs no proof.

So the album ends not with a finish but with a fold, a coming home into an always-held. **The Long Silence** does not resolve with a triumphant key change and a wall of sound, because that would be a lie about how these seasons actually end. It ends the way a long night ends: not loudly, but truly, like a friend who returns and does not need to explain, who simply sits and stays and stays again. The last note of the record is one held breath, and then real silence, six seconds of it, on purpose.

## For whoever is in the quiet right now

If **Yahuah** feels silent to you, hear this from someone who sat in that pew a long time: the rest is part of the song. The pause is where the music breathes. He is not gone; He is holding the longest note He has ever held over your life, and it is doing something in you that the loud seasons never could.

The asking is a kind of faith. The asking is the proof you stayed. A heart that questions has not left. And the silence you are enduring is, I promise you, speaking, hollowing the chamber, widening the soul, loving you the long and patient way, until the morning comes without announcement and you find, waiting in it, a deeper God than you had known to find.
