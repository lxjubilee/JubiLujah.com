Here is the fact that started the whole album, and I still find it almost too strange to believe.

A prophet named Elijah, running for his life and empty to the bone, climbs a mountain to meet God. And God, we are told, *passes by.* First comes a wind so violent it tears the mountains apart and shatters the rocks, *but the LORD was not in the wind.* Then an earthquake, *but the LORD was not in the earthquake.* Then a fire, *but the LORD was not in the fire.* And after the fire, the text says, there came *a still small voice.* A gentle whisper. In the old Hebrew it is stranger still, a phrase that means something like *the sound of a thin silence.* And *that*, that almost-nothing, that hush you would miss entirely if you had not gone quiet, that was Him (1 Kings 19:11-13).

Think about what God did there. He had at His disposal the three loudest sounds creation can make. Wind, quake, fire. Every one of them the kind of sound we would call *the voice of God* if we heard it. And He let them all pass, and He spoke instead in the register we are least equipped to hear.

I have spent my whole life on the wonder of sound, and I cannot get over this. **Yahuah** skipped the earthquake and chose the whisper.

## The quietest sound carries the most

We assume, always, that loudness equals significance. The big sound must be the important one. But it is simply not true, not in music and not in God.

A whisper carries less force than a shout. And yet a whisper can carry things a shout never could. You do not whisper a threat; you whisper a secret, a name, a love. The very quietness *is the meaning.* It says: this is only for you, come close, I am not performing, I am telling you the truest thing I have. In "Still Small Voice," the song at the heart of this record, I sing it the only way I found honest, *You who could have thundered chose the smallest sound. You who could have shaken chose the holy ground of a heart that learned to listen for a Word the noise won't drown.* The smallness is not a limitation on God. It is a tenderness *from* God.

And so the whole album, before it says anything about God at all, has to say something about us. If He speaks in the quietest register, then the problem was never His volume. It was our attention.

## The title is a discipline, not a place

I called it **The Listening Place** because I became convinced that hearing God is less about His willingness to speak and more about our willingness to build a room quiet enough to catch a whisper.

The opening song is literally the sound of a room being prepared, a broom on wood, a candle lit, a phone left outside the door. *I am building something quieter than I've ever built before.* Not a temple, not a tower. Just a little swept square of floor where *my hurry comes to perish.* Because here is the physics of it, and the theology of it, in one breath: a whisper is drowned not by God's absence but by ambient noise. Turn the room loud enough and the most important sound in the universe is simply *masked*, present, real, unheard. We are not usually deaf. We are usually just loud.

That is why the record moves the way it does. "After the Wind, the Fire" walks straight through Elijah's mountain and lands on the line I most needed to learn, *after the noise has had its will,* there is a smaller kind of glory that a louder one can't fill. "Breath Prayer" strips everything back to an inhale and an exhale, because when the words have all gone empty, the body still knows how to pray, *the lungs become a chapel and the diaphragm the bell.* "The Bell at the Hour" is about letting a single far-off sound interrupt your rushing and call you back. Every one of them is a discipline of getting quiet enough.

## What you hear when you finally stop

The turn of the whole album comes in a song called "What I Heard When I Stopped," and it holds the thing I most want you to know.

Because we are afraid of silence. We assume that if we stop the noise, what we will meet is emptiness, or accusation, that the quiet will *scold* us for everything the busyness has been outrunning. And I found the opposite. When I finally stopped the noise of working and the noise of news and the noise of explaining myself, *the quiet didn't punish and the silence didn't scold. It just sat down beside me like a friend grown soft and old.* And the first thing I heard was not thunder and not fire. It was *a love I'd been outrunning*, a Voice that had been there always, underneath the noise of my fears.

This is the promise of the oldest invitation in the Psalms: *Be still, and know that I am God* (Psalm 46:10). Not *strive.* Not *achieve.* Not *get loud enough to be worthy.* Be still. The knowing is on the other side of the stillness.

> The listening place is portable as the heart that turns to hear. And the cedar bench is anywhere the Shepherd's drawing near.

That is the closing line of the record, and it is the whole hope of it. The place is not a corner by a window. The place is a willingness, to turn the volume down and remember His kind Name, on a train, at a kitchen sink, in a hospice room with your hand against the pane.

## For the ones worn thin by the noise

I made this for the person who is so tired of being loud.

The one who has confused a busy life with a full one, and a full inbox with a heard prayer. The one who suspects, somewhere underneath, that if the noise ever stopped they would have to face how far they have drifted, and who is more afraid of the silence than of anything the silence holds. I want to tell you what the mountain told the prophet and what the stillness told me: the God who chose the whisper is not waiting on the far side of the quiet to shame you. He is *in* the quiet, already coming toward you, and He has been speaking the entire time in a register the noise kept masking.

He breathed the first breath into the first human's quiet clay (Genesis 2:7), and He is breathing inside you now, every undefended day. That is how close the whisper is. Closer than the air.

So build the small room. Sweep the corner, set the chair, light the candle, leave the phone outside the door. You are not manufacturing His presence. You are only turning down the interference on a signal that never stopped transmitting. Get still enough, stay long enough, and the smallest sound in all the world will lift out of the silence and you will realize with a rush that it was singing over you the whole time.

He skipped the earthquake for a reason. He wanted to be near enough to whisper.
