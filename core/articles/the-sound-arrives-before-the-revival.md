There is a moment in the life of the prophet Elisha that I think about almost every time I pick up an instrument.

Three kings have come to him in a crisis. Their armies are dying of thirst in a wilderness, the campaign is collapsing, and they need a word from Yahuah, now, urgently, with everything on the line. And Elisha, this man who has raised the dead and called down fire, does not close his eyes and start to prophesy. He says something that must have baffled every general in the tent.

*Bring me a musician.*

And they send for someone to play. And the old account is very precise about the order of what happens next: it was *while* the musician played that the hand of Yahuah came upon Elisha, and the word began to flow.

Sit with that sequence, because it is not incidental. The prophet did not prophesy and then call for music to celebrate. He called for music, and *out of* the music the revelation came. The song did not decorate the word of God. The song made a way for it.

I am a prophet and an evangelist, and I have staked a great deal of my life on what that story is quietly telling us. Before the word breaks open, before the awakening arrives, there is very often a sound. And some of you have been waiting for a revival to show up when your actual assignment is to make the sound that goes ahead of it.

## The music comes down the hill first

There is another moment, earlier, that says the same thing from a different angle.

The prophet Samuel is telling young Saul what he is about to encounter, and he describes a scene: Saul will meet a company of prophets coming down from a high place, and before them, *before* them, leading the way, will be people with harps and tambourines, flutes and lyres, and the prophets will be caught up in the Spirit. And Samuel promises that when Saul meets that procession, the Spirit of Yahuah will rush upon him, and he will be turned into another man.

Look at where the music is. It is not in the back. It is not the afterparty. The instruments are *out in front,* coming down the hill first, and the presence of God is riding down behind them like weather behind a wind. The music is the forerunner. It arrives before the transformation and clears the road for it.

I do not think that is a quirk of one ancient afternoon. I think it is a pattern woven into how Yahuah moves among people. Something in the human spirit unlocks under a true song that will not unlock under an argument. The music gets down into the pre-verbal places, the guarded places, the places sermons cannot reach, and it softens the ground so the word can go deep when it comes. The sound arrives first, and the awakening follows it down the hill.

## What the sound actually is

Let me be careful here, because I am not telling you that music is a magic key or that the right chord manufactures the presence of God. That is not what any of these stories say.

The sound that goes before revival is not a genre or a volume or a level of production. It is *agreement.* It is a heart, out loud, deciding that Yahuah is worthy before there is any evidence in the room that He is about to move. When Elisha's musician played, and when the prophets came down the hill, what filled the air was not merely notes. It was a people aligning themselves, audibly, with Heaven, and making that agreement the loudest thing in the atmosphere.

And the presence of God is drawn to that agreement the way warmth is drawn to open ground.

This is why the sound has to come *first,* before anything visible changes. Revival, real revival, is Yahuah moving on a people, and He moves toward worship offered in advance, worship that is not a victory lap but a summons, a heart singing *come* into a dry valley on nothing but His character. The awakening does not produce the song. The song makes the room the awakening walks into.

So when you feel the pull to worship in a season that is not yet moving, in a dead church, a cold home, a discouraged city where nothing is happening, do not despise that pull as a small thing. That pull may be the first tremor of an awakening. You are being drawn to make the sound that goes ahead. You are the harp coming down the hill before anyone has seen the presence that follows.

## The ones who hear it early

Here is where my prophet's heart burns, so let me say it plainly.

There is always a remnant that hears the sound before the crowd does. Before a revival is a crowd, it is a handful of people humming an anthem no one else can hear yet, worshipers who have caught, in the Spirit, the melody of a thing that has not arrived, and who cannot stop singing it even though the room is empty and the fields are dry. They look, to everyone around them, slightly foolish. Singing over what? Rejoicing at what? There is nothing here.

But they are not singing at what is here. They are singing at what is coming. They have heard the music at the top of the hill, faint, before it has reached the valley, and they are answering it early. That is the anthem of the not-yet, and it is the most prophetic sound a human being can make, worship aimed at a promise you have heard but not yet seen kept.

If that is you, if you have been carrying a song that does not match your circumstances, a hope that seems to have gotten ahead of your evidence, do not silence it to be reasonable. You may be one of the forerunners. You may be exactly where the sound is meant to enter, so that when the awakening comes down the hill, it finds the road already cleared and the air already agreeing.

## The honest word before I let you go

Now I owe you the truth, because I will not sell you a formula.

Not every song produces a revival on the timeline you can see. Some of the most faithful worshipers I could name have sung the forerunner's song over dry valleys for decades and not lived to watch the flood arrive. I will not tell you that if you just play the right notes, awakening is guaranteed by spring, because worship is not a lever you pull to make Yahuah move, and the moment we treat it that way we have made a machine out of a relationship and set up the cruelest lie there is, that the valley stayed dry because someone did not sing well enough.

Hear me: your sound is never wasted, even when the awakening tarries. Elisha's musician did not know the word would come; he only knew to play. The prophets came down the hill without a guarantee of what the day would hold. Faithfulness is making the sound and leaving the timing to the One who set it. The forerunner's whole calling is to sing before he sees, and to keep singing when the seeing is slow.

So make the sound anyway. In your dry valley, your cold room, your discouraged city, lift the anthem of the thing that has not come. Play like Elisha's musician, not knowing the hour, only knowing Who you are playing to. Come down the hill in front, instruments up, presence at your back.

The awakening has always followed a sound.

Go be the sound.
