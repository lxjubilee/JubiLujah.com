I stood at a river once, thinking about a child who did not exist yet.

Not a hypothetical child, a real one, coming someday, to be carried down to that same water and taught the prayers my grandmother taught me by name. And the strange, holy weight that settled on me at that riverbank became this whole album. Because I realized the most important thing I could ever hand a child is not a name, not a house, not a language, not even a song. It is a Person. And that Person already knows the child's name, knew it before the river, before the birth, before the stars were hung.

## Why This Record Had to Exist

I come from people who had things taken. Land signed away under a lie. Wages kept. Years spent and counted as rent. A whole inheritance drained across a century the way you take a breath. When you carry that history, you learn to ask a hard question about anything you try to pass down: *can this be stolen too?*

That question is the engine of this record. I wanted to sing about the one inheritance that no agent can stamp a paper on. On *The Inheritance That Cannot Be Taken*, I name what was lost plainly, and then I sing the thing that outlasts every theft:

> No system erased the Lamb, 
> the inheritance is Christ Himself.

Paul told the Colossians the mystery was *Christ in you, the hope of glory* (Colossians 1:27), and Peter said we have an inheritance *imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, kept in heaven for you* (1 Peter 1:4). Kept. Not by us. By Him. They took the cabin; they could not take the Christ. That is the future I want to leave a child.

## What the Title Means

*Children's Future.* But the future I mean is not a career or a college fund. It is a Name that was spoken over them before they drew breath.

The opener, *The Baby Born This Morning*, lands on a line I built the whole album around: *she is His before she is hers.* We lifted her by her own name, yes, but under the Name above her name, the way **Yeshua** was named on the eighth day (Luke 2:21). And on *When She Asks "Who Made the Stars,"* a four-year-old points at the brightest one and asks the oldest question there is, and her father does not rush. Psalm 8 and Genesis 1 are open in his hands. The answer he has been holding for her is this: the Father made the stars, the Son holds them in place, and the Spirit knew *your* name, daughter, *your name first, the stars second.* That is not sentiment. That is Isaiah's God, who says *I have called you by name, you are mine* (Isaiah 43:1).

## The Heart Behind the Songs

Let me walk you through the ache and the joy of it, because a real inheritance is honest about both.

*What I Want Them to Know* is a grandfather on a kitchen floor, doing what Moses commanded, *teach them diligently to your children* (Deuteronomy 6:6-7). And he boils a whole life of faith down to a chant a child can carry: *Jesus. The Cross. The Spirit. The song.* Christ first, always. *First Words I'll Teach Her* is tenderer still, water, milk, song, light, Father, but under the first words is the first Word: **Abba**. Paul says the Spirit Himself cries *Abba, Father* in us (Galatians 4:6). A child's first true word is His, crying in her chest before her mouth can shape the sound.

But I would not let this album pretend the road is smooth. *The Stories We Will Not Hide* is my vow to tell the family history straight, the hard years, the losses, the graves, because I serve a God who tells His own story straight. *The Cross is over the wound, not in place of it.* And *We Will Not Lose Another* is the line I draw with my whole body: we have lost cousins to the road, brothers to the load, the uncle to the bottle, the sister to the dark, and by the grace of God, *not one more.* I stand that ground on the promise of **Yeshua**: *no one will snatch them out of my hand* (John 10:28), and on Malachi's ancient word that He turns *the hearts of fathers to their children* (Malachi 4:6). The Father holds the cycle. And so we hold the children.

## The Ones Not Yet Here

There is a song on this record I could barely finish. *The Names of the Yet-to-Be-Born* holds two things in the same hand: the names of children coming, and the names of children who never came, a daughter's loss, a sister's two. I wrote it for the arms that are empty and aching. And the only comfort strong enough is the truth of Psalm 139: *in your book were written every one of them, the days that were formed for me, when as yet there was none of them* (Psalm 139:16). He has known every name from the foundation. Not one came late. Not one is lost. If you have grieved a child you never got to hold, that song is for you, and so is that verse. He keeps the Book. He has known.

The centerpiece, *The Child Sings First*, flips the whole thing on its head. A boy sings lead, and the elders hum underneath, not behind him, *under* him, holding him up. Because **Yeshua** said God has *prepared praise from the mouths of infants* (Matthew 21:16). The child sings first because the Father called first. The little one already knows what the elders had to walk a long road to find out: *You see me. You know me. You called me by my own name.*

## Who This Is For

This is for the young parent standing at their own riverbank, terrified of everything they cannot protect their child from.

It is for the grandmother who has kept setting a plate for a boy who has not come home yet, *The Boy Who Came Back* is for her, and the Father in that song is already running down the road while the rest of us stand still, exactly as He ran in Luke 15:20. It is for the family fighting to break a pattern that has run four generations deep. And it is for anyone who feels they have nothing worth handing down. Hear me: you have Christ, and He cannot be confiscated.

The album ends with *Every Generation Singing*, a great-great-grandfather of ninety-four and a great-grandson of two singing the same Name, because *one generation shall commend your works to another* (Psalm 145:4). The eighth generation is not born yet, and the tenth is only in His book, but the Name does not stop. On the final song, *We Hand Them Christ and the Song*, I say it as plainly as I know how: Christ first, the song follows, both kept under Him. Give a child everything you can. But give them Him first. He knew their name before the stars, and He will be holding it long after you and I have gone home.
