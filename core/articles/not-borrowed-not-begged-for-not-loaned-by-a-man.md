I want to sing you a line before I explain it, because the line is the whole argument. In the first song on this album, a woman stands up and says of the voice in her chest: *not borrowed, not begged for, not loaned by a man, Jesus, You gave the voice.* Given from heaven and held in His hand.

I made **Women's Authority and Wisdom** because I have spent a lifetime watching gifted daughters wait for a permission that was never theirs to wait for. And I have watched them mistake the source. So let me be a teacher and get the source exactly right, because everything. Everything, depends on where a woman believes her authority comes from.

## All authority is His before it is anyone's

Here is the doctrine that keeps this whole record from tipping into something it is not. This album is not about a woman seizing authority. It is about a woman *receiving* it from the only One who has any to give. The second song, my hit single, says it in a way I cannot improve on: *all authority is Yours, Lord, not taken, not stolen, not seized by a hand, and You commission whoever You want to in the land.*

That is straight out of the mouth of **Yeshua**. On the mountain, before He sends anyone anywhere, He says: *"All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore..."* (Matthew 28:18-19). Read the order carefully. First He claims *all* of it. Then He sends. Which means every calling that any of us carries, man or woman, is a *loan from the King*, not a possession we generated and not a prize we wrestled from someone else. This matters enormously for a woman, because the moment she understands her authority is borrowed from Christ, two things happen at once: she stops begging men for what only God can give, and she stops swaggering, because you cannot be arrogant about something you're only holding. The song's own line: *I don't carry a single thing that's mine.*

## He has been calling daughters the whole time

Now, the Scriptures. Because I do not build songs on feeling; I build them on the text, and the text is fuller than the church has often let on.

When **Yahuah** poured out His promise through the prophet Joel, He did not restrict the microphone: *"I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh; your sons and your daughters shall prophesy"* (Joel 2:28). Daughters, named, in the same breath as sons. And the Bible does not leave that as a theory. It gives us the women who lived it. Deborah *was judging Israel* under her palm tree, a prophetess leading a nation (Judges 4:4). When King Josiah needed a word from God about the newly found Book of the Law, he sent the priests to *Huldah the prophetess*, and she spoke, and the reformation of a kingdom turned on her word (2 Kings 22:14). That is why one of the songs is "Jesus Called the Elders", because the elders He called into my own life were women like Grandma Ruth, up at four in a cold house with a shawl and His Word, who never preached from any stage I knew but poured half of what I carry into me.

> Jesus, You called Deborah. You spoke through Huldah's mouth. You anointed Priscilla, You sent Phoebe to the south. What Jesus has commissioned, no silence can un-sign.

The song names these women on purpose, and I want you to know they are really there in the text. Priscilla, with her husband, *"explained to Apollos the way of God more accurately"* (Acts 18:26), a woman correcting the theology of a powerful preacher, and the Spirit saw fit to record it. Phoebe is commended as a *servant of the church at Cenchreae*, entrusted by Paul (Romans 16:1). And in that same chapter Paul greets Junia, *"outstanding among the apostles"* (Romans 16:7). Now, I am a careful teacher, so I will tell you the plain truth: the church has argued over that phrase for centuries. But the name is in the text, and the honor attached to it is in the text, and I will not pretend the Word is quieter than it actually is.

## Wisdom, not just a platform

But notice I did not title the album *Women's Authority* alone. It is **Women's Authority and Wisdom**, and the second word is the guardrail, because authority without wisdom is a loud, dangerous thing in anyone's hands.

The wisdom on this record is not the wisdom of the boardroom. It is the wisdom of the kitchen door. "Jesus, Teach Through Me" tells where my own theology actually began: *my first theology was Your voice at a kitchen door, where my mama opened Scripture and You walked across the floor. She didn't have a seminary, she had a worn-out King James book*, but the Spirit of the Living God put the fear of the Lord in it. That is the deep water this album swims in. The daughters God commissions are not usually the ones grasping for a stage. The chorus says it: *not from a podium, not from a stage, Father, write Your Word on the life I turn the page.* Real authority is a life you can read, long before it is a voice you can hear.

And there is a tenderness threaded all through the strength, because commissioned women get tired and shaken like anyone. "Jesus, You Hold Me Steady" is on this record right beside the fierce ones, because the same Christ who says *go* is the One who *holds*. His authority in Matthew 28 comes with a promise in the very next breath: *"I am with you always, to the end of the age."* You are not sent out alone to fend for the calling. You are held while you carry it.

## Who this is for

This is for the daughter who has been sitting on a gift, waiting for a man to hand her a permission slip. Sister, hear the first song: you do not need it. What **Yeshua** commissioned, no committee convenes to un-sign. This is for the older woman who thinks her season of usefulness has passed. It has not; Huldah's greatest word came late. It is for the young bride who found courage on a gravel road, and for every woman who quietly discipled a preacher and never got the credit. And it is written, deliberately, so that the men who love these women can stand next to them and sing it too, because in Christ *"there is neither male nor female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus"* (Galatians 3:28), and a household strong in the Lord raises its daughters, it does not silence them.

I built this record as a chapter-close, a passing of the mantle. Because that is what wisdom does with authority. It does not hoard it, it hands it on. The last song simply says *Jesus, You are forever*, and that is the anchor under all of it. The authority is His forever. He only lets us carry it for a while, in our own hands, for His own glory.

So stand up, daughter. Use the voice. It was given from heaven and held in His hand, and He gave it to you on purpose.
