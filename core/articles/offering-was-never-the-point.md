I made a record with drums loud enough to shake a floor, and its whole reason for existing is one sentence I refuse to let anyone blur.

*The drum is not the prayer. The drum is the floor the praise is lifted on.*

I say it that plainly because embodied worship, the dance, the drum, the lifted hand, the color and the movement, is beautiful and biblical and joyful, and it is also the exact place where a heart can quietly slide from worshiping the King to worshiping the celebration itself. This album, *Honor the King*, is a full-throated feast of praise, and it spends all twelve songs making sure the honor lands where it belongs. Everything we carry into His presence gets laid down at His feet. The offering was never the point. He was.

## A Word Before I Begin

Let me be honest about who is singing. I am a mixed-heritage believer who loves the drum and the dance and the gathered celebration, and I hold these forms with care. On this record they are offered *to* Christ, never venerated, never the recipient. The Grand Entry is a grand entry for the King. The regalia is laid before Him. Every drum bows to Christ. That is not a disclaimer tacked on the end; it is the spine of the whole thing.

Because you can lift your hands to the wrong throne. So I wanted to make joyful, dancing, drum-driven worship that never once forgets Who it is for.

## What the Title Means

*Honor the King.* The album is one long honor song, and it opens by walking through the gates the way the psalmist commands: *enter his gates with thanksgiving, and his courts with praise* (Psalm 100:4).

But listen to how carefully I frame it on *Sing and Celebrate for Jesus*:

> The song is not the recipient, the Lord is.
> The praise is not the destination, Jesus is.

Every drumbeat is a heartbeat *for the King.* The drum is the floor; the prayer is addressed to the Lord. Psalm 150 tells us to *praise him with tambourine and dance* (Psalm 150:4), and David danced before **Yahuah** with all his might (2 Samuel 6:14), embodied, unashamed, physical praise. This record says yes to all of it, and then aims every bit of it at **Yeshua**, the Lamb who was slain. That is the honor. The movement is real; the recipient is settled.

## The Heart Behind the Songs

Let me carry you through the celebration, because each song lays something different at His feet.

*Dancing in the Joy of the Risen Lamb* is the dance clothed in the right garment, *he has clothed me with the garments of salvation, he has covered me with the robe of righteousness* (Isaiah 61:10). And the line I keep coming back to keeps the ownership straight: *the dance is to Jesus, the dancer is His.* I do not dance to display my own righteousness. I dance because He put His robe on my back. The colors on me are the colors of His promise.

*Lay It All Before Him* is the beating heart of the album's theology, and it is drawn from one of the most staggering scenes in Scripture. In Revelation, the twenty-four elders, crowned, honored, seated near the throne, *cast their crowns before the throne, saying, "Worthy are you, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor and power"* (Revelation 4:10-11). Every gift I carry, talent, treasure, strength, song, is beautiful, but the gift is not what the Lord is after. He wants the heart behind the offering, laid all the way down. The crowns come off. He wears the crown.

*Honor Song for the Lamb* is the record's summit, built on the great anthem of heaven: *Worthy is the Lamb who was slain, to receive power and wealth and wisdom and might and honor and glory and blessing* (Revelation 5:12). The drums roll slow under that one, deliberate and weighty, because some honor is not fast and loud, some honor bows low. And *Circle of the Redeemed* gathers the whole nations-wide multitude John saw, *from every tribe and language and people and nation,* around the one center: the Lamb. Not the circle at the center, the Lamb at the center. *Salvation belongs to our God who sits on the throne, and to the Lamb* (Revelation 7:9-10).

## When the Praise Runs Past Words

I did not want this to be only a loud record. Some of the deepest honor happens when the words run out.

*When Words Become a Hymn* is the quietest thing here, a wordless humming that is not a technique or an incantation, but the heart's cry when language fails. It stands on one of the tenderest promises Paul ever wrote: *the Spirit helps us in our weakness... the Spirit himself intercedes for us with groanings too deep for words* (Romans 8:26). When you cannot find the sentence, the Spirit shapes your sighing into a prayer the Father receives. The honor rises even when the vocabulary collapses.

And *The One Who Met Jesus on the Road* is the testimony that keeps the whole record from becoming a performance, a gifted young man whose talent was perfect and whose heart was empty, until the Lord met him on a road he did not know, the way He met Saul on the way to Damascus (Acts 9). The line lands the album's thesis one more time: *the talent did not save him; the Lord did. The gift did not save him; Jesus did.* Same voice, new heart. The gift was never the point. The Giver was.

## Who This Is For

This is for the worshiper who loves to bring everything they have, the drum, the dance, the color, the whole body, and wants to be sure it is all landing on the right throne.

And it is for the worn-down one who feels they have nothing impressive enough to offer. Hear the record: He never wanted the impressive gift. He wanted *you,* laid down. *Healing in Christ* says it about the very music itself, *the music does not heal; the Lord does; the Lord is the Healer above the buzz* (James 5:14-15). Even our best offering is only ever the floor the praise stands on. The Healer is the Risen One, not the ritual.

The album closes twice, on purpose. *Go in Peace* sends the people home under the oldest blessing there is, *the Lord bless you and keep you; the Lord make his face shine upon you and give you peace* (Numbers 6:24-26). And then *Every Heart Bows to Christ* lands the whole feast where all things finally land: *at the name of Jesus every knee should bow... and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father* (Philippians 2:10-11). Bring the drum. Bring the dance. Bring the loudest, most joyful thing you have. And then lay it down, because He was always the point, and every honor was only ever on its way to Him.
