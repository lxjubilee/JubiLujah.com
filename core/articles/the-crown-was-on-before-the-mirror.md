There is a mirror in this album, and I want to tell you about the six o'clock in the morning I have stood in front of it.

Bathroom light too bright. Hair-tie around the wrist. The layoff letter or the breakup text or just the ordinary weight of a night that did not go the way you prayed. And there, in the glass, a diaspora daughter deciding whether she is worth anything today, waiting for the reflection to hand down a verdict about her value. I have been her. I made *Diaspora Daughters Rising* because I finally learned that she has been reading the wrong source for the truth about herself.

## The Reframe the Whole Album Turns On

The peak of this record is a song called **Crowned Before the Mirror,** and it says the one thing I wish someone had said over me a long time ago:

> Before the reflection met the eye, Faithful Jesus placed the crown high. Crowned before the mirror saw.

Peter tells the church that when the Chief Shepherd appears, the faithful *shall receive a crown of glory that fadeth not away* (1 Peter 5:4). But I have come to believe the crowning is not only a future ceremony. The world tells you to *earn* the crown at the mirror, try harder, look better, perform your worth into existence. **Yahuah** works in the exact opposite order. He sets the crown first, in secret, before you are awake, before the world sees, before you have done one single thing to deserve it. Identity is not built in the glass. It is received from the One who crowned you before dawn.

That changes everything about how a daughter gets up in the morning.

## Arise Is a Command, Not a Mood

I open the album with **Lagos to Lagos Rising,** and I anchor it on Isaiah, because the prophet does not gently suggest that we rise. He commands it: *Arise, shine; for thy light is come, and the glory of the Lord is risen upon thee* (Isaiah 60:1). Notice the grammar. You are not told to *generate* light. You are told to *arise* because the light *has already come.* The glory is not something you climb toward. It is something that has already risen upon you, and your only job is to get up into it.

That is why the sunrise runs through this whole record, Lagos at 5:30 a.m., Houston morning, Atlanta glow, London dawn. Same sun, same Lord, one family of daughters scattered across continents and waking to the same mercy.

And I want to say something as a teacher, carefully, because a lot of harm gets done to women in the name of self-improvement dressed up as sanctification. The mirror the world hands you only measures deficit, what is missing, what is aging, what does not compare. But Paul says believers *beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord* (2 Corinthians 3:18).

There is a different mirror. When you behold the glory of **Yeshua** instead of your own deficits, you are not shamed. You are transformed, *from glory to glory.* The direction is always upward, always toward more of His likeness, never a verdict of *not enough.* A daughter who learns to look into that glass instead of the accusing one gets up different.

## The Sisters Who Held the Line

I could not make an honest album about rising without honoring the women who held me up when I could not stand. The testimony at the heart of this record, **Quiet Strength of Sisters,** is the least flashy thing on it and the truest, Tupperware stacked at my apartment door when I could not cook, a Tuesday Zoom Bible study at 6:30 sharp, an eleven o'clock text that just said *"I'm covering you tonight, sister,"* a friend who drove three hours to sit on my porch in silence and did not need me to perform.

> Sisterhood is how Jesus shows up. Diaspora daughters fill the cup.

I recorded that testimony at ninety-six beats a minute, slower than everything around it, because it is the place the whole album exhales. There is no belt on it, no six-deep choir, just a piano, a soft talking drum, and the truth that some of the most important worship a woman ever offers is the plate of food she carries to a sister who cannot get off the floor.

That is not a nice sentiment. That is doctrine. *Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ* (Galatians 6:2). When the sisters carried what I could not carry alone, they were not just being kind. They were being the hands of **Yeshua** in the room. Rising is never a solo act. No daughter rises alone; she rises inside a circle of women who prayed her up.

## Pass the Crown Forward

And then a daughter who has risen has one more thing to do, hand it on. **Pass the Crown Forward** is about the woman who rose in 2025 reaching back for the girl who is twelve now and will rise in 2050. The psalmist charged Israel to tell the coming generation *the praises of the Lord, and his strength, and his wonderful works* (Psalm 78:4). Faith that stops with you is faith that dies with you. The crown is not a trophy to hoard. It is a legacy to relay.

The finale, **Diaspora Daughters Roar,** ends the record with four generations on the risers, kids on the front, mothers on the back row, grandmothers remembering when, every tribe and tongue lifting one sound, exactly the multitude John saw before the throne (Revelation 7:9–10). That roar is not arrogance. It is a daughter who finally knows whose she is.

## Who I Made This For

I made this for the daughter at the mirror. For the woman waking up under the weight of yesterday. For the one who has been taught, a thousand quiet ways, that her worth is a thing she has to earn back each morning at the glass.

Hear me, daughter. The crown was on before you reached the mirror. **Yahuah** placed it while you slept, before the world could weigh in, before you did anything to deserve it, and nothing the reflection says can take off a crown Heaven put on. So arise. Not because you feel ready. Because the light has already come, and the glory has already risen upon you.

Now get up into it. And when you are standing, reach back and crown the next one.
