I make a lot of quiet records. This is not one of them. **The Night He Came For Me** is the most extroverted thing I have ever recorded, Hammond organ, brass stabs, a full gospel choir, a whole album that ends in a shout. But I want to tell you about its softest sound, because that is the sound the whole record turns on. It is a knock.

## The night before the night you remember

Every testimony has a night you talk about, the night you finally opened the door. But there is a night before that one, and heaven remembers it even if you don't.

I wrote the opener, *The Night Before the Night*, from that hidden angle. There was an ordinary evening: same chair, same hum, a kettle on for nobody and a TV on for none. The rain was on the window like it had been for years. I did not know that tomorrow was the door He'd come through. I called it Tuesday. I called it tired. I called it fine. But somewhere out past the streetlights, past the highway, past the years, a Shepherd had already left the ninety-nine and turned His face toward my street.

That is the gospel **Yeshua** told about Himself, the shepherd who leaves the whole safe flock to go after the single lost one, and does not stop *until He finds it.* I built the entire record on that one phrase. Because the thing the parable insists on, the thing I most needed, is that the searching started on His side, long before it ever started on mine.

## I wasn't looking for Him

Let me be honest about the woman I was. I had not asked for **Yeshua**. I had a list of reasons built up like a wall, the hurt, the church I had left, the friend who wounded me when I called. I was not reading Scripture or humming any hymn. I was scrolling through my phone with the volume on slim. If you had asked me what I needed I would have said "another hour," not "the carpenter from Galilee with resurrection power."

The second song is a call-and-response between those two facts, and the choir sings the truer one back at me: *I wasn't searching the sky, but He was searching for me. I wasn't lifting my eyes, but He was lifting toward me.* There is a line in Paul's letter to Rome where **Yahuah** says, through the prophet, that He was found by people who were not even looking for Him, revealed to those who did not ask. That verse used to trouble me. Now it is the ground I stand on. You cannot earn a knock you did not know was coming. He did not wait for me to pretty up the porch.

## The first knock

And then, the sound.

*The First Knock* is the hinge of the whole album, and I produced it so the kick drum lands like a heartbeat in the wood. Three soft knocks on the door at ten, and the world bent in. Not loud, not angry, not the kind you fear, the kind that asks, quietly, whether anyone inside is good. I froze the way a deer freezes near a road. And I thought: *who knocks like that in the rain? Who knocks like mercy at ten o'clock at night?*

> The first knock, quieter than I thought heaven would be. Holier than anything had ever sounded to me. Not a fist, not a force, not a demand, just the patient, holy patience of a nail-scarred hand.

**Yeshua** said it about Himself in the last book of the Bible: *behold, I stand at the door and knock.* I had heard those words once on a Sunday-school felt board and never thought they would come true on a Tuesday, on the porch of a woman who had ignored the Lord. But there He was, patient as the moon above the trees, not breaking down the door, not shouting through the wood, just asking, *will you open up for Me?*

This is what I most want you to hear, because I think many of us are braced against the wrong God. We expect thunder, a demand, a shaming. We have steeled ourselves for a fist. And grace knocks softer than that. He could have kicked the door wide open. He could have summoned wind and fire. But love is gentle. Love is patient. Love just keeps on knocking higher, until the heart admits it.

## The chain I almost left in place

There is a song on this album I could barely finish writing, because it names how close I came to missing everything. In *The Door I Almost Didn't Open*, my hand is on the chain, my forehead is on the cold glass, and I am counting all the reasonable reasons to let the moment slip. *It's late. I'm tired. I don't know who that is. I'll regret this. I'll cry.* And every single reason was a small and reasonable lie, and every single reason was the enemy standing nearby.

If that is you tonight, with your hand on the chain, let me say it to you the way the bridge says it: He is the kindest knock you will ever hear. The voice on the other side of the glass is love, not fear. Slip the chain. Turn the latch. You are already standing on heaven's porch, do not let the morning come and find Him gone.

## He came in my worst hour, not my best

When I finally opened, He did not come in glory-thunder. He sat down on my hand-me-down couch like He had always been invited, took the cup I poured, and laughed at something small I said, the God who flung the stars across the dark, sitting in a living room where a cheap lamp made the wall go red. He asked about the photograph of my grandmother in her Sunday-go hat and said, *she prayed for you, and tonight her prayer is being answered in this room.*

And He came, I have to tell you, at my *worst* hour, not the polished, cleaned-up version I would have chosen. He did not wait for me to wash my face. He did not wait for me to dress the place. The Shepherd comes for the lost sheep in the field of her deepest mess, and He turns the whole hour into holy ground. He knew every secret without my telling Him, Scripture says **Yahuah** searches us and knows us completely, and He did not recoil and He did not list them. He just held the whole of me up like a song, and being known like that is the thing that finally told me I belonged.

## The smallest word that changes everything

The album's loudest moment is built on its smallest word. Three letters and a held breath. *Yes.* I had spent a lifetime not saying it, and He did not pressure and He did not preach. He just waited, patient as a father at the end of the prodigal's road, for the smallest word my trembling mouth could carry. And when it came up from somewhere underneath, the chain I had nailed across my chest snapped like a thread, and the whole room lit up like sunrise on the wall.

So I will end the way I end the record, in the song that gives it its name, with the oldest confession the rescued have ever sung: *I was lost, and now I'm found. I was bound, and now I'm free.* And the reason I made a whole loud, brass-and-organ, choir-shouting album out of one quiet knock is the reason of the very last song, *tell the story.* The Shepherd does not find the lost so they can keep the finding quiet.

There is someone still on the hillside tonight. Someone with a kettle on for nobody, sure that nothing is coming for them. He is already crossing the field. And when the knock comes, it will be softer than they braced for. Open the door.
