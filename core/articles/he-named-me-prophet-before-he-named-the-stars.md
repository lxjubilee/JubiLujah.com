Let me tell you about the moment this whole record was built to reach.

A youth conference. A prophet steps to the door of the auditorium, walks past the platform, past the polished ones, and lays a word on a daughter near the back, the kid nobody was watching. Her grandfather, an old deacon who has sat in a measured Baptist pew his whole life, catches it. His hand goes to her shoulder. And he leans in and says the four words that break every ceiling: *"Speak, daughter. You're bolder than you know."*

That is *Prophecy Rising (Bapticostal).* And I made it for every believer who was quietly, politely, over a lifetime, taught that the Voice of the Lord belonged to somebody else.

## Two Traditions, One Fire

The word *Bapticostal* is a made-up handshake between two rooms I love. The Baptist room gave me gravity, the closed hymnal, the reverence, the weight of a Word that does not need a hype man. The Pentecostal room gave me the fire, the Hammond leaning into the Leslie, the brass, the praise-break, the daughter going higher. I did not want to choose. I wanted the measured Baptist verse to *erupt* into the Pentecostal shout, because that is exactly how the Word moves when it finally lands on somebody who was told to stay quiet. It sits still. And then it can't.

The album opens with the Voice arriving. *"The Voice of the Lord Arrives"* stands on Psalm 29, the Voice of the Lord that breaks the cedars and shakes the wilderness. A woman is reading her Psalter on a Saturday morning and the Word comes near like it never had before, and the Father says the tenderest, most dangerous thing: *Daughter, let it go.*

## The Word Was Never Meant to Stay in the Pulpit

Here is the theology I fought for on this record, and I will put it in front of you plainly.

The Spirit of prophecy is not a title for the few. On the day the fire fell, Peter quoted Joel over the entire room: *"your sons and your daughters shall prophesy... and on my servants and on my handmaidens I will pour out in those days of my Spirit"* (Acts 2:17–18). Handmaidens. Servants. The people who were never given a microphone. And Revelation puts it beyond argument, *the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy* (Revelation 19:10). If you carry the testimony of **Yeshua**, you already carry the thing.

That is why *"Every Saint A Prophet"* stands on 1 Peter 2:9, *ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation.* You do not need a pulpit. You do not need a stage. The Holy Ghost will turn your kitchen into a prophetic age. The brother in the back pew has a word in his bones. The sister in the choir stand is not there to fill a robe. **Yahuah** hands out the gifts (1 Corinthians 12), and He did not run out before He got to you.

## Prophesy to the Bones, Even the Ones in Your Own Chest

The single I would put in your hand first is *"Prophesy Over The Bones,"* and it comes straight out of the strangest, most electric prophet story in the book.

Ezekiel is set down in a valley full of dry bones and **Yahuah** asks him a question no reasonable person answers with hope: *"Son of man, can these bones live?"* And the prophet, humble, says, *"O Lord God, thou knowest."* Then comes the command: *"Prophesy upon these bones... O ye dry bones, hear the word of the Lord"* (Ezekiel 37:3–4). He speaks. Bone comes to bone. Sinew, flesh, breath, and an exceeding great army stands up out of a graveyard.

I wrote that song so you would understand the valley is not always out there. Sometimes the valley of dry bones is *in your own chest.* A dead marriage. A cold call. A dream you buried and marked with a stone. And the Holy Ghost is standing next to you asking the same question, *will you prophesy?* Not wait for the bones to look alive. Speak first. **Yeshua** is *the resurrection and the life* (John 11:25), and He puts the breath in your mouth before the graveyard has moved an inch.

> Open your mouth over the deadest thing you own. The command came before the miracle every single time.

## The Daughters Have Their Say

I will not pretend this record is neutral about who gets to speak. It is not.

*"I Hear Him Say (My Daughter)"* is a woman at 5 AM with a journal open and the pen mid-air, and the Father whispers over her the thing He whispered before the world began, *I knew you before the world began; I named you Prophet, and the call still stands.* That is the title of this whole reflection, and it is the ground I stand on. He named the calling before He named the stars. Your gift is not a late idea. It is older than you are.

*"Open My Mouth"* is Isaiah at the throne, undone, *woe is me, for I am a man of unclean lips*, until the seraph takes a live coal from the altar and touches his mouth, and the Voice asks *whom shall I send?* and the trembling prophet says the bravest words in the Bible: *Here am I; send me* (Isaiah 6:5–8). And *"Speak, Lord, Your Servant Hears"* is little Samuel in the temple, learning to answer the Voice in the dark (1 Samuel 3:9), because before the prophet ever pours, the prophet listens.

I made this for the quiet one. The back-row daughter. The grandmother whose word lay buried in the pew for forty years. The grandson the church didn't know how to receive. The saint who has felt something rising for years and kept swallowing it because nobody handed her permission.

And I made it as a covenant word, too, because the calling was never only about you. *"Prophecy Over The Bloodline"* takes a pencil to butcher paper and names the generations, the ones who carried trauma and the ones who carried gold, and stands on the promise that **Yahuah** keeps *covenant and mercy... to a thousand generations* (Deuteronomy 7:9), while every curse the enemy tried to hand down is crossed out at the cross (Galatians 3:13). When you finally open your mouth, you are not just speaking for yourself. You are prophesying up a family tree and down into children not yet born.

Consider this your permission. The album ends where it began, *"Sons and Daughters Speak"* lifts the Voice that arrived in Track One and says it now lives in every mouth, on the strong and the weak alike, the last-days promise finally landing on the ones who were told to wait. The coal is already on the altar. The command has already come. So lift your head, open your hand, and speak the thing He put in you before the foundation of the world. Heaven has been waiting a long time to hear your voice, and it was always yours to lift.
