I need to talk to the ones who feel nothing. Not the ones having a hard week, the ones for whom worship has gone gray, where the songs land flat, where you stand in a room full of people lifting their hands and you feel like a stone in a river of feeling. You are not faking your faith. You just cannot find the warmth in it, and you have started to wonder if that means something is wrong with you.

Hear what the prophet Isaiah actually promised, because it was written for exactly this. He said Yahuah would give, to those who mourn, *the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness.* And most of us read right past the most important word in the line.

*Garment.*

He did not say the *feeling* of praise. He did not say the *rush* of praise, the *goosebumps* of praise, the *swelling in the chest* of praise. He said a garment, in the Hebrew, a *ma'ateh,* a mantle, a wrap, a covering. A thing you put on. With your hands. From the outside.

## What kind of praise you can wear

Think about what it means that praise can be a garment. A feeling is something that happens *to* you. A garment is something you *do.* You do not wait to feel dressed. You get dressed. You reach for the thing and you put it on, and the feeling of being clothed comes after, because you acted first.

That reframes the whole battle. If praise were only a feeling, then the empty seasons would leave you with nothing to offer, because you would have to wait around for the warmth to show up before you could worship. But Isaiah says praise is *clothing.* Which means it is available on the exact day you feel the least, because a garment does not require your emotions to cooperate. It only requires your hands.

You can put on praise the way you put on a coat over a shivering body. Not because you are warm. Because you are cold, and the coat is how the warmth comes.

## The exact heaviness it is made for

Look closely at what this garment is prescribed *against.* Isaiah calls it the *spirit of heaviness*, and the Hebrew word there, *kehah,* means faint, dim, faltering. It is the word used for a lamp guttering down to nothing, a wick barely smoking, a flame about to go out. That is not dramatic despair. That is the low, gray, flickering exhaustion so many faithful people live in and never name.

Yahuah is not oblivious to it. He does not scold the dim wick. In fact the same prophet promises He will not snuff out a faltering flame. He does something better than scold. He hands you a garment cut to the exact size of that faintness, and He tells you to put it on.

> The garment of praise is not for the day you feel like worshipping. It is for the day the flame is almost out. That is the day it was tailored for.

So if you are dim right now, you are not disqualified from praise. You are the intended recipient. The mantle was measured for people whose fire is low.

## Getting dressed in the dark

Here is where I have to be fierce with you, tenderly, because grace is not the same thing as permission to stay undressed.

There is a lie that says authentic worship must always be spontaneous, that if you do not feel it, offering it anyway is fake. That lie will keep you sitting in your heaviness for years, waiting for a mood that grief and exhaustion may never send. But putting on a garment you do not feel is not hypocrisy. It is faith. It is the deliberate, defiant choice to wear what heaven assigned you, before your feelings have voted, precisely because your feelings are not a reliable witness right now and you know it.

This is what David did in his lowest psalms. Watch him do it. He talks to his own downcast soul like it is a separate person, *why are you cast down?*, and then he *commands* it: hope in God, for I will yet praise Him. That is not a man riding a wave of emotion. That is a man reaching into the closet in the dark and pulling the garment on over a soul that did not want to get dressed.

You can do the same thing. You can stand up when everything in you wants to stay seated. You can open your mouth when it feels like reciting. You can lift your hands when they feel like lead. That is not you lying about how you feel. That is you putting on armor. Because that is the other thing a garment is, a covering, a protection. The enemy of your soul loves you undressed and shivering, convinced that since you feel nothing, you have nothing to give. The garment of praise is how you stop being an easy target.

And no, this is not you working up a feeling by force, and it is not a trick to manufacture a high. You are not dressing up to fool God or yourself. You are obeying the One who said there is a covering for the faint spirit, and trusting that the warmth comes *inside* the coat, not before it.

## Put it on today

Isaiah says the ones who receive this garment get a new name, *oaks of righteousness,* trees rooted and unmoved. But notice the order. The heaviness does not lift and *then* you praise. You put on the praise, and *that* is how the heaviness lifts. The garment comes first. The strength of the oak comes after.

So this is my charge to you, and I am not asking you to feel a thing before you obey it. Get dressed today. Reach for the mantle while the flame is still low. Say the true thing about Yahuah out loud even though it feels like reading someone else's mail. Lift the hands that feel like lead. Wear the praise you cannot feel yet.

The warmth is not the prerequisite. The warmth is what happens once you are covered. Put the garment on, in the dark, before the feeling, on the very day you have none, and let the God who tailored it prove why He never asked you to feel it first.
