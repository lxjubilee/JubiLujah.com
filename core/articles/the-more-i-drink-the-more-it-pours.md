If you have never been truly thirsty, this album will be hard to understand. Where I come from, water is not a background fact. It is the whole conversation. You measure a place by its well. You greet a traveler by offering it. A fountain in a courtyard is not decoration. It is the sound of life not running out. So when **Yeshua** sat down at a well in the heat of the day and told a thirsty woman about water that would become a spring inside her, welling up forever, He was not reaching for a poetic image. He was speaking the one language a desert people never mishear.

*The Eternal Conversation*, the record whose opening song I named "The Fountain Never Ends", is my attempt to live inside that promise for twelve songs. It is about the well that does not run dry, and the endless back-and-forth between a heart and the God who fills it. Because that is what living water finally is: not a single drink you take and leave, but a conversation that never has to end.

## The cracked cup at noon

The whole album begins with an honest admission. The opening verse is me arriving exactly as I am: *I came to the courtyard with a cup that was cracked and dry, I came to the well in the heat of the day with nothing but a thirst.*

That is drawn straight from the fourth chapter of John, the woman who came to draw water at noon, the hottest and loneliest hour, the hour you choose when you are avoiding the other people at the well. She came with an empty jar and a life full of thirst that water could never touch. And Yeshua, breaking every social rule to speak to her at all, offered her a different water, one that would end the endless trips back to the well, because it would become a spring inside her own chest.

I love that He met her at noon. The desert at noon is where you are most exposed, most parched, most alone. That is precisely where the Fountain chose to sit down and wait. So the album opens there, not clean, not full, not impressive. Cracked cup, dry throat, wrong hour. And the discovery that follows is the engine of the whole record.

## The paradox in the title

Here is the strange thing I found, and could not stop singing about. With ordinary water, the more you drink, the less there is. The well goes down. The jar empties. Everything the world pours for you works that way, the pleasure, the success, the applause, the next thing. You drink and it drains.

But the water Yeshua gives runs backward. The chorus of the opening song holds the paradox: *the fountain never ends, the fountain never ends, the water that You give me is the love that never bends; pour it in the courtyard, let it run across the sand.* And the second verse pushes it further: *the more that I am drinking, Lord, the more it pours and bursts.* The more you take, the more there is. Your capacity grows as you drink. That is not how any market fountain works. That is only how God works.

The bridge names the contrast the desert taught me: *whoever drinks the water that the world is always selling will thirst again, will thirst again before the day is done; but the spring that You have opened in the garden of my spirit is rising up forever to the glory of the Son.* I have drunk from the world's fountains. They are real, and they are salt. You drink and you are thirstier than before. Only one spring has ever left me less thirsty the more I drank.

> The clay jar at my shoulder is too small to hold this mercy, so I lift the cup and drink again, I drink it like the morning.

## Why I called it a conversation

I could have named this record simply after the water. I named it after the exchange, because a fountain that never ends makes something else possible: a relationship that never has to be rationed.

Much of my life I treated God like a well you visit. You go, you draw what you need, you carry it away, you ration it until the next trip, and you are always calculating whether you have enough to last. The living water ends that arithmetic. If the spring is inside you and it never runs dry, then you are never carrying a fixed supply that might run out before morning. You are in an unbroken conversation with the Source itself. You can drink now and drink again in an hour and drink again at midnight, and the well is not lower. That changes how a soul breathes.

The songs on the record are that conversation moving through a whole day and a whole culture. "Pour the Qahwa" is the welcome of the Host who never sends a guest away thirsty (Luke 14). "Saffron Morning" is the mercy that is new every morning, the well refilled before you even wake (Lamentations 3). "Indigo Midnight" is the heart still talking to Him in the watches of the night (Psalm 63), because the conversation does not close when the sun does. "The Open Courtyard" throws the fountain open to the outsider, the one who assumed the well belonged to someone else. From dawn qahwa to midnight stars, it is one continuous drink, one unbroken exchange.

## For the ones carrying a cracked cup

I made this album for the thirsty who have stopped believing there is any water that lasts. For the ones who have drunk from every fountain the world sells and are standing in the heat of noon with an empty jar, certain that thirst is simply the human condition and there is nothing to be done but keep hauling water that always runs out.

There is another well. It is not in a far country or a foreign tongue. **Yeshua** is still sitting at it in the hottest hour, still willing to break every rule to speak to the one who came at noon to avoid the crowd, still offering the water that becomes a spring inside your own chest. Bring the cracked cup. Bring it to the courtyard. Drink, and then, this is the whole secret of the record, drink again, and find there is more than before. The fountain never ends. The conversation never has to. Come and be thirsty here; it is the one thirst that answers itself.
