Every month of my life there has come a morning when the figures did not add up on paper, and I have learned to do one thing before I do anything else with them. Before I count the dollars in the drawer, before I write the figures in the book, I sit down on the front-porch floor and I lift my eyes to where the Father looks. **Resource and Stewardship** is the album that came out of that porch floor, a whole record about money and provision and the strange, freeing arithmetic of the kingdom, where the God who owns everything hands it to people who own nothing and calls them faithful.

## Jehovah-Jireh saw the ram before Abraham saw the thicket

The record opens with the oldest name for provision in the Book. When Abraham climbed the mountain with his son and the knife, trusting God for what he could not see, **Yahuah** caught a ram in a thicket behind him, and Abraham named the place forever: *"The LORD will provide"*, Jehovah-Jireh (Genesis 22:14). The song is built on the tense of that mercy, and I want you to feel it: *You saw the ram inside the thicket when I could not see. You see the rent before the paycheck. You see the bread before I knead.* The provision was already caught in the bush before Abraham ever turned around. God's supply is not a reaction to your crisis. It was set in place before your crisis arrived.

That is why the album refuses to worry the way the world worries. **Yeshua** pointed at the birds, *"Consider the ravens: they neither sow nor reap... yet God feeds them"* (Luke 12:24), and drew the only sane conclusion: *"Look at the birds of the air... are you not of more value than they?"* (Matthew 6:26). The song sings it plainly: *the ravens never sowed a single seed, the lilies never spun a single thread, yet You feed them, Father, every one in need, and You have never left me unfed.* This is not a promise of luxury. It is a promise of a Father, and a Father who feeds ravens does not forget His own children.

## I am only the hands that hold it

Now here is the hinge of the whole record, and it is the difference between provision and stewardship. It is one thing to believe God supplies. It is another, deeper thing to believe that what He supplies was never yours to begin with.

The chorus of "Jehovah-Jireh" makes the confession that reorganizes a person's entire relationship to money: *Father, You are the Provider. I am only hands that hold. Every gift inside my keeping is a gift that You controlled.* That is straight from David, at the height of Israel's wealth, praying over the offering for the temple: *"But who am I, and what is my people, that we should be able thus to offer willingly? For all things come from you, and of your own have we given you"* (1 Chronicles 29:14). Of your own have we given you. And **Yahuah** says it Himself through the prophet: *"The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, declares the LORD of hosts"* (Haggai 2:8). If you believe that, really believe it, the whole weight of ownership lifts off your shoulders. You are not a proprietor defending a hoard. You are a steward holding, for a while, something that belongs to Someone kind.

> Before I count the dollars in the drawer, before I write the figures in the book, Father, I sit on the front-porch floor and I lift my eyes to where You look. I am only hands that hold; every gift inside my keeping is a gift that You controlled.

## What the hands are for

If the resource is His and I am only the hands that hold it, then the songs have to answer the obvious question: hold it *for what*? And the record spends its middle answering, because a steward who only clutches has missed the calling entirely.

There is "Jesus, Multiply It," built on the boy who handed over five loaves and two fish and watched **Yeshua** feed thousands, because in the kingdom, what you release multiplies and what you hoard rots. There is "Jesus, I Sit Down First," on His own counsel: *"Which of you, desiring to build a tower, does not first sit down and count the cost?"* (Luke 14:28). Stewardship is not reckless generosity; it is *counted* generosity, wisdom and open hands together. And the Lord who owns it all still asks us to give it back cheerfully: *"Each one must give as he has decided in his heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver"* (2 Corinthians 9:7). He does not need your money. He wants your heart, and He knows the two travel together, *"where your treasure is, there your heart will be also."*

And He measures it upside down from the world. When the widow dropped in her two small coins, He said she *"put in more than all"* the wealthy givers, *"for they all contributed out of their abundance, but she out of her poverty put in everything she had"* (Mark 12:43-44). The kingdom does not count the size of the gift; it weighs the size of the trust behind it. That is the freedom of the record's final songs, "Jesus, Teach the Children" and "Father, You Are the Forever Giver", because the surest sign a steward has understood the lesson is that she is unafraid, generous, and already teaching the next generation to hold with open hands.

## Who this is for

This is for the family staring at a budget that does not close, doing the porch-floor math before the paycheck comes. It is for the person who has enough and is quietly terrified of losing it, sister, you cannot lose what was never yours; you can only steward it or hoard it. It is for the giver and the tither and the one who has nothing but two coins and wonders if they even count. They count the most. And it is for anyone raising children who need to learn, early, that the drawer was never theirs.

Remember how the parable of the talents ends. Not with an audit of net worth, but with a word over a servant who was *faithful*: *"Well done, good and faithful servant. You have been faithful over a little; I will set you over much"* (Matthew 25:23). Faithful over a little, because *"one who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much"* (Luke 16:10). That is the whole assignment. He provides it. You only get to hold it. So hold it well, hold it open, and lift your eyes to where the Father looks before you ever count the drawer.
