## The Direction of the Tear Tells the Whole Story

For centuries there was a curtain in the temple, thick, tall, woven blue and scarlet, hanging between the people and the Holy of Holies, the room where the presence of God dwelled. Only one man, one day a year, could go behind it, and not without blood. The message of that curtain was unmistakable: *stay out. You cannot come in here.*

Then, at three in the afternoon on the day **Yeshua** died, Matthew tells us the veil of the temple was torn in two, *from top to bottom.* And that little detail is the sermon. Sixty feet of temple curtain does not rip itself. And it certainly does not tear from the top, where no human hand could reach. The direction gives away the author. This was not man clawing his way up into God's presence. This was heaven reaching down and ripping the barrier open from above.

The song makes the point unmistakable: "The Father's hands made the curtain fall to the ground; the path is open. There's no more middle ground."

I made a whole album on that torn curtain, and the first thing I had to settle was *why.* Why tear it at all?

## He Tore It for a Home, Not a Peek

Here is the wrong way to read the torn veil, and it is the way a lot of us secretly carry it: as if God cracked the door so we could cautiously peek inside, hold our breath, and hope we do not get struck for being there.

That is not why He tore it.

> He didn't tear the curtain so we'd peek. He didn't tear the curtain for the meek to stay weak. He tore it for a home. He tore it for a heart.

The book of Hebrews says it plainly: we now have boldness to enter the holy place by the blood of **Yeshua**, by a new and living way that He opened for us through the curtain, that is, through His flesh. His own body was the veil that tore so that yours could walk through. He did not open the way so you could stand in the entrance and apologize for existing. He opened it so you could come *home.* The room that was off-limits for the whole history of the world now has your name on the door.

## The Woman Who Was Afraid of the Altar

The tender center of the record is a testimony I could not stop thinking about, because it is so many of us. A woman who grew up terrified of the front of the church. The carpeted altar step felt too steep, the brass kneeler rail too holy for hands like hers. She sang about God's presence from the back pew. *Don't touch the ark* was the rule she lived by, until the day the Spirit said, *the curtain is torn, daughter. Walk in.*

Her knees buckled at the step. She braced for the strike she was sure she deserved.

> I thought He'd say, "You don't belong." He said, "The curtain is gone, child, and the chair is yours."

He did not strike her. He *held* her. That is the whole difference between religion and the torn veil. Religion keeps you at the rail, hoping not to be turned away. The torn curtain says the Father stood up when you walked in, like He had been waiting in that room the entire time for you to finally believe you were allowed. Hebrews invites us to draw near with confidence, not because we cleaned ourselves up, but because the blood already did.

## Not a Peek Through Glass, Face to Face

The album climbs to a moment built on Paul's line in First Corinthians 13, that now we see through a glass dimly, but then face to face. And the revelation at the peak is almost too tender to sing: **Yeshua** is not hiding.

The peak of the record lands there: "He's not hiding. He's standing in the open; the veil is torn, the room is wide and open."

We carry this quiet suspicion that God is elusive, that He tore the curtain but is somehow still tucked away in the dark at the back, difficult to find, reluctant to be seen. The gospel says the opposite. He did not tear the curtain in order to keep residing in the unreachable place. He tore it and stepped into the open. When Moses spoke with God it was face to face, as a man speaks with his friend, and in **Yeshua**, that same access is handed to every believer, north to south, young and old. There is one Mediator, and through Him the throne is not a place you sneak up on. It is a room where the Father's eyes light up when you enter.

### What You Behold, You Become

There is a quieter song late on the record where the Father Himself speaks, and it names what actually happens to a person once they stop being afraid of the room. Paul writes in Second Corinthians 3 that when anyone turns to the Lord, the veil is removed, and then all of us, with unveiled faces, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image, from one degree of glory to another.

Read that slowly, because it is the mechanism of the whole Christian life. You are not changed by striving harder at the back of the room. You are changed by *beholding*, by finally walking in and looking at His face.

In the song the Father says it plainly: "Child, what you behold is what you become, keep your eyes on My face, the veil is undone."

That is why the torn curtain matters so much for who you are becoming, not just where you are allowed to stand. As long as the veil kept you at a distance, transformation stayed out of reach. You cannot be changed by a face you are not allowed to see. But the curtain is torn, and now the beholding is yours, and the beholding is the becoming. **Yahuah** is not only letting you into the room. He is remaking you in it, glory to glory, by the simple grace of letting you look.

## From the Veil to the Throne

I closed the record on the whole arc compressed into one breath, from the curtain that tore at Calvary to the throne where a formerly-terrified believer now stands. From the carpet step she would not cross to the chair beside His hand. From a room you could enter once a year, if you were the right person, with the right blood, to a daily walking-in.

The finale compresses the whole arc: "From the Holy of Holies once a year, to the daily walking in; veil to throne, bold and steady."

So if you are still standing at the back, still convinced the front of the room is for holier people than you, hear me. The curtain did not tear so you could peek. It tore from the top down, by a hand that was not human, so that you could come all the way in and stay. The chair is yours. The Father is already standing. Walk in.
