Here is the image the whole record hangs on, so I will give it to you first.

You are already at the bottom. You made your peace with it, the pit is a basement with no ladder and no friend, and you have decided this is where the story ends. And then the floor of the pit itself gives way. You brace to fall further, to hit something harder, to lose the little ground you had left. And instead of falling. You *rise.* Because it turns out there was a floor beneath the floor, and it had a name, and it was holding you the whole time.

That is *Pit To The Party.* And I dressed the worst night of a life in a warm reggae one-drop and a block-party groove on purpose, because I refuse to let the enemy have the only soundtrack for the low place. He does not get to score your bottom. I do.

## Why the Pit Earns Its Own Album

I could have started at the celebration. Plenty of gospel does. But I did not want to hand you a party and pretend I skipped the pit to get there, because that is a lie, and lonely people can hear a lie a mile off.

So the record starts *in the miry clay.* Track one plants you at the bottom where the concrete met your back, cellphone at four percent, a message you could not send, and then a voice in the dark says *your bottom is not the end.* That is Psalm 40, and I built the whole album on it: *"He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings"* (Psalm 40:2). Notice **Yahuah** does not shout down instructions from the rim of the pit. He reaches *into* the clay. He gets His own hands dirty pulling you out.

And when He pulls you up, He does not leave you standing there ashamed in your rags. He runs. *"Best Robe On"* is the prodigal, the son who rehearsed a servant's speech the whole way home, and *while he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him* (Luke 15:20). The best robe. The ring. The fatted calf. *Let us eat, and be merry* (Luke 15:23). He did not make you earn it. He wrapped you in the best before you got your apology out.

## The Party Is a Doctrine, Not a Mood

I want to be careful here, because "party" can sound like I mean a vibe. I do not. The party in this record is theology.

When **Yeshua** told the prodigal story, He was answering people who were scandalized that He ate with sinners. And the point of the whole story is that heaven *throws a feast* when the lost come home. The celebration is not decoration on top of the grace, the celebration *is* the grace, made loud enough for the whole block to hear. That is why *"Mourning Into Dancing"* stands on Psalm 30:11, *"thou hast turned for me my mourning into dancing; thou hast put off my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness."* He does not just dry the tears. He unbuckles the sackcloth you tied around your own waist and hands you a dance.

> Beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness, Isaiah 61:3 is not a metaphor. It is a trade, and He always overpays.

*"New Creation Now"* is the same grace in a different key, the torn-up ID that doesn't fit the face anymore, the same address with a brand-new tenant and a new lock on the door. *"Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new"* (2 Corinthians 5:17). Old you couldn't stay. That is not you working harder. That is Him rewriting the story and showing you how.

## The Bottom Fell Out for Good

The peak of the whole record, and the reason for the song I put in the frontmatter, is *"The Bottom Fell Out for Good."*

Everything you clung to comes apart in your hand, job, pride, reputation, slipping like desert sand. And you are certain this is the end of you. But *the losing of the bottom was the finding of the Rock.* Every door you thought was slamming shut was **Yahuah's** hand turning a lock you couldn't reach. He was the floor beneath the floor. He caught the falling and turned it into flight.

I have lived that verse. Most people who will ever love this album have lived it too. The night the last thing gave way and you found out you were not going to keep falling, because underneath the bottom of your bottom was the everlasting arms, and they do not have a lower limit. He turned the pit into a *door.*

That is why the tenderest song on the record, *"Mama's Kitchen Prayer,"* matters so much to me. Not everybody at the party got there by their own testimony. Some of us got carried on somebody else's knees, a mother at the burner turned to low, cornbread in the oven, praying for a child longer than that child had been gone. And **Yeshua** followed every syllable of that prayer down every dark road until He brought the runaway home. If you are still out there, hear me: somebody's kitchen prayer has a homing signal on you, and He is honoring it right now.

## Who Gets an Invitation

Everyone. That is the whole point of the last act.

Once you have been pulled out of the clay, you cannot keep the address to yourself. *"Whole Block Invited"* is the overflow, if He did it for the girl on the curb outside the store, He can do it for the whole block that got counted out. His grace is not rationed to the righteous, and it is not a private feast. The table is set for the greatest and the least, the corner boys and the church folk and the sister who lost hope and the auntie on the stoop. *"Beauty for the Ashes"* carries it across generations, grandmama came up out of her own valley and told me how He turned her ashes into gold, and now my daughter is watching the beauty get rebuilt in me. And *"Dance Out the Door"* sends the whole thing back onto the avenue, because the grace that found you at the bottom was always meant to send you out.

I made this record for the one who is sure the pit is the last chapter. For the runaway rehearsing a speech she will never need. For the person who has already braced for the floor to give way again. Hear the good news in the groove: the bottom is not the end, the funeral is about to become a party, and the God who reached into the clay is the floor beneath every floor you will ever stand on.

So dust off. Put the robe on. The music has already started, the whole block is invited, and there is a plate at the table with your name on it. Come on to the party. He has been holding your seat.
