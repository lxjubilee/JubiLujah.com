I built a whole altar record around the one thing I would not lay on the altar.

For nine years I would not say the name. The bitterness wore my face like a second skin, and I brought **Yahuah** a thousand other offerings. I served, I sang, I gave, I showed up early and stayed late, and the one thing I refused to name was quietly naming *me* from the inside. That is where *Burn It All* comes from. Twelve songs about a truth I had to learn the slow way: you can worship at the altar for years while carefully keeping the real offering in your pocket.

I set it to a contemporary band-led praise break, Hammond on the fast Leslie, kick-snare drive, brass punching every modulation lift, because I wanted the surrender to feel like release, not loss. The altar is loud on this record. It should be. Something is finally coming off.

## The Altar Is Set to Take More Than Your Stuff

The record opens with the match. *"Fire Fall (Burn It All)"* is the offering laid on the wood, *every weight I dragged inside, every name I hid behind*, and the one line that governs the whole album: *what survives is what was Yours.* That is the promise and the warning together. The fire does not destroy what belongs to Him. It only burns what was never built to hold.

Then comes the hard part, the part most of us skip. *"Lay Down the Crown"* names the throne I carved out of my own pride and called freedom, a crown built from my own gold, my own name in the band, handed over to the only King who ever wore thorns. *"Tear Up the Blueprint"* takes the plan I drew in my own ink and hands the pen to the Architect who sat down at my kitchen table, because *"a man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps"* (Proverbs 16:9).

> I kept bringing the altar my things. The altar kept quietly asking for me.

## The Refiner Sits Down and Waits

The center of the whole record, the song I put in the frontmatter, is *"What Survives the Fire,"* and it is the most honest thing I have ever recorded.

The anchor is a picture from Malachi that changes how you see every hard season: *"he shall sit as a refiner and purifier of silver"* (Malachi 3:3). *Sit.* He does not rush the fire. The refiner sits over the metal and waits, waits until the dross rises and the gold goes clear, waits until He can see His own face reflected in it. And Peter says the same thing about your trials: *"that the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire"* (1 Peter 1:7). The fire is not proof He left. The fire is proof He is *sitting right there,* refusing to leave until the gold blushes.

For nine years I brought Him everything but the bitterness. And on that song I finally said the name out loud, the lyric says *minute forty,* and that is true to how it happened, and the shoulders I had carried dropped like stone. Here is what I learned in that moment, and it is the whole revelation of the album: *the offerer became the offering.* I came to the altar carrying a thing to give, and the altar wanted *me.* Romans 12:1 had been in front of me the whole time: *"present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service."* Not your stuff. *You.*

## Everything on the Altar Has a Name

I did not want this to stay abstract, so the middle of the record names the specific things we hide. *"Burn the Fear Down"* takes the 3 AM ceiling-stare, the list of worries on the chair, and stands on *"perfect love casteth out fear"* (1 John 4:18), Love as a Person filling the room like incense. *"Burn the Bottle"* names the thing at 2 AM we drink or scroll or swallow to numb the pain, and lets the chains drop in the flame. *"Burn the Anger Out"* is the slow-burn coal I fed for years, handed back to the only One who can hold it, *"Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord"* (Romans 12:19).

And *"Burn the Lust Away"* does something I think the church needs more of: it does not kill desire, it *purifies* it. The heart was built for Him and Him alone. Augustine, not Scripture, but a saint who knew, said it best: our hearts are restless until they rest in Thee. The fire on that track is not shame. It is a want made holy.

There is one more thing on the altar that surprises people, and *"Burn the Wheel"* names it: control. The white-knuckled grip on a road you cannot see at midnight, the plan you steer by fear. Proverbs says it straight, *"Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths"* (Proverbs 3:5–6). The grip is an offering too. The road only opens when the hands finally let go of the wheel.

## Who I Made the Fire For

I made it for the faithful one who has been to a thousand altars and still walks around with the real thing sewn into her chest. For the one carrying a name she will not say, a grudge she keeps rehearsing, a plan she cannot release her grip on. For everyone who thinks the hard season is punishment when it is actually the Refiner sitting down, patient, over gold He refuses to lose.

Because the record does not end in ashes. *"Living Sacrifice Walks Out"* takes the whole self off the altar and back onto the street as the offering. *"Ashes Singing Back"* watches the ash pile start to sing, because *"they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony"* (Revelation 12:11), the very thing that burned becomes the weapon. And the capstone, *"Walk Away Light,"* re-frames the entire fire: it was never anger. It was the kindness of the cross. Greater Love walked in on the first song and walks out on the last, and the same Hand that lit the fire is the Hand holding the gold He pulled from the ash.

So bring Him the one thing, beloved. Not the thousand substitutes, the one you have been protecting. Lay it on the wood and step back. The fire in front of you is not here to consume you. It is here to give you back to yourself, holy and whole, with the gold finally showing. You will not walk away burned. You will walk away *light.*
