I am named for a mountain, so let me tell you what the mountain taught me: nobody makes the long climb on their own strength. They only think they do until the air gets thin.

This is my walking record. It is for the ones who are still on the trail when their own legs gave out miles back, the ones putting one foot in front of the other on a road that has cost them more than they ever planned to pay. I did not build it out of my usual mountain hush. I built it as a full worship-band anthem, drums driving, choir answering, because some seasons you do not need a whisper. You need a cadence to keep walking to. And the cadence of this whole record is one word repeated until you believe it: *walk on.*

## Why This Record Had to Be Loud

Quiet is for the summit. But most of life is not the summit. It is the switchback in the cold, the stretch where you cannot see the bend or the rise, where the only honest prayer is *I am so tired.* I have been on that stretch. And I found out something there that I could not have learned anywhere else.

The engine of this whole album is one verse that sounds backward until the day you need it. Paul begged God three times to remove a thorn, and God answered: *my grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness* (2 Corinthians 12:9). So Paul made the strangest boast in Scripture, *I will boast all the more gladly of my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may rest upon me.* That is the title track, *Strong Heart Walking*: not the strong-of-the-tall, but the weak-made-strong. *My strength has run out; His strength is enough.*

## What the Title Means

*Strong Heart Walking.* But read it right, the strong heart is not mine. It is His, beating underneath a heart that has nothing left.

On *By His Strength*, I sing Paul's other great line, *I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me* (Philippians 4:13), and I anchor it to Zechariah so nobody mistakes it for self-help: *not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit, says the Lord* (Zechariah 4:6). This is not a record about digging deeper and finding hidden reserves. There are no hidden reserves. This is a record about what shows up *after* your reserves are gone, the strength that is not yours, arriving exactly when yours runs out.

## The Heart Behind the Songs

Let me walk you along the trail, because it is honest about how hard the walking gets.

*Grace in the Weakness* is the quietest song here, and it names the real places: *my anxiety is the place of His peace. My sleeplessness is the place of His ease. My family wound is the place of His healing. My addiction history is the place of His feeling the weight of the cross.* Where I am weak, He is mighty in me. And *Step After Step* is the one I reach for when the whole road feels impossible:

> I do not need the whole map of the road, 
> I need the next step under my own load.

That is Galatians 5:25, *if we live by the Spirit, let us also walk by the Spirit*, and Psalm 37:23, *the steps of a good man are ordered by the Lord.* You do not have to see the bend. You have to take the next step. The Spirit goes ahead, at your side, and behind you where the dust would hide.

*Long Road Saints* lifts my eyes to the ones who walked before, Abraham without knowing the land, Moses with a staff, the martyrs through fire, the grandmother on her knees every dawn. Hebrews 12 calls them *a great cloud of witnesses,* and the album is careful here: they are runners we follow, not intercessors we pray to. They finished the race. We run the same place. And *Heartbeat of Hope* strips all the way down to a single kick-drum beating alone, revealing that the rhythm under every gathering was always meant to be *His* heart, Romans 15:13, *the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing.*

## The Song for the Ones About to Quit

There is one testimony on this record I need you to hear, because it might be the reason you keep going.

*I Almost Quit* is Elijah at the cave. The prophet who called down fire from heaven collapsed under a broom tree and prayed *it is enough; now, O Lord, take away my life* (1 Kings 19:4). And here is what wrecks me every time: God did not lecture him. God did not condemn him. God sent bread and water and let him sleep, and then came, not in the wind, not in the earthquake, not in the fire, but in *a still small voice* (1 Kings 19:12). I put myself in that cave in this song, a Tuesday in November, having told nobody, nothing left to remember. And the Lord came the same way He came to Elijah. If you are at the cave right now, hear the verse this song hangs on: He is coming for you with bread and with water. He is not done with His sons and His daughters. The still small voice is for the broken-through.

*Run to the Finish* pulls me back to my feet with Paul's last words, *I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith* (2 Timothy 4:7), and *Heart of a Pilgrim* reminds me I am *a stranger and a pilgrim on the earth* (Hebrews 11:13; 1 Peter 2:11), not building my city on the road but carrying the lamp toward home.

## Who This Is For

This is for the worn-down believer in the driest season of their life, still walking when everyone would understand if they stopped.

For the one who got the diagnosis. The one who lost the work, the marriage, the brother, and still laced up the next morning. *I Keep Walking* is your anthem, built on Isaiah's promise that *they who wait for the Lord shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings like eagles, they shall run and not be weary, walk and not faint* (Isaiah 40:31). Not by your strength. By His, showing up right where yours ran out.

And the record ends where every long trail ends, *Home in the Morning.* The road is over, the morning is breaking, and the Lord at the door says the only words worth walking a whole life to hear: *well done, good and faithful servant* (Matthew 25:21). You did not walk it on your own strength. You never could have. You walked it on His, and it carried you all the way home.
