There is a name the Scriptures give to the night of the Exodus, and once you know it, you cannot un-know it. That night is called *leil shimurim*, a night of watching. A night of vigil. The Hebrew comes from the root that means to keep, to guard, to watch over. It was a night Yahuah kept watch to bring His people out, and in return, it became a night His people were to keep, a watching-night, all the generations after.

A whole nation, awake in the dark, watching. Not because they had insomnia. Because they were *ready.*

## Dressed to run in the middle of a meal

Look at the posture they were commanded into, because it is one of the strangest scenes in all of Scripture. This is a feast, roasted lamb, bitter herbs, bread. But nobody is reclining. Nobody has kicked off their shoes and settled in. The instruction was explicit: eat it with your loins girded, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand. Eat it *in haste.*

Belt your robe up so your legs are free to run. Lace your sandals, the very thing you take off to rest, you keep on. Grip your walking staff while you eat, as though the road could start at any second. This is not the body language of people enjoying dinner. This is the body language of people who have been told the door could open at any moment and the only ones who leave are the ones already standing.

And the bread tells the same story. There was no time to let it rise. That is the whole reason for the unleavened bread, *matzah*, flat and quick, dough hurried out of the pan before the leaven could do its slow work. Deliverance was going to come so fast that a fully-baked loaf would make you late. So they ate the bread of haste, standing up, dressed for the road, watching the dark.

## Watching is its own kind of worship

I need you to feel how much faith that took, because it is easy to romanticize now that we know how the story ends. *They* did not know. They were still slaves when they laced those sandals. Pharaoh had not let them go. Nothing outside that house had changed. The oppression was still the oppression. And in the middle of it, on the word of Yahuah alone, they got *dressed to leave.*

That is worship. Not the singing kind, the readiness kind. Getting your body into the posture of a promise that your eyes cannot yet confirm. Putting your sandals on before there is anywhere to walk. Standing up to eat because you have decided the word is going to come, even though the room is still dark and the master still holds the whip.

> Faith in the dark does not always look like a shout. Sometimes it looks like a household eating dinner with their coats on, refusing to get comfortable in a bondage they have been promised is ending.

There were surely people in Egypt that night, in unmarked houses, sleeping soundly, certain nothing was about to change because nothing had ever changed. And there were these, belted, shod, staff in hand, bread half-baked, awake. The difference between them was not information. It was *posture.* One group treated the promise as real enough to dress for. The other did not bother.

## The staff in the hand of the weary

Let me sit with the ones who are tired, because I know some of you are reading this at your own leil shimurim, your own night of watching that has gone on so long you are not sure you can keep your sandals on one more hour.

Hear me. The command to be ready was never a command to be anxious. Watching is not the same as pacing. Israel did not stay up that night wringing their hands, trying to force the dawn. They stayed up *fed.* They ate the lamb first. They were nourished, marked by blood, sheltered under the covering, and *then* they watched. Readiness rests on a full stomach and a marked door. You are not asked to manufacture the deliverance by staying tense enough. You are asked to eat the Lamb, trust the blood, and keep your shoes on.

And notice, the staff was already in their hand. The tool for the journey was issued *before* the journey started. Whatever Yahuah is about to call you out into, He does not hand you the equipment at the border. He puts the staff in your grip while you are still standing in the old house, in the old dark, so that when the word comes you are not scrambling for what you need. You already have it. You have been holding it this whole tired night without knowing it was for the road.

## When the word comes, you will not have time to prepare

Here is why the posture mattered, and why it still does. When the cry went up at midnight and Pharaoh finally broke and sent them out, the text says they were thrust out so suddenly there was *no time*, no time even to finish the bread. The moment of deliverance, when it came, came *fast.* The people who walked out into freedom that night were not the ones who ran to get ready when they heard the shout. They were the ones already dressed when the shout came.

That is the whole reason for the watching. Yahuah's deliverances have a way of arriving all at once after a long, dark wait, and they do not leave much runway. Yeshua Himself told His people to keep their lamps burning and their waists belted, like servants watching for their master, so that whenever He knocks they open the door *at once.* It is the same posture. Sandals on. Staff in hand. Bread ready. Watching the dark.

So do not let the length of the night talk you out of your readiness. Do not kick off your sandals and decide the promise was a rumor because the door has not opened yet. Stay dressed, beloved. Stay fed on the Lamb, marked by the blood, staff already in your hand. This is a night of watching, and the God who is keeping watch over you is far more awake than you are.

The word is going to come. When it does, it will come fast. Be standing.
