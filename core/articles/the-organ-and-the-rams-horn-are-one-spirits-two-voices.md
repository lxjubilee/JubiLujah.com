Stand in the right room on a Sunday and you'll hear a Hammond B3 organ swirl up under an altar call until the whole place is on its feet. Stand in a different room and you'll hear a ram's horn split the morning open over a gathering that keeps the ancient appointed times. For about a hundred years those two sounds have kept to their own buildings, as if they were playing two different songs, for two different peoples, headed two different directions.

I made *Hebrew Roots Rising* because I stopped believing that. The album's second track says it as bluntly as I know how, and it's the sentence the whole record is built to prove:

> The Hammond and the shofar are the same Ruach's two voices.

## The roots never went to sleep. She did

The opening track, *The Roots Wake*, sets the frame. It pictures the Body of the Messiah dozing in the pew for generations while, down beneath the floorboards, the root system kept the heat true. That image matters, so let me be careful with it. The roots didn't wake up because they'd been dead. They were awake the whole time. It was the Body who slept on top of them, forgetting what she was drinking from.

And **Yahuah** is the Gardener who never once stopped the watering. That's the good news under the whole album. The recovery we're living through is not us inventing something clever. It's a family remembering an inheritance that was always in the account.

## The tree she forgot she was joined to

The theological spine of the record is Romans 11, and it runs through the track *Body Remembers Her Tree*.

Paul's picture there is exact and humbling. There is an ancient olive tree. Some natural branches were broken off, and wild branches, that's the nations, that's most of us, were grafted in. And Paul turns and warns the grafted branch: "do not be arrogant toward the branches... it is not you who support the root, but the root that supports you" (Romans 11:18). For centuries the Body was told the root was old, the branches were now the whole story, the tree could be left behind. Romans 11 never left the page. The grafted branch is still held up by a root it did not plant.

So when the album shouts "the Body remembers her tree," it isn't nostalgia. It's Romans 11 waking up in a congregation that had quietly stopped reading it.

## Two voices, one breath

Here's where the Hammond and the shofar come in, and why I gave them a whole song.

The organ is the voice of a stream of the church that has always known the Spirit falls, that worship can go from a whisper to a roar, that **Yahuah** still moves on a room. The shofar is the voice of a people who never let go of the appointed times, the Hebrew of the Scriptures, the feasts and the fasts and the ancient shape of the calendar. For a long time each side eyed the other. And I've become convinced they are two instruments of the same Spirit, breathing through two horns.

The album makes that case with a story it dares to tell out loud in track eight, *Acts 2 in the Upper Room*. A Pentecostal pastor realizes, after thirty years of preaching Acts 2, that the day the Spirit fell, "when the day of Pentecost arrived" (Acts 2:1), was the Hebrew Feast of Weeks, **Shavuot**, the fiftieth day, set on the calendar centuries before by **Yahuah** Himself (Leviticus 23:15-16). The tongues of fire fell on an appointed time. The Pentecostal fire and the Hebrew feast were never two events. They were one room. The Spirit He poured out is the same Spirit who wrote the calendar it fell on.

I'm not asking either side to give anything up. I'm saying the organ and the horn were always meant to be in the same band.

## The remnant nobody wrote down

An album about recovery could get triumphant and forget the cost. This one doesn't, and I love it for two quieter tracks.

*Daniel Prayed Toward Jerusalem* remembers a man in exile who kept his windows open toward home and knelt three times a day, even when a royal decree made it a death sentence (Daniel 6:10). That's what faithfulness looks like in a foreign land, not loud, just oriented. Facing home when everything around you faces the other way.

And *The Long Way Home* honors the believers in every century who saw the Hebraic roots and reached for them tenderly, whose names are in no book we own. The song leans on the great roll call of faith in Hebrews 11, the ones "of whom the world was not worthy", and the cloud of witnesses that surrounds us (Hebrews 12:1). Most of them walked a long dusty road no one recorded. **Yahuah** saw every torch they carried. The recovery has a genealogy, and it's longer and more faithful than we know.

## Rebuilding what was ruined

Two more tracks give the album its working hands.

*Nehemiah's Wall Goes Back Up* takes that scene of a man riding the broken wall at night by torchlight, surveying the rubble, and then organizing a whole city to build, half the people working, half the people guarding, everyone with a tool in one hand (Nehemiah 4:17). Recovery is not a mood. It's masonry. Stone by stone, and never alone.

And *Living in the Rebuilt House* lands where Paul lands in Ephesians 2, no longer strangers and aliens, but "members of the household of God," built on a foundation with **Yeshua** as the cornerstone the builders rejected (Ephesians 2:19-20). The wilderness season is over. The Father rebuilt the house. And the house is big enough that the Hammond can play the doxology on the kitchen-table chair while the shofar waits on the mantel for the next time it's called.

## Who I made this for

I made *Hebrew Roots Rising* for the believer who has felt, in the quiet, that something got lost somewhere, that the faith they inherited was pulled up out of a soil it forgot it grew in. You're not imagining it, and you're not being divisive to notice. The root is real, and it has been holding you up the whole time you didn't know its name.

And I made it for the two rooms that have been eyeing each other across a hallway for a hundred years, the ones with the organ and the ones with the horn. Hear the album's whole argument: you are not two songs. You are one Spirit's two voices, and He has been trying to get you into the same room so the whole Body can finally get up off the pew and stand on her feet.

The Gardener never stopped watering. The roots were awake the whole time. It's morning now, and the Body is rising.
