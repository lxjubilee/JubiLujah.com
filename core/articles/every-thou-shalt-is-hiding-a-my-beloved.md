I need to confess how I used to read the Bible, because it's the reason this album exists.

I read the commands of God like a debt. Like a bill that came in the mail with my name on it and a total I could never quite pay. Every "Thou shalt" landed on me as a wall, one more brick between me and an ordinary, unbothered Tuesday. I could recite the instructions. I just experienced them as weight.

And then one Sabbath morning, sitting over challah crumbs with the scroll open, something happened that I can only describe the way the album describes it: the ink began to lift off the page like sparks. The wall I'd been staring at turned out to have hinges. *Torah Sings Back* is the record I made to tell you what I heard when the law finally opened its mouth and sang.

## The wall was a window all along

The opening track states the whole thesis in its first breath. I read the words I thought I'd seen, and discovered I'd never really seen them. What I had filed under "law" was written in the grammar of love. That's the line the whole album turns on:

> What I thought was law is a wedding song.

Here's the reframe, and I want to be careful and clear as a teacher about it. The Hebrew word **Torah** does not primarily mean "law" in the cold, courtroom sense. At root it means *instruction*, *teaching*, the guidance a father gives a child he adores. When you read it that way, the commands stop sounding like a fence and start sounding like a hand on your shoulder pointing you home. The wall was a window all along. I had just never walked up close enough to look through it.

And **Yeshua** is the One who handed it back to me brand new. He said plainly He did not come to abolish the Torah but to fulfill it (Matthew 5:17), not to throw the instruction out, but to live it out in front of us and then move it inside us. When I finally believed that, every page changed color.

## Delight is not a thin thing

Track four is called *Delight, Delight*, and it fixed a lie I'd believed about joy.

I used to think delight was flimsy, sugar on a counterfeit grin, the kind of shine that the first storm sweeps off. But then I read David. Psalm 1 says the blessed one's "delight is in the law of the LORD, and on his law he meditates day and night" (Psalm 1:2). The great Torah psalm says it over and over, "I delight in your law" (Psalm 119:70). And David was not a man untouched by storms. His delight was iron-deep, a joy the weather could not reach, because it wasn't built on his circumstances. It was built on the Instruction of a God who never lied to him.

The bridge of that song reaches for the oldest image in the book: the Word is honey. "Sweeter than honey and drippings of the honeycomb" is how the psalmist describes the ordinances of **Yahuah** (Psalm 19:10). Not a diet. Dessert. When was the last time you approached a command of God expecting it to taste like honey? I hadn't. Now I can't unremember it.

## The command that is a kiss

This is the heart of the record, and it's why I named the whole thing around one song: *Mitzvah Is The Love-Line*.

People told me the mitzvot, the commands, were a wall. A pile of chains. There is even a beloved tradition that counts them at six hundred and thirteen, and I'll flag that number as exactly that, a rabbinic tradition rather than a verse you can point to, but the point stands: to a certain kind of ear, that sounds like six hundred and thirteen ways to fail. That's how I heard it, too. Until I picked the scroll back up slowly and re-read the lines I thought I knew, and I found a Father bending down to tie the fringe on His child's garment with His own hands.

That's the whole song, and I'll say it as plainly as it deserves:

> Every "Thou shalt" is a "My beloved."

Every instruction is covenant intimacy in disguise. He commanded rest, because He loves you and knows you were killing yourself trying to earn a Sabbath He wanted to give you free. He commanded remembrance, because love keeps saying *don't forget what I did for you*. He bought your rest by being slain, and He bought your joy in the storm and the rain, and then He handed you the commands not as "obey me" but as "love you, love you, love you." Once you hear a command that way, you stop trying to escape it. You lean in.

## What only the Spirit can do

I don't want to make this sound like I merely adjusted my attitude and everything softened. It went deeper than that, and two tracks tell the truth about it.

*Stone To Flesh* is built on Ezekiel's promise: "I will give you a new heart... I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh" (Ezekiel 36:26). Because here was my real problem. For years I could read the Torah end to end and not feel a single line. Stone in the chest, stone in the spine. No amount of trying loves a scroll. It took the **Ruach HaKodesh**, the Spirit, moving in soft and slow, chisel work no one else could do, to make a heart that could actually delight in what it read.

And *The Law On The Heart* stands on the promise that gathers the whole album up. Jeremiah heard **Yahuah** say, "I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts" (Jeremiah 31:33), the New Covenant, quoted again in Hebrews 8:10. Notice: He never says He'll throw the instruction out. He says He'll relocate it. Off the stone tablet on the wall and into the beating chest. **Yeshua** sealed that move with His own blood. The song puts it the way I'd want it sung in every congregation:

> Not a tablet on the wall, a Torah singing in the chest.

## Who I made this for

I made *Torah Sings Back* for the believer who has quietly decided that most of their Bible is homework. Who reads the commands with a tired, dutiful heart, bracing for a bill. Hear me: you have been reading a love letter as if it were a summons. The Father who wrote it is not standing over you with a ledger. He is bending down to tie the fringe on your garment.

And I made it for the person whose heart has gone to stone, who can recite the truth and feel nothing, and is frightened by the numbness. That is not a character flaw you have to fix by trying harder. It is exactly the thing the Spirit came to do. Ask Him. He does chisel work no one else can, and He does not rush it, and He does not quit.

The scroll is not a wall. It has hinges. Open it, and listen. Some morning, when you least expect it, the ink is going to lift off the page and start to sing, and you are going to realize that behind every "Thou shalt" there was a "My beloved" the whole time.
