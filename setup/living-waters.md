# Living Waters: The Over-the-Counter Soul Medicine Experience on JubiLujah

## What This Is

Living Waters is a section on JubiLujah.com where a person walks in, finds a medicine for their soul, and begins taking it, with no intake and no consultation. It is the over-the-counter counterpart to the prescribed Divine Medicine track (Nova's "doctor's visit"). The heart behind it: as a ministry, we take on the burden of helping people receive rather than blaming them for not receiving. Living Waters removes every possible point of friction between a person and the living Word.

This document is a companion to the Divine Medicine Formulary Standard. All three core tenets (tangible outcome, scriptural warrant, present-tense possession) and the category and tag systems defined there still govern every medicine that appears here. What changes is only the delivery posture: open shelf instead of prescription counter.

### The Nested Metaphor

- **Living Waters** is the front door: the inviting, life-giving doorway. It reframes the posture from sickness to life. You are not being treated for illness; you are being refreshed and given life. It carries deep Scripture without a drop of clinical or legal baggage (John 4, the woman at the well; John 7:38, "rivers of living water"; Jeremiah 2:13, "the fountain of living waters").
- **Divine Medicine** is what you actually receive once you step in. The two metaphors nest rather than compete: drink the living water, receive your medicine.
- We never use the words "pharmacy," "prescription," or any regulated clinical term on this over-the-counter surface. The framing stays spiritual and metaphorical throughout.

### The Welcome Line

The name intrigues; a short welcome line does the teaching once the user arrives. Working direction: "The Word of God is living water. Drink daily and let it heal you from the inside out." The beautiful metaphor stays up front; the prescriptive mechanics reveal themselves as the user steps in.

## Page Structure: The Two-Level Card Browse

The over-the-counter shelf uses the same category spine as the formulary, presented as a friction-free visual browse.

### Level 1: Category Cards

When the user enters Living Waters, they see the five top categories as visual cards (same five as the formulary): Identity, Fear and Anxiety, Guilt and Shame, Provision and Lack, Strength and Endurance. This mirrors the cross-site two-category playlist thinking but is its own Living Waters layout; it is a per-site experience distinct from the standard Thematic/Emotional playlist rows.

### Level 2: Subcategory Shelves with the Medicines

Tapping a category opens its sub-shelves (subcategory by condition, e.g. "identity in depression" vs "identity in pride"), where the actual medicines sit like bottles on a shelf. No intake, no gatekeeping: browse and drink.

### Branded Medicine Names

Each medicine carries a coined, branded product name, the way "Advil" is really just ibuprofen with a memorable face. The branded name leads; a strong functional subtitle rides underneath so the shelf stays honest. Format is branded-name-forward, because browsing a shelf is half the delight.

- Example: **Still Waters**, "for anxiety and a racing mind."
- Example: **Bold as a Lion**, "for courage when you feel small."

The name gives identity and memorability; the description keeps it truthful about what the medicine does. (These are illustrative; the real catalog names are coined per medicine.)

## The Medicine Album

Instead of a music album of tracks, a medicine is a **medicine album** of **doses**. This maps cleanly onto a music site: an album is a set of tracks; a medicine album is a set of doses, one taken per day. This turns a single medicine into a journey rather than a one-off, line upon line, and the pacing itself prevents mega-dosing.

### Dose Counts by Tier

Habit research (the landmark Lally study) puts durable habit formation at an average of about 66 days, ranging widely from 18 to 254; the popular "21 days" is a myth. The honest implication: no single short course cements a lifestyle by itself, so the dose count matches the medicine's job, not one universal number. The tiers below also lean on scriptural number symbolism.

- **Acute course, 7 doses.** A focused reset for a flare-up or felt need. Short, winnable; completing it builds trust.
- **Standard course, 14 doses.** The workhorse. Two weeks is long enough to feel real change, short enough that people actually finish. Completion beats length.
- **Maintenance course, 30 doses.** For chronic patterns and deep identity work; matches the familiar bottle-of-thirty mental model.
- **Breakthrough course, 40 doses.** Special-occasion and deep consecration. Forty is the scriptural number of testing, trial, and transformation (the flood, Moses, Elijah, the wilderness).

The medicine's tier decides its dose count.

### Frequency: Once a Day Only

All medicines are once a day, full stop. Rationale: users may enroll in more than one medicine at a time, and mixing once-a-day and twice-a-day courses across several medicines creates schedule chaos and quiet burnout. One dose, one daily reminder, keeps every medicine consistent. The user sets their own prescription time; a time they chose is a time they will keep. This is the OTC expression of the formulary's "don't overstack" interaction logic, phrased as consistency rather than an enforced clinical rule.

### The Three-Medicine Soft Cap

A gentle, non-blocking recommendation: "For best results, take no more than three medicines at a time." It hands the user wisdom while leaving the choice in their hands. If they ignore it, that is on them, and we have done right by warning them. This guards against the same mega-dosing and burnout the formulary warns about.

### App Required (Framed as Care)

Medicine albums are taken through the Jubilee app, which is where the daily reminder and the private space to receive live. The requirement is stated plainly and framed as part of the care, not a paywall or a catch. Working line: "This medicine is taken through the Jubilee app, your daily reminder and your private space to receive it." A medicine album cannot be run without the app.

## The Daily Dose Flow (Pop-Up)

At the user's chosen time, the app surfaces the day's dose. The flow walks the person through the exact order faith moves in: hearing, speaking, receiving.

### 1. The Confession to Speak

The pop-up presents the confession for the day, built on the Scripture being stood upon. Per the tenets, it names a tangible outcome, is bound to its scriptural warrant, and is written in present-tense possession. The user is invited to speak it aloud. (Speaking aloud is the mechanism; the mouth is where confession is made, Romans 10:10.)

### 2. The Music (Offered, Never Forced)

The dose offers its purpose-written song to play. We recommend it but never require it. The song is structured in three movements (see next section) and is what carries the person past the mind's resistance into receiving.

### 3. Snooze (Grace with a Backbone)

The user may snooze a dose up front, because people are genuinely busy. Snooze is capped: after a set number of snoozes the dose gently rolls to the next day, so snooze never quietly becomes skip.

### 4. The Closing Affirmation

A final confirmation screen reads, in effect: "I believe that I have this now." The user confirms by tapping the closing button labeled **It Is Finished**.

"It Is Finished" is chosen deliberately over any alternative. It is the ultimate present-tense-possession statement and it is what Jesus declared from the cross (tetelestai, John 19:30): not "it will be finished," not "it is getting better," but done, paid in full. Tapping it is the user agreeing with the finished work; the receiving happens in the tap. It is the amen of the whole process.

### Tracking

Three signals are captured for each dose, doubling as adherence and efficacy data:

1. The confession was spoken.
2. The music was played.
3. "It Is Finished" was tapped.

## The Purpose-Written Song (Three Movements)

Each medicine has a song written to help the person emotionally receive the confession. Music does what willpower cannot: it moves the heart past the mind's resistance, which is the whole receiving problem (David's harp moved the tormenting spirit; worship shifts the atmosphere). The song is built in three movements that mirror how receiving actually happens.

1. **Verse, the Scripture.** The raw Word, sung close to the text. This is the warrant, the legal ground: "thus says the Lord" established before the person is asked to claim anything. Faith comes by hearing (Romans 10:17), so they hear it first.
2. **Chorus, the confession.** First person, present tense, the exact medicine verbiage. This is the hook that lodges in memory and comes out of the mouth without effort. Example shape: "I am free, my debt is cancelled, it is done in me now." This is what they hum hours later, dosing without trying.
3. **Bridge, the emotional turn.** The "it is mine now" moment. Musically this lifts and swells; it is where trust breaks through resistance and the heart finally agrees with the mouth. The receiving happening in real time.

**Governing rule: the song resolves, it does not yearn.** Most worship music sits in longing ("I'm waiting, I'm reaching"). This song lands in possession: settled, arrived, done. The music itself is the amen, not the asking. This directly reinforces the potency tenet.

## Effectiveness Enhancements

These ten enhancements make Living Waters more effective at the one thing that matters, helping people actually receive, and extend its reach to users everywhere. They are ordered by impact. Each is tied to the part of the experience it strengthens. Several are expected to harden into core features.

### 1. Hands-free, audio-first dosing

The entire dose can be taken by voice and ear: the app reads and plays the Scripture, prompts the person to speak, plays the song, and accepts a spoken "It Is Finished." This meets people in the car, on a walk, or lying in bed with their eyes closed, which greatly expands when a dose can actually happen. It also makes speaking aloud feel natural rather than self-conscious, which is the exact gate to receiving. Strengthens the daily dose flow and the speak-aloud mechanism.

### 2. Completion moment with testimony capture

Finishing a 7, 14, 30, or 40 dose course triggers a real graduation moment that both celebrates and asks the key question: did it manifest? The testimony is captured and fed into the Backstage section and the Testimony Repository. This is the harvest of Tenet 1, it reinforces the user's own faith, and it supplies the whole ecosystem with genuine breakthroughs. Completes the medicine album journey.

### 3. The Medicine Cabinet

A private space holding every medicine the person is taking, has completed, and wants to keep on hand. A finished course becomes a bottle they can re-dose the moment that symptom returns, with no re-browsing. Ownership like this turns a one-time visit into a lifelong rhythm; a personal shelf people build is a shelf they return to. Extends the medicine album into a persistent, owned collection.

### 4. The daily streak, framed as covenant not performance

Consecutive days are tracked, but framed as faithfulness and consistency (Joshua 1:8, meditating day and night), never as guilt. A missed day invites grace-forward re-entry rather than a broken-streak punishment, because legalism would poison the very thing being built. Adherence is the whole game; the framing is what protects it. Reinforces the once-a-day rhythm without turning it into law.

### 5. Gift a medicine, send living water to someone

A user can send a specific medicine to a friend who is struggling, riding on the site's existing share and QR tooling, with a note such as "I am believing this for you." This turns every user into a minister and makes intercession tangible, and it is one of the strongest growth loops for reaching people everywhere: one person's breakthrough becomes another person's doorway. Extends Living Waters outward into relationship.

### 6. Sample the water before committing

Anyone can take a single free dose of any medicine before enrolling in the full album. It drops the commitment barrier to almost nothing, and a genuinely good first dose sells the course far better than any description. Taste and see, then commit. Lowers the friction on the browse-and-select step.

### 7. A gentle "not sure what you need?" finder

Some people know they are hurting but cannot name the category. One optional, instant tap on the landing page points them to the right shelf. It stays completely friction-free and never becomes an intake, so it solves discovery for the confused without turning the over-the-counter door into a consultation. Supports the two-level card browse.

### 8. Lock-screen and widget dosing

The day's confession surfaces as a lock-screen widget or a rich notification the person can speak without even opening the app. This reduces friction to near zero and keeps the Word in front of their eyes throughout the day, so it works on them even between doses. Extends the daily dose flow and reminder.

### 9. A bridge to the prescribed track

When someone keeps returning to the same category, or a course clearly is not landing, the app gently offers the deeper Nova consultation: "Want a prescription made just for you?" This makes Living Waters the natural on-ramp to the prescribed Divine Medicine system, and it genuinely serves the person who needs more than the open shelf can give. Connects the OTC door to the prescribed track.

### 10. A community testimony wall

Each medicine can show, truthfully and opt-in only, how many people have taken it and real testimonies of what manifested, honoring the site's no-fabrication rule. Faith is contagious, and seeing others receive the very same medicine builds the expectancy that helps a person receive it too. It also feeds Backstage with a steady stream of real stories. Reinforces receiving through witnessed breakthrough.
