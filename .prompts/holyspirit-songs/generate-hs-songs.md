
# CRUSADE WORSHIP STYLE DIRECTIVE

**Type:** Music production style specification
**Applies to:** All albums and singles designated Crusade Worship in the Jubilee music catalog
**Pipeline stage:** Melody concept, Melody blueprints/lyrics, Suno generation, Tahoma QA
**Status:** Draft for review

---

## 1. SCOPE

Follow this directive when producing music in the arena crusade worship tradition: choir-and-orchestra healing service music built for corporate singing, altar ministry, and live recording.

Do NOT apply this directive to band-led contemporary worship, scripture-to-song output, or any album not explicitly designated Crusade Worship.

---

## 2. NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES

1. Build every arrangement around a massed choir. The choir carries the melody. The band supports.
2. Keep tempo between 60 and 76 BPM for ballads and 84 to 92 BPM for gathering songs. Never exceed 92 BPM.
3. Write ballads in 6/8 or slow 12/8. The triplet lilt is the identifying fingerprint of this style. Do not substitute straight 4/4 on a ballad.
4. Restrict harmony to I, IV, V, vi with a ii-V-I turnaround. Reject any lyric or arrangement that requires a chord vocabulary a stadium cannot follow.
5. Modulate up a half step or whole step into the final chorus of every ballad. Two modulations are permitted on the album's still-point track.
6. Address Yahuah in the second person, present tense. Do not write about Him in the third person for more than one verse of any song.
7. Place long vowels (o, ah, ay) on all sustained notes. Never place a consonant cluster on a held note; a choir cannot sustain it.
8. Preserve live-room character. Keep arena reverb, audience presence, and unpolished edges. Reject any master that sounds studio-sterile.
9. Never tie healing, breakthrough, or answered prayer to giving, to emotional intensity, or to the atmosphere in the room. Point every lyric to Yeshua as the Healer.

---

## 3. INSTRUMENT STACK

Specify the following, in this order of prominence:

1. Grand piano (lead instrument, carries intros and turnarounds)
2. Hammond organ with rotary swells (continuous pad under everything)
3. Warm orchestral strings (entry at verse 2, never at bar 1)
4. French horns and soft brass (lift into choruses only)
5. Round electric or fretless bass (felt, not heard)
6. Drums, brushed until the final third, full kit only after the modulation
7. Tambourine and hand claps on gathering songs only

Exclude: distorted guitar, synth leads, programmed loops, trap or EDM percussion, ambient pads with no acoustic source.

---

## 4. VOCAL DIRECTION

- Cast a lead soloist against a full SATB choir. Alternate: soloist states, choir answers.
- Specify wide vibrato, upper-register belt on climaxes, sustained final notes.
- Blend Southern gospel and Black gospel phrasing.
- Prohibit breathy indie delivery, whisper vocals, heavy vocal effects, and rapid melisma runs that obscure the lyric.

---

## 5. SONG FORM TEMPLATE (BALLAD)

Build every ballad to this sequence:

```
[Intro]        piano alone, 8 bars
[Verse 1]      solo voice, piano only
[Chorus]       soloist, choir humming underneath
[Verse 2]      solo voice, strings enter
[Chorus]       full choir, brass lift
[Bridge]       one repeated line, 4 to 8 repetitions, building
[Modulation]   up a half step
[Chorus]       full orchestra and choir, sustained final note, cymbal swell
[Outro]        drop to organ pad and one voice
```

Metatags stay in English at all times, including on foreign-language tracks. Translate lyric content only.

## 6. SUNO STYLE BLOCKS

Use verbatim. Keep under 1,000 characters. English only. No artist names.

### 6.1 Block A: Atmosphere ballad (core sound)

```
Live crusade worship recorded in a large arena, slow 6/8 gospel ballad at 64 BPM,
massed 200-voice SATB choir with a lead soloist, grand piano, Hammond organ with
rotary swells, warm orchestral strings, French horns, gentle bass, brushed drums
entering late. Reverent, tender, expectant. Opens almost silent with one voice and
piano, choir enters humming, long crescendo, half-step modulation into the final
chorus, big sustained final note with cymbal swell, then drops to a quiet organ pad.
Southern gospel and Black gospel phrasing, wide vibrato, hymn-like four-part harmony,
arena reverb, audience presence. Unhurried, atmospheric.
```

### 6.2 Block B: Gathering song

```
Live arena praise, mid-tempo 4/4 at 88 BPM, large gospel choir answering a lead
soloist in call and response, bright piano, Hammond organ, punchy horn section, full
strings, live drums with tambourine, hand claps, congregational singing. Joyful,
declarative, triumphant. Simple singable melody, repeated hook, key change on the
last chorus, big ending. Traditional gospel and hymn revival feel, warm analog live
recording, crowd noise.
```

### 6.3 Generation settings

| Parameter       | Block A | Block B |
| --------------- | ------- | ------- |
| Weirdness       | 40%     | 40%     |
| Style influence | 85%     | 80%     |
| Audio influence | 15%     | 15%     |

Raise style influence on Block A rather than lowering it. The 6/8 lilt drifts first when style influence drops.

## 7. ALBUM SEQUENCE (12-TRACK STANDARD)

Sequence every Crusade Worship album to this liturgical arc. The album is a service, not a collection.

| Track | Function            | Block | Notes                                               |
| ----- | ------------------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Gathering           | B     | Corporate, name of Yeshua, singable on second pass  |
| 2     | Gathering           | B     | Declaration, call and response                      |
| 3     | Ascent              | A     | First descent in tempo                              |
| 4     | Ascent              | A     | Slower than track 3                                 |
| 5     | Ascent              | A     | Slower than track 4                                 |
| 6     | Still point         | A     | One repeated line, 6 to 7 minutes, minimal lyric    |
| 7     | Healing             | A     | Direct address, expectant                           |
| 8     | Declaration         | A     | Nothing is impossible theme                         |
| 9     | Testimony ballad    | A     | Solo voice forward, choir minimal                   |
| 10    | Ministry underscore | A     | Wordless or humming choir, loopable, unresolved     |
| 11    | Release             | B     | Triumphant, key change, full ending                 |
| 12    | Reprise             | A     | Restate track 1 or track 6, quiet, close the circle |

Enforce the reprise. It is what makes the record land as a service rather than a playlist.

---

## 8. MEDLEY CONSTRUCTION

Where a track is designated a medley:

1. Sew three to five songs end to end with no applause gap and no silence.
2. Order them by descending tempo and descending volume.
3. Modulate between segments, never downward.
4. Carry one instrument continuously across every seam, normally the organ pad.
5. Name the medley by its theme, not by its first song.

---

## 9. LYRIC VOCABULARY

Write from this narrow, repeated vocabulary. Narrowness is the design, not a limitation.

**Use:** the name, the blood, the presence, the Comforter, healing, mercy, glory, "I believe," "nothing is impossible," "You are here."

**Avoid:** abstract theological terminology, culturally dated slang, complex metaphor chains, any line requiring more than one hearing to parse.

Cap the chorus at four lines. Cap the bridge at one line.

---

## 10. QA AXES (TAHOMA)

Score each track 0 to 100% on the four standing axes, plus these two additions for this style:

- **Liturgical function (0 to 100%):** Does the track perform its assigned role in the album arc? A beautiful song in the wrong slot fails this axis.
- **Doctrinal anchor (0 to 100%):** Does the lyric direct trust toward Yeshua, or toward the atmosphere, the emotion, or the act of giving? Any track scoring under 90% here is rewritten, not regenerated.

Reject and regenerate on: straight 4/4 where 6/8 was specified, missing modulation, absent choir, sterile mix, tempo above spec.

---

## 11. FOR REFERENCE

The documented model for this style is the arena crusade choir-and-orchestra tradition, in which a music director led a large crusade choir supported by featured soloists, with services opening on solo voices backed by the choir. Album track lists from that tradition are built heavily on themed medleys and closing reprises. The observations on tempo, meter, instrumentation, dynamic range, and modulation in this directive are analytical, drawn from listening, not from published arrangement notes. Treat them as working craft rules and refine them against results.
